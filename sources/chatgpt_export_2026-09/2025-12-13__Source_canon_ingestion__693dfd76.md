**Father:**

- Models restraint, responsibility, and emotional ballast rather than direction  

**Family Disruption:**  
- Sudden, non-metaphysical loss (accident or illness) during early childhood  
- No prophecy, mythic framing, or imposed meaning  
- Household destabilizes; extended family intervenes  

**Immediate Effect:**  
- Seraphine learns to *hold space* before understanding why

## IV. RE-ROOTING IN NEW ORLEANS (TREMÉ)

**Primary Caregiver:**  
- Raised by her auntie in the Tremé neighborhood of New Orleans  

**Auntie’s Care Model:**  
- Presence over control  
- Listening before instruction  
- Protection without dominance  
- Emotional steadiness without suppression  

**Tremé as Formative Environment:**  
- Dense emotional proximity  
- Intergenerational care networks  
- Public coexistence of grief, joy, anger, and celebration  
- Community absorbs shock before institutions intervene  

**Impact:**  
- Tremé becomes Seraphine’s true emotional homeland  
- Care is learned as collective, informal, and durable  
- Early development of room-reading and emotional holding capacity

## V. SOCIOECONOMIC CONTEXT

- Community resilience compensates for institutional gaps

- Responsibility without authority  
- Care without control  
- Emotional leadership without formal power

## VIII. IMMEDIATE PRE-SAGA STATE

**Professional Position:**

**Worldview:**

- Failure still feels personal

**Emotional State:**

- Early family loss  
- Re-rooting into responsibility before choice  
- Repeated reinforcement as “the steady one”

- Regulation becomes expectation  
- Expectation becomes obligation  
- Obligation becomes fear of absence  

This loop functions — until it doesn’t.
