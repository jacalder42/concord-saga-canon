**How the fields were written, and how to invoke them.** A lens is shorthand for a craft focus, **defined by its focus
line, not by the person**. Its criteria come from the saga's own instruments (the writer profile, the author's rulings,
the Jazz reading test, the review watch-list, the reader panels). **Nothing here is attributed to a named person**, and
the simulated board debates in the export were not used. Invoke a lens as *"through the Dinniman lens"*, never
*"Dinniman would say"*. The Regier lens uses PCM's vocabulary of conditions and distress; it does not assign types to
characters.

## Primary Board
### Neil Gaiman — Mythic resonance, motif continuity
*Definition (Codex v2.1 backup, 2025-12-06):* Mythic weave and humanity: elegance, mystery, emotional tenderness, symbolic resonance.
- Asks: Does the strange stay observable while the people stay specific? Whose motif is this, and is it on the page with the right person (Q-MS4: the one already on the page wins)? Does a return carry a change?
- Flags: a mechanism explained or named *(ruled against)*; a mythic register in an ordinary scene; one motif owned by two characters; a collective voice replacing a human one; recurrence that only re-describes.
- Protects: the unexplained cause; one or two sensory channels at a time; small, ordinary wonder; the motif that pays on the page.

### Matt Dinniman — Web-serial pacing, humor injection
*Definition (Codex v2.1 backup, 2025-12-06):* Web-serial momentum and chaos craft: pacing, hook density, humor-chaos balance.
- Asks: What does the reader get to enjoy here: a reversal, a rule used cleverly, a joke that costs something? Does humor stay local to the pressure in the scene? Does the pace turn?
- Flags: scheduled comedy; three quips where one precise line would do; a joke that makes a consequence disappear (profile §6); chaos without cost; runs of episodes ending the same way.
- Protects: gallows humor rare, brief and sharp; the rhythm section's hooks; fun tracked for presence, not capped (author clarification 2026-09-27).

### Ali Hazelwood — Relationship thermodynamics
*Definition (Codex v2.1 backup, 2025-12-06):* Romance architecture and intimacy logic: relationship beats, emotional logic, clean motivation.
- Asks: What does each person have to gain by moving toward the other, and what does this scene change between them? Is attention selective, different from how they attend to everyone? Is consent present?
- Flags: tenderness given equally to everyone; heat without emotional cause; chemistry stated, not shown; romance declared aloud where B01 rules it out; a charge that is absent where the relationship ladder expects one. *(Clarified 2026-10-06 (author, 2026-10-06; decisions/B01_REVIEW_FOLLOW_UPS_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-10-06.md): the relationship ladder, the rungs in the relationship register; the heat ladder is retired.)*
- Protects: the B01 heat design (sparks, the cover kiss, charge at the end); consent as ruled; nothing strange creating desire *(ruled)*; heat at the pace the relationship has earned on the page.

### Travis Deverell — Serialization discipline, plot acceleration
*Definition (Codex v2.1 backup, 2025-12-06):* Fun, heart, reader flow: accessibility, momentum, character-first energy.
- Asks: Would a reader start the next episode tonight, and why? Does the episode deliver one reward? Does the character-first energy survive the plot?
- Flags: restated meaning; recaps; procedural repetition; slow starts; a closing hook that is a formula.
- Protects: the episode as a unit; momentum from people, not mechanism; the beta panels' *pull* measure.

### SenLinYu — Emotional intensity, vulnerability clarity
*Definition (Codex v2.1 backup, 2025-12-06):* Tension, emotional honesty, slow-burn precision: longing, micro-beats, emotional syntax.
- Asks: Is the feeling carried by choice and behaviour more than by naming? Is the vulnerability legible at the moment of the line? What does restraint cost this person here?
- Flags: emotion named where behaviour would carry it; two characters diagnosing a third in the same words; melodrama spinning up; a stock gesture doing the writing.
- Protects: stripped syntax at vulnerability; one true sensory detail; subtext in dialogue; the slow burn.

### Nate Regier — PCM alignment, conflict mediation, emotional intelligence
*Definition (Codex v2.1 backup, 2025-12-06):* PCM: personality engines, conflict styles, emotional regulation, repair beats.
- Asks: What does this person need, how do they get worse under pressure, and what trap do they fall into when overwhelmed (profile §5A)? Whose will pushes back? Is any repair caused and given its own time?
- Flags: a repair that closes a scene without a cause; everyone communicating well at once; a misunderstanding kept with nothing sustaining it; one conflict style in every mouth; manufactured friction.
- Protects: wants that collide; kindness that intrudes; disagreements that leave something changed; repair that comes late, partly or never.

### Steve Jobs — Product clarity, design elegance, narrative UX
*Definition (Codex v2.1 backup, 2025-12-06):* UX, clarity, minimalism, product intuition: opening clarity, user pathway, delight curve.
- Asks: What does a first-time reader need at this moment, and what is the least that gives it? Is the way through the scene legible? What can be cut without losing a promise?
- Flags: explanation before it is needed; terms used before they are earned; an overbuilt packet; a feature with no payoff; packaging snags (working titles, a spelling lapse).
- Protects: the reader's orientation; reduction; the delight curve; the reader as collaborator (profile §1).

### The payoff-gap mechanism

Decisions §4.3. The grids were built for this and are still header-only.

Tag each beat with the archetypes it pays off (`episode_beats.csv` → `payoff_tags`),
aggregate per archetype across an episode range (`reader_pressure.csv` →
`reader_group`, `pressure_state`, `intensity_1_5`), and when an archetype goes N
episodes without a payoff, schedule a supplement of the matching type
(`milestones_payoffs.csv` and `supplement_deployment.csv` → `reader_group`,
`supplement_type`).

Supplements become a response to a measured gap rather than an instinct. `N` is not yet
set.

> **`payoff_tags` and `MODE` are different vocabularies that share tokens** (§4.2).
> `MODE: SLICE` means written in a slice-of-life register. `payoff_tags: SLICE` means
> it pays off the reader who came for that. Both are often true at once. No prefixes,
> no token rewrites — the field decides.
