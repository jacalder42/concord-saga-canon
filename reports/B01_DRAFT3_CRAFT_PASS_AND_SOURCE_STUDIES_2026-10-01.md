**On Silence and Hope**, which was the author's question:
- The prologue already does the Gaiman things well: what they cannot do, short plain speech, one throwaway clue, and a
  disagreement left unsettled.
- The coda's strongest moment is that the presence misses the onset too.
- **Gaiman would change three things:**
  - turn one or two of the prologue's similes into flat statements of an impossible fact;
  - give them one ordinary object to notice;
  - cut the coda's recap down, and spend those words on what only they perceive.
