# Concord Saga — antagonistic creative review before B02/B03

**Status:** EDITORIAL REVIEW / NON-CANONICAL. Findings, repair options and recommended decisions are proposals. Nothing here releases an author hold or changes an existing ruling.
**Date:** 2026-09-27 UTC / 2026-09-26 America/Chicago.
**Reviewed snapshot:** `83ae4c463a1f02d9ee4d83f11c3babe4184454d1` on `main`.
**Request:** A fresh antagonistic review of saga structure and narrative before further B02/B03 development.
**Method:** Read current milestone rows, controlling decisions, character/faction cards, the existing B01–B03 architectures, and prior saga/pressure reviews. Attack the strongest current version. An unwritten scene is not proof of a missing scene; an unresolved dependency is not proof that the eventual story fails. Findings below distinguish demonstrated wording/design tensions from execution risks.

## 1. Verdict

**Retain the nine-book structure. Do not yet give its narrative execution an unconditional clearance.**

The saga has a viable large shape: local inquiry, competing responses to public crisis, and contested repair. The latest routes and decisions have removed several former structural errors. Baz dies in B03; Tahl's death belongs at the end of B06; Kade's public rise follows it; B07 is civic succession and departure; B08 has the wound circuit and complicity; Honey Island becomes known to antagonists only in B09 A3. Those are material improvements.

The remaining weakness is **the distance between a protected endpoint and a persuasive sequence of choices that earns it**. The repo is much clearer about where people must finish than about what they want, accomplish, misjudge and surrender along the way.

My hardest objection is this: **the saga could tell readers that solitary over-responsibility is destructive, then reward its principal caregivers by making them permanently indispensable to the universe.** The finale can resolve that tension, but it cannot rely on the word “shared” to do the work. Seraphine, Lucien and Caro need demonstrably different relationships to responsibility by then.

The most vulnerable books remain **B02, B05 and B08**, for different reasons:

- B02 risks replacing a dramatic pursuit with increasingly convincing observations.
- B05 risks becoming excellent parallel character material without a decisive book-level consequence.
- B08 risks confirming an itinerary the audience already trusts while Kade owns the only irreversible human event.

The earlier [saga-lock check](SAGA_LOCK_CHECK_2026-09-27.md) tests whether downstream work will steal protected material. This review asks whether readers will want the journey. Its “locked enough” verdict does not settle this second question.

**Readiness recommendation:** settle the six concise design directions in §6 before releasing B02/B03. They do not require a complete late-saga outline. Then expand Veil and test the choices at episode resolution. Do not use this review to justify another indefinite cycle of general audits.

## 2. What survives a hostile reading

These elements deserve preservation because they already generate useful dramatic tension:

1. **The truth lineage changes its failure mode.** Baz verifies, Tahl publishes, Kade mobilizes. That can be a causal argument about responsibility, rather than three interchangeable narrators.
2. **Seraphine's B01 miniature has an actual correction:** too late → tempted to intervene too early → timing and restraint. The locked architecture also gives Baz ordinary belonging and gives Caro/Elisabet independent lives.
3. **The Warehouse/Santa Fe mirror is humanly intelligible.** Tahl moves from causing possible consequences at a distance to refusing to send others where he will not go. His fatal contact is accidental, not an approved suicide or chosen sacrifice.
4. **Neon's separated cast can produce genuine dramatic irony.** What Elisabet knows, what Rex can prove, what Tahl risks, and what the NOLA group receives need not arrive together.
5. **B07's funeral is a strong change of pace.** Ritual, food, music, humor and grief can carry a book opening without a fresh attack.
6. **The Mending is an ethical and relational climax with human opposition.** It need not become a final larger explosion.
7. **The last LT reach can close the opening image beautifully.** Its invitation must preserve the survivors' agency and the reality of separation.

None requires a new city, new faction, extra death or larger cast.

## 4. Thirteen adversarial findings

### C01 — Seraphine's ending risks validating her original false belief

**Priority: highest. Demonstrated thematic tension; execution unresolved.**

[SeraphineIdentity](../canon/characters/SeraphineIdentity.md) identifies her false belief as dependence on her regulation for others' survival. Her [POV canon](../canon/pov/seraphine_vael_pov.md) expects shared care after the Mending. Yet the [Mira ruling](../decisions/MIRA_AND_SILENCE_HOPE_ORIGIN_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md) makes the Loom soul-powered, and the [chain/cost ruling](../decisions/LOOM_CHAIN_KNOWLEDGE_AND_MENDING_COST_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md) retains Seraphine as its continuous thread.

**The attack:** she learns to stop carrying everyone, then becomes the thing that must carry everyone forever. Readers may reasonably conclude that her supposedly unhealthy belief was simply correct.

**Recommended repair:** preserve the ruled transformation, but demonstrate what stops being hers to manage. People must retain disagreement, bad decisions, ordinary distress and work she cannot perform for them. Shared human systems must already succeed without her before the finale. Her choice must concern a bounded physical function whose demands and limits the story makes comprehensible, rather than compulsory emotional supervision of humanity. Those limits still need authorial design; this report does not invent them.

**Pass test:** explain, using actions and consequences, how a resident's freedom after the Mending differs from living under Seraphine's benevolent regulation. Also identify a pre-Mending success that occurs because she delegated or respected refusal.

**Why it matters to Veil:** B02/B03 must practice her developing alternative to over-responsibility. They cannot repeat B01's lesson with higher fatigue, then leave her unchanged until Loom.

### C02 — B02 still has stronger evidence progression than decision progression

**Priority: before B02 expansion. Demonstrated architecture gap in specificity.**

[B02 Pass 2](../proposals/B02_EPISODE_ARCHITECTURE_PASS2_2026-09-26.md) has useful ingredients: failed models, public language, useful and dangerous institutional data, consent conflicts, attraction, drift and No Reset. It also labels repeated Seraphine overload and Lucien misalignment. Many rows describe recognition or response; the act-changing decision is less concrete.

**The attack:** the protagonists become more informed while the world supplies the turns. “The baseline does not reset” is an important ending, but it could happen without their choices.

**Recommended design:** center one dispute over acting on incomplete information. My preferred version is a bounded civic warning or care/access decision with explicit uncertainty, a real beneficiary, a foreseeable cost and an institutional response that persists into B03. Baz's evidence and Seraphine's consent boundary should both matter to the same outcome. Exact site, actor and method remain to be selected from recovered material.

This is not a request for another catastrophe or early public rise. Anonymous MT can independently contribute without Tahl joining the core cast.

**Pass test:** remove that choice. If B03 begins with the same access, relationships, public behavior and investigative obligations, the choice is too weak.

**Counterevidence acknowledged:** Pass 2 already contains a grounding choice and institutional consequences. The task is to bind them into a legible causal line, not add a second plot or claim they do not exist.

### C03 — The saga may accidentally argue that public truth is dangerous and secrecy is prudent

**Priority: before B03 expansion. Execution risk created by repeated consequences.**

The [Warehouse decision](../decisions/B03_WAREHOUSE_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md) leaves the coordinates → crowd → delay chain partly hypothetical. Tahl did not know Baz was there. Dominion containment and unexpected Technarc retrieval are delegated working staging. Later, Kade's posts and authorization become hazardous.

**The attack:** accurate reporting draws a crowd; a rescuer dies; the reporter later dies; the successor mobilizes people into another disaster. The slogan may favor truth, while the plot repeatedly rewards those who would have kept quiet.

**Recommended repair:** preserve both the material value and the liabilities of publication. B03 Pass 2 already includes a warning helping someone; retain that as consequential evidence, not a token balancing line. Separate:
- what Tahl could reasonably foresee;
- what his post actually changes;
- what institutional actors decide independently;
- what the Rupture would have done anyway;
- what the official report alleges.

Do not use an official account as narrator-certified causality. Do not give Tahl sole responsibility for a chain he could neither know nor control.

**Pass test:** write the private counterfactual “without the post.” Some harm may change; institutional responsibility and the physical failure must remain distinguishable. If the author wants genuine uncertainty, bound what can and cannot be inferred.

**B03–B06 payoff:** Tahl's decision to witness in person should be a flawed response to real responsibility, not the narrative endorsing limitless guilt.

### C04 — The two deaths can make the truth lineage feel like a disposal schedule

**Priority: saga design before Veil is finalized. Execution risk, not a recommendation to undo deaths.**

Baz dies rescuing a child in B03; Tahl dies after accidental VT contact in B06; both are absent from principal witnesses. The recent [Tahl design](../decisions/TAHL_DEATH_SPECIFICS_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-09-27.md) gives the second death a strong Warehouse mirror.

**The attack:** a new male truth lead becomes beloved, dies at a trilogy boundary, and advances someone else's metaphysical or emotional story. Once readers spot the pattern, Kade's survival becomes a formal puzzle.

**Recommended repair:**
- Baz's rescue should complete a choice that belongs to his life and present investigation, while leaving unrelated desires unfinished. His only successful function cannot be dying nobly.
- Tahl needs a demonstrable reporting success and human attachments apart from the fatal clue. Give a rational reason the evidence or people need his bodily presence. His motive cannot be exhausted by “I deserve the risk.”
- Differentiate aftermath: Baz leaves an evidentiary and relational absence; Tahl leaves a public vacuum, an accidental metaphysical residue and contested inheritance.
- Let Kade break the pattern through costly living accountability, not merely by being prevented from dying.

The B01 child and the Warehouse child also require differentiation. The second child's survival should have human consequence, not function only as a diagrammatic answer to the first death.

**Pass test:** describe what readers lose when each man dies without mentioning his successor, another protagonist's growth, or the Mending.

**Open human obligation:** Tahl's parents/Chicago ties deserve a place in the funeral decision. A NOLA jazz funeral can stand while acknowledging who authorized it, wanted it, or could not attend.

### C05 — B05's major choices need to produce a book ending, not just a B06 forecast

**Priority: saga commitment now; scene design later. Residual risk.**

The recent decisions supply the Last Clear Act in A1, the private channel, Caro's Chicago crack, the long-distance commitment, Seraphine/Lucien's reconnection and consolidated Filament divisions. These are substantial. They do not automatically combine into one dramatic conclusion.

**The attack:** B05 is emotionally busy but removable: summarize its meeting and travel, then proceed to Santa Fe.

**Recommended repair:** make the “bad adaptations” idea specific enough to challenge. One chosen institutional/community arrangement should initially do something useful, then impose a cost or restriction the cast cannot simply undo before B06. Link an existing anchor—preferably Tahl's publication or the Filament division—to that consequence. Do not invent another B05 climax to compete with its relationship rewards.

**Pass test:** identify what a B06 character can no longer do because of a B05 choice, and why a sensible person supported that choice when it was made. “Warnings were ignored” alone is insufficient.

### C06 — B07's civic break is still doing too much work as an unspecified phrase

**Priority: before B07 expansion; protect its distinction now. Recognized open causal design.**

M42 rules the post, movement of people, NOLA break and Elias's subversion. [B07's qualified answers](../decisions/B07_REGIONAL_SCENES_AND_POV_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-09-27.md) keep Uptown provisional until the cause is designed.

**The attack:** a post “moves people,” the city “breaks,” and the protagonists depart. This can conceal any number of mutually incompatible plots. It can also spend Kade's first knowing complicity before B08.

**Recommended repair:** choose a civic mechanism with an owner: a shelter or route loses viable protection, an institution withdraws cooperation, or an allocation decision becomes untenable. These are alternatives, not events approved here. Map post → interpreters → concrete decisions → loss → accountable departure. Local people act within the chain.

Keep the moral ladder distinct:
- B07: public authority produces consequences and Elias begins capturing it.
- B08: Kade first becomes complicit in splinter violence.
- B09: he attacks Rex out of the ruled fear for Elias, then must choose differently.

**Pass test:** name the decision that makes remaining impossible, who benefits from it, who loses, and what the departing protagonists owe those staying. Do not equate leaving with moral escape.

### C07 — B08's wound circuit risks becoming compulsory confirmation

**Priority: major saga design risk; outline later.**

The [chain ruling](../decisions/LOOM_CHAIN_KNOWLEDGE_AND_MENDING_COST_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md) deliberately gives Tahl's message the shape and the circuit the particulars. The later Tahl design makes “Honey Island last” the group's private interpretation. These are useful protections, but they weaken a conventional discovery engine.

**The attack:** the reader trusts the destination. Three stops tell the group it was right. Kade makes the consequential decisions elsewhere, leaving the saga lead on a guided verification tour.

**Recommended repair:** each site's particulars must change what the group can do. Different scenery, local tragedy or technical vocabulary is not enough. Suggested functional distinctions, all proposals:
- Santa Fe changes what repair means in practice, including what it cannot restore.
- Mound City disproves an assumption about implementation or reveals a constraint that forces a choice.
- Serpent Mound makes that chosen course costly or exposed enough to generate the escape.

Do not move Seraphine's doubt to “the sites teach her the answer”: it grows from becoming the Loom. Sites supply material evidence and consequences; they do not supply a complete breathing-veil recipe.

**Pass test:** remove one stop. The subsequent plan must become impossible or materially different. A missing map confirmation is too little.

**Additional requirement:** the wound group needs a chosen success or mistake with a lasting consequence. Kade's complicity cannot carry the entire book's irreversibility for them.

### C08 — The antagonists may be too wrong to be persuasive

**Priority: saga-wide design direction before Veil. Demonstrated pressure in card language.**

[Technarc](../canon/factions/Technarc.md) is described as unable to adapt or learn empathy; [Choirless](../canon/factions/Choirless.md) permits neither moral correctness nor faction redemption; [Brightbreak](../canon/factions/Brightbreak.md) aggregates certainty without doctrine. Each has a coherent function, but scenes assembled literally from prohibitions can produce a rigged argument.

**The attack:** every proponent of order, prediction or calm becomes harmful; every relational protagonist is ultimately vindicated. Why would competent people ever support the alternatives?

**Recommended repair:** give antagonists a correct diagnosis of some real danger, useful capacity, and an initially understandable offer. Harm emerges from what they demand, conceal, universalize or sacrifice. This does not make coercion morally correct, redeem a faction, or amend its card.

Make the opposition operationally distinct:
- Dominion controls permission, access and legitimacy.
- Technarc controls services, measurements and dependencies.
- Choirless offers relief whose conditions erase choice.
- Elias gets cooperation by solving an immediate human coordination problem, then redirects the resulting trust.

These are editorial emphases consistent with the existing differentiation, not new powers.

**Pass test:** a sympathetic, informed person should have a reason to accept the first offer without becoming foolish. The protagonists must sometimes need something the opposition can genuinely provide.

**End-state obligation:** weakening an institution does not explain what happens to its workers, equipment or beneficiaries. Show representative consequences; a farewell scene for every named antagonist is unnecessary.

### C09 — Kade's interruption can be mistaken for redemption

**Priority: major endgame risk; seed human standards in Veil.**

M46 requires a concrete complicity victim; M52 requires the Rex attack and Tahl flare; M35–M53 carry reintegration, the night-sky payoff and LT reach. The flare's identifiable appearance is known by Rex. Do not assume every witness recognizes Tahl or receives the same information.

**The attack:** Kade does harm, a supernatural intervention stops worse harm, and a few days later he receives romance and privileged cosmic contact. The injured people disappear while the narrative congratulates him for stopping.

**Recommended repair:** distinguish interruption, changed action, accountability and forgiveness.
1. The flare stops the immediate action as ruled.
2. Kade makes an observable human choice afterward, including a cost to status or control.
3. Repair answers to people he harmed; they may decline trust or reconciliation.
4. The epilogue begins this labor. It does not certify its completion.

Lacuna's biggest relationship payoff remains available. It must be her choice, not compensation for his suffering or proof that he is now safe. LT access cannot function as divine acquittal.

**Pass test:** remove an explanatory “he understood” paragraph. Can the reader still see what Kade relinquishes and what accountability remains?

**Brightbreak problem:** a decentralized movement does not plausibly disappear because one rear confrontation ended. Its ruled dissolution needs ordinary distributed causes: incentives change, testimony travels, local circles stop reproducing it, practical alternatives become credible. Do not stage worldwide instantaneous conversion through Resonance. The precise duration and observable degree of dissolution require care.

### C10 — The surprise guide cost risks turning consent into a technicality

**Priority: highest alongside C01. Ruled surprise; fairness of execution open.**

The cost is fixed: Seraphine expects only her own loss; Lucien and Caro realize at the Mending that they must become guides. The one-at-a-time order and Elisabet's farewell are protected. [Mira](../decisions/MIRA_AND_SILENCE_HOPE_ORIGIN_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md) widens Seraphine's thinking; she does not provide an already complete solution.

**The attack:** the book spends nine volumes criticizing coercive care, then reveals “your loved ones must also go or everything fails” after the process is underway. Surprise alone does not make this a free choice. Caro's sustainable-care arc can end by demanding her final total self-sacrifice.

**Recommended repair:** preserve the late revelation, but seed relevant capacities, dependencies and the limits of the old guides' knowledge. Readers need retrospective intelligibility without advance knowledge of the price. At the moment, Lucien and Caro require comprehension, distinct motives, and enough narrative space to register a decision.

The story should acknowledge how constrained those choices are. If no safe refusal exists, do not describe the choice as costless consent. Their prior lives and desires must matter to the loss. Elisabet's farewell is a relationship event, not her endorsement of a cosmic transaction.

**Pass test:** separately answer “why Lucien?” and “why Caro?” using learned behavior, capability and their own values. “Their roles correspond to Silence and Hope” is a symbolic explanation, not sufficient dramatic evidence.

**Veil implication:** seed the people and competencies; do not seed their future guide job titles. The exact mechanism and staged reveal can remain late-book work.

### C11 — The mechanics can become a moral scoreboard, and caution can flatten the fantasy

**Priority: Veil guardrails now; individual physical models before causal staging.**

[Resonance v1](../rules/Resonance-v1.md) says the system does not reward virtue or judge, while later sections broadly connect suppression and weaponization with failure. The [systems audit](MECHANICA_RESONANCE_SYSTEMS_INTEGRITY_AUDIT_2026-09-23.md) already identifies unresolved coupling, transfer, meta ontology and observation issues. D5 remains a hold on causal physics.

**The attack has two sides:** good people feel correctly and therefore win; or every interesting effect is hedged away until the fantasy consists of electronics glitches and exhausted conversations.

**Recommended repair:** retain definite events with bounded evidence, physical affordances and costs, while allowing characters to misunderstand them. Ethical correctness must not guarantee physical success. A compassionate attempt can fail; a disciplined harmful actor can produce a bounded local result without proving the ideology sustainable.

Progression need not mean larger force. Readers can see improved discrimination, repeatable limited skill, deliberate restraint under harder conditions, cooperation and a newly understood limit. Do not replace an action promise with vocabulary.

**Pass test for each major event:** what happened; what someone did; what enabled it; what prevented more; who paid; what remains? A change in hue is insufficient if no action or consequence changes.

**Meta ethics:** a restriction on usable Intent or ascension must not become a shorthand for lack of personhood or entitlement to care. The altered-human/construct conflict is real and remains open. It must be settled before those scenes depend on it.

**Scope:** B02's public event and the Warehouse need their own bounded event models before dependent causal architecture is locked. Finishing the entire Neon transfer system is not a prerequisite for every ordinary Veil scene.

### C12 — Place can become scenic testimony, and the feint can betray the saga's care ethic

**Priority: design principle now; local particulars later.**

The current route decisions intentionally separate the cast. The B07 receipt pool is selective, not a required international montage. Lacuna builds the NOLA/Gulf feint; Seraphine is elsewhere.

**The attack:** foreign cities prove the crisis is global, then vanish. NOLA supplies culture, deaths and a diversion for the important people. Named locals are scenery with jobs.

**Recommended repair:** every used outside case needs a local want, a decision and a consequence that survives the principal's departure. Some competent responses should work without the core cast. Existing supporting characters should earn this work before new ones are added.

For the NOLA feint, ask who accepts the risk, how participants understand it, what protection or exit remains, and what Lacuna owes the neighborhood. The feint should exploit an adversary's inference, not casually expose civilians to buy the ascendants time.

Lacuna also needs an ambition, cost or relationship independent of regulating Kade. Her local command of resources is promising evidence of this, not yet its complete dramatic realization.

**Pass test:** tell a local's version of the scene in which the protagonist is a visitor. It should still contain a meaningful decision.

### C13 — The narrative can become worthy, repetitive and joyless despite “reward” labels

**Priority: monitor during B02/B03 architecture. Execution risk with strong existing countermeasures.**

B01's twelve Life/Reward units and its ordinary social continuity are genuine strengths. Later summaries are more heavily dominated by care, collapse, warning, institutional failure, grief and restraint. The existing B02/B03 drafts do protect pleasure and Baz's social life; preserve them.

**The attack:** everyone is an excellent thematic representative, every romance is a regulation lesson, and every enjoyable evening exists to make the next death sadder.

**Recommended repair:** retain incompatible tastes, lust, vanity, playful competition, mistakes without catastrophic meaning, delight in skill, and people who want things unrelated to repair. A successful joke need not de-escalate a crowd. An enjoyable scene need not plant a clue. Romance needs chosen pleasure and changing behavior, not merely milestones in a relationship status chart.

The C/E ladder is paced, but the long distance between attraction and kiss needs evolving obstacles and satisfactions. “Slow burn” cannot mean repeated near-identical withholding. S/L's separation and reunion also need an outcome beyond restored availability.

**Serial test:** a reader stopping after a short run should be able to name something gained, enjoyed, decided or newly understood. Do not prescribe a universal episode quota. A cliffhanger is not the only reward, and a supplement should not hold the missing cause of a main-story event.

**Packaging implication:** this is currently strongest as an ensemble speculative saga with intimate, civic and metaphysical stakes. Its architecture does not yet prove a combat-led or quantitative progression promise. That is a promise-to-page issue, not a request to add fights.

## 5. Revelation, agency and payoff checks across trilogies

### Knowledge must change available choices

| Stage | Reader gain to protect | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B01 → B02 | A bounded local prediction earns inquiry beyond NOLA; an independent distant case becomes credible | Claiming that B01 already proves global synchronization; relabeling the same observation as a new discovery |
| B02 → B03 | Drift and No Reset constrain practical choices; public evidence starts changing behavior | Another book spent proving that something is wrong |
| B03 → B04 | Tahl's identity attaches a human being to an already consequential anonymous voice | Treating an unfamiliar name as sufficient twist or erasing Baz's ownership of Veil |
| B04 → B06 | Useful public truth and flawed adaptation become personally costly | Endless warnings without decisions or benefits |
| B06 → B08 | The fragment gives a shape; the circuit supplies necessary operational particulars | Treating an itinerary as a mystery whose answer is withheld after it is already known |
| B07 → B09 | The reader can understand Elias's exploitation before Kade can relinquish it | Requiring Kade to be obtuse so the manipulation lasts |
| B09 | New repair becomes intelligible and costly; the LT invitation changes the closing relationship to the opening | Introducing the decisive capability and its justification in the same instant |

**Mira's timing is a genuine early-book dependency to bound.** Her full schedule is unresolved. Before B02/B03 are frozen, decide whether Veil owes any perceptible seed at all. My recommendation is not to force a named appearance into Veil; use only a compatible subtle seed if recovered material and the knowledge ceiling support it. The essential precondition is that she cannot arrive late solely to supply the winning answer. Her actual substantive entry can be later.

**Tahl's B02 brush needs fair concealment.** It is noticed by Silence/Hope, not Tahl. Preserve his anonymity and lack of named POV without making the narrative conspicuously withhold an identity a viewpoint should know. An anonymous mortal artifact and a bounded guide observation can serve different functions.

**Progression of the core trio:** audit choices, not symptom counts. Seraphine learns limits and shared responsibility; Lucien must change what his structures permit others to do; Caro must achieve something through delegation that endurance could not achieve. None should arrive at B09 with only a larger tolerance for pain.

## 6. Six recommended decisions before releasing B02/B03

These are my concrete recommendations for author review. They are **not recorded acceptance** and not new canon. They establish a working creative contract; their execution belongs in the existing architectures when the hold is released.

| # | Recommended direction | Minimum evidence before architecture is signed off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Seraphine's agency** | Every book gives her a consequential decision; Veil develops bounded, shared responsibility. The ending must visibly differ from compulsory emotional caretaking of everyone. | For B02 and B03: chosen action, credible alternative, affected person, persistent consequence. No extra catastrophe required. |
| **2. B02's engine** | Bind existing material around a civic decision to act on uncertain evidence. End with a gained capability or commitment and its cost, alongside drift plus No Reset. | One causal paragraph connecting the decision to B03's starting conditions. Select existing material before inventing an event. |
| **3. Warehouse responsibility** | Keep public truth both useful and consequential. Separate Tahl's contribution, institutional choices, Baz's rescue and physical failure. Do not certify the crowd hypothesis through an official report alone. | A private counterfactual with/without the post; credible expected benefit of posting; bounded event model before dependent causal staging. |
| **4. Earned opposition and competence** | Show why a sensible person accepts institutional help, and why a protagonist's alternative is costly. Preserve real successes in care, verification, engineering and public warning. | At least one materially useful institutional action and one limited protagonist success with consequences beyond the scene; no faction redemption implied. |
| **5. Finale fairness and accountability** | Seed capabilities and values that make the late cost intelligible, without revealing it. The flare interrupts Kade; his subsequent choices begin accountability. Romance and LT access do not absolve harm. | A short backward dependency list identifying what Veil actually owes Lucien, Caro, Seraphine and the truth lineage. Do not import Kade into Veil. |
| **6. Distinct book rewards and future obligations** | Preserve B01's ordinary-life standard, differentiate the two deaths, and require B05/B08 to change practical options through choices. Bound Mira's Veil obligation before the trilogy is finalized. | One non-substitutable turn and one substantial human reward per book as diagnostics, plus a clear “no early answer” boundary. These are not quotas or new episode counts. |

**My editorial recommendation is to accept these six directions.** They preserve the saga's approved shape while correcting the most expensive potential failures. They do not require ruling all proposed rows.

### What genuinely needs an early answer

- B02's act-changing choice and what B03 inherits from it.
- The writer's account of Warehouse responsibility, including what remains uncertain.
- What the trio's early actions must establish for the ending's fairness.
- Whether Veil owes a Mira seed; if not, an explicit allowance to defer her.
- The distinction between observational confidence and asserted physics in the relevant Veil events.

### What can remain deferred

- Kade's exact B08 city and Elias's location when he receives the opening report.
- Specific receipt carriers not used in Veil.
- Atlanta as Rex's optional stop.
