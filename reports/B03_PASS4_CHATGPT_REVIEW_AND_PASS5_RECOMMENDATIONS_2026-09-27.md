### Q1. The protocol's reinterpretation: a named decision (finding 1)

**Recommended:** show it in **E09**, *"Two Accounts"*, as **the protocol review's revised rule:
*advise and contain*.**

- **Who:** the protocol review board, the body behind B02's *"MANDATORY SAFETY REVIEW"*.
  - **Helena's analysts** propose it.
  - **Councilwoman Baptiste** argues against it, and loses on cost and on displacement politics.
- **The evidence behind it:**
  - Bywater's clearance became **indefinite displacement** when the pocket did not end (B02 E47), and
    it was costly to house people.
  - **No one was hurt in Bywater**, and a resident who stayed inside came through unharmed.
  - The standing zones hurt property, not people.
- **What the new rule does:** it advises residents to leave (voluntary departure) and puts up a
  perimeter. Mandatory clearance is kept for *"imminent threat to life"*, **judged by the models.**
- **Useful:** it ends open-ended displacement, and some people leave. **Dangerous:** no model forecasts
  a shock, so *"imminent"* is never declared.
- **The irony, stated and never certified:** the review cites Rosette's survival as evidence. Her
  respected refusal becomes, in institutional hands, a reason not to clear. **Seraphine hears it
  cited** [P]. Without that beat, the evidence is Bywater's lack of injuries alone.
- **E27** then shows the rule applied: the lofts are advised, and a perimeter is planned. **Baz asks
  for *"imminent"*, and no model supports him.**
