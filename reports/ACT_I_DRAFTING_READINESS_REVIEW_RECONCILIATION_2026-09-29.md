## Appendix: the review, as received (a recommendation, not a decision)

> This is ready to move toward drafting. The conflict revisions are materially better. I would make two small packet corrections and carry one substantial pacing concern into the first read.
>
> I checked the Veil conflict review, B03 E26, the Act I derivation report, and sampled prose packets E01, E04 and E14. This is not a complete audit of all sixteen packets or an independent validation run.
>
> The best improvement is that disagreement now changes behavior without automatically destroying cooperation. Lucien openly sending the grounds, Baz setting a condition while continuing the logs, and Kesi telling him about the question mark give these characters different ways to handle conflict. That is a better application of the Companions than making everyone secretive.
>
> Two corrections before drafting:
>
> * E01 brings up the coverage confession during resuscitation. The fixed sequence has Seraphine working on Dré, starting to disclose the unfinished renewal, and being stopped by Odile before the ambulance arrives. Unless the paperwork affects an immediate decision, that risks making her attend to her guilt during a child's emergency. Place the exchange in a credible lull or after the immediate resuscitation effort. Preserve the concealment without manufacturing implausible conduct.
> * E14 upgrades "finds no fault" to "not faulty." Its ending says the amplifier is "not faulty," although the scene establishes only that Lucien finds no fault. Keep the narrower wording. Also make the steady phone recording physically credible when its operator falls with everyone else.
>
> My main disagreement with the review is its confidence about B01's opening.
>
> It identifies the first externally directed action at approximately 56,000 words, then dismisses the concern because that fits the planned Veil opening. That establishes consistency with the design—not whether the design holds reader interest.
>
> Those opening pages can work through the death, Lucien's false report, anomalous events, competing explanations and developing relationships. But your original complaint was precisely that enjoyable neighborhood life and mysterious experiences were not producing enough forward movement. "No trigger fires" does not settle that complaint.
>
> I would therefore release Act I drafting with one clear review question:
>
> Before the public event, what specific outcome is the reader anticipating, and what are the protagonists doing to bring it about or prevent it?
>
> The answer need not be a faction. It must be more definite than waiting for another strange occurrence. Treat 46,000 words as a planning allowance, with permission to finish shorter when the material earns less.
>
> On the optional ideas, §6(b) actually contains five, rather than four:
>
> | Proposal | My recommendation |
> | --- | --- |
> | Bring Seraphine, Caro and Elisabet to B03's second line | Support, if their attendance is natural. Let readers enjoy the group together. Keep "last whole good day" out of the drafting brief; it invites farewell staging. |
> | Give B02's opening more initiative | Favor Baz initiating contact with Naima, if it creates an ongoing investigative effort. Visiting a rejecting block is useful only if he has a concrete purpose beyond demonstrating rejection. |
> | Keep B03's opening quiet | Accept provisionally. Quiet can carry anticipation and decisions; ten episodes should not become a waiting room for the book's main action. |

> | Turn Lucien's drift withholding into a bargain | Leave unchanged for now. The other revisions already diversify the conflict. |
>
> The pump-station research is an Act II prerequisite, so it need not delay Act I.
>
> My recommendation: correct E01's timing and E14's evidentiary wording, then release Act I drafting. The architecture has had enough refinement to deserve a prose test. The next review should judge sustained curiosity, character initiative, pleasure and pace—not just compliance with the packets.
