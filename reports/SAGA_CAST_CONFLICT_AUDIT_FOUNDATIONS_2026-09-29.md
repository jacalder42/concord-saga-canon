# Foundations audit: is the saga built on "perfect people, perfect care"?

**Scope.** This audit covers the milestone spine (`grids/milestones_payoffs.csv`, all 59 rows), the main-cast and
antagonist cards (`canon/characters/`), the relationship architecture (the ladders in `decisions/`, `proposals/` and
`grids/breadcrumbs.csv`) and the thesis lines. Episode beats for B02–B09 are covered by other auditors.

**Lenses.** The recovered Companions system (`recovery/CALDER_COMPANIONS_PANTHEON_SOURCE_RECOVERY_2026-09-29.md`), with
the 11-20 definitions governing (`decisions/CALDER_COMPANIONS_AND_B01_SHAPE_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-09-29.md` Q2):

- modes, not traits;
- integration is rare;
- conflict loops end in consequence, and repair is a separate event;
- *"each party has legitimate goals but incompatible methods"*;
- the Anti-Calder applies to protagonists too (Q8).

Also *1.7 Relational Physics* §1.7.19, which gives five end states: Convergence, Divergence, Rupture, Evolution and
Suspension.

---

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**The foundations lean heavily, but not entirely, toward "perfect people, perfect care".** The saga does have real
opposition, but it is placed in three narrow channels:

- **ideological antagonists**, who are care-gone-wrong (control, calm or certainty passed off as compassion);
- **one volatile protagonist, Kade**, whom every other card is written to regulate;
- **two protagonist errors**, Tahl's coordinates post and Kade's complicity.

The core ensemble has no wants except protecting others. Every flaw is some form of caring too much. Every
relationship that is designed to strain is also designed to be repaired, and apart from the deaths no bond among the
protagonists ends in Divergence or Rupture. The Anti-Calder principle holds that *"Every protagonist carries him. Every
antagonist externalizes him."* **The foundations externalize almost all of him.**

The evidence against the verdict is real, and is set out in §5:

- Veil's approved design lets the institution be right;
- M18 splits the carers against each other;
- Seraphine's own card says *"Calm imposed is violence"*;
- Elias walks away unpunished.

Each active row is classified by the **primary driver of its turn**: what causes the change. Secondary colouring is
noted where it matters. The four classes are:

  crowds, with no named target).
- **E**: a protagonist's choice or error with a lasting cost.
- **P**: the phenomenon, events or conditions.
- **C**: care, understanding or reconciliation.

### 2.2 Card-level findings

1. **No card has a Wants field.** This was already found by the pressure cards, and it matters more at foundation
   level. Wants are what collide. The cards supply fears, and fears pointed at other people's safety converge rather
   than collide.
2. **Every core Tier-1 ID card opens with a Hard Rule that forbids its own shadow reading.** Lucien, Baz, Caro,
   Elisabet, Rex and Trip all have one. Each is written as *"X is never [the ugly version]; it is care"*. They are
   guards against caricature, but they also tell every reader and drafter that the flaw is secretly a virtue.
3. **Leadership ceilings are locked for Elisabet, Rex, Trip, Lacuna and Kade.** Nobody in the protagonist cast is
   allowed ambition. The only character who wants power, reach or authorization is Elias (*"He wants **Kade's
   authorization**"*).
4. **The relational lines resolve the friction inside the card.** Examples:
   - *"Seraphine slows Caro without judgment"*;
   - Caro and Elisabet: *"mutual regulation through friction"*;
   - Lacuna and Seraphine: *"Mutual restraint ethic"*;
   - Trip with Lucien: *"boundaries respected without challenge"*.

   The method collisions (pressure cards §8) are real, but each card also prescribes how they end.
5. **Kade is designed as the object of everyone else's care.** His EBCI card:
   - *"Presence of Seraphine, Caro, Elisabet, or Lucien is required"*;
   - with Seraphine, *"Interrupts runaway amplification"*;
   - with Caro, *"Prevents collapse via connection"*;
   - with Lacuna, she *"Names manipulation"*.

   Seraphine's card makes her his *"moral north star"*. **The saga's structure is one volatile person and eight
   regulators.** That is the B01 "more considerate than you" contest, scaled up.
6. **The Veil trilogy lines tell the drafter the methods work.** *"restraint rewarded"*, *"action-driven care
   rewarded"*, *"clarity rewarded"* (pressure cards §0(b)). The costs are all placed in Neon.

## 5. The thesis

**The source.** The author, 2025-11-08: *"No matter how dire or the level of strife, these are stories of hope and
empathy, colored **equally** with splashes of conflict, humor, and sensuality."*

The writer profile renders this as *"coloured with conflict"*. **"Equally" is gone.** That is small, but it is
exactly the weighting in question. `canon/saga_overview.md` *Purpose* and all three trilogy summaries are TODO, so
no canon thesis line exists to correct.

**Does care become the answer to every problem?**

**Evidence that it does:**

- Mechanica §6: *"No ascension without empathy + Intent"*. The metaphysics rewards empathy, and the Mending (M33) is
  solved by self-giving.
- Han Wei's card: *"The world proves it [empathy] indispensable."*
- The finale's order: the fight is stopped by the flare, then comes the Mending, then the stitching back together
  (M35), then the handshake (M53).
- Kade's arc ends in *"repair through listening"*.

**Evidence that care stays costly and contested:**

- Seraphine's LOCKED moral frame: *"Calm imposed is violence"*; *"Care must not erase agency"*.
- The B04 *"too early"* failure (Neon N3, approved design): she pushes calm into a panicking crowd *"faster than
  consent allows"*.
- Mechanica §42A: *"Empathy is its medium, not its judge"*; regulation is *"structural, not moral"*; the veil is
  held jointly and does not obey her.
- M18: the carers split over *"how much risk, intervention and coercion care can justify"*.
- M40: Lacuna's kindness (*"she inspires Kade to post"*) starts the chain that breaks New Orleans. **Kindness
  intrudes, at saga scale.**
- The Mending's cost: Seraphine *"thinks only her"*, which is wrong, and she hides it
  (`BC-SERAPHINE-HIDES-PRICE`).
- Adversarial direction 1: *"Her ending must visibly differ from compulsory emotional caretaking."*

**Assessment.** At the level of ideas, the thesis is contested rather than self-congratulating. Care is argued over,
has victims and can be coercive. **But the argument is staged between protagonists and antagonists, not among the
protagonists.** Bad care belongs to Saeko, Virelli and Han Wei. The core cast's care is costly to themselves and
almost never wrong about, or harmful to, each other.

That is the structural problem. It sits in the cast design, not the thesis.

## 6. Verdict, with the evidence both ways

**About two-thirds of the way to "perfect people, perfect care"**, concentrated in the core ensemble and its
relationships. It is not true of the saga's antagonists, its metaphysics or its Kade and Tahl line.

**For "perfect care":**

- No main card except Kade's (and modestly Trip's) has a want that is not about protecting others.
- Every core flaw is caring too much, and each card's Hard Rule forbids the ugly reading.
- No milestone sets two members of the core ensemble against each other.
- Every designed strain among the protagonists is repaired, usually within a book, and none ends in estrangement.
- Tahl's confession is met with *"No one certifies blame"*.
- The one volatile protagonist is written as the object of everyone else's regulation.
- The Neon antagonists never touch a named protagonist.
- The saga ends on three care beats.

**Against it (real opposition exists):**

- Tahl's M11 post is the ruled moral engine: an error with a death attached.
- Kade's M42, M46 and M52 are escalating harms that differ in kind (consequence at a distance, estrangement,
  complicity, near-murder: `decisions/LOOM_SPINE_AND_ANCHORS_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md`).
- M43 and M44 are a ruled split between protagonists.
- M05: Lucien leaves Seraphine in grief.
- M18: the carers divide.
- Veil's approved design lets the Dominion be right about the clearance, and Lucien partly obeys it and keeps that
  from Baz.
- Elias survives unpunished.
- Seraphine's own card names coercive calm as violence, and her B04 design has her commit it.

**The raw material for a less perfect cast is already in the repository.** The foundations schedule it away:
repair is always designed, and the harmful half of each card is fenced by a Hard Rule.

## 8. Where the Companions system should enter

**Recommended level: the relationship architecture and the non-canon pressure cards. Not the canon cards, and not the
packets only.**

- **Not the packets only.** Packets derive from the architecture, and the architecture currently schedules the
  repairs: *"No one certifies blame"*, *"stands with Rex"*, the M57 reconnection, and the B01 packets' *"comes
  round"* and *"repair it, and go on"* (recovery §6.5). A packet cannot give a consequence lasting force when the
  next book's architecture has already repaired it.
- **Not guide mappings on canon cards.** The author has ruled to start less invasively (Q5), and the cards already
  hold the fears, stress behaviours and failure modes the system needs. **Any card edit should be limited to changes
  1 and 2** (a Wants line and less exculpatory Hard Rules), and those are author edits, not guide assignments.
- **Yes, as a design checklist for relationships.** Use two pieces of the 11-20 map:
  - the §7 conflict loops (Trigger → Reaction → Escalation → Break point → Consequences);
  - the §9 break states, with the 1.7.19 end states.

  Apply them to the eight or so main relationships in one register (change 11). That is where the foundations fail
  the lens: the relationships, not the individual cards.
- **The milestone spine needs only rewording, not the system.** Rows M18, M57 and M55, and the breadcrumb payoffs
  for Kade and Rex, can carry the changes as `proposed` or approved design. No ruled row has to change.
- **The Anti-Calder (Q8) is the diagnostic to run once over the core cards:** where does this protagonist enter
  *"Rigidity or refusal to revise"*, and who pays? The cards' answer today is "only the character".
