## 3. Act II as an act (the reader, as an editor)

- **Spine:** three people turn private unease into a written, falsifiable bet. The event arrives inside their window
  and proves each of them wrong in a different way, while an unnamed institution collects the same times they do.
- **It escalates in kind:** a street that stops, a ring that holds still, glass and injuries, then unreported hits
  coming closer together. The pressure goes from a reserved floor to a highlighted file, a man at a door, cases on
  fences, and a man in a good suit at the glass shop.
- **The protagonists act.** The one real stretch of pure watching is Lucien in E32.
- **Strongest stretch:** E21–E26, then E32. **Weakest:** E27, E28 and E30.
- **The editor's verdict: keep reading; Act II scores 4 / 5.** Its faults are sameness and too much warmth, not structure.

**On Seraphine:** she owns the told/untold log, which beats Baz's clean test and becomes the page's rule. She also owns
the ring-walking method and the case on the fence. Her ownership is weakest in E31–E33, and the act's discoveries mostly
go to the others. **Fixed in E34:** Ursulines challenges her theory (the grocer was not alone), and she now notices it
and writes it down with a question mark. It is hers to pursue.

**On Baz:** the reader found him close to too perfect, and the costs to him are mostly defused within the scene. The
end-of-act fixes cut the remaining omens around him, and Lucien's *ask me* now costs him.
