**Date:** 2026-10-01

recovered Jazz Framework (`recovery/JAZZ_FRAMEWORK_SOURCE_RECOVERY_2026-10-01.md`), to test whether the framework helps

**What it does not change:** no ruling, card, rule, profile, packet or grid, and no manuscript text. **It quotes no

## 1. What was read, and how

**The text:** the complete reading copy of draft 3 after the focused pass and the credibility repairs (ledger §342–§343),
read once, in order.

**The lens** was the forwarded review's four tests (returns, act transitions, rests, peaks), plus the author's own rules
from the recovery:
- *"Without effective syncopation and accent, it's just elevator jazz"*;
- the rhythm section as the accents and payoffs, and *"breadcrumb pace should be driven by our rhythm section"*;
- *"jazz is our structure not our product"*;
- the 2-and-4 pulse. This one is an accepted assistant reading, used as an observation only.

**The reader was given the earlier reads, so that it would not count known problems as new ones:** the beta panel, the
standalone read, and the focused-pass report.

## 2. Findings

**The book is not elevator jazz.** Nearly every chapter turns at least one expected beat. Its boldest is in Act III, where
the heroine does not do what the reader expects and a side character does.

**The flat stretches** are where each consequence arrives exactly as foreshadowed:
- E33–E34;
- E37–E39;
- E48's single ordered run of disclosures.

**Returns.** Most recurring elements return changed. The motifs of water, the plumb line, the Square, the column and the
door are among the book's best effects.

**What repeats is recital and description,** not whole scenes:
- the folder's facts, recited four times before E44's full telling;
- the case, described nearly word for word in E31, E39 and E48;
- the *None* signed twice, recapped inside the E46 and E47 peaks;
- the caseworker's voice taken up and set down, three times;
- one side-character tag line, shared by two men word for word and by a third in a shortened form.

**Rests.** This read splits the panel's "troughs" into two kinds:
- **Rests that change a relationship or a choice.** E19 and E30 are slow only because of where they sit. Keep them.
- **Rests that only recover:** E11, E43, and half of each of E33 and E35. Shorten these. Do not move them, because the
  days are fixed.

The costlier valley is **E41–E43**: three low-tempo pieces running, at the point where the disconfirmation run peaks.

**Peaks.** Act I's peaks put action before interpretation. In E36, E40, E46, E47 and E49, explanation arrives inside the
peak or immediately on top of it. E29's misreading arrives the same way, but it is the character's, so it stays.

**Cadence.** The ends of the acts vary well. In mid-act, **E37–E44 end eight times running on a quiet fall-off, six of
them on someone leaving.**

**The rhythm section:**
- **Humour and side characters never go dry, but the humour is nearly one instrument,** dry elder wit.
- **Sensuality is absent** for E32–E37 and E40–E45.
- **Candour clusters late.**
- **Hooks outrun payoffs.** The watchers' thread is hooked at seven points and paid off once before E48, and E24–E35
  carries no long-range payoff at all.

**Spotlight and call and response.** The spotlight rotates more than earlier reads credited. Both act seams hand the
unresolved line to a different character, from a different floor.

The pairings worth protecting:
- **E40/E41:** the same hour on two floors;
- **E44/E45:** two confessions made to a face;
- E12/E14;
- E21;
- E32.

**Solos nobody answers:** E15, E41 and E48. The ensemble also thins in Act III.

## 3. What is new

**The reader says so plainly: about two-thirds of what the lens finds re-finds known issues.** That shows the lens is
sound. It does not show it is needed.

**New and usable:**
1. **A rule for which watcher hints to keep, from the author's own sentence on breadcrumb pace.** Hints a side character
   carries land as accents. Hints carried by Lucien's private restatement read as exposition. The standalone read said
   to thin the senders' hints but gave no rule for which. This is the read's strongest contribution.
2. **Rests that change versus rests that recover.** Shorten the recovering parts; do not move rests. That is cheaper and
   safer than the panel's *move the rests*.
3. **A keep-or-cut test for refrains.** A return is kept if the element, the person or the cost changes. Re-reciting is
   a repeat even after the plot has moved.
4. **The cadence damage is located**, at E37–E44.
5. **Pairings to protect** by name, and **the Act II → III seam's late answer**, which is good delay and should not be
   fixed.

## 4. The read's view of the tests

| Test | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Returns | **Keep.** Make a re-description or a re-recital count as a repeat |
| Act transitions and spotlight | **Keep,** as a call-and-response map: who answers whom, and which solos go unanswered |
| Rests | **Keep,** with *changes or recovers* as the question |
| Peaks (action before interpretation) | **Fold into the line pass** as *no interpretation inside a peak*. It re-finds a known concern |
| The 2-and-4 pulse | **Drop.** The empathy beat is everywhere. The chapters missing a choice beat are the ones the rest test finds |
| *Added:* rhythm-section carriage | **Add.** Who carries each hook and payoff, and how many kinds of accent there are |
| *Added:* cadence by position | **Add.** Runs of five or more endings of one type in mid-act |

**The guard held.** The lens recommends no music on the page. Three small musical similes in the narration, all early,
are noted and left alone.
