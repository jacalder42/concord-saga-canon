# B01 redraft: plot summaries and a story tangibility review

**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Status:** EDITORIAL REVIEW, non-canonical. Written at the author's request, while he reads the manuscript by hand:

> *"I get the impression that we have good storytelling and good individual scenes. I wonder if we are thin on Plot... 1-
> Lets build some summaries of the book... 1 page ish whole book summary, then similar length ones for each act, and then
> finally a few sentences per chapter. 2- ... a story tangibleness review- ... asking all the questions a fifth grader
> would: who did that, to whom, what did the do, why did they do it, why should I care, etc. If there is a clear and
> tangible plot, I think the book will naturally keep readers more engaged, even if the elements are left mysterious.
> They still need to be noticeable to the average reader."*

**What it does not change:** no ruling, card, brief, packet, grid or manuscript text. **Nothing is edited while the
author reads.** It quotes no manuscript text.

**Where the summaries are.** They retell the private manuscript, so they are kept in the private manuscript repository,
`draft-notes/plot-review/`:
- `SUMMARIES.md`: the one-page book summary, a one-page summary of each act, and a few sentences per chapter.
- `ACT_I_CHAPTERS.md`, `ACT_II_CHAPTERS.md`, `ACT_III_CHAPTERS.md`: each chapter's fifth-grader card, the act's threads
  ledger, its thin stretches and its story question.
- `ACT_III_CHAPTERS.md` §5: an end-of-book fifth-grader test.

## 1. How it was done

- **Three readers, one per act, in order.** Each read **only the manuscript** (no design notes, briefs or canon) and
  wrote a card for every chapter before reading the next.
- **What each card asks:**
  - who did what to whom;
  - why, as the page shows it;
  - what changed;
  - why I should care;
  - whether there was a **plot event**: a choice or event that causes later events;
  - whether the mystery is **noticeable** to an average reader;
  - what a fifth grader would ask, marked **good** (an open question that pulls forward) or **confusing** (a gap).
- **Context for later acts.** Acts II and III had the earlier readers' notes as their memory of the book, so the review
  measures what the page delivers, not what the design intends.
- **Checking.** The claims that matter below were checked against the manuscript.

## 2. The short answer

**The book is not thin on events. It is thin on plot spine.**

| Measure | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Chapters with a real plot event | **35 of 50** (Act I 10/16, Act II 14/21, Act III 11/13); 8 partial, 7 none |
| Runs of two chapters with no plot event | **One** (S02 → E08) |
| Fifth-grader questions | **96 good, 20 confusing.** The book mostly raises questions that pull forward |
| Threads by the end (77) | 41 paid, 12 open on purpose, **13 open but unclear, 11 dropped** |

Things happen in almost every chapter. What a fifth grader cannot do is **say what the plot is**. The end-of-book test
shows why:

| Question | The fifth grader's answer |
| --- | --- |
| What was the book about? | Answerable: a caseworker feels a strange pressure the night a boy dies, and she and two friends try to find out what it is |
| What did she want, and did she get it? | **Two answers.** The truth about the boy's paperwork on the record: **got it.** Understanding the strange thing and keeping her people safe: **didn't** |
| **Who or what was against her?** | ***Nobody with a face.*** The thing itself, secret people "upstairs," the city's rules, her boss, the neighbors, and herself |
| **What was the big problem, and was it solved?** | ***No.*** They learned what it isn't, and one small clue |

**Those last two answers are the thinness.** A book can leave its mystery unsolved and still have a tangible plot, but
only when somebody wants something, something stands in the way, and the reader can say what was won or lost.

## 3. What makes the plot thin

### 3.1 The story question changes every act

The readers stated each act's question:

| Act | The question the reader carries |
| --- | --- |
| I | *What is this thing, and can Seraphine find out?* It is an observer's question: watch and learn |
| II | *Can they predict it, and who is watching them?* |
| III | *Can they learn anything true, and will they tell each other the truth?* |

**Only one question runs from start to finish:** will Seraphine tell the truth about the folder? It is the book's one
fully tangible plot, and it is paid in E44. But it is quiet for most of the middle. It moves at E09 and E10, then E21
and E25, then nothing until E44. The Act I reader notes that Caro, Renée and the folder are all absent from E11–E15.

**The mystery has no finish line a reader can see.** *Find out what it is* cannot be won in Book One, so readers feel
the book end without a result. The Act III reader calls the tins *small on purpose* and reads the church lot, not the
festival, as the real climax.

### 3.2 The plot is tangible exactly where the three have a plan

The strongest stretches, by every panel so far and by these readers, are the ones where the crew **has a concrete plan
with a test**:

| Plan | Chapters | What makes it tangible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The page** | E26 → E32 | A written prediction, a window, a place |
| **The trap** | E36 → E39 | The false report and the stakeout |
| **The gathering** | E41 → E46 | Seraphine's promise that being together keeps people safe |

In each, a reader can say what they are trying to do and see whether it worked.

**Before the page, the book has no plan.** Act I's action is mostly witnessing: a freeze, a lurch, a fall, then comparing
notes. Its first plan is E12's *text each other the time*, and then calling Baz (E15). **The fix direction is to make the
spine a sequence of plans.** Each plan has a goal, a method, a test, a result and a cost, and the result forces the next
plan. Act I needs its first plan earlier. This builds on the approved engine: surface rules, wrong theories that cost,
clocks as contested objects (Q-EN, ledger §274).

### 3.3 The opposition acts, but never wants anything the reader can name

The watchers' moves are clear and the readers notice them: the tenth floor, the turned badges, the man at the family's
door, the gray cases, the hooded figure at Kerlerec.

A fifth grader still cannot answer **why** they do any of it. The cards mark it *not shown* from E20 to the end. Nor does
the opposition ever **push against a plan of the protagonists'**. The city's "holds" in Act III (oxygen, food, vans) are
the nearest thing to that, and the Act III reader marks them *never explained or tied to the watchers*.

This is the same finding as C5, ledger §313 (7 of 10 readers), and §315 (4 of 4). **L4 or L4b** in the 4.5 plan is the
lever.

### 3.4 Key turns happen off the page

Three of the plot's decisive moments are summarized or implied instead of shown:
- **Seraphine learning Lucien's *None*.** It is resolved by a phone call and a nod (E48). The Act III reader lists it as
  *open, unclear*.
- **Lucien's disclosure of the false-report chain to Seraphine,** told in summary (E39).
- **The choice to leave a block untold at Ursulines** (E26 → E32; §315).

A fifth grader asking *who did what to whom* gets no scene for any of them. They are Q-P45-1 and Q-P45-10.

## 4. Mystery the average reader may not notice

**Present on the page but easy to miss:**
- **The city's notice about glass, and the taped windows, appear before any glass event** (E32, verified). Nobody remarks
  on it. If it is a clue (the watchers knew), it is the best clue the watchers thread has, buried in a clause. If it is
  not, it is a slip. **This is the author's call.**
- **The plumb line hangs still while everything else shakes** (E46). The readers call it the strongest late clue, and no
  one follows it up.
- **Still water rings first** (E45, E49) comes from a bystander.
- **Seraphine's collapse after calming Jody** (E49) reads as exhaustion.
- **Curtis taking Lucien's name** at the pump station and R.'s *crossed a desk* (E38) are never linked on the page.
- **Seraphine's early warning sense fades by Act III.** Nobody remarks on the change.

**Mysteries raised and then dropped:**
- the prologue's two presences (E00);
- the note over the river (E23);
- the fish auction abroad (S02);
- the hum only other people hear;
- Baz's dry wind (E43), kept private and never returned to.

Readers take a dropped mystery as either a mistake or noise. **Each needs a decision: pay it with one line, make it
noticed and carried, or cut its setup.**

| Where | The fifth grader asks | Check |

| Act III | Elisabet and *October*; Caro and Elisabet | Absent from Act III. Readers mark them *unclear*, not *hook* |

## 6. Thin stretches, by act

**Act I**
- S02 → E08 (setup, with nothing decided).
- E09–E11: the strange thing goes quiet for three chapters.
- E11 (no event, straight after Lucien's worst choice).

**Act II**
- It alternates plot chapters with rest chapters (E16, E19, E23, E27, E30, E33, E35).
- E33–E35 after the act's biggest event: E34 carries all the plot.
- Seraphine is absent from E35–E36, the chapters that close the act.

**Act III**
- E40–E42: nothing strange happens (they run on rumor, holds and exhaustion).
- E43, a rest day late in the book.
- The ending is smaller than the climax at the lot.

**Read with the author's Q-EB3 caution.** This is about *placement* and *what a chapter decides*, not about cutting quiet
chapters. §315's refinement holds: keep the quiet, vary the form of recovery, and end each rest on a decision.

## 7. For after the read

These join the one consolidated revision list (§316) and the 4.5 plan (Q-P45-1–12).

| # | Question | Recommended |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Q-PL1** | **A plot spine for Book One.** Name one question B01 can actually answer, and make the book a chain of plans that each fail forward toward it | Yes. The strongest candidate is *can they protect people from it*: the calls → the page → the trap → the gathering → the tins, with the last plan partly working (the positive rule, Q-P45-8). *What it is* stays for later books. The folder stays the human spine and needs one more visible move in the middle |
| **Q-PL2** | **The 24 unclear or dropped threads** | Sort each into one of three: pay it with a line, make it noticed and carry it to B02, or cut the setup. The prologue (Q-P45-7), the river note and the dry wind (L11), and the glass notice (§4) need the author's call |
| **Q-PL3** | **The verified confusions** (§5) | Fix mechanically after the read, in the verified-errors class of the consolidated list |

**The one thing to take from this review:** readers can follow the book and want to continue, but at the end they can
name no opponent and no result on the mystery. **A plan-by-plan spine, with an opposition that pushes on each plan, is
the most direct fix.** Most of it can be built from what is already on the page.
