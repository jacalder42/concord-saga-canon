## 1. What was done

Two designers worked independently, from the same brief, on the ~150k outline. Each built:
- surface rules the reader can learn about the strange;
- a question ladder;
- about twenty clues found by action;
- five wrong theories;
- traces of other actors;
- protagonist action in every piece;
- a treatment of the counting;
- a 52-piece table of about 150k words, with an obligations check.

**Both keep every ruling:**
- no Elias;
- M51;
- D1–D7, with T7;
- the child named, and not Mira;
- no return to the camp;
- no *Resonance* and no mechanism on the page;
- B02 E08 and E13 unspent.

**Caveat:** the convergence below is partly inherited. Both designers read the same brief, the same antagonist study
and the same manuscript. The pump theory in particular grows out of the manuscript's own E23.

## 2. Where they agree

**This is the core of the engine.**

1. **The strange becomes learnable without being explained.**
   - Both derive about eight regularities from the approved private causal cards and the manuscript. Among them:
     - it keeps to places, and a slow one has edges you can stand on;
     - it pushes nothing sideways: people fall every way, a plumb line hangs still, and still water shivers without
       slopping;
     - it takes one material at a time, a different one each time;
     - machines fail after it, never first;
     - there is a slow kind and a sharp kind;
     - the gaps shorten, but it doesn't grow.
   - A reader can hold these by mid-Act II and **predict *"watch still surfaces"* before the three do.**
   - Nothing states a cause; the private account stays in the cards.
2. **Two protagonists commit to wrong theories, act on them, and pay.**
   - **Lucien: *"it's the ground, the pumps."*** He goes into a pump station to get the records the city is holding.
     The cost stands. The theory breaks when the glass goes indoors while the street stays still.
   - **Seraphine: *"it comes where people carry too much; being looked after keeps it off."*** She urges Mara's
     gathering, and the rebound hurts the people she told would be safer. The narrator never says the gathering drew
     it.
3. **Other actors, felt and unattributed:** *"the file that went upstairs"*.
   - The reserved floor, and cards on strings turned face in.
   - The man at Renée's family's door who wanted only the time.
   - The index card the policeman reads from.
   - *Upstairs asked for a copy first.*
   - Grey cases on fences.
   - **One signature:** they want *only the time*. A cover-up would want damage, so by the end of Act II the public
     conspiracy theory no longer fits its own evidence. The narrower truth, that *someone is collecting*, passes to B02.
4. **Counting stops being the activity.**
   - Clocks become things that are fought over: Clement's original recording, the glazier's carbons, the pump logs,
     Caro's run logs, Mrs. Carmouche's book.
   - The signed forecast stays, as a bet with an ethic in it. Watch-setting, frame counts and tally recitals go.
5. **Every piece has a protagonist-initiated act with a risk,** and both name the pieces where the three were only
   replying, and what each does instead.
6. **Both widen the field across New Orleans without leaving it:** Poydras, Metairie, a pump station, a glass shop on
   St. Claude, the oxygen depot, Carrollton, the lot.
7. **The cost is similar.** About 54–57% of the prose survives, against the 150k outline's 62%. About 26 pieces are
   redrafted at beat level, against 16.
8. **The shared risk is procedural drift.** The shared guard: the pleasures stay inside the action, costs land in
   ordinary life, and no piece ends on a penalty without a life beat nearby.

**One source conflict for the author.** The E45 private causal card says hanging things swing. The manuscript, and
Q-DR29, keep Lucien's plumb line still. **Recommended:** keep the manuscript's version, the more learnable rule, and
note the card: *loose things jump*.
