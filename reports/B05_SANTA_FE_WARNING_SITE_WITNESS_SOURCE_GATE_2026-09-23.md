## Recommendation: two distinct claims, staged in the story

**First claim — evidence of a public hazard (can stand with mundane explanation).** If a local site is chosen later, demonstrate a reproducible *material* condition, independent testimony or records, ordinary alternatives and specific affected people. A building assembly is a viable **one-of-many** construction, not the preferred historical site. Routine inspection and protective closure can occur before anyone understands Resonance.

**Second claim — Santa Fe threshold forecast (requires separate validation).** Elisabet's cross-site analysis can create a *provisional* geographic inference, with Tahl communicating what is verified and what is uncertain. The Dec 7 historical “six weeks” and “drift lines” are candidate prose, not a sensor protocol or quantitative law. A reliable forecast needs defined raw observations, clocks, site inclusion/exclusion, alternate interpretations and a way to be wrong. Local damage plus a directional map does **not** automatically establish correlation or causation. The December 8 B5 close can later validate the warning dramatically without making the earlier evidence infallible.
