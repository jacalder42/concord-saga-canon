## 0. The short answer

**Neon is not mainly a care engine by episode count.** It is an **events-and-systems engine**
(institutions, the phenomenon, investigative and technical work, distance), with grief interiors and a
care engine at its **relationship joints**. It has real external opposition, one real protagonist
error with lasting cost (Seraphine, B04 E21), and one fatal protagonist choice against a colleague's
warning (Tahl, B06 E18).

**But the "perfect people" diagnosis holds for the cast, in a different shape from B01's prose.**
Four structural habits recur:

1. **The protagonists are always right.** Their turns are refusals of wrong things (*"He refuses"*,
   *"She refuses both"*, *"he will not"*, *"holds a door, not a crowd"*). Opposing wills belong to
   institutions, faceless officials or secondaries whom the protagonist ethically outclasses.
2. **Protagonists almost never want incompatible things from each other.** Across 138 episodes there
   are three real cast-on-cast collisions (§1), and the most loaded one available to the trilogy (the
   people Baz belonged to meeting the man who posted the coordinates) is designed to end in no blame,
   a shared channel and Tahl's first laugh, in three consecutive episodes (B05 E05–E07).
3. **Separation is used as friction avoidance.** *"Separation is the design"* (reconciliation §5) puts
   each protagonist in their own theater with their own institution to push against. They meet in the
   channel, and the channel is an agreement machine: data converge (B05 E42), people receive (B06 EP).
   Departures are *"planned honestly"* (B05 E10) and taken *"by choice"* (B05 E15).
4. **Every relationship ladder climbs, and the turns are worded as therapy.** Caro–Elisabet: kiss →
   *"Openly"* → commitment across distance. Seraphine–Lucien: strain → *"careful"* → reconnection
   *"without making her regulate his grief"*, then two episodes where the new rule is *"tested"* and holds.
   Seraphine–Rex: a halt where *"Her account … changes his plan; his limit changes hers."*

## 7. Counts

**Method.** Each episode is assigned by the **primary engine of its turn**, from the pass-3 "Story job
and turn" text as amended by pass 4. Five buckets, collapsed into the three requested:

- **CARE:** someone understands, comforts, steadies, heals or reconciles with another, or the turn is
  mutual understanding.
- **OPP:** two wills collide on the page (protagonist vs protagonist, or a named or specific opposing
  party acting against a protagonist).
- **EVENT/WORK:** the phenomenon, an institutional event, investigative or technical work, or travel.
- **GRIEF/INTERIOR:** a person alone with grief, remorse, dread or a private decision.
- **LR:** Life/Reward units, counted separately.

| Book | CARE | OPP | EVENT/WORK | GRIEF/INTERIOR | LR | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B04** | 4 (E04, E24, E39, E42) | 6 (E08, E16, E17, E26, E28, E29) | 20 | 10 | 5 | 45 |
| **B05** | 10 (E03, E05, E06, E10, E15, E17, E19, E27, E30, E35) | 7 (E11, E21, E29, E36, E37, E39, E41) | 19 | 5 | 4 | 45 |
| **B06** | 8 (E01, E08, E14, E28, E29, E31, E32, E38) | 3 (E03, E19, E22) | 20 | 14 | 3 | 48 |

**Of the OPP episodes, protagonist vs protagonist:** B04 one (E08), B05 none, B06 one (E19). The rest are
institutions or secondaries against a protagonist who is right. Of the six B04 OPP episodes, two
(E26, E29) end with the protagonist protecting someone, and the collision is between others.

Full assignments are in the appendix. Borderline calls: B04 E21 (counted EVENT, flagged as an error),
B04 E36 and B05 E26 (EVENT; they could be CARE), B06 E29 (CARE; it could be OPP with the locals). Moving
all borderline calls the other way changes no verdict.

## 8. Verdicts

- **B04: a conflict engine at world level, a care engine at cast level.** The civic argument, Protocol
  9, the Dominion demand, the Riot and Seraphine's real failure give it genuine external opposition.
  Between protagonists there is one collision (Lucien–Seraphine), and Rex's entry is a considerate
  contest. **The healthiest of the three.**
- **B05: a care engine with conflict around it.** Its cast turns are M37 (understanding), M54 (growth
  through handoff), M56 and M57 (two recommitments), plus the meeting-channel-laugh sequence. The
  opposition (hardliners, a smear, a knock) is real but sits outside the cast. Zero protagonist
  collisions in 45 episodes. **The weakest of the three, and the one most at risk of producing B01's
  texture.**
- **B06: an event engine (distance and the phenomenon) with care in it.** Its one great conflict is
  Tahl against the evidence and against the people who ask him not to go; the architecture reduces
  those people to an interior (Elisabet) and one line (Seraphine). Everyone else receives, remotely,
  and is comforted.

## 10. Levels of fix

### Prose-level (profile, pressures, a conflict brief in packets)

These can be fixed without touching architecture, because the architecture already contains the
opposition and the prose risk is softening it:

- **Episodes with real opposition that prose could smooth:** B04 E16, E17, E26, E28, E29, E30; B05 E11,
  E21, E29, E37, E39, E41; B06 E03, E22, E29. A conflict brief (objective · opposition · turn ·
  consequence · unresolved), which the author already approved in preflight Q3 and which was lost in
  compression, is enough for these.
- **Modes and shadow per character** (stress lines already on the cards: Lucien *"responsibility
  hoarding"*, Rex *"over-control"*, Caro *"overextension"*, Elisabet *"over-analysis"*, Tahl
  *"over-identification with crises"*, Kade *"righteousness escalation"*). The profile can make people
  act from these in scenes the architecture leaves neutral (the grief interiors, the work episodes).
- **Dialogue texture:** people not articulating each other's feelings; not repairing in-scene; the
  ban on therapeutic vocabulary (*"regulate"*, *"hold"*, *"carry"*) in character speech.
- **LR units** stay ordinary and pleasant; that is correct and needs no fix.

### Beat-level (architecture revision needed)

Prose cannot fix these, because the episode's job or turn **is** the care beat, or the conflict is
absent from the architecture entirely:

- **Turns worded as mutual understanding:** B04 E24 (*"his limit changes hers"*); B05 E05 (*"No one
  certifies blame"*, *"they learn MT is a person"*), E10 (*"honestly"*), E15 (*"by choice"*), E17, E27,
  E30, E35; B06 E08, E28. A packet derived from these will instruct understanding.
- **Collisions that do not exist in the architecture:** Lucien–Tahl (none); Rex–Tahl (none); Caro–
  Elisabet (none); Elisabet–Tahl (never in contact). Prose cannot invent them without the beats.
- **Errors converted to lessons within one to three episodes:** B04 E21 → E24; B05 E24 → E26. The
  cost must be placed in later episodes to stand.
- **Unpressed claims:** the sister, the parents, the mentor, Baptiste, the Dominion's second act.
- **Relationship ladders that only climb:** at least one step down is a beat, not a tone.
- **The channel as agreement machine:** B05 E42 and B06 E47 need at least one disagreement in the
  channel to exist as an event.

**Rough split:** about a third of the needed change is prose-level; about two thirds is beat-level.
B04 needs the fewest beat changes (B04-1, -2, -4 are the core); B05 needs the most (B05-1, -3, -4, -7);
B06 needs its conflict staged where it already exists (B06-1, -2) and its inheritance made visible
(B06-8).
