# B01 redraft: review of the Sudowrite beta comments and of ChatGPT's review of them

**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Status:** EDITORIAL REVIEW, non-canonical.

**What it reviews.** The author forwarded two things:
- Sudowrite's beta-reader export: 728 comments from three reader profiles, Maya, Anton and Joan.
- ChatGPT's review of those comments, with its in-chat summary.

**What it does not change:** no ruling, card, brief, packet, grid or manuscript text. **Nothing is edited while the
author reads.**

**Privacy.** It quotes no manuscript text. The export and ChatGPT's review both carry manuscript passages, so they are
kept only in the private manuscript repository (`draft-notes/beta-read/sudowrite/`).

**Method:**
- Every comment was mapped to its chapter by matching its attached passage against the reading copy. All 728 mapped,
  so the export is of the same text our panels read.
- The 274 comments that criticize or question were read in full.
- ChatGPT's claims, and a sample of the comments' own claims, were checked against the manuscript.

## 1. Verdict

**ChatGPT's review is right, and its caution is the most useful thing in it.**
- The Sudowrite comments are **passage-level feedback, not evidence of a sustained read of the whole book.**
- They are good at telling you what a passage does.
- They cannot tell you whether the book's promises, pacing or repetitions work across 50 chapters.
- Applied wholesale, they would push the book back toward explanation and reasonableness, the tendencies the redraft was
  built to escape.

**Three structural facts:**
- **The comments are spread evenly:** 3 to 28 per chapter, with no chapter unread. That is consistent with passage-by-passage
  processing, not a reader carrying the book in memory.
- **They almost never address book-level promises.** Across 728 comments:
  - the prologue's return comes up only on E00 itself;
  - romance comes up about four times;
  - the offstage *None* is never raised as a book-long debt;
  - the official thread appears only locally.

  **Their silence on these is not evidence against our two panels.** They are not measuring them.
- **They carry no memory across chapters** (§3).

## 6. What to reject

ChatGPT names four; I agree with all of them:
- Baz's dissent after he warns Mr. Benoit (E26);
- Seraphine's advice to stay away from windows while uncertain of the cause (E34);
- requests to explain every motive;
- requests to restore unity.

**Three more:**

| Where | Ask | Why it should be refused |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **E49** | *Why can't she help the other man too?* | The choice is the point. Choosing one person over the record, and over everyone, is her arc's landing |
| **E01** | Odile's *we didn't call because of the card* is said during the CPR | It is overheard, not a confession to anyone, and the E01 design keeps Seraphine's own confession for after the death (ledger §292). Moving it would soften the chapter's worst moment |
| **E23 and E43** | Lucien and Baz *choosing not to record* a wonder | **Leave it to the author.** Sudowrite praises both choices as turning points, while two of our panel's readers (4 and 7) object to them. This is audience taste, already covered by lever L11 |

**The general danger.** Joan's questions reward the explicit (*clarify, explain, add a beat of internal conflict*). Anton's
reward the rendered (*show more, dramatize*). Taken together, they would lengthen the book and make its people more
articulate about themselves. The redraft's gains come from the opposite direction.

## 7. On ChatGPT's recommendation

ChatGPT recommends **one consolidated revision list after the author's read**, in three classes:
1. verified errors;
2. sustained reading problems;
3. preference-dependent suggestions.

**I agree, with one addition:** the list should draw on all four sources at once, with the author's read first:
- the author's own read;
- our ten-reader panel (§313, §314);
- ChatGPT's four readers (§315);
- this export.

The 4.5 plan's levers and the questions Q-P45-1–12 are that list's middle and third classes. The verified snags (§315
§3.6) and this report's §5 fact-checks are its first class.

**No new question is added.** This review refines Q-P45-1 (E39 as the first beat) and adds five fact-check items to
Q-P45-12. **Neither changes any recommendation's scale:** no structural overhaul and no word-count target, as ChatGPT
also says.
