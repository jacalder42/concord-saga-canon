## 1. The short answer

**The readers are not asking for a different book.** All ten would read Book Two, and every one of them names the same
strengths: the people, the voice, the human promise paid in E44, and the E45–E47 run. What holds them at 3.5–4 is
**promises the book makes and does not pay**, plus one structural cost that all ten felt.

| The book's promise | Where it is made | What the readers got |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A romantic promise:** the confession the reader has carried since E04 | E04, E12, E30, E46 | Resolved offstage in E48, inside a list |
| **An investigative promise:** at least one true thing about how this world works | The crew's method, from E18 | Honest negatives; the one positive clue comes from a bystander |
| **A mythic promise:** a presence behind the strange | E00 | Never glanced at again in 49 chapters |
| **An adversarial promise:** someone wants something | The cases, the tenth floor, the collector | No person, no sentence of intent |

**The structural cost** is that the rest chapters come straight after peaks (E11, E33, E43) and the same scene shapes repeat.

**Paying the first three promises and fixing the placement plausibly lifts seven of the ten readers to about 4.5.** The
other three need more:
- **The Dinniman fan** needs real antagonism and physical risk to the core cast.
- **The SenLinYu fan** needs the confession to be a rupture with a cost, not a landing.
- **The LitRPG fan** needs the knowledge made visible. That is the recovered supplement design in §4.3.

**Most of this sits inside approved design, which the author can change.** Very little is ruled (§6).

## 3. Romance and conflict

### 3.1 Romance

**What the design allows now:**
- The B01 ceiling is **hand on hand, with no declaration**. It is approved design, derived from the later rungs:
  - B02 E12 has him *nearly* tell her;
  - B03 ends *emotionally undeniable* but *physically and verbally unresolved*.
- The recovered romance reconciliation (Tier D, 09-19) left the first kiss open (B03 or Neon). It leans to Neon, after the
  grief fracture.
- The heat lineage is approved (Q-WP2): H0–H1 common, H2 rare, no H4, an R ceiling. In the first three books, *real heat
  is rare*.
- **No ruling excludes a private confession scene.** No brief schedules the moment Seraphine learns of the *None* (the
  panel, §4).

**What the author said himself, 2025-11-08** (export, *Resonance system options*):
> *"romantasy wants an escalating curve of sensual tension and release… sensuality should have an intentional rhythm to
> keep the reader hooked, with layers and veils of overtness."*

**The redraft has tension but little release.** It carries one release (the waltz), one moment of contact (the hand on
the lot), and no *layers of overtness* between them.

**Three options, smallest first:**

**(a) Inside the current ceiling.**
- **The scene:** one private Seraphine–Lucien scene in Act III (L1). He tells her the *None*; she answers.
- **The through-line (L2):**
  - the waltz gets an aftermath, one beat between them the next day;
  - their banter continues after E19;
  - Lucien, in his own point of view, names what he feels once.
- **Why it fits:** words are not touch, so an acknowledgment of *what the other has become* fits *hand on hand*. It must
  not be a declaration, must not withdraw his E39 blame, and its repair needs its own cause (profile §5A).
- **Cost:** about 3,000–4,000 words.

**(b) One rung higher in B01:** an interrupted near-moment in the aftermath of danger (never during it; profile §11).
- This spends part of B02 E12's *nearly*, so B02's ladder would need re-spacing.
- **It needs approval.**

**(c) A trilogy-level question, not B01's:** where the first kiss falls.
- The romance-forward readers (3, 6, 9) all came back *to see whether they ever say it*.
- Holding the first kiss for Neon asks those readers to wait three books. **That is a real retention risk** for the
  audience the author named, so it belongs in front of him, not in a default.

**The placement tension inside L1.** The readers split:
- **Readers 3, 6 and 9** want the scene **after the gate**, as a landing.
- **Reader 5** wants the *None* discovered **before the lot**, as a rupture, so that turning his hand over in E46
  *costs* him.

**One way to serve both:**
- She learns before E46, from the file or from Guidry. Her reaction is on the page, and it breaks something.
- After the gate, they talk, and the acknowledgment lands.

That is two beats rather than one, and it gives E46's hand a price. The author's choice between one beat and two is
Q-P45-1.

### 3.2 Conflict

The readers are describing two different gaps.

**1. Between the leads.** The book resolves most disagreements with a dignified refusal. By one reader's count this
happens about ten times, and the Q-A2C4 pass varied the shape without removing it.
- Refusal is conflict that ends before it collides. The *None* is the one collision the book has earned and not spent.
- Lucien's E39 blame of Seraphine, which the register says is never withdrawn, is the other one.
- Profile §5A already asks for this: *"Wants collide… Repair has its own cause."*
- **Nothing needs approval here** except the scene itself.

**2. With an opponent.** Q-EN2 (approved design) keeps the other actors *B-lite*:
- unattributed;
- wanting only the time;
- with no man at the end;
- never seen by Baz.

The private working lines add *"in B01 the will never asks, it only takes"*. Every reader who asked for a face (2, 8, 9,
10, and 4 in passing) is asking the will to **ask**.

**The smallest change that meets them without naming a faction:** one Act III scene.
- A person sits across a table from Lucien or Seraphine, knows something they should not, and wants something from them:
  a log, a book, a login.
- They are refused, and the refusal costs.
- No name, no institution, no stated agenda.
- Baz is not present.

This amends the private *never asks* line, and possibly Q-EN2's *no man at the end*. **Only the author can approve
it.** It must not spend B02 E08 or E13, where the hands are named, or the Warehouse's *no one expects Technarc* (A9).

**Physical jeopardy and lasting cost (L9):**
- **Nothing forbids real bodily danger to Seraphine, Lucien or Caro**, and Q-EN8 already asks for *a risk* in each piece.
  The injury ceilings apply to the public events, not to a lead in a specific moment.
- **A lasting cost** is already in motion (Denise's write-up, carried to B02; Sal's red tag). What is missing is a cost the
  reader feels *on a lead* inside B01.
- **The Baz guards stand** (his death is ruled for B03). Two readers already read his E35 decision to stay as a death flag,
  so any new jeopardy must not land on him.

### 4.3 The conflict, and a reading that honors both

**The conflict.**
- The author's own 11-08 words want the math *backgrounded in chapters and paid off in supplements*.
- The Tier C permission rule forbids supplements that explain mechanics.
- By the precedence rule (C3), the author's own words beat accepted assistant text. **The permission rule's §6 is
  therefore overridable.**
- **Q-V3-2 is later than the 11-08 words and is the author's own approval.** I do not pick a winner (§4).

**One reading reconciles all of them**, and it follows the author's *felt / quantified / understood* ladder:

| Trilogy | What the supplements carry | Why it fits the rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Veil (felt)** | **The characters' own data:** Lucien's intervals, Baz's ticket table, Caro's dispatch printout, the crew's observed / ruled-out / bet ledger, a timeline, and the E26 page and the E48 account as documents. **No Resonance vocabulary, no mechanism, no narrator cause.** It is fallible, first-hand and diegetic | This is the 11-08 *field note takers* design (Seraphine and Lucien, then Baz), inside Q-V3-2, profile §9 and D1: it is the crew's math, not the world's |
| **Neon (quantified)** | The instruments arrive. Technarc's meters and dashboards become diegetic, and the word *Resonance* is earned. Mechanica's RP triad and the ~20% boost cliff can appear as *someone's* model, still fallible | *"let Neon earn it"*. The interface grows by trilogy |
| **Loom (understood)** | The math as understanding: the one full payoff | It avoids the math payoff arriving before the story has earned it |

**On *overlays*.** Rendering the control layer's codes (corridor U1–U7, weather, FX, RES, LOAD) as a reader-facing
display in Veil would break Q-V3-2 and profile §9. **I recommend against it in Veil.** In Neon it can arrive as a
faction's instrument readout inside the story, which makes it a plot object, not a narrator's display.

**What this would do for B01 specifically.** The book already *writes* most of these documents on the page:
- the E26 signed page;
- the E48 honest account;
- the interval sheet;
- Baz's columns.

Rendering three or four of them as short supplements adds almost no new facts. It gives:
- **readers 4, 7 and 8** what they asked for (*"I kept wanting to see the tables"*);
- **reader 2** the nearest thing to the system messages of the author he reads;
- **the book** a place to *show* knowledge accumulating (C9) without breaking *no mechanism*.

**One document is excluded:** Mara's book. The page's *"don't write it"* is the people's own condition.

**The bonus-content idea** (optional chapters by reader group) is a different mechanism: segmentation instead of
compromise.
- It lets a reader who wants the lab session, or the steamier scene, get it without the book changing for everyone.
- **Inside Veil, heat is capped** (profile §11), so a romance bonus would be sensual and emotional, not explicit.
- It is also a publishing question (serial platform, print edition), so it is Q-P45-6.

## 5. Story elements we are overlooking

These are grouped by tradition. Each line gives the element, the evidence in the panel, and the lever it points to.

**Classic craft**

| # | Element | What is missing | Lever |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E-1 | **The obligatory scene** (*scène à faire*). A story that sets up a confrontation owes the reader that scene on the page | The *None* reckoning and the gate reunion are both reported, not dramatised. Five readers flagged exactly these two scenes | L1 |
| E-2 | **Scene and sequel** (Swain). A sequel runs reaction → dilemma → **decision**, and the decision is what pulls the reader on | The rest chapters have the reaction and often skip the decision. That is why all ten stopped *willingly* there. The fix is a forward decision at each rest's end and a rest never placed straight after a peak, not cutting rests (Q-EB3) | L7 |

| E-4 | **One mentor, not a chorus** | Several elders do the same truth-telling job in the same kitchen shape (C4). Each is loved; together they cushion. Folding the job into fewer people gives each more weight | L7 |
| E-5 | **Chekhov's ledger** | Unpaid setups: Trip's erased tally, the preacher, the mule that didn't fall, the hum on some recordings, Dolores and the card, the bent table, and E47's meeting-place line (unplanted) | L8 |
| E-6 | **Dramatic irony** (the reader knows the bomb is under the table) | The reader has known about the *None* since E04 and never gets the explosion. The prologue could give the reader knowledge the characters lack, and never does | L1, L3 |
| E-7 | **Case of the week inside a mythology arc** (the *X-Files* model) | B01 closes its human case but no investigative sub-case. Readers 2 and 10 want one *closed* question, not an explanation of the whole | L5 |
| E-8 | **All is lost for a lead** | The costs land on bystanders, the one lead readers braced to lose lives, and nothing irreversible lands on the heroine inside B01 | L9 |

## 8. How to test before adopting

A projected 4.5 is not a measured one. Once the author answers, the fastest honest test is:
1. Draft only the adopted additions (for example the *None* beats and one field-note supplement).
2. Have the same personas re-read those chapters and their neighbours, blind to this report.
3. Compare their ratings and pull against the first panel.

The test costs a fraction of a full re-read, and it shows whether a lever does what the readers said it would.
