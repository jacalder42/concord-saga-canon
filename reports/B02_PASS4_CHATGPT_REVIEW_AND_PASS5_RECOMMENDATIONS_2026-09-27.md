## 1. The review, as relayed

> Reviewed Claude's B02 work through c252951, including the 47-episode pass 4, pass 3 architecture, and your recorded approvals.
>
> B02 now has a convincing central story: the group makes a useful civic intervention, protects people, and creates an institutional commitment it cannot fully control. I support that architecture. Five issues remain before I would call the beats worked out.
>
> 1. High — The Dominion acquires a method that Lucien hasn't demonstrably sent.
>     E26 specifies a time window, place, and margin of error. E31 says the Dominion now holds Baz's method, and E34 has it being used elsewhere. A forecast's results are not automatically a reusable method. Specify what reusable reasoning travels with the bounded submission and how the Dominion extends its use. Also sharpen why accepting this risk is justified after declining Technarc's offer over loss of control in E24. That difference should expose the group's difficult judgment.
> 2. High — The finale still describes its intended effect rather than its actual event.
>     E44 says something exceeds previous limits without identifying what happens. E46 waits for a reset; E47 repeats that it does not reset. Meanwhile E31 already declares that everyone knows the old baseline is gone. Distinguish the lasting civic consequences at Act II's close from the newly demonstrated physical persistence at Act III's close. Name the observable change, the expectation it defeats, and the response it forces. The underlying physics can remain unexplained.
> 3. Medium — Seraphine's ethical success needs a visible causal chain.
>     E25 says withholding confidences makes the clearance "narrower and blunter," but the beats don't show how that changes its boundary or treatment of a particular person. E28–29 respect Rosette's refusal and keep her safe, but safety alone doesn't establish the stated story job, "success through respecting refusal." Give Rosette a concrete reason and show what she and Seraphine do together that helps. Preserve the approved outcome; make their agency produce something readers can recognize.
> 4. Medium — Protecting Caro's later milestone has become too restrictive.
>     E15 explicitly makes her work alone with "No handoff"; E20 repeats it. The protected B05 milestone concerns entrusting consequential unfinished work to others and leaving. That need not prohibit ordinary teamwork throughout B02. Let colleagues contribute while Caro retains excessive responsibility, checks their work, or cannot bring herself to leave. That makes her overcarrying more believable and her later change more meaningful.
> 5. Medium — Several episodes remain assignments for future scenes.
>     E20 offers possible care problems; E43 describes unspecified people making unspecified choices; E44 leaves its event open. Some ordinary-life episodes similarly offer menus of activities. This is acceptable scaffolding, but their presence does not yet demonstrate that all 47 episodes earn their space. The next pass should choose the actual encounter, desire, obstacle, and change—especially across the extended E22–29 decision sequence. Quiet episodes can turn on pleasure or intimacy rather than danger.
>
> The strongest improvements are worth preserving: Baz fails and revises before succeeding; officials provide real protection; displacement complicates that success; Elisabet has independent work; and ordinary pleasures give readers reasons to want this world to survive. Mira and the anonymous Tahl material also respect the current reveal decisions.
>
> My recommendation is a focused pass 5, prioritizing the method transfer and the concrete ending, then strengthening Seraphine's causal contribution and relaxing Caro's blanket restriction. The direction is sound; the remaining work is making the promised effects happen through specific actions.

## 3. Recommended fixes for pass 5, as questions

Each fix stays inside the approved design: option A with E declined, Rosette, Bywater, M06, drift
plus No Reset, and the six directions. **The answers would be approved design.**

### Q1. How the method travels (point 1)

**Recommended: the grounds are the method.**

- **E23:** Helena says the Dominion **will not clear a block on a number**. It needs the grounds.
- **E26:** Lucien sends the window, the place and the margin **and the reasoning that justifies
  them**: which signs mark a pocket, and the interval between pulses that Baz's version 2 depends on.
  That reasoning is the minimum the Dominion needs to act, and **it is the part that
  generalizes.**
- **E34:** Dominion analysts **apply the same signs-and-interval rule** to other districts'
  reports. That is how the method travels.
- **Why the Dominion and not Technarc (E24 against E26):**
  - Technarc wanted **the whole map**, kept up to date, in exchange for data, for purposes it would
    not name.
  - The Dominion asks for **one block**, for **one public-safety act, in days**, under **named,
    accountable officials** (Baptiste in public, Rieux on the street), with **residents to protect**.
- **The difficult judgment:** they drew the line at the map, and missed that **the reasoning is the
  map.** Baz realizes it later than Lucien does (E34: Lucien knows and does not tell him).

### Q2. The finale's event (point 2)

**Recommended: a pocket that does not end.**

- **E44, the event:** along the drift line, a stretch of a few blocks where the observed effects
  (lights, sound, devices) **do not subside after the pulse.** They persist for hours, then days.
  - Observed, not explained (D5).
  - No casualties required. It is **not a catastrophe and not synchronized** (Veil brief).
- **The expectation it defeats:** *"it passes"*.
  - It is the city's folk rule from E32 (*"wait an hour"*).
  - It is Baz's model's premise (pulses are episodes, with intervals).
  - It is the clearance order's premise (clear for a window, then return).
- **The response it forces:**
  - The Bywater residents' promised return is postponed without a date, so a temporary order
    becomes standing. That is the approved B03 handoff, *"a standing restricted-zone protocol"*,
    **now caused rather than asserted.**
  - Baz's model has to change category, from a forecast of episodes to a map of standing
    conditions.
- **Placement:**
  - **E46:** Baz waits for the stretch to decay, and logs that it doesn't.
  - **E47:** the city lives with it. The ending is uncertain, not despairing.
  - The two stop repeating each other: E46 is the test, E47 the consequence.
- **E31 becomes civic.** It is retitled (e.g. *"What the Order Made"*), and its exit changes to
  *"the civic arrangements will not go back"*: the protocol, the method, Lucien's standing.
  **Physical persistence is Act III's discovery.**
- **Alternative (b):** pulses citywide at once. **Not recommended:** it risks catastrophe and
  synchronization.

### Q3. Seraphine's causal chain, and Rosette (point 3)

**Recommended: one visible consequence of withholding, and one thing Rosette and Seraphine make.**

- **E23/E25, what is asked:** Helena asks for **a list of vulnerable residents** to prioritize.
  Seraphine refuses, so **no list exists.**
- **E27, narrower:** the order is drawn on **the block's footprint only**. **The off-registry
  household two streets over**, who confided in her (E09), is **not named, not registered and not
  moved.**
- **E27, blunter:** the order treats everyone alike. **One resident is moved without the
  accommodation they need**, and fixing that falls to Caro. That ties into Q4.
- **Rosette's reason (E28), recommended:** she **left once, in 2005, and it took her years to come
  back.** She will not leave on someone else's forecast. It is concrete and local, and neither
  foolish nor prophetic.
  - It names Katrina without saying so. If you would rather not tie her to a real disaster, the
    alternative is the family house: she will not leave the one thing left to her.
- **What they do together (E28–E29):** Rosette **keeps the block's book**. She is the weather-vane:
  for decades she has written down its signs by the hour.
  - Through the cluster, she and Seraphine **keep it together**, at her table.
  - It becomes **the only record from inside the cleared block.**
  - It is a neighbour's habit, not expertise. Her registry line is *"ordinary neighborhood
    'weather-vane,' not secret expert"*, and the book must stay that: times, sounds, which dogs went
    quiet.
  - Afterwards, Rosette gives it to Baz, **unofficially**. Its timings **refine his model** (feeding
    E37 or E40).
- **Why this is success through refusal:** the only inside record exists **because her refusal was
  respected.** It is useful, not vindicating: her staying is neither punished nor proved right (the
  guardrail holds).
- **The exit state is kept:** she is *"a bounded witness nobody official interviewed"*.

### Q4. Caro (point 4)

**Recommended: help is present, and she won't use it.**

- **The standing guardrail changes** from *"Caro makes no first handoff"* to ***"Caro does not
  entrust consequential unfinished work to others and leave (M54, B05)"***.
- **E15:** Leila Broussard (registry `B06`, the recurring medical face) and the responders work
  beside her. Caro **re-checks their triage** and will not go home.
- **E20:** the fix is **the resident the blunt order moved without accommodation** (Q3). Colleagues
  help. Caro **keeps the follow-up herself** when she could pass it on. Elisabet sees it.
- **The overcarrying is a choice she keeps making.** That makes B05's handoff a change she makes,
  not a permission she is given.
