**Strongest in the book:** the death and the family's own words about why they waited (E01); the whole Square falling
at once on a steady phone (E14); the silence at the gate (E47). **Weakest:** E43, the E35 ferry, and E42's painting
party.

**The ending:** it ends as a book.
- The inciting death's file closes.
- Seraphine's own theory is falsified by her, publicly.
- The forecast is closed in a signed account.
- Her arc runs from arriving too late to choosing the person over the record, in time.

What stays open is the right kind (who is *upstairs*; Elisabet; Seraphine and Lucien; the cases), with the exceptions
in §4.

**On Seraphine:** she decides more than she observes. The reader named her three most consequential decisions:
- the calls, and her veto of Baz's untold street, which set the page's method;
- bringing the people who live alone to the lot on her theory;
- writing the lapse onto the boy's file.

The reader's one weakness here: her decisions are all of care and conscience, while the instruments and bets belong to
the men, and **her own onset log was never paid**.

**Patterns across the book:**
- An elder delivers a verdict and the protagonist absorbs it.
- Chapters end on a withheld act.
- *I'm fine* recurs.
- *Only the times* is shown in six official scenes.
- Feeding scenes recur.

Each is good alone; together they make a rhythm.
