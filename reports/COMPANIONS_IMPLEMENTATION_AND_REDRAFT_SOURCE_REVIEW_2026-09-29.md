## Appendix: the forwarded review, verbatim

> The Companions are being used substantially better, but their implementation is not yet complete. Before redrafting, B01 needs revised episode architecture and reconciled EBCI briefs. The saga milestone grid needs selective updates, not wholesale regeneration.
>
> I reviewed canon through 518429d and manuscript through 5411d64, including the amended profile, approved wants and relationship register, mystery-engine decisions, Design B and current drafting instructions. No files changed.
>
> What is working
>
> The current approach follows your approved, less invasive use of the Companions:
>
> * No permanent animal labels have been assigned to characters.
> * Character pressures now reach the drafting stack.
> * Narrative empathy no longer obliges characters to understand or accommodate each other.
> * Relationship loops distinguish consequences from repair.
> * Characters have personal wants alongside their protective responsibilities.
> * The new mystery design makes those pressures produce actions: Lucien's false report costs Sal; Seraphine conceals disconfirming evidence; Baz privately investigates a suspicion about her.
>
> Those last examples are the strongest improvement. The machinery is beginning to change decisions and relationships, rather than merely the tone of conversations.
>
> The approved relationship register also permits divergence and suspension. Cooperation at the finale no longer automatically means everyone has reconciled.
>
> Where the Companions still need careful handling
>
> 1. They risk becoming a system for making everyone worse.
>
> The profile describes ordinary behaviour, deterioration under pressure and an overwhelmed state. That covers an important part of the Companions, but their constructive expressions and movement between modes are less explicit.
>
> A character should sometimes respond to pressure with unexpected competence, humour, restraint or generosity. Stress is not automatically shadow; rare integration does not mean ordinary competence is rare.
>
> I would preserve the compact pressure cards and check, during episode design:
>
> Why does this particular pressure produce this response now—and what other response was available?
>
> That is more useful than adding all six guides to every packet.
>
> 2. The relationship register is an approved destination, not proof that the journey works.
>
> Its trigger → reaction → escalation → break → consequence structure is useful. Applied mechanically to every relationship, it could become as predictable as automatic repair.
>
> The three approved non-reconciled outcomes should stand. Their execution still needs varied trajectories: disagreement without escalation, cooperation without forgiveness, affection alongside distrust, and conflict that changes subject as circumstances change.
>
> 3. Some personal wants remain close to the original care pattern.
>
> Seraphine's "to be indispensable" makes her caregiving more complicated, but still centres her life on caregiving. Baz's belonging and Caro's Chicago life provide broader possibilities.
>
> The new wants should generate pleasure, ambition, attraction and competing commitments as well as damage. Otherwise, the cast becomes a group of troubled caretakers rather than distinct people.
>
> 4. "Action with risk in every piece" could become the next formula.
>
> That is now an approved diagnostic question. I would apply it as a question, not require every quiet episode to purchase a clue through wrongdoing or loss.
>
> Design B repeatedly says every clue must cost someone. That can produce an exhausting pattern of transgression and punishment. A clue can also follow from skill, curiosity, persistence, pleasure or a relationship freely offered.
>
> There are concrete reasons to reconcile the architecture before drafting.
>
> The newest decisions explicitly say the merged outline comes first. It has not appeared among the commits reviewed. Design B remains an input with superseded elements—including the dropped urgent-care chart and the excluded final watcher.
>
> The drafting README currently tells the redraft to use the old EBCI Narrative Brief plus the new outline row. That leaves the drafter resolving disagreements between two versions of the book.
>
> Examples needing reconciliation include:
>
> * The relationship register's B01 summary still describes E24 as "fracture, mended."
> * Baz and Seraphine now enter B02 with the hidden-column grievance.
> * Elisabet's revoked access changes the circumstances of her departure and return.
> * Lucien's work with Sal and Seraphine's caseload change.
> * The new evidence-collection thread creates B02 recognition and knowledge obligations.
>
> These are changes to what happens and what later scenes depend on. They belong upstream of prose.
>
> There is also a causal issue to settle in the mystery design. Its surface patterns sometimes become stronger conclusions than the evidence supports. For example, sound, glass and ground having appeared does not by itself justify predicting water or "still surfaces" next. Likewise, a box appearing after a false report supports access to that information; it does not alone establish who collected it or why.
>
> The merged outline should distinguish observation, hypothesis, test and warranted conclusion. Concrete mystery is good; inadvertently giving the characters reliable rules they have not earned would create a new problem.
>
> What should be generated before the redraft
>
> Layer | Work needed now
> Saga milestones | Review affected dependencies and descriptions. Add a milestone only where a new development creates a durable cross-book obligation. Keep existing ruled anchors.
> Relationship and mystery tracking | Reconcile the approved relationship register with B01's new grievances, clues, knowledge states and B02 pickups.
> B01 episode architecture | Produce one merged, approved outline with settled piece numbering, actions, turns, consequences and approximate lengths.
> B02–B03 architecture | Complete the approved targeted conflict pass and reconcile the consequences inherited from B01.
> B01 EBCI | Revise changed briefs, create briefs for new pieces, map merges and retire superseded briefs from active use. Retain unchanged material after checking it.
> Prose packets | Derive from that reconciled source, act by act, with current pressures and continuity.
> Neon/Loom EBCI | Not needed to begin B01's redraft. Their approved relationship destinations and dependencies need to remain visible.
>
> The false report, revoked access and hidden column do not each automatically need a new saga milestone. They do need reliable episode placement and explicit consequences that downstream work inherits. Otherwise, the milestone grid becomes a second episode outline.
>
> My recommended order is:
>
> 1. Merge the B01 engine and reconcile its B02–B03 consequences.
> 2. Complete the targeted Veil architecture pass.
> 3. Update affected milestones, breadcrumbs and relationship states.
> 4. Revise B01 EBCI and derive the first act's prose packets.
> 5. Redraft sequentially, checking that the new choices create momentum without merely lengthening arguments.
>
> An approved merged outline could technically supply the necessary EBCI-level information itself. But the repository must then explicitly designate it as the replacement source. What should not happen is drafting from an old brief, a new outline and several amendment files while expecting the writer to adjudicate among them.
