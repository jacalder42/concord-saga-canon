## 2. Market position

- **Shelf:** literary speculative / book-club crossover, not urban fantasy. The first three chapters say literary and
  uncanny; the Prologue promises mythic fantasy (a jacket and first-page risk).
- **Comparable titles (editor A):** *Starling House*, *The Ministry of Time*, *Sea of Tranquility*, *Hummingbird
  Salamander*, *Remarkably Bright Creatures*, *Let Us Descend*. Pitch: *Starling House* meets *The Ministry of Time*.
- **Length:** 121k is long for that category for a debut; the editor's target is 100–110k. Under the author's
  120k soft cap it is about 1% over.
- **Standalone:** it closes emotionally (Renée's kitchen, the filing on day 90); the phenomenon stays unexplained, so SFF
  readers may read it as a long first volume.
- **Serial (Ream):** most chapters end on a pull; Ch 1 and Ch 4 (*None*) convert. Ch 3 is the drop point. Chapters 9,
  10, 29 and 38 (4.2–5.2k) bury their cliffs.

## 3. Where the readers converge

1. **The phenomenon gives too little.** No reader learns what it is; *the Veil* (Ch 38) still lands cold. Editor A asks
   for one or two clear, staged findings.
2. **The opposition is clearer but still blurs at the edges.** Caldas is visible from the funeral (Ch 7) and clear by
   the desk note (~35%), but the Institute, the Review, N., the askers, the boxes, the cream-shoes woman and the City run
   together for a once-through reader; his only violence (Ledet, Ch 30) is offstage; he plays the same scene each time.
3. **The middle still repeats** (withholding, drafting and logging scenes): Ch 15–20, 24, 27, 31.
4. **Too many names**, especially about fifteen new ones in Act III.
5. **What sells it:** the voice, the New Orleans ensemble (Miss Tavie, Odile, Caro, Mrs. Arceneaux), Seraphine's guilt
   arc, the set pieces (Ch 1, 4, 6, 13, 17, 21–22, 29–30, 34–36, 38–39) and a timely spine about consent and records.

## 4. Regressions and new problems (reader D)

- **Lost:** Caro's comic Palm Sunday breakfast; two shared-work beats in Ch 9; the mid-book watcher interlude (the
  watchers vanish until the Epilogue).
- **Not met:** counting restraint (density unchanged, 1.27 per 1,000 words); scene endings on silence have become a tic.
- **New:** Ch 38 does about eight jobs; Sal's resolution (Ch 37) and the pump station (Ch 16) are told in summary; Ch 30
  is overloaded and one of its confessions repeats in Ch 37; the green earrings are never paid.
- **Record mismatch:** the two-page synopsis says *wrist* where the page has a hip, and has Lucien declare love, which
  the adopted stair test removed. The page is right.

## 5. Suggested next decisions (for the author)

None is applied. Recommended order: a short braid-polish pass first, then length and market choices.

1. **Polish pass (small–medium):** split Ch 38; put one act of Caldas's force on the page; fixed descriptors for the
   recurring unknowns; cut Act III names; one staged finding about the phenomenon before Ch 38, so *the Veil* has
   something to land on; restore one watcher touch mid-book; a counting and silent-ending line pass.
2. **Length:** stay at about 121k (serial-first makes this soft) or cut to about 110k for a trade submission.
3. **Market path:** serial-first on Ream as planned (re-cut to about 50 modules; Prologue to Ch 6 free) and/or a
   literary-speculative query positioning.
4. **Prologue:** keep, shorten, or move the watchers' first appearance after Ch 1.
5. **Authenticity reads** for New Orleans Creole and Black community voices before any submission.
