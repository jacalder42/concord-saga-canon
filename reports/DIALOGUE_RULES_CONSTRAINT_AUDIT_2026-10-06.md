# Dialogue rules: what holds back dialogue quality in B01

The author asked: *"Evaluate if there are any current rules or restrictions that holding back dialogue quality."*
This report inventories every rule that touches dialogue, tests each against the merged B01 page, and recommends the
smallest change for each that costs something.

**What it does not change:** no rule, ruling, profile line, packet, template, card, voice sheet or manuscript text.
Every recommendation below is a proposal and waits for the author's answer (§7). No manuscript prose is quoted here;
page habits are described and counted. Short quotes with line locators are in the private manuscript repository,
`draft-notes/voice/DIALOGUE_RULES_EVIDENCE_2026-10-06.md`. **No edit to the merged B01 is proposed during the author's
listen and read**; the changes would reach B02's packets and the author-led line edit.

## 2. Verdict

**The profile is not the problem.** §5 *Dialogue* and §5A *People under pressure* are the best dialogue instructions
in the system, and the page proves it: wherever a scene has a will that pushes back (E27's refusal, E36's argument),
an elder who wants something of her own (E33), or a non-plot subject to play with (E19's street names), the dialogue
is quick, specific, funny and in character. Every sampled scene of that kind is strong.

**The dialogue is weaker in one place, and the rules plausibly cause it there.** In the disclosure chapters (E39, E44,
E46, E48, E49), where plot information passes between the leads, the talk turns to statement and assent: one person
tells, the other says *Yes* or *I know*, and the obvious question is not asked. Three earlier reviews found the same
place independently (the staging pass: disclosures about the hidden organisation *"read as delivered"*; the beta panel's
M12, *E48 reads as a list*; the compression audit: the leads' disclosure scenes *"need new design, not recovery"*).
The mechanism is not one rule. It is the **packets** and their **guard lists**, which do three things the profile
and template forbid: they script conversations, they gag characters with the narrator's limits, and they cap
resistance at one. Two smaller causes sit underneath: the leads have no designed dialogue difference, and several
narration rules are read as speech rules.

## 3. Top findings, ranked by impact

### F1. Packets pre-write the conversations in the disclosure pieces (high)

- **The rule says one thing; the packets do another.** The README forbids *"dialogue plans"* between packet and prose;
  the template says *"Do not schedule spontaneity: no packet says … 'one joke here'"*, and compression rule 3 says a
  relationship packet names the rung, *"never the choreography of the change"*; the pilot's third question asks
  whether *"the best dialogue … [is] still unwritten"*. The redraft's packets for the disclosure pieces nonetheless
  specify who says what to whom, in what order, and how the other answers.
- **Measured.** Prescribed speech acts below the rule (*says, tells, asks, answers, admits, names*): E48 48, E46 36,
  E49 35, against 10–15 in most packets. Those same chapters hold the most bare one-word assents in the book (E48 and
  E46 eight each, E44 and E39 seven; every other chapter four or fewer).
- **On the page.** At least seven packet clauses arrive almost word for word (evidence file §1): E39's *one question
  from her about him (… who taught him to waltz)* becomes exactly that question, the only one; E39's *both of them say
  the kiss was nothing, and that it did not count*; E39's *one laugh, at nothing*; E46's *someone says, flatly, that
  it came all the same*; E48's *she tells him he never put it to her; he answers that he knew what she would say*;
  E48's scheduled *dry remark … about something Lucien is wearing*; and E48's guard, spoken aloud by Baz.
- **Why it suppresses quality.** A drafting model given a paraphrased line writes the line. The writer's best move in
  a conversation (the deflection, the wrong answer, the question nobody planned) is pre-empted, and the scene turns
  into delivery of the brief. **Status:** the clauses are assistant derivations (U7, the Rebuild blocks, the
  *What happens* lists); the rules they break are approved design.

### F2. Guards written for the narrator bind the characters' mouths (high)

- **The guard lists.** Packets carry about twenty *nobody says* clauses and twenty-seven *aloud* clauses: *nobody says
  the three are watched*; *nobody says who upstairs is*; *no judgment of the Institute aloud except to Baz*; *nobody
  connects it aloud*; *Baz says that they watch this … never how it works*. Most restate what the **page** may not
  establish (who sent the collector, what causes the events). Written as *nobody says*, they also forbid what a
  frightened, intelligent person **would** say.
- **On the page.** After a stranger at the table quotes their private log back to them, nobody says aloud that they
  are being watched; the scene closes on a joke. In Baz's confession, Lucien's only question is what the man wants;
  Baz pre-empts the rest by saying he doesn't know how any of it works (the guard, in his mouth). Lucien's answers
  are assent. The profile's own test (§5: *check whether an obvious question would plausibly resolve it*) is failed by
  construction: the obvious question is ruled out, so the listener has nothing lawful to do but agree.
- **Sources.** §9 *Not the mechanism (ruled): nobody explains it, and the narrator doesn't either* is the one ruled
  line that names characters; §9's later line already permits *people can argue about causes, and the page does not
  settle the cause*. The rest are assistant guards. Some predate the 10-03 reopening of *no blame, just inference*,
  which now permits **characters' suspicion** of Caldas and the Dominion, with only a narrator statement of fact still
  open (`decisions/HAN_WEI_ON_PAGE_AUTHOR_DIRECTION_2026-10-03.md`, addendum 2).
- **Interaction.** The packets are absolute (*nobody*, *never*) where the profile, since Q-CS3, is costed. The drafter
  receives a costed profile and an absolute packet, and the packet wins.

### F3. The leads have no designed dialogue difference, so short replies converge (medium-high)

- **Evidence.** 41–47% of Seraphine's, Lucien's and Baz's turns are four words or fewer (Caro 20%); a blind classifier
  told the three apart at 34% against 29% chance; Seraphine is the hub of the confusion (private voice report).
- **Rule chain.** Q-WP3 (production rule) keeps any per-character voice layer out of drafting: one card line about
  how a POV character *notices*, at first appearance, then *the preceding prose governs*. Q-VI1 lets the author pull a
  **designed** trait back, but the residue here is not designed: it is emergent habit, and nothing lets a review
  finding stop it governing. §12B (approved design) sets Seraphine's *work voice* to *neutral*, and she is at work in
  most scenes, so she sounds like the two non-Louisianans. The voice sheets (review-side) give six characters *dry*
  humour (Q-VI8), record Lucien's near-total absence of questions as *leave it*, and find Seraphine asks in under one
  turn in ten, so a Seraphine–Lucien two-hander has nobody whose habit is to ask. Four cards share one voice verb,
  *translating* (voice report V6).
- **Patched, not fixed.** The listening pass met the interchangeable replies by adding speaker tags (F7).

### F4. "Nothing romantic declared aloud", read broadly, leaves the leads only denial (medium; largely by design)

- **Source.** Q-PI8 as amended by H1 (approved design, B01 only); profile §11; U3 makes witnesses *"the lawful channel
  for heat in B01, where nothing romantic is said aloud"*.
- **Effect.** Read as *nothing romantic said aloud* rather than *no declaration*, it leaves the leads' private talk
  with one romantic register: calling things nothing. The play audit found the leads' play gone to silence by E32;
  the beta panel's M11 (*the romance ends colder than its middle*, 4 of 10) asked for a private two-hander without a
  declaration, which the guards already allow. Elisabet flirts aloud on the page without breaking anything. The
  design (no declaration in B01) is sound; the over-reading is the cost.

### F6. Floors written as ceilings (medium)

U7 gives each disclosure piece **one non-answer and one interruption**, which helps (a blind reader found E44–E46's
extracted disclosures better). But the packets write it as a cap: *"One only"* (E46), *"Keep that one; do not add a
second evasion there"* (E48). With E39's *one question* and *one laugh*, the packets ration exactly the behaviour §5
asks for (people deflect, interrupt, change the subject, answer the question they wish they'd been asked).

### F7. Listening attribution by run length (low)

Speaker tags rose by about a hundred (1,142 to 1,240 tag forms) under a reviser rule that tags *runs of three or more
consecutive untagged lines*. In two-person exchanges with distinct speakers that slows the staccato §3 asks for in
banter, and it labels the F3 sameness rather than curing it.

## 5. Interactions

1. **Scripted disclosure + speech gags + assent.** The packet requires the information to pass (F1); the guards
   forbid the questions that would make it pass by extraction (F2); Lucien's designed scarcity of questions and his
   withholding arc (beta M6) leave him one lawful move. The result is the *Yes* run, concentrated exactly where the
   specification is densest.
2. **No voice layer + neutral work voice + *dry* for everyone + listening tags.** Difference was left to emerge; the
   emergent habit was the short reply; the patch was a tag. Each step is reasonable alone.
3. **Romance declaration ban + witnesses as the channel + the kiss *called nothing*.** The only spoken romance between
   the leads is denial, so the one private scene the beta panel asked for has no permitted words but silence.
4. **Absolute packets under a costed profile.** Q-CS3 turned the profile's absolutes into costed defaults; the
   packets were never converted, so the strictest text in the stack is the assistant-written part.

### Missing permissions that would help

Proposed as additions to profile §5, approved-design level (author answer):

- **"Talk overlaps.** In a group, people talk across each other, finish a sentence wrongly, answer the line before
  last, and run two conversations at once."
- **"Argument escalates in words as well as behaviour:** the raised voice, the unfair line, the thing said and taken
  back."
- **"Someone has to want an answer.** In a two-hander where neither person would ask, give the want to the listener
  or let a third voice in."
- **"Being wrong out loud is information.** A confident wrong theory, said and believed, tells the reader about the
  speaker, and may stand."
- **"Small talk may be small.** Not every exchange carries subtext; some only sound like people."
