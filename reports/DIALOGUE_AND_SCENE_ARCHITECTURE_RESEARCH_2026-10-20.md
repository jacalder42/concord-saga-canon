# Human dialogue and scene architecture: research summary

**What it is.** An independent research study. Its question is how successful fiction creates believable, emotionally
credible, distinctive and dramatically effective human interaction, toward a transferable system for planning and
generating scenes with AI assistance. The full study is private, in the manuscript repository's
`draft-notes/craft-study/dialogue-architecture/`. It discusses copyrighted works in detail, so only its method and
conclusions are summarised here.

**What it does not change:** no canon card, rule, grid, ruling, writer profile, packet, template or manuscript text.
None of it enters the drafting stack without the author's decision.

## 1. Method

- **The reference works** were *He Who Fights With Monsters*, *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, *Bride*, *Rivers of London*
  and *The Graveyard Book*.
- **They were not available to this session.** Phase 1 is built from the readers' notes of 2026-10-01 (ledger §357),
  made by readers who read the full texts.
- **Phase 1 has 48 scene dossiers.** Each records what each participant wants, knows, believes, fears and
  misunderstands, how the exchange develops, what shifts, and what is left unstated. Every claim is tagged by source,
  with observation kept apart from interpretation.
- **Six control scenes,** from memory, test the findings outside the five works' genres. The forms are the short
  story, the novel of manners, the stage play, the film, the unreliable first-person novel and the psychological short
  story.
- **The project's own experiments** bear on the drafting-method question: the table-read pilot, the screenplay-model
  revision and the dialogue-rules audit (ledger §443–§444).

## 2. Conclusions

1. **Twelve mechanisms recur across at least three of the five works.** Nine of them also hold in the controls.
2. **No single source produces the strong scenes.** The commonest kernel has three parts:
   - an asymmetry of knowledge, want or weight;
   - an offer;
   - a refusal or swerve in the person's own idiom, settled more often by an act than by a line.

   History sets the meaning, structure sets the timing, and voice sets the texture.
3. **Speech wins when the business is truth; acts win when it is feeling.** Moral speeches are the most-flagged
   failures.
4. **No real rupture is repaired verbally in the same scene** anywhere in the evidence.
5. **Dialogue without conflict works only when it spends banked history,** with something exposed, timed or done.
6. **No drafting method is superior in general.** The evidence favours specifying conditions (knowledge, wants,
   history, the offer, the non-verbal currency, the cut) and leaving lines free. In contest scenes, each side is
   generated as an independent will, and the results are merged with the outcome held.

## 3. Output

- **Thirteen scene architectures,** each with the ten required parts: function, starting conditions, asymmetries,
  pressure, alternative pathways, possible changes, prose opportunities, failure modes, supporting and contradictory
  evidence, and when not to use it.
- **Combinations and rules of thumb.**
- **A verification list** for the primary texts, if they are resupplied.
