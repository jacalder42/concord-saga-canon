## 1. Method

1. **A whole-book recurrence baseline** from a regex script over 14 watch families.
2. **Three act auditors.** They read in full, classified each family as signature, motif, one-off or tic, named about
   125 passages to protect, and proposed 67 candidate edits (light in every act).
3. **Seven editors.** They accepted, adapted or declined each candidate, and read for further redundant
   interpretation and restating reception.
4. **One continuity checker** over the whole diff.

## 2. Result

- **Edits:** 68 (A 20, B 32, C 2, D 7, E 7), plus three continuity repairs.
  - 61 audit candidates taken; 6 declined.
  - 6 editors' own additions.
- **Words:** 173,341 → 172,818 (−0.3%).
- **Recurrence:**
  - *as if* / *as though*: 220 → 208;
  - *plain* / *plainly*: 22 → 17;
  - the hand and stillness families were left almost whole, judged signature or climax work.
- **Untouched:** 20 of 52 pieces.

## 3. Findings

1. **The families are broader than the tics.** Counted by regex, hands (210) and *as if* (208) look like tics. Read in
   context, most are character signatures or a scene's own work. The real tics were narrow:
   - a one-sentence gloss after a gesture has landed;
   - lines said "to" an object, clustered in Act II;
   - the triad look;
   - *against her chest*;
   - two habitual *as if* shapes;
   - striking phrases used twice.
2. **Restraint holds the voice.** Every change is a cut or a simplification. No chapter was substantially
   rewritten.
3. **Uncertain voice-or-tic items** go to the author unedited (`AUDIT.md` §4). So do eight flags where a cut may touch
   emotional consequence, attraction or characterization (`REPORT.md`).
