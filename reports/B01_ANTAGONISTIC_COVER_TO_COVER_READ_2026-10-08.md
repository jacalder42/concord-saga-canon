## Method

Three blind readers each read the whole book in order: an acquisitions editor, a plot-forensics analyst (question
ledger and causal chain), and an impatient genre reader (patience by chapter, DNF point). They were hostile by brief,
and every claim needed a chapter locator. Scores are not comparable with the 10-07 blind panel.

## Findings

- **Verdicts:** 3 / 5 standalone from all three; all would finish; Book Two conditional or cautious.
- **Plot for the length: not enough, by all three.** The strange escalates well. The weakness is density in named
  stretches (Ch 11, 14, 17, 32 by two or more readers) and an investigation that never advances *what* or *who*.
- **Resolution:** the personal and evidential questions resolve (the folder, the *None*, Seraphine's retraction, the
  Batiste closure); the genre questions are deferred. The ending is a pause on one word. Tallies: 17/14/8/6 of 45
  (reader 1) and 25/7/10/8 of 50 (reader 2), answered/partly/deferred/dropped.
- **Convergent:** the opposition is first named about 81% through (verified: Ch 40) and never meets the crew in a
  contest they can win or lose. Payoffs come by confession, not discovery. There is no central question stated by
  the midpoint.
- **Unanimous strengths:** prose, place, community cast, escalating phenomena, Act III from Ursulines, the kitchen
  scene (E44) and the retraction (E46).
- **Reader-reported dropped threads:** about fifteen, to verify; some are deliberate (the prologue camp, ruled
  09-28; the protected mistral; series breadcrumbs).
