## 4. A refinement to ChatGPT's "three extractions"

ChatGPT proposes extracting three things from Act I:
1. **What Act II must remember:** the continuity layer, which already exists (`draft-notes/B01_ACT_I_CONTINUITY.md`).
2. **What Act II should emulate.**
3. **What Act II must not learn.**

The trouble is with 2 and 3. **An "emulate" list in the drafting stack would teach the drafting model Act I's
successful shapes**, which is the failure ChatGPT's own headline warns against. The em-dash rule already shows how a
drafting-side instruction gets overlearned.

**Recommended division:**
- **What to emulate is carried by the corpus itself.** Revised Act I is already the preceding prose in the stack
  (Q-CAL3). It gets no list.
- **What not to learn is a review-side diagnostic.** It is used at checkpoints and by revisers, not fed to drafting.
- **The drafting stack gains only a few lines:**
  - one instruction;
  - the semantic dash rule;
  - US orthography;
  - the three character guards.

## 5. The consolidated answer set, for the author to confirm

**Q-AR1. US spelling** *(the panel and ChatGPT agree)*
- US orthography throughout the manuscript.
- American vocabulary in American mouths and POVs.
- Lucien keeps European word choices where they are his character.
- **Apply it to Act I now**, as one mechanical, spelling-only copyedit with a reviewed diff. It is a named exception to
  the close, because the contaminated prose would otherwise become Act II context.
- **Scope:** about 96 forms. *grey* 53, then *colour*, *neighbour*, *favour*, *harbour*, *rumour*, *organised*,
  *recognise*, *apologising*, *aluminium* and *metres*. The vocabulary cases are *trousers* and *trainers*, fixed only
  where the POV is American.

**Q-AR2. Titles are working labels**
- Final titles are an author task at publication. No creative effort goes into them now.

**Q-AR3. Punctuation of dialogue**
- **The profile line reads:** a dash for an interruption or cut-off; an ellipsis for a real trailing-away.
  *"Sparingly"* still governs em dashes in narration.
- It applies to Act II onward. Act I's instances go to the line edit.

**Q-AR4. The Act I watch-list is diagnostic, not a drafting checklist**
- **It lives on the review side.** It is used at checkpoints and by revisers, and it is not in the drafting stack:
  - endings;
  - motifs changing what they cost, not their wording;
  - the resolution template;
  - recaps and thesis exits;
  - the shared register;
  - friction.
- **The drafting stack gains one instruction:** *Continue these people and this novel. Do not reproduce Act I's
  successful shapes.*

**Q-AR5. Length** *(ChatGPT's modification, which Claude adopts)*
- **No number in any prose packet.**
- **Scale is monitored externally.** An episode over about 4,500 words is flagged afterwards, and the reviewer asks why:
  duplicated meaning, or genuine event? Events may exceed it.

**Q-AR6. S03**
- Leave it and fact-check it. Judge it as prose at the line edit.
- **Supplement packets carry their scale as form guidance** (for example, *a short piece, about a page*), not as a hard
  target.

**Q-AR7. A decision guard in `ebci/prose/README.md`**

  disagreement about interpretation, witnesses sought, trouble caused by what they think they know, and obligations

  - **Lucien:** physical evidence, measurement, contradiction;
  - **Baz:** testimony and memory;
  - **Seraphine:** people, access and care.
