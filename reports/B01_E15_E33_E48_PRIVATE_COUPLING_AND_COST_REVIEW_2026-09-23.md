## The question beneath the three events

The [Mechanica/Resonance audit](MECHANICA_RESONANCE_SYSTEMS_INTEGRITY_AUDIT_2026-09-23.md) distinguishes controlled human action under Will × Emotion × Intent from ambient load and uncontrolled failure **as a proposed reconciliation**. Historical rules speak of environmental pressure, crowd multiplication and material interaction but provide no measured law by which that pressure produces a sound, moves a pane or alters a circuit. `RP` is a qualitative potential proxy, not a joule ledger. A quiet audible tone, shuddering glass and changing light must not become three interchangeable “emotions made it happen” examples.

**First private design fork, OPEN:** are these events (A) a distributed environmental phenomenon that characters observe/respond to, (B) specific characters' directed Resonance actions, (C) mixtures of ordinary failures and a separate not-yet-specified anomaly, or (D) deliberately unresolved *even to the author*? A, B and C can be designed rigorously. D can support ambiguous prose temporarily but cannot satisfy a causal physics gate.

## Cost is not one bucket

1. **Ordinary consequences**: lost work, interrupted care, transport delays, repairs, fear and changed civic routines. These exist even if the Resonance attribution is wrong.
2. **Cost of a deliberate Resonance-influenced action**: Mechanica requires immediate cost plus lingering consequence. Assign to a named acting person and actual intervention; describe baseline capability, boost or regulation, and how the action pays. E15/E33/E48 observation alone is not such an action.
3. **Ambient event budget / residue**: if A is chosen, define what local condition is spent, displaced, sustained or damaged, its recovery and detection. Current source material gives qualitative pressure and persistence, **not a conservation law**. Do not charge crowds or bystanders an invisible exact energy amount to make the scene work.
4. **Interpretive/social cost**: poor classification, rumor, lost trust and institutional overreach follow evidence and choices; they cannot substitute for a missing physical cause in a narrator-certified effect.

### Minimum decision before causal EBCI

For one small **E15** test case, author/editor should choose whether the sound and amp behavior are (i) ordinarily explainable, (ii) separate ordinary plus genuinely coupled effects, or (iii) both related to a local ambient effect. If (ii)/(iii), privately specify the **first** physical input, coupling and energy/cost ceiling before extrapolating to glass or lights. A rigorous E15 could still end with characters not knowing which account is true. Then test whether that rule predicts anything limited about E33, and permit a **different** coupling or an ordinary co-occurrence; do not manufacture one all-purpose law.

For E33, the chosen technology expression can be a minor degradation. It need only be defined to the level implied by its scene and checked against ordinary behavior, and it must not drive a new obligatory failure sequence. For E48, decide whether the [light variation candidate](../proposals/B01_E48_QUIET_EVENT_OBSERVABLE_CANDIDATE_FOR_REVIEW_2026-09-23.md) earns its place at all before assigning causality.
