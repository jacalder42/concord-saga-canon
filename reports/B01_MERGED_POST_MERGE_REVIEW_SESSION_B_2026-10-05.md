## 1. Task 5: voice distinctiveness

**Method:**
- 2,984 dialogue turns were attributed by explicit tags and adjacent action beats: 697 turns at about 95% precision or
  better. Turn-order propagation was tested and **rejected for voice work** (68% recovery).
- Features per speaker, on samples of 40 lines.
- A blind classifier on stripped lines, 5×5-fold.

**Results:**
- **V1.** Seraphine, Lucien and Baz are barely separable from their lines alone: 34% accuracy against 29% chance.
  Seraphine–Baz is the most confused pair. **Caro is the most distinct voice.** The brief's seed guess that Lucien and
  Caro converge is not supported: they are the least confused pair. *drift*
- **V2.** Lucien's card calls him formal. On the page he contracts as often as Seraphine (share 0.92); his precision
  shows as brevity and rare questions. *drift*
- **V3.** Elisabet's register switches: contracted in E22 and E24, uncontracted in E28. She has 7 attributed lines in
  the whole book. *drift*
- **V4.** Baz's card tell (translating for others) appears once. On the page he is the questioner. *drift*
- **V5.** Caro appears in 8 of 49 chapters (about 11% of the book), **with none from E04 to E26**. When present she
  speaks most. The panel's "under-used" is about presence and spacing, not voice. *gap*
- **V6.** Four cards describe voice with the same verb, *translating* X into Y. That is a likely source of convergence.
  *gap (cards)*
- **V7.** No lead has a habitual form of address, oath or hedge. *gap*
