## 1. Note 1: *she X, not Y*

**The count.** A pattern search of the whole redraft (about 138,000 words) finds **about 170 candidates**, roughly 1.2
per thousand words, or three or four a chapter. It covers four forms:
- *X, not Y*;
- *not X but Y*;
- *Not X.* as a sentence of its own;
- *not X; Y*.

**It is an estimate.** The search overcounts ordinary uses and misses variants such as *It did not X. It Y.* Only a
reader can sort them.

**Where it is densest:** E32, E07, E36, E38, E14, E17 (about 9–13 each).

**How it relates to the existing watch-list.** It sits close to the watch-list's *negative confirmation* item from the
author's 09-29 read. That item was about stating what didn't happen; this one is about the correction, *this, not that*.

**Applied now (review side only):** a question in `ebci/prose/REVIEW_WATCHLIST.md`.

**Open:** whether the writer profile, which is drafting side, also carries it (Q-HR1).

## 2. Note 2: E00's two presences

**E00 as it stands** is about 440 words. Two attentions feel a strain below them and cannot reach it or each other. It
has **no identifying behaviour**.

**The rule it was written to.** The prose packet keeps off the page *"names of anyone or anything. What they are, how
the world works."* The author's own 09-27 wording was *"two unnamed presences perceive strain but cannot intervene;
reveal no cosmology or future"* (Q-AI2).

**The note fits that rule.** Clues in what each **does** are not names and not cosmology. The packet's *"what they are"*
line needs a narrower reading, or one clause: behaviour may point to what they are, and nothing may state it.

**The pronouns match canon use:** Silence *he*, Hope *she*.

**The reprise.** It answers Q-P45-7 (A8) in part: they return before B01 ends, perhaps at the book's close, listening or
watching.
- **B01's close** is E49, with the S06 coda inside it after the break.
- **Open:** whether the reprise is the coda's last beat or its own short section. Also whether A8's optional mid-book
  interlude is still wanted (Q-HR2).

## 3. Note 3: E01

**(a) *The mom is too chill.*** The check reads *the mom* as **Renée**, Dré's mother (26). On the page she:
- is on the floor with him;
- makes one sound;
- holds his hand;
- goes to the kitchen and sits with her back to the door.

**Odile, the grandmother, is dry-eyed by design.** She holds the family together that night. **Q-HR3 asks which of them
he means**, or both.

**(b) The renewal stays in the car.** As written, Seraphine tells Odile on the porch step, after the death, that the
renewal sat on her desk. Odile stops her: *not tonight*. That confession-after-the-death placement was restored on
purpose (ledger §292) and released for drafting (§293).

**The author's direction reverses it.** She says nothing to Odile that night.

**What already fits:**
- **Odile's overheard line about the card**, during the CPR, stays. It is Odile's, not a confession by Seraphine.
- **E02 already has the folder in her car** and has her waiting for Odile to repeat that line.
- **E03's telling to Caro** would become **the first time she tells anyone.**

**What would change:**
- the E01 step scene (Odile's *not tonight*, and the hand on her knee);
- E09's calls, where Odile passes on Renée's question, and whatever Odile is assumed to know by then;
- the thread through E25, E27 and E44, where the Batiste form closes by Seraphine's choice.

**Open: where *later* is.** It could be E09's calls, E25, or held until E44 (Q-HR3).

**The word *card*.** The author's *"the card"* is read as **the renewal (the blue folder).** In E01, *the card* is the
lapsed coverage card in Odile's line. E02 joins the two: the card is in the folder in her car.

## 4. Note 4: names

**(a) The workplace.** It is *the center* throughout. Even her business card (E08) is only *from the center*. **A name is the
author's to give** (Q-HR4).
- **It should be fictional.** It must not be a real New Orleans agency.
- **Every use of *the center* stays as a short form** once the name is established.

**(b) The boss.** **Seraphine's boss is Denise** (E03, E06, E09, E12; then E21, E37, E41, E44). **No boss is named
Mara.**
- **Mara runs the food pantry** at the Knights' hall (E08). She is the community figure in cast row A01.
- **The confusion comes from E03.** Trip greets Seraphine as *one of Mara's caseworkers* five chapters before Mara
  appears. Before E08, that line reads as though Mara were the boss.
- **Fixing Trip's line** removes the confusion (for instance, she knows Seraphine because Mara's pantry sends people to
  her).

**The note is still right about *later*, for another reason.** The saga has three near-names:
- **Mara** (A01, this book's pantry);
- **Mara Niht** (G08, a place anchor);
- **Mira** (L01, the echo guide). She is seeded wordlessly in B02, and her first named appearance is B07 E13.

**Renaming A01 is a canon decision** (Q-HR4).

## 5. Note 5: Lucien's *None*

### 5.1 What the *None* is now

- **E04.** On the coroner's contract-inspection form, the last line asks for conditions observed that affect the health
  or safety of the occupants, *including the inspector*. He writes *None*, although he went down in that room.
- **Seraphine's statement** sits beside it on Dré's file, describing the same pressure.

**The lie is about his body and the room.** Its uses:
- E10: he signs it a second time;
- E12: he recalls it;
- E21: the fight over the form, her paragraph above his line;
- E22;
- E36: his correction letter;
- E48: Guidry says the *None* wasn't so, and Seraphine nods at *my letter*.

**A1 is built on it.** It is the book's first-priority repair: the injury is his denial in the official record beside
her testimony, signed twice.

### 5.3 The two ideas are separable

**(i) A named front for Lucien's office in B01.**
- **It fits B1 and A4.** A named bureau is the legitimate cover; the reader later learns, at B02 E08, that it answers to
  the Dominion. That opens the intrigue the author wants: what did Lucien know?
- **It would amend:**
  - the B01 act overlays' forbidden shortcuts (*"a bureau name; who sent Lucien"*; *"any faction or organisation named"*);
  - the E04 and E10 packet guards (no sponsor or office figure named);
  - the stale *"OPEN"* line in B01's entry state.
- **Fictitious or real.** *"Fictitious"* sits against A4's *legitimate*. It is closer to the recovered *fabricated
  academic cover* variant. **A fabricated cover makes Lucien a knowing agent in B01.** That also sits against:
  - the entry state's *"not a field agent"*;
  - the overlays' ban on him voicing distrust of his office.

**(ii) Replacing *None* with a false affiliation.**
- **This changes what the lie hides:** his sponsor instead of his episode in the room.
- **What it would cost:**
  - Seraphine's statement no longer contradicts his line;
  - A1's injury changes object;
  - E10, E12, E21, E22, E36, E38 and E48 each need a new version;
  - E14 and E24, where he gives his office's name *in full, because it was true*, would reverse.
- **A plausibility point, not a rule.** A contract inspector's credentials and payer are checkable, so a fictitious
  employer on a coroner's form is itself a falsehood someone could catch. That could be a strength, if the author wants
  that risk.

### 5.4 What *wrong word* may mean

The note keeps the mechanic and faults the word. There are two readings:
- **The word itself.** *None* may read as too bare, or too odd for a form. Then the fix is a different false entry for
  the same lie about the room, such as a full false sentence in the form's own register.
- **The kind of lie.** The author may want the lie to be about affiliation (5.3 ii).

**Q-HR5 asks which.**
