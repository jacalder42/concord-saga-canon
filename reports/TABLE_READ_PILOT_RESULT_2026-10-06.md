## 1. What was run

There were three B01 disclosure scenes:
- E39, the car (Lucien's POV);
- E46, the curb (Seraphine's);
- E48, the river (Lucien and Baz).

Each was played by two actor agents with private cards and rendered by a writer agent. Two blind judges then compared
each rendering with the revised draft (2026-10-06) of the same span, in randomised A/B order.

## 2. Result

| Scene | Judge 1 | Judge 2 | Preferred |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E46 | revised, clear | revised, clear | **revised draft** |
| E48 | table read, clear | table read, slight | **table read** |
| E39 | revised, slight | revised, clear | **revised draft** |

## 3. Findings

1. **The table read wins where one character presses and the other refuses** (E48). Wanting an answer, and refusing to
   give one, produced the most human exchange the judges saw.
2. **It loses on choreography.** Every actor turn supplies an ACTION, so the page gets a gesture every turn, and the
   judges read that as an inventory. This is a format artifact, not a fault of the actors.
3. **Lean actors produce telegraphic speech.** Clipped confessions and cryptic references read as mannered. The revised
   draft's longer, argued speech read as more human.
4. **Actors can under-reach the outcome card.** E46 was played quieter than required, and the judges preferred the
   draft's raised voice.
5. **The table read finds single moments a draft misses.** These include a small physical concession, a joke that
   triggers a laugh, and an action that gives up a habit. They are worth grafting.
6. **The revised draft's own weakness is stated feeling** in narration and in a few lines of speech. The judges flagged
   three such lines across E39 and E46.
7. **Continuity.** The judges raised three items in the revised E39 (a slip in speech, a late call, a doze that E41
   contradicts). They are flagged for the line edit and not changed now. Every table-read continuity point raised was
   verified consistent, or flagged.

## 4. Recommendation

- **Not actor-first generation as a default.** It lost two scenes of three, for format reasons.
- **The screenplay model, as the author framed it.** The writer drafts; each actor re-performs only their own lines and
  mannerisms against the draft; the director merges and holds the outcome. This keeps the draft's argued speech and
  takes the moments an actor finds.
  - Actors should be told **not to supply an action every turn**.
  - The director should cap gestures at the merge.
- **Full actor play for pressure scenes:** interrogations, confrontations, and anything where one person wants an
  answer the other will not give.
- **A test of the screenplay model** on the same three spans, judged the same way, before it enters B02 packet
  practice.
