## Appendix: the review, as received (a recommendation, not a decision)

> The external-pressure addition strengthens the book, but I would not approve the combined set unchanged. I checked the full proposal and relevant parts of the merged outline. Several recommendations improve agency; others risk replacing excessive care with another rigid formula.
>
> The most important correction is Q-XP1: approve the ordering, drop the quota.
>
> Want → external pressure → protective reflex → misreading → possible responses is a useful drafting format. But "at least two of every three pieces" is not justified by the evidence, and "what comes first leads" is a working hypothesis about instructions, not a reliable law.
>
> More importantly, some of the apparent improvement is relabelling:
>
> * "Keep Mr. Vidrine on air" becomes "get a held tank out." The second states the practical obstacle better, but the purpose remains care.
> * "Warn her households" becomes "run warnings without clearance." That adds institutional conflict; it does not replace the motive.
> * "Be there first" becomes "be the one who got there first." That genuinely changes motivation toward pride and indispensability—and therefore needs character development, not just a revised label.
>
> Care can drive decisive action. The problem was care repeatedly producing observation, accommodation and repair. We should evaluate what characters choose and change, rather than count which category heads the objective.
>
> Three further corrections matter before approval.
>
> 1. Preserve ordinary institutions outside the conspiracy.
>
> The progression from everyday friction to recognisable organised pressure works. But not every supervisor, refusal or missing record should eventually point upstairs.
>
> Keep some pressures independent: Denise's judgement, Sal's business, patient confidentiality, infrastructure access. Otherwise the living city becomes an apparatus for the mystery, and the protagonists' legitimate opponents become proxies for hidden factions.
>
> Also replace "every source has a legitimate claim" with:
>
> Identify its actual authority, its stated justification and whose interests it serves. Some demands are reasonable; others are self-serving or abusive.
>
> We already removed the requirement that every character's goal be legitimate. It should not return through institutions.
>
> 2. Narrow Q-MO2's timing claim.
>
> "It doesn't grow, and the gaps don't keep a schedule" overstates the negative findings described in the outline.
>
> The evidence supports:
>
> The events have not grown consistently larger, and the observed intervals do not support their proposed schedule.
>
> That disproves their forecast without establishing permanent laws about resonance. Keep that distinction in EBCI; dialogue can remain natural and provisional.
>
> 3. Clarify what Lucien recognises in Q-XP7.
>
> "His own unsent recommendation carried out by someone else" risks suggesting access to his private correspondence or thoughts.
>
> The intended observation appears to be:
>
> Someone else has independently deployed instruments—the approach he considered recommending.
>
> That is a strong moment of recognition. It is not evidence that they obtained his recommendation. If the equipment contains a uniquely matching detail, that becomes a different clue requiring explanation.
>
> His unsent question to R. is otherwise workable. The blank inference column is less essential: another notebook gesture could repeat the counting-and-recording habits you disliked. Keep it only if it contributes something his subsequent choice does not already show.
>
> Seraphine's new want is useful, but pleasure should not be demoted to decoration.
>
> "Her own account on the record" gives her a concrete claim that can oppose Renée, Lucien and an institution. I support it.
>
> However, refusing to retract an honest account is not automatically pride. The scene must establish whether she protects truth, seeks recognition, refuses correction, or mixes those motives.
>
> And "a room that doesn't need her" is a substantial personal want. It directly conflicts with indispensability. Let it occasionally determine where she goes, whom she chooses and what obligation she leaves unanswered. It need not become her primary arc to matter.
>
