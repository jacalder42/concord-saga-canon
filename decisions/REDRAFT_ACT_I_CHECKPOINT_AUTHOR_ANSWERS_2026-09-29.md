## 1. The author's message, verbatim

> I support the focused revision pass. I would qualify the claim that the opening now "holds up on its own." The
> reported reads show improvement, but an editor nearly stopping at E09 and a reader declining to start Act II
> immediately are meaningful reservations. The scores do not cancel those findings.
>
> Based on this checkpoint—not a fresh manuscript read—my recommendations are:
>
> | Decision | Recommendation |
> | --- | --- |
> | Q-AC1: retain the E05 fold | Yes. Record the delivery inside E03 and keep one clear reading sequence so nobody later restores the duplicate. |
> | Q-AC2: trim E11 | Yes, provisionally. Preserve the particular pleasure or vulnerability that makes Lucien worth knowing. About 1,200 words is an estimate, not another target to fill. |
> | Q-AC3: focused revision | Yes, with the scope clarified below. |
> | Q-AC4: camp-rule interpretation | Yes. Remembering a witnessed collapse does not establish the camp as its cause or source. Characters need access to their own experience. |
> | Q-AC5: two-records realization | Keep it, with a causality check. Different accounts of two different incidents are not automatically contradictory. |
>
> E15's agency problem may be improved rather than solved. Seraphine defining the outsider's qualifications and saying
> "call now" gives her authority, but the substantive work still appears to pass to Baz.
>
> The scene needs a convincing answer to: what can Baz contribute that Seraphine and Lucien cannot? Being someone who
> asks questions is insufficient, because Seraphine has already been doing that. His particular method, independence,
> contacts or expertise must justify the invitation within what is already established about him. Seraphine should
> retain a distinct investigative task that advances alongside his—not merely permission to keep questioning her
> clients.
>
> Likewise, I would revise the proposed Act II obligation from "Seraphine acts first" to "Seraphine initiates a
> consequential line of inquiry and retains ownership of it." First position is bookkeeping. Causal importance is
> agency. Her action need not become another mistake with a penalty; competence, curiosity or a successful connection
> can move the story.
>
> For the two records, preserve Lucien's fear without overstating what the documents prove. A claim that nothing
> happened at the camp and a record of his later collapse at the Square can both appear consistent to an outsider. What
> may endanger him is that the later event invites scrutiny of his earlier account, especially with Seraphine as a
> witness. That is a credible threat. An automatic documentary contradiction needs stronger support.
>
> The revision pass should address the remaining patterns through choices and scene function, not just language:
>
> * Older characters: changing their vocabulary will not solve the chorus if all deliver accurate moral judgments. Give
>   them different interests, incomplete knowledge and different relationships with Seraphine. Some can be wrong; some
>   need something from her.
> * E09: identify its indispensable turn, then shorten the approach and aftermath. Don't preserve the sag merely because
>   the funeral matters.
> * Folder and Renée: keep the unresolved obligation alive through a decision, avoided opportunity or practical
>   consequence. Repeated guilty recollections would revive the same disclosure-heavy structure.
> * E14's retelling: retain aftermath that changes interpretation or behavior; cut replay that merely reminds readers of
>   the scene they just experienced.
>
> Finish this revision, then move into Act II. Give the revised E09–E15 stretch one continuous read focused on forward
> pull. The test is whether the reader wants the next development—not whether another round produces a higher score.
