## The momentum and serialization audit (diagnostic; no revisions drafted)

**The working assumption:** **the existing chapters are the serialization modules.**
- Do not start from the older idea of subdividing B01 into 1,200–1,800-word daily episodes.
- Treat chapter boundaries as presumptively correct, not untouchable.
- Recommend a split, merge, relocation or new boundary only on strong evidence of a meaningful momentum problem; never
  merely to create more frequent release units.
- Word count alone is not a defect. A long chapter is fine if it keeps changing, escalating, complicating or
  rewarding attention.

**Three scales:**
1. **Whole-book momentum.**
   - Find stretches where investigation, character, relationship, mystery, external pressure or consequence stops
     materially changing the board.
   - Distinguish deliberate pauses (grief, dread, recovery, consolidation) from genuine stalls.
2. **Internal chapter momentum.** Does a reader who begins the chapter have reason to continue through the middle?
   Look for:
   - dead middles;
   - repeated information;
   - delayed scene engines;
   - long setup before the real question;
   - several scenes doing one function.
3. **Chapter-to-chapter propulsion.** Each exit and the next opening are judged as a pair: what creates the desire to
   continue?
   - It might be an unresolved question, consequence, discovery, threat, relationship movement, anticipation,
     reversal, emotional residue or curiosity.
   - **No cliffhanger requirement**; variety is better than one manufactured hook shape.

**Each chapter as an installment.** Read discretely, it need not be a complete miniature plot, but it should give
enough movement, development, revelation, pleasure, tension or consequence to be worth the reader's time.

**Quiet chapters doing emotional or atmospheric work are protected.** Low banter, humor, heat or action is not low
momentum if another engine carries the chapter.

**Rest chapters,** especially those the beta readers flagged. Do not assume the complaint means shorten, accelerate or
remove.
- Ask:
  - what the reader is resting from;
  - what the chapter gives them during the rest;
  - what makes them want to resume.
- Classify each as:
  - **earned deceleration**;
  - **necessary but underpowered rest**;
  - **actual stall**.
- Examine **placement and accumulation**:
  - a good rest can still hurt if it follows another deceleration, interrupts an engine as it becomes compelling, or
    delays a promised consequence too long;
  - a quiet chapter after sustained pressure may help.

**Beta evidence against the text.** Where beta complaints and the text disagree, report the disagreement; neither is
automatically right. Where they converge, treat that as stronger evidence.

**Per chapter:**
- the primary momentum engine;
- where it begins;
- whether it strengthens, changes or stalls internally;
- the exit's continuation engine;
- whether the next chapter pays, redirects or needlessly dissipates that energy;
- **Protect / Watch / Revise**, and for Revise only, the smallest likely intervention.

**Then:**
- a whole-book momentum map (accelerates, cruises, deliberately decelerates, actually stalls);
- attention to **cumulative effects**: three competent chapters can make a flat run if they share an engine, repeat
  an investigative motion, or defer payoff the same way.

**The final serialization recommendation:** does the existing chapter architecture work as B01's serial release
architecture?
- If yes, name only exceptional chapters.
- If no, say exactly where and why before proposing alternatives.

**The goal:** not a book impossible to put down through constant escalation, but one where *whenever the novel asks
the reader to slow down, the reader still wants to stay.*
