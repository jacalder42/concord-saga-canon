## 3. Execution principles (for EBCI and prose)

1. **"Keep every element; point at none"** is elevated from B09 E48 to a saga-wide principle.
2. **"A breadcrumb may be structurally deliberate while remaining narratively incidental."** The
   breadcrumb ledger is for the writers, not the reader. The reader should occasionally realise
   something was there the whole time, and should not repeatedly recognise a callback as a callback.
3. **The ordinary world needs a great deal of material that pays off nothing.** That protects the
   sense of life.
4. **Mira's eight appearances:** *"Every Mira appearance must change something Seraphine decides. If
   it doesn't, cut it"* is an **EBCI deletion test**. No appearance is cut now.
5. **The B08 wounds:** the remaining burden belongs to EBCI and prose: make the **human experience**
   different at each wound, not merely the technical problem.

## 4. The pilot's gate

- **E31 and E33 test opposite failure modes.** E33: physical causality, observation class,
  uncertainty, breadcrumbs, limits, cost, mechanism. **E31: whether EBCI can handle two people having
  a night.** If the E31 packet acquires obligations, opposition codes, a Resonance beat, breadcrumbs
  and a dramatic turn because the template expects them, **the EBCI system has failed**.
- **The gate is a narrative review, not schema validation:** *"If I handed this to a good novelist,
  would it help them write a better scene—or would they spend their energy satisfying the packet?"*
  If E31 feels loose, human and inviting, proceed. **If it feels predetermined, simplify EBCI before
  multiplying it.**
- The two packets are **independently reviewed** before any further B01 packet is built.
