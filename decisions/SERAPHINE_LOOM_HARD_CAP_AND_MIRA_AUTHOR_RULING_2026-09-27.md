## 2. What stays ruled and unchanged

- **Seraphine is the Loom and the continuous thread** (the chain ruling §4). Q1 removes the claim
  that the veil depends on her alone. It does not remove her role.
- **The Loom is powered by a soul.** Every earlier cycle required a Mira / Seraphine
  (`decisions/MIRA_AND_SILENCE_HOPE_ORIGIN_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md` §5).
- **The cost:** Seraphine believes it is hers alone. Lucien and Caro realise at the Mending that they
  must become the guides (the chain ruling).
- **The Mending order:** Seraphine → Hope → Silence (after powering Tahl's echo) → Lucien → Elisabet's
  goodbye → Caro (M33).
- **Mira:** perceived only by Seraphine; she guides her past recreating the hard cap; her echo is
  released during the Mending. Her appearance schedule and the Veil seed's form stay **open**.
- **The answer is reserved for the very end** and *"blindsides all of them"* (the author's 11-15
  choice).

## 3. What follows

These are consequences of the rulings, not new rulings.

- **The Regier concern (C01) now has a ruled answer.** Seraphine's false belief, *"If I falter,
  everything collapses"*, is held by Silence and Hope as their old logic (*"One Loom, one Thread, one
  Ascendant"*). She holds it too, until the Mending, when the veil turns out to require guides.
  - The finale no longer repeats that belief (Q1).
  - The new veil is bounded (Mechanica §42: *"Human failure remains possible"*).
  - She tethers; she does not regulate (Q1).
- **The recorded tension with the author's 11-13 line, *"through Mira Seraphine believes D"*, is
  resolved by Q3.** Mira's own history teaches Seraphine that a soul must be given, which is D. Her
  guidance teaches that recreating the cap is not enough. She does not supply the alternative.
- **M21's *"first crack in the cycle of hard-cap veils"* is now the start of an ending** (Q2).
  Mira's release at the Mending is the visible end of the succession (Q2, Q4).
- **Mechanica §40**, *"The Mending permanently alters how resonance behaves"*, agrees with Q2.
