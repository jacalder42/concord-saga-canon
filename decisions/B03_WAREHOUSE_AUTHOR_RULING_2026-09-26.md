## 1. The post and the aftermath

**The post's staging narrows to two options.** A **visible supplemental text** puts the
anonymous post on the page as an in-world document. The book's supplement form is already a
structural axis. **Effect-only** shows the crowd without the post. Either way, the reader meets
the post's consequence before learning its author in the epilogue. The card's interlock (§2)
stands under both options: anonymous post, then epilogue reveal, then B4 remorse.

**The epilogue now has an aftermath beat, hedged.** Tahl reads reports. This does three things
for the card:

- **It gives the crowd hypothesis (link 4) an on-page form.** *"Unexpected bystanders"* are
  people who were not supposed to be there, which is what the post would produce. The
  hypothesis gets stronger but is still not ruled: the author's example says *"unexpected
  bystanders,"* not that his post drew them.
- **It ties the crowd to the delay (link 9).** *"Hampering efforts"* makes the bystanders an
  obstacle to the response. That is the crowd → delay edge the card marked HYPOTHESIS.
- **It places Tahl's discovery of what the post did** in the epilogue, next to his reveal. He
  learns the consequence from reports, as the public does.

**"A first responder perishing" has two readings. Neither is chosen here.**

1. **The report is about Baz, unnamed.** Baz is an investigator by role (`BazID.md`), but at the
   Warehouse he acts as a rescuer. He goes in and frees a child (link 11). A report could call
   him a responder without naming him. This fits answer 2: Tahl reads about the death without
   knowing whose it is.
2. **A separate responder dies.** That would be a second death, which no recovered source
   contains. It would add a casualty to the event, so it needs the author's confirmation.

The author's *"or etc."* marks the example as illustrative. The card records both readings.

## 2. Tahl did not know

**RULED: Tahl did not know Baz was there.** The card (§5) said his remorse turns on whether he
knew. The answer settles it: **Tahl's remorse is for a consequence he did not foresee, not for a
risk he took knowingly.** He posted accurate coordinates. The accuracy drew people in (link 4,
still hedged), and someone died.

**The hedged half** (*"may well not even know Baz's name until much later"*) sets up a delayed
recognition. Tahl carries remorse for an anonymous death, and learns later that it was Baz.
The rest of the cast learns of Baz's death at the start of B04 (Baz death timing ruling).
**Tahl learns it later than they do, perhaps much later.** When, and from whom, is open.

`BazID.md` records the truth lineage as **Baz → Tahl → Kade**. If the name comes late, the
lineage's middle member inherits from someone he could not name. That is an observation, not a
ruling.

## 3. Links 7 and mechanisms A and B — the delegated recommendation

**Recommendation (PROPOSAL, delegated):** keep **both** mechanisms, alongside the crowd, and
split them along the author's lean.

| | Actor | Presence | Role in the delay | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mechanism A** | **Dominion**, the perimeter; Caldas Ren recovered there | **Expected.** The official, sanctioned response | Contains the zone instead of evacuating it | Warehouse recovery: *"Dominion enforcer appears at warehouse-zone perimeter"* |
| **Mechanism B** | **Technarc** retrieval, Rook & Harlow | **Not expected.** Nobody on the page accounts for them | Holds the site for evidence; obstructs egress; withholds danger information | Warehouse recovery: *"retrieval duo attempts to secure evidence from warehouse"* |
| **Link 7** | **Technarc** is the antagonist who "intervenes" | The unexpected presence **is** the intervention | Delays; does not cause the Rupture or kill Baz | 09-19 recollection; the recovery's boundary |

**Why this fits the saga, and not only the scene:**

1. **It follows the author's lean exactly.** Dominion expected, Technarc not.
2. **It keeps the recovery's boundary.** Neither actor causes the Rupture or directly kills Baz.
   The recovery's verdict holds: *"No individual needs to murder him."*
3. **Technarc's unsanctioned presence gives it something to hide afterwards.** The recovery
   already says the retrieval *"establishes Neon evidence-control behavior"* and that the
   institutional lesson feeds *"Protocol 9/Neon."* A presence that was not supposed to happen is
   a presence to conceal. That feeds the B05 function the author agreed to on 2026-09-23:
   *"fallible technical evidence and possible institutional concealment."*
4. **It puts a cost on Technarc's own model.** The Warehouse recovery says Han Wei *"fails to
   predict the Warehouse Rupture."* Under this split, Technarc's own team is inside a site its
   model misjudged. That is a consequence, not a punishment, and it gives the Neon hardening a
   reason from inside Technarc.
5. **The expected Dominion presence explains the reports.** The epilogue's *"bystanders hampering
   efforts"* reads naturally as the official, Dominion-side account of events. Tahl reads that
   account knowing what the reports do not: that the post drew the bystanders there.
6. **Mechanism C stays off.** Lucien's guilt already has an author-locked cause (*"he asked Baz
   to come to New Orleans"*, Warehouse recovery). An expected Dominion presence keeps Lucien's
   Dominion thread nearby without writing an extraction into the collapse, which the recovery
   warned may conflict with the B04 notification ruling.

**Expected by whom** is open. The author's lean does not say. Three candidates, each changing
the scene: expected by **the public and the reports**, so Dominion's presence reads as normal; by
**Baz**, who plans around a perimeter and meets retrieval agents he did not plan for; or by
**Tahl**, whose post assumed a sanctioned response would handle the site. This document does not
choose.

**How to override:** the author can reject the split, pick one mechanism, or name another actor.
Card links 5–7 then revert to their earlier statuses. No other document depends on this
recommendation yet.
