## 1. Decided

| # | Decided |
| --- | --- |
| **Q-WP1** | **Veil: third person limited, past tense.** It is a Veil production rule, not a universal rule for every J.A. Calder project. **Multi-POV episodes hand over at a clean scene or section break; no head hopping** |
| **Q-WP2** | **Veil takes the later romance lineage:** H0–H1 common, H2 rare, **no H4**, an **R-rating ceiling**. The trilogy context's H0–H4 is **system capability, not a requirement**. The standing image list (*"hands, breath, mouths, pulse points"*) is replaced with: *"Physical detail stays selective and character-specific. Sensuality comes through attention, proximity, choice and response rather than anatomical inventory or explicit mechanics."* The list risks becoming its own AI tell |
| **Q-WP3** | **No permanent per-character voice layer.** The drafting input stays: **writer profile + the episode's prose packet + the immediately preceding prose and context.** For a character's **first substantial POV appearance only**, one compact card line may be promoted, preferring **how the character notices or thinks** over verbal tics. **Once prose establishes a voice, the preceding prose governs its performance** unless it creates a canon contradiction |
| **Refinements** | **1.** Near the start: *"These are tendencies, not required moves. Never demonstrate the voice guide for its own sake. If a technique becomes noticeable as a pattern, stop using it."* **2.** *"Prefer rhythm, image, choice and behavior to naming an emotion directly. Name it when the character genuinely would."* **3.** *"Don't substitute stock physical tells for emotional work. Characters may smile, laugh, look away, etc.; the gesture just can't do all the writing by itself."* **4.** *"Be suspicious of unearned narrative certainty. Absolutes belong where the POV has earned them—or where their certainty reveals character."* *"The profile should shape taste, not behave like a linting program."* |
| **No new layer** | **No further planning or prose-preparation layer** |

## 2. The calibration

**Three calibration drafts, not canonical prose:**
- **E06:** ordinary life, voice, culture.
- **E13:** relationship, interiority.
- **E15:** the event, the strange, ensemble pressure.

**The stack is only:** the writer profile, the episode's prose packet, and the preceding context needed to
understand the scene. **No EBCI control layer, writer options, causal cards or future architecture
reaches the engine.**

**Then stop for a qualitative editorial review. It is not scored.** The questions:
- Does one voice survive all three modes?
- Do the characters act as people, not as endpoint-delivery systems?
- Did prose discover worthwhile material?
- Does the strange stay observable?
- Does humour come from character and pressure?
- **Did the profile itself create repeated Calderisms or AI tells?**

**Distinguish engine or draft problems from instruction problems before changing the profile.** Do not
draft the rest of Act I.

*"We have crossed the planning/drafting boundary now. From here, a messy but alive scene is more valuable
to the project than another perfectly structured planning artifact."*

## 3. What this does not change

The architecture, EBCI and packets. The profile changes are the four refinements and the three answers
above.
