## 2. The six directions: approved as criteria

The review's six directions (§6) are **approved design**. They are the criteria that B02/B03 episode
architecture must meet before sign-off, when the hold is lifted:

1. **Seraphine's agency.** Every book gives her a consequential decision. Veil develops bounded,
   shared responsibility. Her ending must visibly differ from compulsory emotional caretaking.
2. **B02's engine.** A civic decision to act on uncertain evidence, ending in a gained capability or
   commitment and its cost, alongside drift plus No Reset.
3. **Warehouse responsibility.** Public truth is both useful and consequential. Tahl's contribution,
   institutional choices, Baz's rescue and the physical failure are kept separate. It is now read with
   answer 2: no blame, just inference.
4. **Earned opposition and competence.** A sensible person has a reason to accept institutional help,
   and the protagonists' alternative has a cost. No faction redemption is implied.
5. **Finale fairness and accountability.** Veil seeds the capabilities and values that make the late
   guide cost intelligible, without revealing it. The flare interrupts Kade, and his choices afterwards
   begin his accountability. Romance and LT access do not absolve harm.
6. **Distinct book rewards and future obligations.** B01's ordinary-life standard is kept, the two
   deaths are differentiated, and B05 and B08 change practical options through choices. The Mira
   obligation in Veil is now bounded by answer 1: subtle.

**Scope of this approval:**

- The **"Minimum evidence"** column of the review's §6 table is the test for each criterion. **It is
  not a quota or an episode count.** The review's own caution applies.
- The repair options in the review's **thirteen findings (C01–C13)** are **not** approved one by one.
  They stay proposals that the architecture may draw on.
- The review's **"What can remain deferred"** list stays deferred. These directions **do not add a
  gate**.
- **No canon, card, row status or ruling changes.**
