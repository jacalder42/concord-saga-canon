## 2. The author's caution: the contrast family

*"A caution on blanketing removal of x, not y rule. Ultimately we want less artificial sounding prose."*

The audit's R9 would have scoped the contrast family (*not X, but Y*; *it wasn't X, it was Y*) to narration only. That
would have freed it wholesale in dialogue. That is **not adopted**. The test is artificiality in both places:

- **Narration:** as before. The family is costed (Q-HR1), with the line's existing wording.
- **Dialogue:** judged by whether this person would say it, here, to this listener.
  - **Speech:** a correction made in an argument, to someone, about something concrete. It comes in the speaker's own
    words, often unfinished or interrupted.
  - **The narrator speaking through a character:** a balanced, quotable reversal that sums a scene, a feeling or a
    person up. It is cut or rewritten.
  - **When in doubt,** read it aloud as that person.
- **Neither place gets a quota or a blanket ban.** The governing aim for every dialogue rule here is **less
  artificial-sounding prose**. A rule change that makes speech more written-sounding is the wrong reading of it.
