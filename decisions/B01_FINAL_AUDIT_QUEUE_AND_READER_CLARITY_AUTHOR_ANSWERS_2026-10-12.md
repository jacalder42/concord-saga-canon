## Author direction: reader clarity

Verbatim: *"the readers have been right that the observers need to be visible, more aggressive, and more clear. I think
several of your observations require perfect memory and recognition from the reader, which will not reflect how human
readers behave. Consider how the prose needs to be handled so that the average adult reader will be able to follow
along. The mystery can stay mysterious but if only a handful understand there was even a mystery at all, we have
failed. Same goes for romance, and conflict. We've nailed social issues but the rest are under resolved."*

Applied as standing direction for B01 work from here:

- **The test is the average adult reader**, reading once, not a reader with perfect memory. A beat that only lands for a
  reader who recognizes an initial, a tab, or an object from many chapters earlier does not count as landed.
- **The opposition must be visible, aggressive and clear** on the page.
- **The mystery may stay unexplained, but must be legible as a mystery**; the same for the romance and the conflict.
- Assistant analyses and proposals must not rely on subtle callbacks as evidence that something is on the page.

"Observers" is read as the opposition watching the crew (Caldas's side and the collectors). A clarity proposal comes to
the author before any broad pass; the guards stay binding until he answers it.
