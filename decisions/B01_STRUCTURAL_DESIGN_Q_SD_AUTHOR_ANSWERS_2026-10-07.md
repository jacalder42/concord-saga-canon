## The author's execution cautions (binding on the revision)

- **Caldas.** *Control of the record* is his observable want, never a stated thesis. The reader infers it from
  copies, custody, witnesses, logs and names. If anyone says it, it is overdone.
- **The fourth at the table.** The recognition is not made explicit. The reader needs only enough matching detail to
  think *that's the same man*; there is no narrator confirmation.
- **The prop rule** is a pattern, not heraldry. No Caldas-side person visibly completes a checklist.
- **The walk** may become one of the book's most important scenes. It is not to be overburdened with plot.
  - Its function is Seraphine choosing proximity, then discovering the limit of Lucien's honesty.
  - The Guidry and addition material passes through that emotional line; it is not an evidence conference.
  - The key beat: **she intends to ask him upstairs; he gives the careful non-answer; she doesn't.** It is not
    explained afterward.
- **C1.** Keep it sharp: he corrected his record before the kiss, so the correction is not romance-induced repair. She
  confirms the duration he would not confirm for her account.
- **E48.** The full cut of about 1,400 words. *Faster was wrong* survives only because it affects the planning. No
  miniature remnants are kept because they are good sentences.
- **Exits.** Not louder: some stay quiet. **The quiet image must be the consequence**, not a tasteful paragraph after
  it. A phone face down is a great ending when putting it down is the decision.
- **Length.** The projected 164–166k is a result of removing duplicated work, not a target.
