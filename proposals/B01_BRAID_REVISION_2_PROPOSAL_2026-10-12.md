## 1. The shape in one paragraph

**Act I** keeps ordinary life whole longer:
- Ch 9–11 lose investigation weight.
- The first crack in Lucien's trust in Vienna comes at Ch 10–11. That is what sends him to Baz.

**Act II** lets the investigation eat their days:
- Every test ends in a counted result.
- The repeated table arguments and confessions are summarized.
- Silence and Hope return once at the midpoint.
- Elisabet and the Vein carry more of the middle.

**Act III** becomes a race:
- After the lot, the crew forecasts the festival from the closing gaps between events.
- They fail to get it stopped, partly because the false Kerlerec report has spent their credibility.
- Two days before it, Caldas's side takes a "first" into custody.
- The crew opens Baz's folder and learns the Institute's own picture of what is happening. They have already tested it, and won, with counts.
- After the festival Caldas pays for Mr. Ledet's wrist.

The book lands at about **117–120k**.

## 2. The proposals, by direction item

### 2.1 Caldas pays for hurting someone (item 1)

Two linked consequences, both inside the prop rule and the "no narrator certifies cause" rule:

- **L2, the cause the reader sees.**
  - After the festival, Mr. Ledet takes his wrist to Rieux, because the man now has a spelled name (R-E-N).
  - Rieux writes it up and the report "goes up". Rieux keeps his own copy.
  - No prosecution is certified.
- **L1, the cost on his own side.**
  - A short unsigned desk note follows, mirroring the S2 note.
  - It records that the landlord's wrist is on a hospital record and that the gentleman was named to a police officer in front of a crowd.
  - Its decisions: by paper from here, not in person; no further approach to Baz this season; nothing to the file.
  - Caldas loses what he wanted most in S2, which was to speak to Baz himself.
  - This follows the Dominion card's own rule that harm is done through procedure, not personally.
  - It leads into B02, where Caldas works through paper, and it keeps his Book 4 role.

Cost: about +0.6–0.9k, in the C41 Monday coda or a short Baz beat, plus the note.

### 2.2 A drastic act against a "first" (item 5)

**What canon recommends.** A coercive "interview", not a disappearance.
- **Support:**
  - The Dominion card describes coercion presented as protection, harm by paperwork, and "do not escalate theatrically".
  - The one recorded Vienna precedent is people used as observations: warnings withheld to keep the data clean.
  - The draft's own intake question is the method.
- **Against a disappearance:** it is supported only by old ChatGPT plans (Tier D). It also spends B03's dread and leaves B02 little room to escalate.
- **No author words exist** on what the Dominion does to "firsts".

**Recommended (option A): Vernice Pellerin is held for "evaluation".**
- **The order.** A city-signed protective-custody order takes her on Thursday 30 April.
  - She is a recorded first: she put her pen down before the fall at the wedding in Ch 6.
  - Rieux carries out the order and dislikes it.
- **Who sees it.** Baz, scouting the Square, hears his own Vienna question asked of her.
- **Caldas.** He alludes to it in one sentence at the Pontalba table. That makes Seraphine's refusal cost something she can see, and her leaving her post at the festival becomes protective.
- **Release.** She is out on Monday 4 May, having signed a statement, so they got a first's account after all.
  - The countable result: one person held three days, one account taken, one voice gone from the Square.
- **The plant.** The bullhorn preacher (Ch 6) is quietly gone from his spot from mid-book. Nobody remarks on it until Vernice is taken.
- **The ladder this makes.** Caldas's escalation becomes one person (B01), then a block (B02's clearance), then lethal (B03).
- **What remains to settle:**
  - Who signs the statement behind the order (Q-RV3).
  - A realism check on Louisiana's coroner-issued protective-custody procedure. The page states no statute.

Cost: about +1.2–1.8k, in a short Baz section or at the head of Ch 37's Square scene. Ch 36 (the silence) is untouched.

### 2.3 Real payoffs: the Veil and resonance (item 4), revised

The author has lifted the silence and confirmation guards, and *resonance* may appear. That opens the win the book has
lacked. **The crew's investigation produces a working model of resonance, names it, and uses it to make a forecast
that comes true.**

**The investigation's arc (Acts II–III):**
1. **The crew builds its own rules from counted tests.** Each rule is scored on Lucien's board (§2.6):
   - It gathers where many people carry a lot at once: grief, crowds, the repast, the wedding.
   - It keeps to places where it has gathered before: two tins of four moved, both on St. Ann.
   - Water and old glass go first: ripples before the fall, three times of three.
   - It comes faster as it builds: the gaps between events close.
   - It is not her: her tin moved while she was away.
2. **The folder gives the other side's word for it.**
   - The page Baz took from Vienna is an Institute sheet. It names what the crew has been counting **resonance**: feeling pressing on the world, gathering, keeping to places.
   - It names what holds it **the Veil**, thinner in some places, where things come through.
   - It says why the Institute wants people who feel it first: they find the thin places.
   - The crew reads it and recognises its own rules. **The page confirms that their model is right.** This is now allowed, and it is the investigation's earned win.
   - Baz's "I think they're looking for the Veil" becomes knowledge, not a guess.
3. **The model forecasts the festival, and the forecast is met** (§2.7).
   - From the closing gaps, the returning places and the biggest crowd of the year, they forecast the Square, Saturday, a two-hour window.
   - The city refuses to cancel. They prepare instead: posts, tins, the microphone, sitting.
   - It comes on the Square inside the window. That is the book's biggest counted win: a prediction met, and the injury score of people who sat against people who stood.
4. **Seraphine's net is the same thing.**
   - She says aloud that what she has felt since the Vein is resonance between people.
   - The Silence and Hope interlude (§2.4) shows the reader it is true.
   - Nothing names her role or what she will become.

**What stays reserved** (their own rulings, not silence rules):
- the hard cap, the Loom and the soul that powers it, the cycle, Mira;
- the four-wound chain and the original tear;
- the Mending site;
- Tahl's B06 wound pattern;
- the finale's answer.

**What stays as a world rule:**
- The public does not acknowledge any of it (Mechanica §6). The city refuses the forecast as a man's notebook.
- No powers.

**The model's limits.** The Institute's sheet is their picture: it says *where* and *what feeds it*. It does not say *why it is wearing thin* or *what holds it*. Those stay open questions for B02 and B03.

**What this spends:**
- **B02:** "New Orleans is not unique" stays B02's, as long as every B01 finding stays in the city. The E22 and E41 control notes change from "he has nothing" to "he has their picture".
- **B03:** E01's framing of Lucien's map becomes an extension of the model, not its discovery.
- **Neon:** "emotional amplification is visible" (Mechanica §7.2) is partly anticipated; Neon's version becomes public and quantified.
- **Breadcrumbs:** the `BC-WEB-FELT` rung moves earlier.

**One open question (Q-RV6): does calm soften the event itself?**
- Canon says crowds multiply resonance. A told, seated, calm crowd could measurably shrink the festival event, and that would be a second, larger win.
- Until now the "no control" design guard said the warning helps people and never touches the event.
- **Recommended:** a partial yes.
  - The festival event is measurably smaller than the lot's: shorter, fewer windows. The crew's count shows it.
  - The page lets the reader and the crew wonder whether the calm did it, and the Silence and Hope interlude or the Epilogue confirms that the net held better where people held together.
  - It must not become a technique the crew can switch on. That keeps control out of B01 and leaves Neon room.

### 2.6 Experiments end in countable wins or losses (item 7)

**The rule.** Every test states its result in the same chapter: a number, or met / failed. A character says it or the board carries it, with what it cost or saved. Scenes that only set a test up are compressed.

**Five wins already on the page are never scored:**
- the signed page's forecast held: Ursulines, day 5 of 10, inside the lines;
- Seraphine was right to keep the window open past day 4;
- Baz's "coming faster" bet was met by Carrollton;
- the Kerlerec bait worked;
- the sit-down injury counts.

**The sit-down score, totalled once at the festival.** Sitting, nobody hurt; standing, several. The figure must be counted from the final text. This is the crew's own win. It is about injuries, never about the event.

**Lucien's board becomes the scoreboard.** Each line carries a score, for example "ripples, then legs: 2 of 3".

**Losses count too:**
- the failed midday forecast (Ch 29–31);
- Mrs. Arceneaux's hip, the cost of their choice not to tell;
- the failed attempt to stop the festival (§2.7).

**Six repeated shapes are named.** No two tests are staged in the same shape:
- **The "table page"** (an argument that ends in a written or signed line) occurs 11 times. Two stay in scene: Ch 18, the signed page, and Ch 37, the plan, compressed. The rest are reduced to their decision line.
- **The field walk** is front-loaded, with five of six instances by Ch 13. Ch 10's paces are cut and Ch 11 is halved.

### 2.8 Lucien's Vienna contact (item 9)

**What canon says.**
- N.'s identity is left unstated.
- Helena Kael (C04) cannot be N. in B01 without spending her B02 entrance.
- The best fit is **Marius Holt** (registry C03, an "archive conscience" who leaks).
  - He matches N.'s "as a friend, not as the office" in Ch 29.
  - On the page he would be **Marius**, because "Holt" sounds too like "Hollen".
  - Lucien's notes would be initialled to him.

**Recommended: name him Marius.**
- That honours the author's "named contact" and reverses Q-ST7's "initial only".
- The audit's alternative is to keep "N." and give him a voice and a history instead.

**The trust break (the author's order).**
- Today the trust breaks only after Baz arrives (Ch 16 → 29 → 30), and Lucien's Ch 11 call to Baz is about method.
- **Recommended:**
  - In Act I, Marius gets a voice in his weekly replies.
  - At Ch 10, Marius forwards something Lucien never sent: a summons that shows his own reports are being read elsewhere. Or Marius answers Lucien's letter with "passed up; you will be contacted locally".
  - At Ch 11 that crack sends Lucien to call Baz, a man outside the office whom he can trust.
- Baz still arrives at the start of Act II, as ruled, and Lucien remains the reason he comes.
- The trust then decays as now, through the request for Seraphine's particulars, the 12:06 line coming back, and the weekly note left due.

**Cost:** about +0.3–0.5k. Every N. on the page (20 instances, listed in the audit's §6) is renamed.
