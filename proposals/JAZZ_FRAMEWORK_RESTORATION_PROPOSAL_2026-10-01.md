# Restoring the Jazz Framework: proposal and questions

**Date:** 2026-10-01
**Status:** PROPOSAL, for the author. It builds on the recovery
(`recovery/JAZZ_FRAMEWORK_SOURCE_RECOVERY_2026-10-01.md`) and the diagnostic read
(`reports/B01_DRAFT3_JAZZ_LENS_READ_2026-10-01.md`).

**What it does not change:** nothing is applied until the author answers. No writer profile, template, packet,
substrate file or manuscript text changes. Everything here is Tier D until it is approved.

## 1. What was lost, and why it matters

The author's framework is five axes:
- **Melody:** the protagonists.
- **Harmony:** the world.
- **Rhythm:** the structure, carried by a rhythm section of accent beats and reader payoffs.
- **Jazz (Performance):** the *how*.
- **Composer's Hand:** the *why*.

From 2025-12-06 to 2025-12-09 every Concord episode packet carried an *Episode Jazz* block, with one line per axis.

**Only the five names reached this repository,** in `canon/saga_overview.md` and `rules/saga_context_S1.json`. The packet
block was dropped when the B01 EBCI template was built at the 09-27 preflight. **No ruling dropped it.**

None of the following ever reached drafting or revision:
- the author's own rules: *structure not product*, *the internal soundtrack*, *elevator jazz*, and *breadcrumb pace
  driven by the rhythm section*;
- the techniques: syncopation, motif return with variation, call and response, rests and drops.

**The forwarded review's claim is verified:** the layers survived as names only, and the block was lost from the packets.

**Does it help B01's rhythm and pace problem?** The diagnostic read says yes, modestly.
- About two-thirds of what it finds was already known.
- **What it adds is mostly decision rules:**
  - which watcher hints to keep;
  - which rests to shorten;
  - which refrains to cut;
  - where the ending run sits.

**The most useful of these rules is the author's own sentence, on breadcrumb pace.**

## 2. What to restore

### 2.1 Recommended: two things, both small

**(a) A short writer-profile section,** *§3A Structure under the page*, written from the author's own rules. Proposed
text, not applied:

> - **The book is built like jazz, and the jazz stays under the page.** The protagonists carry the melody; the world
>   carries the harmony; the rhythm section (side characters, humour, sensuality, candour, surprise) carries the accents,
>   the hooks and the payoffs. This is structure, not product: no musical figure for the story's own shape, and the
>   strange is calibrated to light, sound, vibration and weight, not to music. Music the characters make or hear
>   belongs to their world.
> - **Accent.** Let an expected beat arrive late, shifted or turned, and let an unexpected one come forward. A run of
>   beats that each land exactly when foreshadowed goes flat.
> - **A return carries a change:** in the thing, in who meets it, or in what it costs. Re-describing or re-reciting is
>   not a return.
> - **The rhythm section carries the hooks.** A breadcrumb lands best in a side character's mouth, a joke or a candid
>   line, not as the protagonist's private restatement. Vary the instruments.
> - **A rest changes something,** a relationship or a choice. A rest that only restores breath is short.
> - **A conversation of solos.** A character's move is answered by another's, from a different floor. A solo is for
>   revelation, not for spotlight.
> - *Optional, the author's own rule:* **one-word emotions are words of power,** used sparingly.

**Three guards:**
- The profile's opening line governs: *tendencies, not required moves.*
- §3 *Rhythm* is unchanged. It already carries the sentence-level rules (the drop, white space, tempo).
- The section names no axes and no music terms on the page side.

**(b) A revision reading test,** used at checkpoints and in revision passes. It is never given to the drafter as a
checklist. It follows the read's §8:
1. **Returns:** varied or repeated. A re-description or re-recital counts as a repeat.
2. **Rests:** does it change something or only recover? Shorten the ones that only recover; do not move them by
   default.
3. **Rhythm-section carriage:** who carries each hook and payoff, and how many kinds of accent there are.
4. **Cadence by position:** runs of five or more chapter endings of one type, in mid-act.
5. **Call and response:** who answers whom, from what floor, and which solos go unanswered for longer than a chapter.
6. **In the line pass:** no interpretation inside a peak.

**Not used:** the 2-and-4 pulse, and the five-axis checklist.

### 2.2 Not recommended

**(c) Restoring the five-field *Episode Jazz* block to the EBCI packets.**
- The author's *"structure not product"* argues against it.
- The read found that the axes do not discriminate at chapter level.
- Prose packets derive from the Narrative Brief alone, so a control-layer block would never reach the page anyway.

**(c′) A narrower option, for later: one rhythm-section line in the EBCI control layer,** naming who carries the
episode's hook or payoff. It could be tried when B02's packets are derived (Q-JZ5).

## 5. Not proposed

- Music on the page as the narrator's figure.
- Any axis field in the prose packets.
- New scenes or new plot.
- Naming the factions, explaining the phenomenon, or using *Resonance*.
- A score.
- Restoring the era's superseded material:
  - the 2-and-4 placements;
  - the trilogy time signatures and key changes;
  - the Melodic Continuity Map;
  - the Gladiatrix fork.
