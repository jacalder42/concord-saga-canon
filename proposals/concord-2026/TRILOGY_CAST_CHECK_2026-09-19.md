# Concord 2026 — Trilogy Cast Check — Veil / Neon / Loom

Status: EDITORIAL RECONCILIATION PROPOSAL — NONCANONICAL UNTIL AUTHOR PROMOTION
Date: 2026-09-19

## PURPOSE

Cross-check the recovered B1–B9 character lists against the 2026 secondary/tertiary editorial resolutions.

This is not another source-canon rewrite. It identifies:
- obsolete identities embedded in historical book lists;
- characters entering too early or carrying too long;
- overloaded clusters;
- missing recurrence/payoff;
- opportunities to turn isolated utility beats into relationships with retained recurring characters.

Authority order remains:
A current author rulings > B authoritative historical backup > C approved development > D summaries/Notion > E recollection.

## I. GLOBAL FINDING

The historical trilogy-level lists are structurally healthier than the later per-book unified lists.

The trilogy lists generally ask for:
- a small primary ensemble;
- a few recurring community/institutional faces;
- representative anonymous civic populations.

The per-book lists then over-expanded those functions into dozens of named tertiary characters.

Therefore migration should generally preserve the **trilogy-level population shape** while replacing generic clusters with the best recovered recurring identities from the 2026 editorial passes.

Key principle:
**The reconciliation is not shrinking the world. It is increasing recognition inside a large world.**

## II. VEIL — B1–B3

### Desired cast feeling
Veil should remain intimate:
**neighborhood → city → widening institutional pressure.**

Readers should repeatedly encounter the same handful of people before the world becomes enormous.

### A. Tahl chronology — CRITICAL MIGRATION

Historical unified lists incorrectly name:
- B1 “Tahl Morgan — compliance clerk beginning MT”
- B2 “Tahl Morgan — Missing Thread founder”
- B3 “Tahl Morgan — MT gaining traction”

CURRENT LOCK:
- B1–B3: MissingThread / anonymous Threadnaut persona.
- B3 epilogue: reveal = Tahl Morgan.
- B4 onward: named/public Tahl.

Migration rule:
**No public/supporting character in Veil may interact with “Tahl Morgan” as the known MT identity before the B3 epilogue reveal.**

All old Naomi, Zane, Gianna, Filament, institutional and commenter beats must be audited against this.

### B. Filament load — TOO MANY EARLY NAMES

Historical B1 already carries Mira, Sparrow, Arianna, Malik, Rosette plus generic elders/youth.

2026 resolution should reduce reader-facing early Filament recognition to:
- **Mara / M** — recurring NOLA community gravity; can absorb unnamed E08 Filament local.
- **Arianna Duplantier** — ethical/grounding lineage.
- **Jae “Sparrow” Nguyen** — moving information.
- **Mme Rosette** — neighborhood intuition, explicitly not Filament leadership.
- **Rootkeeper** — local-only texture if beat retained.

Introduce **Samir “Pivot” Choksi** in B3 as recovered.
Introduce **Liyun Park** in B3 if her pattern-watcher beat remains useful.

Do NOT introduce the entire eventual Filament ensemble in B1 simply to seed them.

### C. Retired Veil Filament identities
- Mira Tremeaux → retired/split into Mara/Arianna/Ren functions.
- Malik Price → absorbed into Ren Bellande's radicalization ancestry; do not maintain separate saga arc.
- Auntie Mae Broussard → Mme Rosette/Arianna.
- early generic Filament elder proliferation → Mara/Arianna/local unnamed members.

### D. Civic recurrence — STRONGER AFTER CONSOLIDATION

Veil recurring civic faces should preferentially be:
- **Leila Broussard** — ER/medical system.
- **Ramon Espina** — transit/infrastructure; replaces Jeremiah Knox and later duplicate transit engineers.
- **Anaïs Baptiste** — formal civic system.
- **Daniel Rieux** — ordinary public servant.
- **Kesi Albright** — cultural visual witness.
- **Shirin Petrov** — civilian empirical mapping.
- **Trip / Velvet Vein** — cultural/social information.
- **Mme Rosette** — neighborhood weather-vane.

This is enough to make NOLA feel inhabited without naming every witness.

### E. Institutional Veil pressure — REDUCE FRONT-LINE VILLAINS

Historical B1–B3 over-seed named Dominion/Technarch operators.

Dominion Veil spine:
- Marcellus Virelli = distant/approaching institutional authority.
- Marius Holt = archive conscience.
- Helena Kael = family/inheritance.
- Caldas Ren = field consequence, used sparingly.

Retire Severin Virelli, Arel Tovin, Nadia Verenz, Eryk Sorensen as recurring identities.

Technarch Veil spine:
- Han Wei = distant system authority.
- Rex = inside-system viewpoint.
- Shun Watanabe = mentor/methodological conscience.
- Gianna Locke = protocol/security pressure.
- Kasumi Arendt can enter B3 when manufactured-meta evidence becomes relevant.

Retire/demote Yara Kint, Pierre Morozov, Tamsin Kho unless a specific scene needs an unnamed technician.

**Harlow should not need a prominent B3 entrance merely because the old list seeds Rook & Harlow.**
If covert retrieval is needed, a brief Harlow/unnamed-team appearance is sufficient.

### F. PureTone/Choirless timing

Historical B3 “White Echo” cameo risks over-signaling a later antagonist architecture.

Current resolution:
- Choirless overt violence begins B6.
- PureTone is genealogy/campaign/technology vocabulary, not separate major faction.

Therefore:
**Remove White Echo as a required named B3 antagonist.**
If anti-resonance rhetoric/tech appears, keep it ambient and deniable.

### G. Media Veil load

Keep:
- Naomi Clairborne
- Zane Rowley
- core MT handles: GlassHarbor, Ribbon_9, NthDaySurvivor, tinfoilmage

This creates useful continuity.

Avoid a “Chronicle reporter duo” if Naomi can carry the human Chronicle function.

### H. Elisabet entrance

Historical material conflicts between:
- B1 distant/light reference;
- B2 late arrival;
- B3 small cameo/remote support;
- other character summaries giving stronger Veil involvement.

Do NOT resolve exact scene count in this cast pass.

Editorial requirement:
**Elisabet must feel like a real person with an existing Reykjavík life before she becomes the ensemble's stabilizer.**
Use the Reykjavík resolution to give her pre-ensemble relational texture. Avoid treating her as merely “the stabilizer arriving from Iceland.”

### I. Baz

Baz's B3 death remains locked and should receive enough B1–B2 relational investment to make the death belong to more than Lucien.

During beat migration, look specifically for meaningful Baz intersections with:
- Caro;
- Seraphine;
- Rex;
- local civic/support cast.

Do not solve this by adding scenes indiscriminately; ensure the ensemble has memories of him.

## III. NEON — B4–B6

### Desired cast feeling
Neon is where the world becomes crowded.

The solution is not to make the cast tiny. It is to let existing faces acquire new jobs under pressure.

### A. Filament ensemble expansion is earned here

By B4, introduce/activate the fuller recurring Filament spine:
- Mara / M
- Arianna
- Sparrow
- Pivot
- Nara
- Stitch
- Liyun
- Corrine Dalcourt
- Ren Bellande

Narrow/late:
- Ishaan Virk
- Blue String Kids

Important sequencing:
- Ren must first be useful/earnest before coercive.
- Nara's signal language should grow from practical need.
- Corrine should exist as material-care infrastructure before her refusal of weaponization matters.
- Ishaan should enter through a concrete rescue/relationship, not “future Kade follower” setup.

### B. Neon civic cast — REUSE BEFORE ADDING

Historical B4–B6 introduce many one-book professionals:
psychologists, mechanics, shelter operators, triage chiefs, viral mothers, tourists, dockworkers, clergy, nurses, programmers, etc.

Before retaining any, test whether the beat can belong to:
- Leila — medical;
- Ramon — infrastructure;
- Shirin — data;
- Anaïs — governance;
- Roland — informal civic trust;
- Ms. Leontine — refuge/hospitality;
- Corrine — supplies/food;
- Trip — social information/culture;
- Kesi — cultural interpretation;
- Sparrow — street documentation.

One-scene named civilians remain welcome when individuality matters. They should not automatically become recurring cast.

### C. Daniel Rieux exit

Daniel's usefulness peaks as ordinary policing becomes impossible.

Recommended window:
**B4 or B5 = final substantial Daniel material.**
By B6, his absence/exit should be legible.

Exact exit remains beat-level.

This prevents him from becoming another combat/support operator in a story already full of them.

### D. Mme Rosette

Rosette should peak in Veil and early Neon.

Use her later only when returning to her neighborhood/community has emotional value. She should not become a resonance forecaster who follows the plot globally.

### E. Mara Niht

If Mara Niht enters the saga, Neon is the most natural window for a NOLA/Velvet Vein crossing after Reykjavík groundwork.

However:
**No exact biography/style/instrument/nationality is canonically recovered yet.**

Do not force her into B4 merely to seed her.
Her appearance should occur only after her historical “Develop Singer Style” material is recovered or the author explicitly approves a fresh editorial build.

### F. Institutional antagonists — DIFFERENTIATE PRESSURES

Neon should clearly teach the reader the distinction:

Dominion:
- Marcellus = legitimacy/purity/inherited authority.
- Caldas = field consequence.
- Marius = conscience/records.
- Helena = Lucien's inherited family pressure.

Technarch:
- Han = prediction/control.
- Rex = countertrajectory.
- Shun Watanabe = methodological conscience.
- Kasumi = evidence of human cost.
- Gianna = protocol/enforcement conscience.
- Harlow = human consequence of engineered control.

Do not replace retired supporting names with new equivalents.

### G. Manufactured metas — DO NOT CREATE A SUPER-VILLAIN ROSTER

Historical lists escalate Silver Pattern Man, Blinking Girl, Null and unnamed prototypes.

Current editorial rule:
manufactured metas are primarily **people/consequences/evidence**, not a collectible enemy ensemble.

For B4–B6:
- one or two recognizable survivors/prototypes may recur if relationships justify it;
- others can remain de-identified cases, victims, reports or incidents.

“Null” especially should not become a cosmological rival by inertia from old late-Loom lists without current Mechanica review.

### H. Anti-resonance escalation

Use the rebuilt ideological ladder:
- Saeko Morita = public safety legitimacy.
- Tessa Vane = neighbor-level exclusion/enforcement.
- Koro Ito = Erasure doctrine / Choirless.
- Silas Moreau = ideological media normalization.

Do not restore Marcus Kell, Mara Kell, Priya Sen, Jonah Grieves, Yusef Harrow as a parallel named villain ensemble merely because old Neon lists contain them.

B6 remains the qualitative threshold for first Choirless violence.

### I. Tahl death and supporting cast

B6 Tahl death should affect more than the primary ensemble.

During beat migration ensure aftermath registers through different lenses:
- MT chorus loses the creator/voice they knew.
- Naomi confronts public record vs person.
- Zane attempts to monetize catastrophe.
- Gianna sees the consequence of protocol.
- Sparrow/Filaments experience network/community loss.
- Kade inherits a community that does not automatically trust him.

This makes the death a world event without requiring dozens of reaction cameos.

## IV. LOOM — B7–B9

### Desired cast feeling
Loom should feel enormous geographically but **recognizable emotionally**.

The old lists do the opposite: they repeatedly add new shelter leaders, reporters, couriers, pastors, doctors and civilian heroes just as the saga needs accumulated recognition.

### A. Stop late-stage cast inflation

Historical B7–B9 introduce:
Maya Duplessis, Jonah Halley, Pastor Elise Moreno, Jan Foix, Clio Harmon, Sara Nguyen, Darnell Ross, Carmine Goodwin, Lina Harrow, Emmanuel Drake, Francis Arnaud and others.

Not all need deletion, but none should be presumed recurring merely because old lists named them.

Migration rule:
**At Loom entry, new named characters require a relationship or function existing cast cannot provide.**

### B. Filament diaspora payoff

Loom is where the retained Filament cast earns its existence:
- Pivot = routes/mediation.
- Nara = universal civic glyphs.
- Stitch = tactile triage infrastructure.
- Liyun = pattern perception and its cost.
- Corrine = material survival and anti-weaponization.
- Arianna = ethical continuity.
- Mara = community memory.
- Sparrow = information under collapse.
- Ren = consequence of militant fracture.
- Blue String Kids = recognizable youth/community symbol.

This is preferable to generic “three Filament diaspora leaders” from the old trilogy list.

### C. Arianna must not become generic “leader of survivors”

Her B8/B9 old descriptions drift toward formal leadership.

Preserve distinction:
- Pivot coordinates.
- Arianna teaches/holds ethical continuity.
- Mara holds human/community memory.
- Corrine makes survival materially possible.

No single “Filament leader.”

### D. Ren Bellande needs a Loom decision/payoff

Historical militant Mira/Dome/Malik trajectories imply the radical Filament fracture persists.

Because those identities were consolidated into Ren, Loom must not simply forget him.

Before final beat migration choose a real outcome:
- recognition and costly de-escalation;
- tragic commitment to coercion;
- protection act that contradicts his rhetoric;
- survival into post-Mending accountability.

Do not leave him as “militant splinter exists” abstraction.

Exact outcome remains for beat/manifest approval, but **payoff is required**.

### E. Ishaan / Kade

Ishaan's role should narrow as Kade's symbolic status becomes dangerous.

He is useful only if he forces Kade to confront what followers are making of him.

Do not make Ishaan Kade's uncomplicated loyal sidekick.

### F. Chronicle collapse

Historical supplement architecture says:
**Loom = Chronicle dead.**

Therefore Naomi may survive, but the Chronicle should not continue operating as a normal institution through B7–B9.

Zane's platform power should likewise collapse with infrastructure.

Signalman East and MT fragments become more valuable precisely because polished media disappears.

### G. Choirless / Brightbreak endgame

Historical B7–B9 lists overemphasize Dominion/Technarch/Quiet Doctrine as equal final villains.

Current architecture:
- Dominion = containment.
- Technarch = prediction.
- Choirless/Ito = erasure.
- Brightbreak/Elias = certainty/influence.

By B8–B9, earlier institutions should be **collapsing consequences**, not competing for equal climax bandwidth.

Honey Island conflict should preserve the clear opposition:
- Choirless wants resonance/emotional amplitude removed.
- Elias/Brightbreak wants a post-crisis order in which his leverage/influence remains meaningful.
- Mending represents coexistence/transformation rather than either solution.

### H. Dominion/Technarch endings need controlled migration

Old B9 list contains several disposable off-page/death/disappearance endings:
Virelli dies off-page; Caldas killed in storm; Harlow vanishes; etc.

Do not canonize these merely to clear the board.

The Character Reconciliation Manifest should mark each major recurring institutional character:
**ENDING LOCKED / ENDING OPEN / OLD ENDING RETIRED.**

A character who has occupied multiple books deserves either a narratively meaningful end or a deliberate unresolved survival—not administrative cleanup.

### I. Global/place anchors in Loom

The global-place pass established that world scale should be carried by recurring place relationships, not only crisis reports.

During B7–B9 migration, intentionally revisit surviving anchors from:
- Reykjavík / Elisabet ecosystem;
- Vienna / Lucien-Dominion civic texture;
- Singapore / Rex-Technarch civilian texture;
- Marrakesh;
- Tokyo;
- Santa Fe;
- NOLA/Honey Island.

A one-paragraph return to a known face can make collapse feel more global than five new named refugees.

### J. Post-Mending recurrence

Do not give every retained supporting character an epilogue card.

Post-Mending should selectively show:
- a few lives continuing;
- cultural memory;
- institutions being rebuilt differently;
- unresolved grief;
- changed ordinary practices.

The absence of an epilogue mention does not equal death.

## V. CAST-LOAD FLAGS BY BOOK

### B1 — RED FLAG: too many named seeds
Old list tries to establish primary cast + Filaments + civic workers + Dominion + Technarch + witnesses + media + antagonist shadows.

Resolution:
foreground neighborhood/civic recurrence; keep institutional figures mostly peripheral.

### B2 — YELLOW FLAG: repetition without relationship deepening
Many B1 names simply repeat jobs.

Resolution:
each returning secondary should gain a relationship, choice or changed condition—not merely perform the same function again.

### B3 — RED FLAG: foreshadow pile-up
Old list adds Pivot, Liyun, multiple Dominion observers, Kasumi, Rook/Harlow, White Echo, more civilians, more cultural figures while also carrying Baz death and Tahl reveal.

Resolution:
B3 must prioritize **payoff and fracture**, not introduce the entire future saga.

### B4 — YELLOW/RED: intentional expansion
Neon may widen sharply, but introductions should be clustered through already-known networks.

### B5 — RED FLAG: historical tertiary explosion
This is the worst tendency: every social consequence receives a new name.

Resolution:
reuse the consolidated civic/Filament/institutional ensemble.

### B6 — RED FLAG: climax overload
Tahl death + crackdowns + Filament fracture + anti-resonance violence + Technarch/Dominion escalation.

Resolution:
reduce new introductions close to zero. B6 should cash established faces.

### B7 — RED FLAG: old list resets the supporting cast
Collapse generates a new roster.

Resolution:
Loom must instead reveal what happened to people readers already know.

### B8 — YELLOW/RED: endgame crowding
Retained supporting arcs need decisive movement; do not add replacement helpers.

### B9 — RED FLAG: checklist endings
Old list attempts to close almost every named person's fate.

Resolution:
only narratively relevant endings appear on page. Others remain alive/dead/unknown according to actual story evidence, not bookkeeping pressure.

## IX. CONCLUSION

The saga does not need a smaller-feeling world.

It needs:
- fewer duplicate names performing identical jobs;
- more accumulated recognition;
- more intersections among retained secondary characters;
- stronger consequences when a familiar face changes, disappears or returns;
- fewer late-stage introductions;
- less pressure to close every character administratively.

With this trilogy check complete, the next artifact should be the **Character Reconciliation Manifest**, consolidating all 2026 character resolutions into one author-reviewable migration document.
