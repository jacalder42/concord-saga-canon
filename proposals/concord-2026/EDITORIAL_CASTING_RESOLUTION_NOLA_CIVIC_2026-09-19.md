# Concord 2026 — Editorial Casting Resolution — New Orleans Civic & Cultural Cast

Status: EDITORIAL RECOMMENDATION / PROPOSAL — NONCANONICAL UNTIL AUTHOR PROMOTION
Date: 2026-09-19
Builds on: Secondary/Tertiary Audit Passes 1–4 and Filament Editorial Casting Resolution
Scope: New Orleans civic, cultural, medical, infrastructure, neighborhood, and witness recurrence.

## Editorial objective

New Orleans should feel inhabited by people whose lives continue when the protagonists leave the scene.

The solution is not the smallest possible cast. It is a legible civic ensemble with recurring place anchors and professions, while eliminating duplicate nurses, elders, technicians, viral witnesses, and “person who notices anomaly” slots.

# 1. Trip — KEEP / PROTECT

Trip remains the strongest non-core New Orleans social anchor.

Function:
- Velvet Vein bartender and cultural host;
- gossip archivist / informal information switchboard;
- humor and social-pressure valve;
- Filament-adjacent without becoming Filament leadership;
- continuity from nightlife and rumor → sanctuary/informal intelligence → diaspora morale → post-Mending cultural rebuilding.

Trip should know many of the recurring NOLA cast personally. This turns Velvet Vein into intersection infrastructure rather than a protagonist-only haunt.

# 2. Mme Rosette — KEEP

Historical recurrence is unusually clean:
- B1 senses resonance “pressure drops”;
- B2 recognizes resonance “weather”;
- B3 warns neighborhood children about “thin air”;
- B4 warns of “thin air days.”

Editorial assignment:
**neighborhood weather-vane.**

She should not be made a Filament elder or secret expert. Her value is that ordinary local knowledge notices the impossible before institutions can name it.

Keep the name **Mme Rosette**.

Use sparingly after early Neon; later appearances gain power if the reader remembers her from quiet Veil scenes.

# 3. Auntie Mae Broussard — MERGE INTO MME ROSETTE / RETIRE IDENTITY

Historical function:
- local elder;
- superstition-coded warnings;
- grounding rituals;
- cultural warning language.

This overlaps Mme Rosette, Arianna, Mara/M, and other elder-grounder concepts.

Transfer to Mme Rosette:
- culturally encoded warnings;
- a few memorable folk/superstition formulations.

Transfer formal grounding/ritual work to Arianna/Mara as appropriate.

Retire Auntie Mae as a recurring identity.

This also reduces Broussard surname density.

# 4. Ms. Leontine — KEEP / PROMOTE PLACE ANCHOR

Historical trajectory:
- runs Creole tea shop offering grounding rituals;
- develops it into an herbal refuge;
- refuge is overrun/destroyed;
- later opens her private home to refugees;
- ultimately walks children to safety.

This is a complete civic arc.

Editorial assignment:
**place → loss → hospitality without place.**

Her significance is not mystical herbalism. It is that she keeps offering shelter after the physical shelter is gone.

Keep **Ms. Leontine**. Do not require a surname unless later needed.

Connect her socially to Trip, Mara/M, Corrine Dalcourt, and the Blue String Kids so her refuge participates in the recurring community network.

# 5. Roland Baptiste — KEEP / DEFINE AS NON-FILAMENT CIVIC COUNTERPART

Historical progression:
- district elder organizing calm circles;
- non-Filament community grounding circles;
- nightly grief circles;
- last-standing grief-circle leader;
- final community grounding circle.

This is valuable specifically because he is **not Filament**.

Editorial assignment:
ordinary civic practice independently converges on some of the same human needs as Filament emotional literacy.

Keep **Roland Baptiste**.

This also gives the Baptiste surname one strong community identity rather than scattering it across unrelated characters.

# 6. Councilwoman Anaïs Baptiste — KEEP, MAKE ROLAND'S FAMILY

Historical progression:
- pushes against secrecy;
- resists Technarch overreach;
- raises emergency transparency measures;
- attempts emergency governance;
- tries to restore civic trust;
- mediates between factions.

Editorial resolution:
**make Anaïs and Roland Baptiste siblings.**

Reason:
Two recurring Baptistes with complementary civic roles become stronger as an intentional family than as surname coincidence.

- Anaïs = formal civic systems / government.
- Roland = informal civic trust / neighborhood circles.

Their disagreement should be affectionate but real: Anaïs believes institutions can still be made accountable; Roland trusts people before systems.

This creates a family-level lens on civic collapse without adding exposition characters.

# 7. Leila Broussard + Nurse Camille Dupré — MERGE

Historical Leila:
- B1 ER nurse handling odd cases;
- B2 triage during shard flickers;
- B3 overwhelmed by shard-induced panic cases;
- B4 triage collapses during prismatic surge.

Historical Camille:
- Core Visual Canon ER nurse during Neon blooms;
- “exhausted resilience; civic heart; quiet heroism”;
- visually and functionally overlaps the same recurring-nurse slot.

Editorial resolution:
**one recurring ER nurse.**

Keep the stronger continuity name:
## **Leila Broussard**

Transfer Camille's useful visual/emotional material:
- mid-30s;
- exhausted resilience;
- civic heart;
- hands shake after danger passes rather than during it;
- patched field jacket / visibly accumulated wear.

Retire **Camille Dupré** as a separate identity.

Leila becomes the saga's recurring medical-system face. Later anonymous doctors/nurses should route through her when plausible.

Potential family link to retired Auntie Mae is **not** required. Shared historical surname should not force a family relation.

# 8. Shirin Petrov — KEEP / PROMOTE CIVILIAN DATA ARC

Historical trajectory is exceptionally coherent:
- B1 student building anomaly spreadsheet;
- B2 maps Ghostwaves across districts;
- B3 spreadsheet gains Filament attention;
- B4 public shard spreadsheets;
- B5 public shard/bloom database;
- B6 global shard → bloom → colorstorm map;
- B7 catastrophic bloom-mapping expert;
- B8 final stable bloom map;
- B9 last accurate map of resonance fractures.

Editorial assignment:
**amateur truth becomes indispensable public infrastructure.**

Keep **Shirin Petrov**.

Important distinction from Liyun:
- Shirin = empirical aggregation, public data, maps built from reports.
- Liyun = perceptual/pattern sensitivity with personal neurological cost.

Shirin should occasionally be wrong or data-starved. Her power is method and persistence, not supernatural accuracy.

# 9. Kesi Albright — KEEP / PROMOTE CULTURAL LONG ARC

Historical trajectory spans all nine books:
- B1 color-tremor paintings;
- B2 first viral shard murals;
- B3 fractured-sky mural;
- B4 Red Nights fear series;
- B5 fear → hope transition;
- B6 grief murals;
- B7 murals become global hope symbols;
- B8 first Hope Walls;
- B9 Mosaic of Hope on riverfront.

Editorial assignment:
**the city paints back.**

Keep **Kesi Albright**.

Kesi should not become a magical artist whose work literally stabilizes resonance. The art records, interprets, and changes people.

Tie Kesi naturally to Velvet Vein/community spaces; murals can become visual continuity markers as NOLA changes.

# 10. Zoe Carignan — MERGE INTO KESI / RETIRE AS FULL RECURRING CHARACTER

Historical Zoe:
- AR poet documenting distortion;
- emotional collapse / despair / heartbreak;
- later poems become hope focal points;
- B9 “Breath After Light.”

This is attractive texture but structurally duplicates Kesi's art-as-city-memory trajectory.

Editorial resolution:
**do not maintain two parallel nine-book civic artists.**

Transfer selected functions to Kesi's cultural orbit rather than making Kesi both painter and poet:
- Zoe may survive as a named collaborator/cameo poet in Neon/Loom;
- “Breath After Light” can be a poem/installational text paired with Kesi's final mural;
- she does not require an independent recurring arc.

Classification:
**DEMOTE to cultural collaborator/cameo.**

# 11. Officer Daniel Rieux — KEEP THROUGH NEON, THEN ALLOW EXIT

Historical progression:
- B1 sympathetic cop confused by shard incidents;
- B2 quietly assists Filament rescues;
- B3 first on scene during warehouse escalation;
- B4 overwhelmed by riot cascade.

This is enough for a meaningful limited arc.

Editorial assignment:
**ordinary public servant watching his job become impossible.**

Keep Daniel through Veil and early Neon.

Do not force him through B9 merely because recurrence is desirable. A believable exit — resignation, reassignment, injury, departure, or simply choosing family — can demonstrate institutional attrition.

Exact exit remains a beat-level decision.

# 12. Transit-tech collision — CONSOLIDATE TO ONE PERSON

Historical identities collide:
- Jeremiah Knox = B1–B3 city transit technician seeing AR/signal anomalies and trying to stabilize the grid;
- Ramon Espina = Core Visual Canon blue-collar transit worker / early anomaly witness;
- Cole Maddox = B4 transit engineer documenting resonance interference;
- generic “transit tech” appears in compressed character plans.

Editorial resolution:
**one recurring infrastructure face.**

Keep:
## **Ramon Espina**

Reason:
The visual canon gives Ramon stronger immediate human texture — blue-collar pragmatism, calloused hands, reflective/transit workwear, early anomaly witness — while Jeremiah/Cole are primarily functional labels.

Transfer:
- Jeremiah's B1–B3 anomaly reports and grid-stabilization attempts;
- Cole's B4 interference documentation;
- generic transit-tech beats where geographically plausible.

Retire:
- Jeremiah Knox
- Cole Maddox
as recurring identities.

Ramon's arc should evolve from “the system is glitching” to “the system cannot be trusted” to improvised physical infrastructure during collapse.

# 16. Intersection plan

Recurring characters should meet each other outside protagonist scenes.

Examples of useful intersection architecture:
- Trip hears from Ramon that transit glitches are not normal before official reports acknowledge them.
- Leila recognizes Shirin's public map because ER admissions match its hot zones.
- Anaïs privately uses Shirin's data after official channels fail.
- Roland sends people from grief circles to Leontine for food/rest, not “treatment.”
- Kesi paints a wall near a route Nara has marked safe.
- Sparrow physically carries information when networks fail.
- Corrine's pantry supplies Leontine's refuge.
- Daniel knows Ramon from ordinary city incidents before either understands resonance.
- Mme Rosette knows everyone by family/history and refuses their jargon.

These intersections should be distributed, not all forced into one ensemble scene.

# 17. Editorial effect

The civic cast now provides recurring human measurements of the saga:
- What does the hospital see? → Leila
- What does infrastructure see? → Ramon
- What does government see? → Anaïs
- What does the neighborhood believe? → Rosette / Roland
- Where do people physically shelter? → Leontine / Corrine
- What does the public data show? → Shirin
- What does the city remember visually? → Kesi
- What does the city say over a drink? → Trip
- What happens when systems fail at street level? → Daniel / Sparrow / Pivot

This is preferable to creating a new expert or witness for each crisis.
