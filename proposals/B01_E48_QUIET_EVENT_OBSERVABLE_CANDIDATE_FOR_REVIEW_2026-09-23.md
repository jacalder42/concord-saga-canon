## Leading candidate: bounded light fluctuation

During a previously declared *local* observation window, a **small bounded group of ordinary building lights visibly varies in brightness or briefly flickers and returns to service**. This revisits the recovered old E41 image without converting it into an orderly citywide cascade. A person at a different vantage, or an ordinary independently kept recording or electrical observation, can corroborate **that the light changed**, with uncertainty about the source and precise timing. A shared circuit, building control, voltage dip, visual occlusion, camera exposure behavior or unrelated fixture problem could account for it. A film alone may misrepresent flicker; direct witness and device/fixture comparison matter.

The observation is **positive**: something visible changes during a predeclared approximate window. It is **quiet**: no outage, no universal synchronization, no mass panic, no automatic Resonance declaration. It need not resemble E15's amp cutout or E33's pane motion. E33's required technology degradation can separately be a scaled effect short of failure; E48's visible brightness variation likewise need not damage anything or prove the same cause. Avoid making every event a compulsory tech symptom: E15 human/public shock, E33 partially predicted material motion with a smaller device-quality effect if specified, E48 restrained chosen observation are different *dramatic functions*. If a third electrical symptom makes the trilogy of beats feel repetitive in the actual scene, reject this candidate and develop a different bounded positive material observation under a new explicit proposal.

### What would make this a weak clue

- Ordinary circuit conditions fully explain the brightness change and the timing coincidence; it remains a valid event of observation but **not** material evidence for Resonance.
- The video alone flickers because of frame timing or auto-exposure while direct witnesses see no change; downgrade or reject the physical claim.
- The team chose this specific bank of lights only after seeing it change; the “prediction” is retrospective and fails the E48 payoff.
- The allowed window/site is so broad that common light variation would be a frequent hit. Use a meaningful local comparison, including intervals when no predicted event was logged.
- Every E15/E33/E48 anomaly now reads as an electrical glitch; retain the E48 observation role but choose a different physical candidate after review.
