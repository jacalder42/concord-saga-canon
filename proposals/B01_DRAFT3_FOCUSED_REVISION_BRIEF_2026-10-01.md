## 1. The reckoning (Q-D3-1)

**Its job is accountability.**
- Seraphine seeks the answer to why he let her account stand alone.
- The answer changes their working terms.
- Attraction may complicate the scene, **but his explanation is not rewarded with closeness.** Attraction surviving anger
  is the point; anger dissolving because he is vulnerable is not.

**The changed term (revised in version 2).** It is **her control over the use of her testimony: he may not submit,
paraphrase or speak for her account without her agreement.** Version 1's *"nothing of hers in anything he writes"* would
have blocked the shared work, including records she wants circulated. **At E48 her part of the honest account is in her
own hand**, which shows the term on the page.

**What he admits, withholds, and what it costs (revised in version 2).** His cover limits what he can disclose. **It
does not excuse evading why he signed.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **He admits** | He went down in that room. He wrote *None* because a second account of something he could not explain would keep the file open, travel wherever hers went, and put his name to what had happened to him. He was afraid of what it meant |
| **He withholds** | Who reads what he writes. **In her point of view** she hears where the answer stops, and says so; he does not deny it. **In his point of view at E48** the reader learns what the stop was. Romance candidate 8 (§7) carries this |
| **The cost** | She leaves dissatisfied. She sets the term. There is no touch, and the hand at E46 is not repeated that night |

**Where:** the evening of E46, after the lot, alone, in her point of view **[execution]**.

**E48 then:**
- he reports the file's closing and his letter;
- her nod is informed and no longer a discovery for him;
- what passes between them is cool and specific (F6).

## 2. The plant (Q-D3-2)

**Version 1 overstated what the head count gives.** Three visitors going up and four people at the table establishes an
extra person. It does not establish that the woman at the table is that extra person: she could be the woman Baz saw.

information about her institutional access, even with no faction named. **And recognition alone does not give the

## 7. The romance through-line (new in version 2, separately justified)

**What the trace found** (a dedicated romance read of all fifty pieces):
- **The romance is lopsided:** the reader gets Lucien's inner life and Seraphine's actions.
- **Seraphine** seeks him for his own sake in five clear places. She never seeks his touch. Her point of view registers
  wanting him twice, and the next line caps it both times.
- **Lucien** seeks her ten times. His point of view registers wanting her in six chapters.
- **The joins:** the waltz grows out of E27's texts; **E30 into E31 is the best join in the book.**
- **E32–E38 is the longest stretch with no charge.**
- **E48's named feeling half-jumps:** nothing she does in that chapter sets it off, and he registers little of her
  between E39 and E48.
- **After E44, her care survives** (the hand at E46, the gate at E47). **Her attraction barely shows. His restraint costs
  him nothing on the page.**

**The aim, within A5's ceiling.** A clearly reciprocal, unresolved development. By the end a reader can say:
- why these two, beyond proximity;
- what each wants and has not asked for;
- what makes being together harder now;
- what they are anticipating next.

The means are **more felt desire and flirtation, not more explicitness.** No exchange needs to be charged, and the
withheld secret is not the romance's only obstacle.

**Eight in-scene candidates.** Each is one line or one beat, with no new scene, no kiss and nothing declared aloud.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | E19 | S | Her deliberate flirtation | At her car window she calls one more name after him to keep him a moment longer, and knows that is why | Shut Baz out; go past a line |
