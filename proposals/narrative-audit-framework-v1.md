# Concord Saga Narrative Audit Framework v1

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANONICAL DIAGNOSTIC
Purpose: Peer-calibrated structural review before deep prose expansion.
Scope: All nine Concord Saga novels; first application Veil Book 1.

## Core principle
Diagnose the reader experience before rewriting beats. Measure not only plot advancement, but pleasure, relationship movement, wonder, place-memory, anticipation, recovery, callback, and payoff.

## Episode audit fields
Episode ID/title; movement/act; dominant Narrative Mode; primary and secondary Reader Rewards; active emotional-core and ensemble characters; location and location tier; anticipated event; new concept; existing concept deepened; callback/payoff; relationship movement; danger/action; wonder; humor/pleasure; recovery/aftermath; end-state change; flags.

## Narrative Mode codes
LIVE — ordinary existence / social / domestic / cultural life
SEEK — investigation / research / pursuit / discovery
CONNECT — relationship / friendship / intimacy
WONDER — awe / strange supernatural encounter
PRESSURE — threat / tension / deadline / dread
ACTION — sustained physical conflict / chase / escape
RECOVER — aftermath / grief / healing / regrouping
CONVERGE — previously separate threads materially collide

## Reader Reward codes
CHAR — character insight/development
REL — relationship movement
WON — wonder
FUN — humor/pleasure/competence/music/food/social enjoyment
MYS — mystery movement
DISC — discovery/revelation
ACT — action/danger payoff
HEAT — attraction/romance/intimacy
WORLD — meaningful world texture
PLOT — consequential external development
PAY — payoff/callback

Every episode should normally provide at least one meaningful reward. No reward type is privileged.

## Character bandwidth
Tier A Emotional Core: roughly 3-6 simultaneously important characters.
Tier B Active Ensemble: roughly 6-12 recurring characters.
Tier C World Cast: roughly 10-25+ across a book.
Tier D Local Cast: temporary act/book/location characters.

Audit simultaneous cognitive load, not total named cast.

## Location bandwidth
Tier A Home: roughly 1-3 dominant emotional anchors.
Tier B Recurring World: roughly 4-10 significant repeated locations.
Tier C Set Piece: roughly 3-8 event locations.
Tier D Texture: incidental locations.

Primary test: does revisiting a place accumulate history, or merely reuse a backdrop?

## Anticipation track
Record future events readers can look forward to or dread: performances, parties, dinners, dates, expeditions, rituals, meetings, festivals, arrivals, confrontations, funerals, weddings, scheduled anomaly windows, etc. Anticipation is propulsion without mandatory danger.

## New vs Deepen vs Pay
Tag major material as NEW, DEEPEN, or PAY.
Expected saga trend:
- Veil may introduce relatively more.
- Neon increasingly recombines.
- Loom relies heavily on deepening, return, callback, payoff, and convergence.

Flag long NEW-heavy runs.

## Pleasure track
Explicitly test for music, food, beauty, humor, competence, friendship, attraction, intimacy, local culture, playful magic, successful small goals, private jokes, and ordinary routines.

Readers need reasons to enjoy inhabiting the world before later loss can become expensive.

## Set-piece definition
A set piece is a sustained sequence with distinctive circumstance/location, heightened anticipation/stakes/emotion, and persistent consequence. It may be Action, Wonder, Social, Romance/Heat, Mystery/Discovery, Ceremony/Ritual, or Grief/Aftermath.

## Sequence-level flags
Flag:
- 4+ consecutive episodes with same dominant mode unless deliberately justified;
- action/pressure runs without contrast or recovery;
- investigation/exposition runs without interpersonal or experiential reward;
- episodes with no identifiable state change;
- reader-reward droughts;
- overuse of PLOT as primary reward;
- major set pieces without anticipation;
- major consequences without aftermath;
- home locations failing to accumulate history;
- too many new concepts in a short span;
- romance isolated from plot/world;
- wonder appearing only as threat/exposition;
- humor/pleasure disappearing for long stretches without intentional tonal reason.

## Book-level output: Narrative ECG
Produce:
A. episode table;
B. mode sequence;
C. reward distribution;
D. character-bandwidth curve;
E. location-use map;
F. NEW/DEEPEN/PAY curve;
G. anticipated-event timeline;
H. action/danger cadence;
I. wonder cadence;
J. relationship/heat cadence;
K. pleasure/ordinary-life cadence;
L. recovery/aftermath cadence;
M. set-piece map;
N. diagnostic flags;
O. strengths to protect;
P. proposed revisions ranked by structural leverage.

## Veil Book 1 questions
1. Does New Orleans become home rather than scenery?
2. Is there enough ordinary Seraphine/Lucien/Baz/Caro/ensemble life to make disruption costly?
3. Are magical concepts introduced faster than emotionally metabolized?
4. Does each episode provide a reader reward?
5. Are action/pressure balanced by pleasure, wonder, connection, and recovery?
6. Are relationship beats integrated with discovery/consequence?
7. Do supporting characters recur with purpose rather than utility?
8. Are there anticipated events beyond threats?
9. Does the midpoint expand perceived story rather than merely intensify danger?
10. Does the ending include consequence and aftermath?
11. Are breadcrumbs/callbacks spaced to reward attention?
12. Does B1 leave the reader wanting more time with the people, not merely lore answers?

## Governance
Diagnostic only. No finding automatically changes canon or approved beats. Preserve beat IDs and provenance. Proposed changes remain proposals until author review. Peer series are calibration references, not authorities over Concord.
