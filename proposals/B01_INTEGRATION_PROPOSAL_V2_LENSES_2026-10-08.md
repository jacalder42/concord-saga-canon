## 1. The genre frame

B01 is a hybrid: urban fantasy (Book One of a long series), supernatural mystery, slow-burn romantic suspense and
upmarket community fiction. Measured against what each expects of a Book One:

| Track | B01 now | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening** (inciting death, the strange on the page, a testable property by Ch 2) | 0–4% | **On norm. Protect.** |
| **Romance** (meet ~19%, first touch ~22%, charged peak ~57%, cover kiss ~75%, rupture over the secret 85–90%) | every beat in range | **On norm. Protect** (the folds in R2 must carry its beats) |
| **Opposition** (face seen and speaking; first act on the leads; the personal offer) | speaks ~82%; offer ~94% | **35–45 points late** (norm: face speaks by ~50%, offer by the middle) |
| **Central question** said aloud | ~94% | **Very late** (norm 15–25%) |
| **A working rule of the strange, named and used at the climax** | never | **Missing** (the one UF convention B01 misses completely) |
| **Climax** caused by the protagonists | watched | **Missing** |
| **Book-scale question closed, frame left open** | frame open; nothing closes | **Missing** (the *In the Woods* risk) |
| **Rests** placed after peaks | Ch 11, 14, 17, 32, 39–40 | commercial readers stall here; literary readers do not |

**Where the genres pull apart, the recommended convention:** the opposition's **face** by the middle (UF) while its
**identity** stays hidden (series frame); **close the book's own case, leave the frame open**; commercial **placement**
of rests with literary **content**; **one** imperfect witness-built rule used at the climax; the climax order kept
(moral peak at Ch 41–44, plot peak at Ch 46) with the reveal pile-up in Ch 45 emptied; the hook as **a decision, then
the frame word**.

**Length.** Trade norms for this hybrid run about 100–130k; 150–160k suits serial or independent release. The genre
analysis recommends taking the package plus D's Act I trims and letting the length land (about 135–145k) as a result,
not a quota. **The publishing channel decides the target** (Q-V2-2).

## 2. The author's concern: emotional weather and emotional intelligence

A full read scored every chapter 0–3 on both.

- **EQ inflation is the larger problem** (mean 1.4 per chapter against 1.0 for weather). Worst: Ch 3, 14, 19.
- **Both fall by about half once outside pressure arrives** (from Ch 31). Act III is the book's best emotional writing.
  **The inflation lives where nothing external is pushing**, which is why the plot changes are also the first fix.
- **Five habits make most of it:**
  1. minor characters as oracles who read the lead at a glance and are right (~15);
  2. strangers who comment correctly on the romance (~10; the one that works is wrong);
  3. a breach repaired in the same scene (5);
  4. **the contract reflex**: a breach answered by agreeing a rule (Ch 19, 36, 43, 45);
  5. subtext said aloud in finished sentences (~9).
  Seraphine also confesses the folder three times within a month, and every listener answers wisely.
- **Grief and guilt behaviour is mostly excellent** (the folder moved and put back, the form she cannot save, the
  half-full tank, Renée's paperwork grief, Lucien's rationalising shame). **What is too clean is the talk about it.**
  Fear of being watched barely reaches behaviour: nobody changes a route or locks a drawer.
- **The causes are in the drafting system more than the profile.** The profile already says articulacy is uneven and
  repair takes its own time. The packets override it:
  - they name relational end-states as a scene's purpose (*seen clearly by each other*);
  - they schedule relationship "rungs" as story jobs, so breach, confession and repair land in one piece;
  - the README allows a repair "later in the same piece";
  - packet rule U3 makes strangers *the lawful channel for heat*;
  - no card gives anyone a misread of **a person**;
  - *nobody reads minds* is honoured for the strange and broken for the social: every read of a person is confirmed.

**Ten revision rules (for approval, Q-V2-6):**
1. Behaviour over naming in narration: no sentence glosses what a gesture or object meant.
2. Readings of people are fallible: at least once per act a POV reads someone wrongly, acts on it, and pays later.
3. No repair in the scene of the breach; terms are shown by acts, not agreed.
4. Confessions come out badly (partial, defensive, late or forced); the listener answers as themselves.
5. Minor characters have agendas and bad days; a stranger who comments on the leads is wrong, crude or selling something.
6. A no that matters stays a no at least once.
7. Articulacy is spent: under pressure, one articulate sentence at most, more often an accusation than a fair self-account.
8. Feelings ride on records and decisions (the book's best beats already do: a form field, a struck name, one cup).
9. Grief and guilt leak sideways: one unfair, unrepaired act of displaced anger for Seraphine; one more defensive act
   for Lucien before the correction.
10. Catharsis is deferred: no mood lifts on cue.

**Drafting-system fixes** (so the revised packets and B02/B03 do not reproduce it): packets state plot function and
observable exit facts, never relational states; *Reader experience* stays in EBCI and is not carried into prose
packets; "rung" retired as a story job; README Q-IT2a's same-piece repair struck for breaches; U3 amended (strangers
may be wrong); a *Misreads (a person)* line in each pressure stack; one line added beside *nobody reads minds* in
profile §9 (a read of another person can be wrong, and the narration need not correct it).

**Fifteen passages** are named with a one-line behavioural alternative (private lens file, Part 4), led by Ch 19 (fight,
confession and treaty in one hour), Ch 3 (Caro's diagnosis and the dance that lifts the mood), Ch 14, Ch 8, Ch 43, Ch 36
and Ch 45. **B02/B03 briefs at high risk of the same pattern:** B03 E17, B02 E21, B03 E18, B03 E26.

## 3. What each lens adds

### 3.1 Mystery

- **A question ladder, said aloud once per act** (the book now has the answers and not the questions):
  Act I *who is writing this down?* → midpoint *how do they know before we do?* (from a neighbour, off the glass notice
  that really did precede the glass) → *there are two of them* (the sort) → Act III *who reads you?* → the *Veil* as a
  guess.
- **Clue work by trade, done once, each at a cost:** Seraphine catches the sting from the hold list; Lucien plants a
  true private detail in a report and learns who reads it; Seraphine matches *the review*, said at Lorraine's door, to
  the form she read at Renée's; Baz recognises the roll-call card's question as his old office's intake wording.
- **Solved in B01:** the folder; the *None*; the city line feeds the cases; how the log leaked; whom Lucien reports to;
  three wrong theories killed and one positive rule earned (*it keeps to places*). **Half-solved:** the two hands (a face
  each, no name); the precursor; the two rival readings of the WHAT, each half right. **Deferred with a device:** what it
  is; whose cases; how the notice-writer knew; elsewhere-first; what it is all for.
- **The climax as a test plus a catch, not a triumph**; the book ends on a question the reader can state: *are they
  following the ground, or following us?*

### 3.2 Conflict and action

- About 30 of 46 chapters hold a real contest, but only five are with the opposition; in fifteen the opposition is
  weather. **A 41-point action hole:** from the Tremé stop (33%) to the stakeout (74%) the leads act physically under
  pressure twice; Carrollton, the largest event, is a phone call.
- **Governing principle for B01:** *the record is the weapon; the crowd is the danger.* Caldas watches in B01,
  intimidates in B02, pursues lethally in B03, so B01's physical danger comes from the phenomenon and from crowds the
  crew's own words move. Warning makes them visible; that is the price.
- **A twenty-rung escalation ladder** (eleven rungs already exist): from giving nothing, to losing at a door, to
  refusing and winning, to attacking and losing, to discovering, to failing, to causing.
- **Three small grafts close the hole:** Terrence says his hands would not move at the wheel, and Caro logs it (Ch 26);
  a guest goes down inside the repast in Seraphine's view (Ch 27); Carrollton heard live through Caro's open line, one
  afternoon of triage (Ch 34).
- **Costed crew conflicts** (none a failure to talk; all about method and whose safety): the vow fought, not signed
  (Ch 24); **Caro would have taken the oxygen deal** (Ch 34; it plants B02 E31); Inez tells them their notebook put a
  crowd in her lot (Ch 37→38); Seraphine finds Baz's copy of her client list herself (Ch 38/41); Elísabet names
  Lucien's cover as the cause of her revocation (Ch 26); the climax plan contested three ways (Ch 45).

### 3.3 Systems

- **The phenomenon is not vague; it is unwritten.** Seven regularities are already earned on the page; none is ever
  written down. **A rule page on the back of Lucien's board**, five lines with a source and a status: *the eye goes, the
  line holds*; *an edge you can walk, not always*; *it keeps to places*; *one minute, many places*; and, only if A4
  reopens, *rings, then legs, not every time*.
- **Name the wrong rule before it breaks:** Seraphine's *it comes up slow; I'll have it first* (with its twin, *looked
  after is safer*) said aloud before Ursulines, so the reader watches it break there and at the lot.
- **Three continuity slips to fix:** Ch 45 claims every event had an edge (Ch 10 and 44 had none); Ursulines' old
  windows rattle against Ch 33's finding about new glass; Clement's amp dies while Ch 16's radio plays on.
- **Every vague pressure becomes a document**, adding no obstacle: the hold as a numbered vendor bulletin built from 311
  reports (which makes the sting-catch mechanical); the "upstairs" copy request as a dated line in the coroner's release
  log; a records request with a three-day reply that someone owes the crew; Caro's printout carries real legal
  exposure. (Legal points marked *verify* in the lens file.)
- **Escalation is preserved:** B01 owns *what* and *where* (presence, order, side, place); B02 *where again* and *when*;
  B03 *how long*; Neon *the number*.

### 3.4 Romance

- The ladder is complete (26 beats) and on norm. Weaknesses are placement and closure: Seraphine's want reaches the page
  at 34% and her body at 56%; Ch 11, 17 and half of Ch 28 are romance rests with nothing at stake; **the book's last
  exchange between them is his refusal to name his employer.**
- **R4 is the largest romance gain:** Baz's confession after the stakeout kiss puts every Act III tender beat on a
  secret Lucien has chosen to keep. **G13 supplies the missing closing gesture**, placed after the refusal, her yes as
  terms rather than forgiveness.
- Add one early bodily beat of hers; uncertify the Ch 46 face-reading; break the Ch 43 car-roof speech (cracked ribs).
- **B01 must not spend:** a declaration, a second kiss, forgiveness of the *None*, any hint of Rex.

### 3.5 Books Two and Three

- **Plants absent from B01:** Lucien's aunt in Vienna (B02 E08, E23; B03 E14, E16, E44); Baz's mother's chorba and his
  sister's planned visit (B03 E08, E41, E44, E45); Leila as Caro's partner (B02 E20, E30; B03 E22, E39–40); Han Wei's
  idiom in the anonymous records buyer's email (B03 E28, B04); a councilwoman on the news (B02 E27; B03 E09, E11); the bus
  driver named Ramon (B02 E16+). Each is a line or a name.
- **Weak or contradicted:** Baz is never told Rieux's paper goes up (B03 E31); **B02 E13's "never seen one of the
  cases" is already false** (he passes one in Ch 45–46): keep the tag off his sightline in B01, and split B02 E13's
  recognition (Baz the face, Lucien the tag) by a packet edit.
- **Shaping guidance:** end B01 with the Dominion holding *what happened*, not the crew's grounds or names (B02 E23
  asks for those); phrase the central question as *who* is on the record (people), so B02's list reads as the same
  ask; Caldas stays at surveillance, a man near whom things go wrong, never by his hand; Baz's belonging beats survive
  the cuts (B03 spends them); B01 hands B02 a crew that has won one contest with each hand.
- **One caution:** Baz's mother's Ch 32 line about not living on the ground floor is comic and stays; B03 must never
  echo it.

### 3.6 Saga

- **The saga's question:** who holds the world together, and what does holding cost: one soul under a fixed ceiling
  (the hard cap, Mechanica §42A) or many hands that let the pressure pass (the breathing veil).
- **B01's version of it: *keeping it* against *carrying it together*.** Keeping: Lucien's *None*, the private log, the
  vow, the collectors. Carrying together: Renée's addition to the file, Inez's canvass, Mrs. Carmouche's open line, a
  gallery sitting together. Better than *watch vs act* or *records vs people* (the saga needs good records).
- **Most saga plants already exist, buried,** and several sit in the rests R2 folds: the dryer-vent screen line (Ch 11),
  *Lucien stopped counting* (Ch 21), the chair Caro sends him to (Ch 39), the hidden Lacuna cameo at the band night
  (Ch 28, LOCKED), Baz's *I'm staying* (Ch 32), the mistral (Ch 40). **Only two new plants:** Caro turns Seraphine's own
  "carrying more than they can hold" reading back on her, once, unanswered (Ch 39 or 25); Renita's screen door seen
  finished, one clause (Ch 24).
- **Seraphine's Loom seed in B01 is her Intent** (*we chose*; *something I wanted to be so*; the gate), not an ability.
  No graft may make her right instead, or make her uniquely able to move inside a stop.
- **A risk version 1 missed:** if *who had it first* reads as a **place**, the answer is the camp, which presses M51.
  *First* must always mean a person's onset; no case or instrument goes near the camp.
- **Climax success must mean carried, not prevented** (the breathing veil in miniature; PH-08).

## 7. What the book becomes

1. **By about a quarter**, someone is writing it all down, the summons has come on a name Lucien gave a policeman, and
   Seraphine's own log is the record they will want. The crew has a page of working rules, one of them wrong.
2. **By the middle**, Caldas has spoken to Baz and Baz has told no one; the crew has signed a vow, chosen whose
   households to warn, and been wrong at Ursulines on the woman they did not tell. A neighbour asks how they knew
   before anyone. Seraphine sorts the two hands, strikes the vow, refuses the oxygen deal to the woman's face and beats
   the hold herself; Caro says she would have taken it.
3. **By three quarters**, Seraphine has caught Lucien's sting from a form, the stakeout has lost the phone and won a
   face, and Baz has told Lucien who reads him. Lucien keeps it from her.
4. **At the lot** the rule half-works and her promise breaks in front of the people she brought; **at the Square** the
   warning works, the gallery is carried rather than saved, and the price is that they are seen. Lucien sends a true
   report with no people in it on her conditional yes; Baz guesses at a word.
5. **Closed:** the folder, the *None*, the sting, the leak, whom Lucien reports to, the wrong rule. **Open, by design:**
   what it is, who they are, what the cases are for, and *are they following the ground, or following us?*
