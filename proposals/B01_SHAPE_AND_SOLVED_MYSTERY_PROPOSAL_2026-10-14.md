## 1. The diagnosis in one paragraph

The author is right that this is shape, not length. Thirty thousand words have come out, and the same moves remain:
- **The loop runs complete nine times across the book:** event → tally → table argument → refusal → conditional yes (Act I 2,
  Act II 5, Act III 2). Partial runs come to about 20.
- **Refusal-then-conditional-yes is the default conversation.** Act I has about 14 in 11 chapters. Act III turns it inside out
  into *"You can tell me no"* from Ch 38 to Ch 42.
- **Most scenes end on a written line.** That ending closes 7 of Act I's 14 units, and Act II has 13 counting scenes that end on
  a notebook, board or sheet.
- **A second loop takes over in Act II's back half:** withhold → found out → confess → pay, six times in seven chapters.
- **Information arrives by phone or text:** ten deliveries in Act III, four of them in Ch 38.
- **The final 8,600 words** (Ch 40–43) contain five doorstep reckonings in a row, a memo stand-down (S4) and outcomes by phone.
  Nobody acts against resistance in the present.

Every one of these is a shape, and each recurs whatever the chapter's length.

## 2. The principle: every event teaches one new thing

repeatedly demonstrating that something strange happened, the protagonists can progressively reconstruct a causal chain."*

of action each time: a test, a chase, a refusal, a bet, a rescue. It is never the same debrief.

## 4. The shape rules for revision 4 (drafting rules, not quotas)

1. **One property, one shape.** Each event chapter is staged around the property it teaches (§2). The response to it is a
   different kind of action each time.
2. **No debrief as its own scene, unless it turns something.** A post-event table scene must change a plan, cost someone or
   carry a reveal. Otherwise it folds into the next action scene as a few lines.
3. **Vary the way conversations resolve.** Refusal turning into a conditional yes stays where it is the character, and is cut
   where it is habit. Use the others the book underuses: a flat no that holds, a yes with no terms, a yes that is a mistake, a
   walk-out, an interruption, a deal.
4. **Vary the endings.** No more than two consecutive scenes end on something written. No chapter ends in a parked car
   immediately after another that did.
5. **Information by action, not phone.** A phone or text delivery stays only where the distance is the point. Otherwise the
   information comes in scene.
6. **Withhold → confess, at most once per character per act.**
7. **The last act acts.** In Ch 40–43, at least one scene has a protagonist acting against resistance in the present (§5.3).

These are costed defaults in the profile's sense: the writer may break one where the scene earns it, and must say why in the log.

## 5. The change map (chapter level)

The proposals are by operation:
- **MERGE:** two or more scenes become one.
- **RESTAGE:** same content, different shape.
- **CUT:** a repeated beat removed.
- **END:** a new ending type.
- **ADD:** new material.
