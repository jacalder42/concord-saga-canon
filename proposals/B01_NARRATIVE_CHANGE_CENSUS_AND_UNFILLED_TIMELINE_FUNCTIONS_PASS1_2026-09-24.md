## Result

**Episode math is not event math:** the architecture has **48 narrative episodes = 36 causal-spine + 12 Life/Reward**, plus one prologue and six supplements. A Life/Reward scene can contain a genuine changed relationship; a causal episode can be investigation/setup/aftermath without a fresh independently carried event. The comparison corpus's 35/45 are *summary-visible candidates*, not proven novel-wide targets or evidence that every Concord volume needs exactly 40.

This first read finds **22 relatively clear event-candidate-bearing episode slots, 6 boundary-sensitive candidate slots, and 20 predominantly lived-life/receipt/setup/negative-space slots**. Those are **slot classifications, not a count of distinct events**: E23/E40/E45 might each have multiple independently consequential changes; E07/E16/E35/E36/E47 might partly merge with neighbors; E30's relational change may be small but valid. Nothing here asserts B1 has only 22 events, already has 40, or needs twelve added scenes.

The actual missing measurement is: how many *separately carried before → action/discovery → after* changes survive event-boundary review within the existing 48 slots? Distinguish narrative changes from physical Resonance manifestations, plot pressure from reader reward, and initiating event from receipt/cost. Aiming to **test the story against a rough 35–45 local-or-greater candidate range** is an author/editor diagnostic choice, **not** an industry standard, a per-episode quota or an instruction to fill every L row.
