### The times block (Q-TM1–3)

The trace found 139 time-recording passages, rising by act, with recaps rising from 5% to 25%, and the watchers' interest in
times met about eighteen times. In none of eleven ethics arguments is the time itself the private thing. The proposal offers
four options; **C** (privacy and the record in the crew's arguments, recaps and bookkeeping cut, the watchers' line thinned
to five or six occurrences) is recommended. The reviews add:

- **E27, E38 and E42 must be revised as one set.** Caro's E27 refusal makes the time the private thing; E38 and E42 depend
  on that. Whatever E27 names as private must be what E42 visibly strips. The E42 patient-detail correction belongs in this
  set.
- **Times work when giving or getting one costs something** (a minute shared, a time bought with testimony, a time given,
  minutes held). Recorded-only times are the bookkeeping. That is the keep-or-cut test.
- **The word window is overloaded**: the forecast's name in a book about breaking glass. Under C, keep it for the forecast
  page and the final account only.
- **Inez's book already models the answer**: names, never times, contested twice, with privacy as the stake.
- **The book already turns** in late Act III, where characters refuse to time things.

**Questions:**
- **Q-TM1.** Which option? Recommended: C, with the E27, E38 and E42 set.
- **Q-TM2.** Should clocks as contested objects be restated as records as contested objects? Recommended: yes, by dated
  note.
- **Q-TM3.** Should the stranger in E48 keep asking for the times? Recommended: yes.
