existing contests' stakes on the page and stop explaining after peaks. It cannot give the faceless opposition a face or a
defeat; whether more is needed is for the author's read.

## 3. T2 The line and narration pass (20 patterns)

**The test.** Does this instance express something particular, or repeat an effect the book has already achieved?
Recurring vocabulary may belong to a person; a shared practical gesture may belong to a workplace or a trade. Neither is a
defect by recurring. The defect is a narrator using one tag for different people, or one effect landing twice with nothing
changed. **No quotas**: counts show where to look, never how many to keep; no word or gesture is assigned to one person
alone. Never the first instance that does new work, a protected passage or a causal step.

1. A characterising simile comparing someone to a man or woman who has had some experience, shared by every point of
   view (about 45; worst in E20 and E10).
2. Disclaimed decisions, where the body moves before the choice (26; those where the non-decision is the point pass).
3. A stock intensifier for quantity that is Lucien's own vocabulary (voice in his speech and view; tested elsewhere) and
   its sibling for manner.
4. A short closing formula meaning that much and no more (10; most in Act III).
5. A nearly-smiling gesture (10).
6. Plain speech described as read off something (5).
7. One laughter sentence across several characters.
8. An object described as liable to leave or to change its mind (6).
9. Future-retrospective frames inside the Act I peaks.
10. Explaining what the scene has shown, and explaining just after a peak. This does not cover a statement of what
    evidence establishes where the investigation needs it.
11. Epigram exits and double endings (about 20 chapters).
12. A rehearsed speech that does not survive the room; the instances that cost or are comic pass.
13. The object riding in the car as a closing coda in early Act I.
14. Disclosure staged in absences: dense negation in E46–E49.
15. The front-room fall in E04 recapped in fixed phrasing; the returns that change something pass.
16. Simile clusters inside peaks (the prologue, E49's Jody sequence).
17. Overloaded working words, where the defect is ambiguity: the word for line carries about ten senses; the forecast has
    five names; the word for window names both the forecast and the glass; column names five sets; post collides in E49.
18. Narrator phrases that cross narrators (a plumb-bob locator, a trade metaphor of Lucien's, a finger's-width measure, a
    coarse gull, Lucien's crack simile).
19. Shared tags and refrains, each tested for whose it is: a pencil behind the ear (Trip's signature and a working

### The times block (Q-TM1–3)

The trace found 139 time-recording passages, rising by act, with recaps rising from 5% to 25%, and the watchers' interest in
times met about eighteen times. In none of eleven ethics arguments is the time itself the private thing. **Option C**
(privacy and the record in the crew's arguments, recaps and bookkeeping cut, the watchers' line thinned to the occurrences
that do work) is recommended, with **Caro's compromise kept**:

- **E27, E38 and E42 are revised together.** E27's refusal rests on what is entrusted to her service: the call belongs to
  the patient, and people ring because they trust it stays with the service. Its objection is to using entrusted
  information at all, so it must **not** be narrowed to match what E42 removes. E38: she gives only what is hers, her own
  tone-out time. E42: she removes the identifying fields, which **reduces the risk but does not settle her objection**;
  the page already has her acknowledge that she refused exactly this and was right, and print anyway. She proceeds for a
  reason strong enough to outweigh it, taken from what is already on the page, and owns the compromise. The narration never
  lets the redaction make it all right.
- **The evidence test** decides which times stay: a time shared, a time bought with testimony, a time given, minutes held,
  a timed onset set against still water.
- **The word window is overloaded**: under C, keep it for the forecast page and the final account only.
- **Inez's book already models the answer**: names, never times, contested twice, with privacy as the stake.
- **The book already turns** in late Act III, where characters refuse to time things.

**Questions:** Q-TM1, which option (recommended C, with the set as above); Q-TM2, restate clocks as contested objects as
records as contested objects (recommended yes, by dated note); Q-TM3, whether the stranger in E48 keeps asking for the
times (recommended yes; it is the book's one completed contest with the other side).
