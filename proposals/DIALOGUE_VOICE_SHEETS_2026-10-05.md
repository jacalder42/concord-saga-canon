# Dialogue voice sheets (side by side)

## 0. How to read a sheet

Each row has three columns:
- **Intent**: the designed trait, tagged by source.
- **Page**: what the merged B01 does (manuscript `ef0caef`, attributed lines only).
- **Decision**: one of four outcomes, as approved:
  - **page updates design**;
  - **design pulls back**;
  - **both, as modes**;

**Defaults with prices, not laws** (profile, *No always, no never*). **Use as taste, not as a linter.** One departure
is rarely a problem; a pattern is. The test for any line is whether anyone else in the scene could have said it.

## 1. What applies to everyone (from the profile)

- Dialogue is emotionally honest, low on exposition, carries subtext; articulacy is uneven; two characters diagnosing
  a third in the same words have become one voice (§5).
- Nobody explains the world to someone who lives in it unless that says something about them (§5).
- A cut-off line ends in a dash; an ellipsis is for trailing away (§12). The contrast-construction family is a tell in
  speech too (§12).
- Native words in plain type, meaning from context (§12A); Louisiana speech by §12B.
- Nothing spoken requires reading minds *(ruled)*.
- **Regulation shows in cadence** ([VS] rail 5): how a person steadies themselves is audible in how they talk.

**Humor defaults (Q-VI8).** Defaults, not quotas: Seraphine dry and observational; Lucien deadpan; Baz teasing, fed
through food; Caro hyperbole, then truth; Elisabet dry verdicts and plain flirtation; Trip dry and kind.

| --- | --- | --- |

| 2 | Full first names | Stable baseline trust |
| 3 | Softened first names | Leaning, vulnerability |

## 11. Narration (Q-VI12)

The author's 2025-11-01 words describe how each character *describes things* when they lead. They are applied to **what
each point of view notices and names** (Seraphine systems and procedure; Lucien structure and manners; Elisabet plain
fact), not to the narrator's syntax. No B01 change; a review test for B02.

## 12. How to use these in revision

1. **Voice-swap test.** Cover the speaker tags. Could another character in the scene have said the line?
2. **Regulation test.** Can the reader hear how this person steadies themselves?
3. **Listener test.** Does the line change with who it is said to?
4. **Cost test.** If a character breaks a default (Lucien candid, Elisabet raw), does the page pay for it?
5. **Collision test.** Check §10's shared words for who holds each.
6. **Pull-back check.** Before a revision, look for a **design pulls back** decision above. It is the only kind of sheet
   line that may change the page against the preceding prose (Q-VI1).
