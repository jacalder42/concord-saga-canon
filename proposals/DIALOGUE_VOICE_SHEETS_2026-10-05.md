# Dialogue voice sheets

## 0. Why these exist

The POV cards describe how each character **perceives and narrates**. They say little about how each one **speaks**. The
author's suspicion is that nuance was lost in archiving and compression. A survey of the repository and the export
supports that, in four specific ways:

1. **The author's own 2025-11-01 words differ from the distilled cards, and the difference was recorded, not resolved.**
   Seraphine, when she leads, was *"more complex and technical"*. Lucien was *"proper and aloof"* with moments of candour.
   Elisabet was *"raw, upfront and simple"*. The cards now say warm and breath-paced, cold geometry, and spare and
   withheld. Q-MS5 (`decisions/SYMBOLISM_AND_LANGUAGE_RECOVERY_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-10-01.md`) chose no standing layer and
   left the conflict open. §3 records each conflict beside the sheet it touches.
2. **The 2025-11-30 *Voice systems* design carried speech rules that reached no card.** They are in
   `sources/chatgpt_export_2026-09/2025-11-30__Voice_systems__692bde50.md`: a six-step naming ladder, naming
   suppression under pressure, per-character metaphor domains and the rule that no POV leaves its own, *emotional
   regulation must appear in cadence*, and voice modulation by MODE, HEAT, FX and RES. The language recovery carries
   fragments. I did not find the ladder or the suppression rules in `canon/` or `rules/`.
3. **Speech tells exist only as a language permission** (`proposals/LANGUAGE_AND_REGISTER_CHARACTER_NOTES_2026-10-01.md`)
   for six characters.
4. **The antagonist cards have a speech field; the protagonist cards do not.** Han Wei, Ito, Saeko and Virelli each carry
   language constraints. Seraphine, Caro, Rex, Baz and Trip carry a surface-affect line.

## 1. Evidence tags and limits

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **[C]** | A line in a card (POV, ID, EBCI) in `canon/` |
| **[A]** | The author's own words or a dated ruling |
| **[L]** | The approved language notes (Q-FL5) |
| **[S]** | A design in the export (assistant text, evidence only, not ruled) |
| **[I]** | **Inferred by Claude** from the above. Confirm before use |
| **[OPEN]** | Sources conflict or are silent. Not resolved here (CLAUDE.md §4) |

**Limits, stated plainly:**
- **I could not read the manuscript.** It lives in the private repository. Every speech habit below is derived from
  cards, the export and rulings, so none is tested against actual dialogue. Q-WP3 says that once prose establishes a
  voice, the preceding prose governs. **These sheets should be checked against three or four pages of finished dialogue
  per character before anyone relies on them.** Where the page and a sheet disagree, the page wins unless it contradicts
  canon.
- **No sample lines.** A sheet that quotes lines becomes a script and then a tic. Each sheet describes tendencies.
- **No phonetic spelling and no dialect grammar.** Regional speech belongs to the lived-in Louisiana map
  (`decisions/B01_LIVED_IN_LOUISIANA_LANGUAGE_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-10-05.md`) and the pending profile §12B, after the
  community readers report. These sheets do not duplicate or pre-empt them.
- **Defaults with prices, not laws** (profile, *No always, no never*). Rulings are marked as such.

**Use as taste, not as a linter.** One departure is rarely a problem. A pattern is. The test for any line is whether
anyone else in the scene could have said it. If so, look at it.

## 2. What applies to everyone (from the profile)

- Dialogue is emotionally honest, low on exposition, and carries subtext (§5). People answer the question they wish
  they'd been asked, interrupt, deflect, lecture, go quiet.
- Articulacy is uneven. When two characters diagnose a third in the same words, they have become one voice (§5).
- Nobody explains the world to someone who already lives in it. When they do, it says something about them (§5).
- Dialogue changes through the listener's response, and a misunderstanding may persist if evidence, trust or stakes
  sustain it (§5).
- A cut-off line ends in a dash. An ellipsis is for a speaker trailing away (§12).
- *"It wasn't X, it was Y"* and its family are tells in speech as well as narration (§12).
- Native words go in plain type, meaning from context, no gloss (§12A). A character's other language is a permission, not
  a quota *(ruled: no quotas)*.
- Nothing is spoken that requires reading minds *(ruled)*.

**Regulation shows in cadence** [S, Voice systems rail 5]. How a person steadies themselves should be audible in how they
talk: Lucien pauses before a name, Caro breathes through a sentence, Elisabet modulates herself. This is the single
most useful speech idea that never reached a card.

## 3. Cast sheets

### 3.1 Seraphine Vael

**Basis:** [C] `seraphine_vael_pov.md`: warm, steady, human-scale, non-authoritarian; breath-paced; stillness allowed;
reassures by presence, not explanation. Consent precedes regulation; no savior framing, destiny language or authority
tone. Approved metaphor domains: breath, warmth, water, rhythm, weather, gathering circles. [A] Cajun-dominant with Black
Creole family; a few words of her elders' Louisiana French, mostly as forms of address (Q-FL4, Q-LL1). [A] 2025-11-01:
*"more complex and technical"* when she leads.

**How she uses talk** [I]: to check before she acts. She asks and offers more than she tells, and leaves the other
person room to decline. Her attention order (group, then individuals, then place, then herself) predicts what she says
first in a room.

**Sound:** short to mid sentences at breath length; few commands; questions that are real questions. Louisiana forms of
address (*Miss* or *Mr.* with a first name, family terms) come naturally with elders, and denser there (Level B).

**Avoids** [C]: an authority tone; telling people how to feel; promising an outcome; certainty offered as care. Metaphor
from outside her domain, architecture above all [S].

**Under pressure** [C]: language tightens slightly and she slows her actions. [I] She says less, not more, and what she
says gets more concrete.

**Humor:** [OPEN]. The card is silent. [I] If she has it, it is observational and kind, and it does not hurry anyone.

**Shifts by listener:** with Lucien, silence given shape, and the Louisiana forms soften toward what he offers in French
[L]; with Caro, near-overlap, shared labour [C]; with Elisabet, warmth meeting precision [C]; with Kade, a compass without
certainty [C].

**Collision watch:** *hold* (Tahl, Lacuna, Elias use it too). Hers is about rooms and people, not signals or windows.

**Revision tells:**
- Does anyone in her scenes get a real choice about her help?
- Did she name the room before herself?
- Is her calm described, or audible in her sentences?
- Did a line sound like a counsellor? Her warmth should not be professional.

**Open:** [OPEN] "complex and technical" versus the card's breath-and-light register. Plausibly both: technical in what
she notices, warm in how she says it [I]. The author decides.

### 3.2 Lucien Kael

**Basis:** [C] `lucien_kael_pov.md`: clean sentence structures, spatial and mechanical language, minimal emotional
labelling; warmth appears only under safety, and Seraphine is the trigger. [A] *"proper and aloof"* with candour
(2025-11-01). [L] Vienna; Austrian mother, French father. English precise and lightly accented; German when stressed
or unguarded; French in intimacy. His cover is Austrian, so the German fits it.

**How he uses talk** [I]: as a probe. He asks what would break if something moved, and he puts the question where a
structural argument would be. He is cautious about what he reveals, and he can conceal where he comes from while openly
pursuing what he wants to know (profile §5A).

**Sound:** full sentences, formal word choice, few contractions at distance and more with Seraphine [I]; architectural
and spatial figures; negative space, pressure points, symmetry [S].

**Avoids** [S, C]: glow, humidity, breath metaphors. Naming his feelings. Rushing a name: he pauses before naming [S].

**Under pressure:** German surfaces [L]; sentences shorten; silence arrives. His end state is silence with intent, so
quiet is a choice and not a gap [C].

**Humor:** dry, only when safe [I]. Trip punctures his rigidity with sharp humor, and he allows it [C].

**Address and language:** surnames and titles at distance, first names as trust grows; French intimacy names for
Seraphine, only after a native-speaker check [L, Q-FL6]. Keep his German to stress and privacy.

**Shifts by listener:** most candid with Seraphine [C]; structurally collegial with Tahl and Elisabet [C].

**Collision watch:** *pattern* and *pressure* (Tahl, Elisabet). His are physical and structural, not analytic.

**Revision tells:**
- Is he formal because he is reserved, or just stiff?
- Does any candid moment arrive without a cause, a safe room or a person?
- Does he explain more than a man who conceals would?

**Open:** [OPEN] how far "aloof" shows in speech versus manner.

### 3.3 Baz (Bastien Arnaud)

**Basis:** [C] `baz_arnaud_pov.md`: observational, plainspoken, quietly precise, lightly dry; names emotion when he must

**How he uses talk** [I]: to find out what a person can survive hearing, then to say it in a form that lands. He restates

**Sound:** plain, concrete, short to mid; asks about who is affected; a notebook is his, so writing things down is a

**Avoids:** speeches, big framing, performed empathy.

**Under pressure** [C]: he slows the room and absorbs panic; his calm is practised, not innate. [I] His speech gets plainer,

**Collision watch:** plainness shared with Rex and Tahl. His differs because it is about people first.

### 3.4 Caro (Carolina Alvarez)

## 5. Address and naming (source design, partly unadopted)

From *Voice systems* [S]; none is ruled:
1. Titles or surnames: distance, formality, danger.
2. Full first names: stable baseline trust.
3. Softened first names: leaning, vulnerability.
4. Nicknames: chosen intimacy.
5. Native-language pet names: intimate, often withheld under pressure.
6. Ascendant naming (LT): Book 9 only.

**Suppression** under trauma, distortion or proximity: stuttered, truncated or avoided names, formal regression,
last-name fallback. **Naming position:** front-loaded names mean urgency, back-loaded mean tenderness. A character does
not use a naming level above what the arc has earned.

*Louisiana forms of address* are separate and belong to the lived-in Louisiana map.

## 6. How to use these in revision

1. **Voice-swap test.** Cover the speaker tags. Could another character in the scene have said the line? If so, look at
   it.
2. **Regulation test.** Can the reader hear how this person steadies themselves?
3. **Listener test.** Does the line change with who it is said to?
4. **Cost test.** If a character breaks a default (Lucien candid, Elisabet raw, Kade silent), does the page pay for it?
5. **Collision test.** Search the scene for the shared words in §4 and check who holds each.
