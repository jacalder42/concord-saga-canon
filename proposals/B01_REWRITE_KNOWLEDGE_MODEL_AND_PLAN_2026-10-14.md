## 2. The knowledge model (draft for Q-KN1–3)

- **What Dominion wants (Lucien's *what*):** a record of where and when it happens; the people who feel it first; Baz.
  Lucien's weekly note and monthly report feed that. He knows his review is a collector for them. He does not know why they
  want it.
- **Why they want it (Baz's *why*):** Baz holds **Dominion's working model**: there is a Veil; resonance is how it shows;
  it is thinning in places; people who feel it first are the instruments of the thinning (and the data). Their doctrine
  is control: manage and quantify to survive instability (`canon/factions/Dominions.md`).
- **Bounded by the reserved reveals:** Baz does **not** know the cap, the soul/Mira, the cycle, the chain or the Mending site.
  His model is *the Dominion's* model and is **partly wrong or incomplete**. The crew's work can correct it. That keeps the
  mystery open, keeps the reserved truths reserved, and gives the investigation something to find that Baz does not hold.
- **Asymmetry, not parity:** each man knows only half and each knows the other keeps something. Lucien does not know Baz knows
  the why; Baz does not know exactly how much Lucien has been told. (Q-KN3.)

## 3. How this changes the mystery

The question stops being "what is happening" for the two men and becomes **"what is true, and who will say it first."**
- **Reader layer:** the reader learns what Baz and Lucien know by watching what they *do* (what they avoid, what they ask for,
  what they leave out of a note), not from announcements.
- **Crew layer:** the investigation tests the Dominion model against the ground: Elisabet's data, Lucien's counts, Seraphine's
  people. Findings can **confirm** a piece of Baz's model (he flinches), **contradict** it (he is wrong; he has been acting on
  a bad map), or **go beyond** it (something he never knew). Those three outcomes give each discovery a different shape.
- **Payoffs:** the camp; the box side (who holds the instruments); what *persons who feel it first* means in practice; the
  folder sheet becomes a document that **Baz kept**, not a stolen answer key; Caldas's "who had it before the others" stops being
  a repeating question and becomes the thing the opposition is actually after.

## 4. What it does for Baz's and Lucien's POVs

- **Baz:** an internal ledger of *what he could say, to whom, at what cost.* Each Baz chapter is a decision about disclosure
  (a half-truth, a deflection, a lie by silence, a warning given sideways). His reason to stay quiet is real: people who know
  become liabilities.
- **Lucien:** he knows what his notes do. His "None" on the form, his omissions and the way he words the report become
  deliberate choices from page one. His slide into confessing is no longer a mystery that needs an off-page cause.
- **Backstory** comes in as pressure in the present (Vienna, the table, the neighborhood that was not warned), not as monologue.

## 5. The confession budget (answers the author's concern, Q-KN4)

The diagnostics found confession-shaped scenes everywhere, with accurate self-diagnosis and no resistance. Rules for the rewrite:

1. At most **three** on-page disclosures of something a character has been holding back, in the whole book; the rest are
   partial, indirect or taken.
2. Every disclosure is **forced by evidence, a deadline or a person who is owed it**, never offered because the scene needs it.
3. The teller **tells it badly**: incomplete, defensive, out of order, aimed at the wrong person, or in the wrong register.
4. The hearer **does not answer perfectly**: they misread, get angry, change the subject, or use it later against the teller.
5. It **costs something on the page** (trust, a job, a person, safety) and the cost is carried into the next scene.
6. Knowledge can also arrive by **deed** (a character acts on something they should not know), by **overhearing**, by a
   **document read wrongly**, or by **another person telling first**. A chapter's "reveal" is a different one of these each time.
7. **No self-diagnosis in a complete sentence.** A character may say what they did; they may not name why, accurately, in
   a line a reader could quote.
8. The reader sees a character **not** telling at least as often as telling.

Why this should land where polish did not: the unit of change is the **outline**, not the sentence. Every scene is born with a
required change and a cost, the payoffs are tracked as the outline is written, and the draft is checked against the same
instruments that diagnosed the old one.

Scope of the fresh draft: **Ch 1–2 (the CPR and the camp) are the anchor and are kept as the opening base unless the outline
shows a conflict;** Lucien and Baz's first appearances (Ch 4 onward) are rewritten under the knowledge model.
