## Candidate observation, not explanation

Keep the recovered amp interruption. A **low but ordinarily audible, uneven thrum** may be present **before** the amp pops. The recovered old E13 then has a heartbeat of quiet after the cutout, with ordinary sound returning later and the hum eventually resettling. Any *recorded* return of the same thrum after that gap is a NEW TEST, not recovered continuity. Do not make it uninterrupted. Two separate, locally stored recordings made for ordinary reasons happen to catch a portion of the sequence from different positions: for example, the performer's independently powered portable field recorder and an unrelated bystander's phone video. These identities are examples, not cast additions or facts. Both may contain the amp pop as an approximate synchronization marker and a pre-pop thrum; whether either captures a later return of a comparable sound is OPEN; microphone filtering, position, crowd sound and different start times prevent a clean match.

On-page E15 does not announce a frequency, an origin, or a “signature.” A listener might hear a generator, traffic, feedback, building vibration, bells, or an unlocated mundane source. If a later sound is independently captured after the quiet interval, similarity to the earlier thrum does not prove a shared source: another speaker, nearby machinery and editing/phone processing remain candidates. Nor does acoustic correspondence alone prove Resonance. Witnesses are allowed to disagree about whether the sound preceded the pop, how long the quiet lasted, whether a similar hum returned, or whether they heard it at all.

This is one proposed **acoustic observation with two potential independent records**; it is not two different physical effects. The new trace can make the event investigable and portentous, but by itself may **not** meet the stricter “second independent non-startle physical anomaly” requirement. If the author wants E15 to demonstrate Resonance objectively at this stage, that decision and a bounded causal mechanism remain separate.

## Failure tests and author decisions

1. **Audibility:** Is the thrum objectively acoustic, or merely subjective/attuned? If the latter, ordinary microphones cannot catch it; choose a different second trace rather than blurring registers.
2. **Independence:** Does a hum on two independent originals predate the pop, and is the brief quiet preserved? If a similar sound is later heard, does it have the same mundane source or only resemble it? If the amp or another ordinary source accounts for both files, the evidence is still useful as a failed hypothesis, **not** an independent anomaly.
3. **Footprint:** Can two positions pick it up without implying a perfectly coherent city-scale event? Choose bounded listener/recorder positions and avoid false precision.
4. **Story economy:** Does a brief E15 sensation plus a short E16 preservation beat suffice, leaving Baz's serious comparison to E25? If not, omit one recording or defer the clue; do not replace human aftermath with an audio-lab scene.
5. **Escalation:** Does E33 change expression and invalidate some E15-based expectations? Does E48 reward choosing observation over control even if no hum returns? Do not manufacture a recurring sonic brand.
6. **Public ceiling:** An ordinary-source alternative must remain credible to outsiders. S02 precedes E15 and cannot bear this evidence. Later clips and rumors need not establish a global pattern or reveal MT.
