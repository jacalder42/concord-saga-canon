## Source, verification and measurement limits

Inspected the [URSummary book-one recap by numbered chapter](https://ursummary.com/dungeon-crawler-carl-summary-chapter-by-chapter/), Chapters 1–47 and epilogue. Independently spot-checked the opening against the unofficial [DCC Wiki Chapter 1](https://dungeoncrawlercarlwiki.com/wiki/dungeon-crawler-carl-chapter-1/), the first boss against [Chapter 11](https://dungeoncrawlercarlwiki.com/wiki/dungeon-crawler-carl-chapter-11/), and the goblin aftermath against [Chapter 18](https://dungeoncrawlercarlwiki.com/wiki/dungeon-crawler-carl-chapter-18/). Agreement on the major transitions does **not** validate 47 chapters. The Chapter 11 sources differ on the boss's exact finishing action; consequently the register avoids relying on specific combat moves. Both are secondary sources, not an authorized full text.

A row requires a distinct changed condition/choice/role/resource or actionable inference. A multi-chapter encounter counts once at its resolution; individual attacks, loot notices, rests, and commentary remain attached as receipts unless they independently change later options. The first-floor bomb and its aftermath are one initiating event plus a separately coded durable status/mark when supported by the recap. One primary tag per row; mixed types are expected. Chapter position is **not** prose percentage.

## Boundary decisions and source sensitivity

- DC-05 joins Donut's transformation and first partner-like conversations across Chapters 5–6. DC-06 is the subsequent party agreement; it might merge under a broader unit of analysis.
- DC-08 treats the Hoarder preparation, fight, loot and moral reaction as one encounter. The two secondary pages disagree about exact finishing blows, so no combat-technique inference is made.
- DC-13 treats goblin bombing and noncombatant deaths as one physical incident with enduring cost. DC-14 records a separate permanent-game-status consequence; remove DC-14 if the full novel shows no independently changed access/position. The [detailed aftermath](https://dungeoncrawlercarlwiki.com/wiki/dungeon-crawler-carl-chapter-18/) stresses that successful tactics can coexist with costly loss and altered relations.
- DC-15 groups safe-room standoff and pursuit as a single linked threat, rather than counting every provocation and device trigger. DC-23 (improvised recipe) may be just a one-off tactic unless later reuse confirms a durable capability.
- DC-24 joins the fairy trap, Krakaren conflict and Donut's rescue under one sustained sequence. With full prose these may prove separate state-changing events. DC-32 and DC-33 distinguish a boss victory from Mongo joining the group through an independent subsequent choice.
- DC-35 is an **epilogue information/choice setup**, not proof that a next-book class decision has already happened. Don't turn it into a new progression milestone.
- If DC-06, DC-14, DC-23 and DC-31 are dropped as boundary-sensitive, the summary-visible register falls from 35 to **31**. The physical/logistical/informational/relational mix and the need for a full-text check remain. The 31 is a sensitivity count, **not** a confidence interval on the book's actual event total.

## Implications for the comparative task

The recap's most informative pattern is **incident → rule/resource change → social/moral consequence → altered next choice**, repeatedly across the journey. Not every interesting part is a fight: a guide, a party, a trade route, a responsibility for vulnerable people, a broadcast audience, and information about who controls the game change the options available to Carl and Donut. This is a narrative diagnostic for Concord's event web, not an invitation to add combat or rewards mechanically to B01 or distribute all B07–B09 incidents evenly.

A true peer benchmark requires full-length authorized text or high-resolution verified notes, chapter/page/word anchors and a second independent boundary coding. The [ACOMAF scan](ACOMAF_COMPLETE_CHAPTER_SUMMARY_EVENT_REGISTER_PASS1_2026-09-24.md) uses a different secondary source and has 45 rows; **do not interpret 35 versus 45 as an event-density difference between novels**. Rivers still lacks accessible chapter-resolved bodies and its earlier twelve-chapter estimate conflicts with a later fourteen-chapter guide, so no Rivers event count is attempted.
