Status: PROPOSAL, 2026-10-05. Not applied; Tier D. It drafts the §12B that Q-LL4 left open
(`decisions/B01_LIVED_IN_LOUISIANA_LANGUAGE_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-10-05.md`). Nothing in it is approved until the author

**Why side by side.** The author, 2026-10-05: *"Q-LL5 has been done, proposed form is approved"* (the community-reader
check is done; the pilot's form is approved and is applied to the merged B01). And: *"Voice, language and lens - we need

repository. Single Louisiana French or Louisiana English words (*mais*, *cher*, *by*) are named as vocabulary.

## 1. How to read §2

Each area has four lines: **Intent** (with source), **Pilot** (what the approved form does), **Agreement / tension**, and
a **proposed §12B line**. Lines are costed defaults in the profile's style. *(ruled)* appears only where an author ruling
says so; most of this is approved design.

## 2. Rule areas

### 2.1 Frame: local speech is not a foreign word
- **Intent.** The author, 2025-11-10: *"Seraphine should be cajun, while the New Orleans group is creole"*; *"local
  cred, not… the bus full of tourists"*; *"Concord is not Disney World."* §12A was written for foreign words and frames
  another language as a permission at stress or tenderness, but also says *"a local's own words are not foreign to
  them."* Q-LL1 approved four communities.
- **Pilot.** Treats Louisiana English as the elders' everyday register, not a switch. Changes only words inside existing
  lines; no scene, beat or line function moved.
- **Agreement.** Full. §12A's local-words line becomes operative.
- **§12B line.** *For Louisiana-born speakers, Louisiana English is the ordinary register, not an other language: §12A's
  sparing applies to foreign words, and a local who speaks in neutral American English for several lines costs the
  place.*

### 2.2 Seraphine: Acadiana Cajun, Black Creole family
- **Intent.** 2025-11-10 (assistant, released): Cajun French inflections in emotional cadence, about 10% of dialogue,
  glossed in narration. 2025-12-09 (assistant, for a Tremé origin the author later changed): AAVE, Creole phrases from
  elders, soft NOLA inflections. **Q-FL4 strand (2026-10-01): her elders' Louisiana French, both registers, mostly as
  forms of address.** Q-LL1: Cajun-dominant with Black Creole family. Card CP2: born Abbeville, raised Lafayette to about
  11, then Tante Colette in Tremé. The page (E03) gives her a work voice.
- **Pilot.** At work (E34) she stays neutral; at home and exhausted (E33) one discourse marker (*mais*) and one Louisiana
  calque (*bring* for *take*); the narration names her aunt by family title (*Tante*). A sentence-final disjunct pronoun
  was withdrawn as stacked and performed.
- **Tension.** Q-FL4 says *mostly forms of address*; the pilot's two spoken markers are a discourse marker and a calque,
  and her one address form is in narration. And her Tremé years from about 11 (CP2, after Q-FL4) are not in either
  strand. → **Q-LB1, Q-LB2.**
- **§12B line.** *Seraphine code-switches by setting: her work voice is neutral; the Cajun of her Acadiana childhood
  comes out at home, with elders, tired or afraid, a word or two at a time. Stacking several markers in one short
  exchange makes the switch look performed.*

### 2.3 Odile: Honey Island Cajun
- **Intent.** Q-LL1: Odile's family is Cajun, an eastern enclave on the Pearl River (the author's sources are travel and
  tour pages); *cher* is invariable. Q-CP1 (a) re-anchors her camp east to Honey Island. Author, 2025-11-15: *"No one is
  a caricature."*
- **Pilot.** The densest voice: seven active items in about a dozen short lines in E01: a grief exclamation, *cher* as
  an address form (invariable), *mais*, a right-dislocated pronoun, the emphatic disjunct-pronoun pattern, topic-first
  left dislocation, and a sentence-final *yeah*. *Bon* was withdrawn (the next sentence read as its gloss).
- **Tension.** Intent and pilot agree on community and forms. The pre-screen flagged a stock "Cajun grandma" stack in one
  grief paragraph (kept after one withdrawal) and asked how much French a Pearl River elder keeps; the readers' answer
  is not recorded. Density is Q-LB5.
- **§12B line.** *Odile speaks Pearl River Cajun English: Louisiana French address and exclamations, French-patterned word
  order, and plain English for anything the plot needs. Her family's French is Cajun usage (cher, invariable).*

### 2.4 Downtown Black New Orleans elders (Tremé, Seventh Ward)
- **Intent.** 2025-11-10: *"the New Orleans group is creole."* The Q-LL proposal, approved as a map: New Orleans English,
  **not Kouri-Vini**; Creole French survives in names, Catholic practice, food and family words (*Nannan, Parrain,
  Tante*). §12A: *Miss*/*Mr.* with a first name, family terms and pet names *"as people would, not as color."*
- **Pilot.** Shared New Orleans features: *by* for at someone's place, the greeting *where y'at*, *your mama and them*,
  sentence-final *yeah*, *child* as an elder's address (once), Louisiana *bring*, topic-first dislocation, address to the
  dead at the tomb; *cher* once (Mrs. Arceneaux, sardonic). No Creole family titles downtown. No new shared *baby* or
  *Hm*.
- **Tension.** The author's *creole* for this group is carried by one *cher*; the Creole family words the map names are
  absent. The pre-screen also found convergence: *by* four times across three speakers in E34, final *yeah* across three
  speakers, *cher* shared with Odile across the community line. → **Q-LB3.**
- **§12B line.** *Downtown elders speak New Orleans English. Creole French lives in their names, saints, food and family
  titles, not in sentences. Give each elder their own few habits: a community feature handed to every speaker in a
  scene makes them one voice (§5).*

### 2.5 Yat
- **Intent.** Not in the author's 2025 words. Added by the Q-LL proposal (approved map) so that regional marking does not
  fall only on Black characters.
- **Pilot.** Two items stand: *cap* as a man-to-man address (Tony) and a sentence-final *yeah* (the corner-store owner).
  Sal's *yeah, you right* (wrong tone for confirming a mistake, a souvenir phrase) and his *cap* (duplicating Tony) were
  withdrawn. Sal's existing lines already carry Yat rhythm.
- **Tension.** See 2.6.
- **§12B line.** *Yat speakers carry their own greetings, address and set phrases. A city slogan in the wrong tone reads
  as a T-shirt; spend the famous phrases on the moments whose tone they fit.*

### 2.6 Balance across communities
- **Intent.** Q-LL proposal guardrail: *no single group is the only one marked as nonstandard.* Author: no caricature.
- **Pilot.** After withdrawals: about 12 applied items across four downtown elders against 2 Yat items. The pre-screen
  recommended trimming downtown rather than adding; the applied form withdrew Yat and one downtown item (#27).
- **Tension.** The guardrail is met (both groups are marked) but the ratio moved further toward the Black speakers. Some
  of it reflects dialogue volume and the base text's existing Yat rhythm, which was not measured. → **Q-LB4.**
- **§12B line.** *Mark every community by its own features, and check the balance per book, not per scene.*

### 2.7 Density
- **Intent.** *No quotas* *(ruled, §12A)*. Q-FL2: a review-side watch item, roughly one non-English word **or local
  term** per scene, a handful per chapter (`REVIEW_WATCHLIST.md`). Earlier figures (10% of dialogue; LR-3's five per
  chapter) are assistant text. The Q-LL proposal inverts the flag for locals: a local with several lines and no marker
  is the fault. Q-LL2: Level B, **denser for the elders**.
- **Pilot.** Elders carry markers in most turns (Odile 7, Miss Tavie 5, Mr. Vidrine 3, Mrs. Arceneaux 2, Inez 2);
  Seraphine 2 spoken; outsiders none.
- **Tension.** The approved elder density exceeds the watch figure, which as written covers local terms. → **Q-LB5.**
- **§12B line.** *No quotas (ruled). An elder may carry a marker in most turns; a younger or working speaker fewer. The
  watch is two-sided: a local with no marker, or a marker in every line of a scene.*

### 2.8 Phonetic spelling
- **Intent.** §12A: phonetic spelling of accent or dialect costs the speaker their dignity (place-name pronunciation as a
  scene's subject excepted, Q-CA4). Every 2025 source agrees.
- **Pilot.** None: the proposal's *dawlin'* was excluded as eye dialect; *where y'at* and *y'all* stand, the pre-screen
  reading *y'at* as the conventional spelling of a fixed phrase.
- **Agreement**, with one boundary to name: established print spellings of fixed phrases versus dropped letters. →
  **Q-LB8.**
- **§12B line.** *Spell words standard. A fixed phrase keeps its established print spelling (y'all); a dropped letter or
  respelled vowel is eye dialect.*

### 2.9 Plain type
- **Intent.** Q-FL3 (2026-10-01): plain type, no first-use italics; supersedes LR-9 and CR-Lang-02.
- **Pilot.** No italics on any Louisiana word.
- **Agreement.** Full.
- **§12B line.** *Louisiana French and Louisiana English go in plain type (§12A).*

### 2.10 Grammar features
- **Intent.** Q-LL proposal §4 and Q-LL2: habitual and completive forms and copula deletion only after a community
  reader; *no one is a caricature*.
- **Pilot.** Level B word order only: right and left dislocation, the emphatic disjunct pronoun, topic-first sentences.
  Excluded: habitual *be*, completive *done*, copula deletion. The base text already gives some elders Southern
  nonstandard grammar (a handful of uses before the pilot).
- **Tension.** The reader condition is now met, but no grammar feature was put before them. Whether the exclusion
  continues is open. → **Q-LB6.**
- **§12B line (holding form).** *Word order carries the voice: dislocation, fronting, the emphatic pronoun. Habitual and
  completive forms and copula deletion stay off the page until a community reader has seen the line.*

### 2.11 Folklore guard
- **Intent.** Q-LL decision §2: the Honey Island Swamp Monster, *letiche* and *loup-garou* are not for the page; they
  would read as an explanation of the phenomenon (§9 *(ruled)*) and are the tourist set. Needs its own ruling to change.
- **Pilot.** None used.
- **Agreement.** Full.
- **§12B line.** *Swamp and Cajun monster lore stays off the page near the phenomenon: next to the strange, it reads as an
  explanation (§9, ruled) and as the tourist's set. Changing that needs an author ruling.*

### 2.12 Who may code-switch, and when (incl. narration and outsiders)
- **Intent.** §12A: another language is a permission at stress, tenderness or habit; LR-10: point of view sets what is
  understood (a native speaks freely, an outsider hears a paraphrase). Q-LL proposal: Lucien may misparse, Baz may
  translate, Caro (Chicago) hears it as foreign. Profile §12: American vocabulary for American points of view.
- **Pilot.** Only Seraphine switches; elders do not (it is their register). One narration change, a family title in
  Seraphine's point of view. Outsiders' hearing was not tested.
- **Tension.** Whether Louisiana words enter third-limited narration beyond family titles and place words is unruled. →
  **Q-LB7.**
- **§12B line.** *Elders do not switch; Seraphine does, by setting. Outsiders hear local speech through their own point
  of view: a misheard greeting or a translated phrase is information about them. Narration in a local's point of view
  may use the family titles and place words that person would use.*

### 2.13 Attestation
- **Intent.** LR-5: genuine usage only, no invented blends. Author 2025-11-11: editors and language experts should weigh
  in. Q-FL8: a per-book list of non-English words with a native-speaker read. Q-LL5: community readers.
- **Pilot.** Pre-screen flagged *bring* for *take* as likely but unconfirmed in a scholarly source, and *need* plus a
  gerund as non-local (revised). Readers done; findings unrecorded.
- **§12B line.** *Use only attested Louisiana usage. A feature not yet seen by a reader from its community is listed
  for the book's language read (Q-FL8) before final.*

## 3. Where §12B would live

- **Intent.** Q-WP3 (approved design, Veil production rule): no permanent per-character voice layer; one compact card
  line only for a character's first substantial POV appearance. The Q-LL proposal §5 wanted a speech-community note in
  each packet with local speakers and a line in each local's identity context. The dialogue voice sheets stay
  review-side (author instruction, 2026-10-05).
- **Tension.** A per-character packet note may be the voice layer Q-WP3 excludes; a per-community profile section is not.
  → **Q-LB10.**

## 4. Questions for the author (Q-LB)

- **Q-LB1. Seraphine's markers.** Q-FL4: *mostly forms of address*. Pilot: a discourse marker and a calque in speech, the
  family title in narration. (a) Both stand: forms of address first, discourse markers and calques permitted when she
  is tired or at home. (b) Q-FL4 governs: forms of address only; the pilot's two spoken items are revisited at the line
  edit. (c) The pilot governs: Q-FL4's note is amended.
- **Q-LB2. Seraphine's Tremé years.** CP2 puts her in Tremé from about 11 with Tante Colette. (a) Cajun-dominant only,
  as Q-LL1. (b) Cajun at home and with family, plus the downtown features she grew up among (*by*, *where y'at*). (c)
  Leave unstated until B02 packets need it.
- **Q-LB3. Downtown *creole*.** The author's 2025 *"the New Orleans group is creole"* against the pilot's shared New
  Orleans English with one *cher*. (a) As the pilot: Creole lives in names, saints and food; no Creole family words added.
  (b) Add Creole family titles (*Nannan*, *Parrain*, *Tante*) downtown where a family scene calls for them. (c) Also keep
  *cher* as a marker of particular Creole families, assigned per speaker so it does not echo Odile.
- **Q-LB4. Balance.** About 12 downtown items against 2 Yat after withdrawals. (a) Trim downtown at the line edit
  (the pre-screen's lean). (b) Give Yat speakers a feature where a scene fits. (c) Accept it as dialogue volume and the
  base text's Yat rhythm; make it a per-book review check only.
- **Q-LB5. Density.** The watch item's *one per scene, a handful per chapter* covers local terms; *denser for the elders*
  exceeds it. (a) The watch figure applies to foreign words only; for Louisiana speech the flag is two-sided (2.7).
  (b) Elders are exempt; Seraphine and younger locals keep the figure. (c) The figure applies to everyone and the pilot's
  elders are thinned.
- **Q-LB6. Grammar features.** The readers are done; the pilot tested none. (a) Stay excluded for Veil. (b) Permitted
  line by line, each seen by a reader from the community. (c) The base text's existing elder grammar stays; no new
  features are added.
- **Q-LB7. Narration.** (a) Family titles and place words only, as in the pilot. (b) Also the POV character's own
  calques where they are how she thinks (*bring*). (c) Narration stays neutral; titles appear only in speech.
- **Q-LB8. Fixed-phrase spelling.** (a) Established print spellings of fixed phrases (*y'all*, *where y'at*) are
  standard spelling; dropped letters are not. (b) Standard spelling only, so the greeting is rendered another way.
- **Q-LB9. The community read.** (a) Record a summary of the readers' findings (private notes, with a public abstract)
  so §12B can carry them. (b) The author's approval of the form is the record; no summary.
- **Q-LB10. Where it lives.** (a) Profile §12B at community level only; per-character speech stays in the review-side
  voice sheets (Q-WP3 unchanged). (b) Also a one-line speech-community note in packets with local speakers, as an
  exception to Q-WP3 that needs a ruling. (c) Also a line in each local's identity context, under Q-WP3's
  first-appearance rule only.

**Applying §12B** after the answers: the profile's status header gains a dated line; `REVIEW_WATCHLIST.md` changes only if
Q-LB5 (a) or (b) is chosen; a ledger entry records it.
