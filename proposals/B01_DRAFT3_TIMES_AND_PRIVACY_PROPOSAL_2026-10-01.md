## 1. What the trace found

**Volume.** 139 passages make time-recording their subject: what is recorded, asked for, given, withheld, compared or
argued about.

| | Passages | Per 1,000 words | Recaps of established material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Act I | 22 | 0.6 | 5% |
| Act II | 57 | 1.1 | 14% |
| Act III | 60 | 1.3 | 25% |

- **It thickens as the book goes on,** and so does the share of recaps. The densest chapters are E36, E38, E48, E46 and E49.
- **About half the passages are the crew's own method.** About half of those are bookkeeping: they carry no decision, no
  test and no cost.
- **The watchers' signature** (*they want only the time*) **occurs 13 times, about 18 counting recaps.** By E46 it reads
  as a tag rather than a clue.
- **The essential occurrences:** the first statement (E20), the clerk who was asked (E25), one at the glass shop (E36),
  and the woman at E48. The trap and the stakeout work through the **address**, not the time.

**The author's reading is right on the ethics.** The trace found 11 arguments about recording people that are framed
through times. **In none of them is the time itself the private thing.** What is at stake is:
- the person's medical event;
- their name and their door;
- a file kept open;
- a stranger at a grieving family's house;
- consent;
- a professional's duty.

**E27 is the clearest case.** Caro is right to refuse, but not for the reason she gives:
- A call's time and block are often public; some cities publish them.
- **A medical call is a patient's record.** Her access to it is logged and personal.
- What could identify the woman is **the fall, the hour and the block together**, not the hour alone.

**The book also contradicts E27 itself:**
- At E36, Baz treats the time as the one safe field.
- At E42, Caro hands over a printout from which names, addresses and ages are removed and the times are kept.

That E42 printout is her change of position, and its cost is the breach of Stroud's memo. It reads better if E27's
refusal was about the record, not the clock.

## 2. A framing that fits the note and the design

**For the people and the crew, what matters is the person:** the record, the privacy, the consent. **Only the watchers
want the time.** That inversion is already the design: the watchers' interest is strange precisely because they want the
one field the people would think matters least.

So the crew's arguments should be about privacy and the record, and *the times* should be **the watchers' word**, not the
book's.
