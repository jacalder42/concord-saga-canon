## Q-SR2 — The prologue's counting presence

In the prologue, one presence keeps count and the other does not. The Graveyard study notes that this ties him to the
book's record-keeping. The author's 10-01 note was that the book dwells too much on times.

- **Recommended:** keep it. It is a near-know about the presence, in a different register from the crew's counting.
- **But this is the author's call.**

## Q-SR3 — Guidance

- **Writer profile §9, a costed line on presences:**
  - A presence's point of view carries what it chooses and what it cannot do, not what it is, its history, or how the
    phenomenon works.
  - It can be wrong, and it can miss things.
  - The more a presence says, the more human it becomes, so the strangest moments get the fewest words.
  - A name arrives in passing between them, not from the narrator.

  **Recommended: yes.** It has no absolutes and leaves the ruled limits unchanged.
- **The review watch-list, under *records as contested objects*:** keep a time where it contradicts something or is
  impossible; cut a time that only logs. **Recommended: yes**, review side only.
