## 1. The diagnosis in one paragraph

B01 already has a mystery engine. It lacks **assembly**. The book has a central question (Seraphine's: *is it only where
I am?*, Ch 7, restated at 26%, 39%, 62%, 94%), four competing explanations, a real midpoint (a false confirmation at the
repast, Ch 25, and its reversal at Ursulines, Ch 28), many tests and a solve that sits in the Ch 44 evidence. Nobody
names the question as the question, the explanations are argued against each other once (Ch 43), and the solve is in
nobody's mouth. The resonance events are all there (17 live, 17 reported); what is missing is **dread before them**:
aftermath outweighs event almost everywhere, Act II has a nine-chapter stretch with no live event (Ch 15–23), and the
climax costs less than the lot. The opposition is **one want with one scene** (Caldas speaks once, at 49%) and **a mood
with one face** (the retriever, Ch 33, who then loses his face to the stolen phone). The two hands want the same
objects at least five times and never meet on the page.

## 2. The mystery (Q-ES1–3)

**Central question:** *Is it only where I am?* **B01's definite answer:** *it keeps to places, and it does not come for
her.* It answers how it behaves, not what it is, so it sits inside every RULED guard (no certified mechanism or cause;
imperfect prediction; M51) and needs no exception. It also gives the two hands conflicting interests without naming
either: Caldas's side asks *who*; the collectors ask *where and when*. The answer says which question fits the thing.

**Three competing explanations**, each landing **aloud, once**, in a character's mouth:

| Explanation | Who holds it | Where it lands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It comes for people (the burdened; she brings it) | Seraphine; the street; Baz's rumor column | broken at the lot (Ch 40–41); **closed at Ch 44 by Baz's own column** (she was a block away when it went first) |
| It keeps to places | Lucien | already lands at Ch 28 (*not the ground*; the place survives); written at Ch 43 |
| Somebody does it (the cases) | the street | **the sting lands at Ch 33**: Seraphine draws it, angry, in the car: the cases come when called; they do not bring it |

**Clue ledger:** 20 items, 10 already exist, 9 need one line or a small act, 1 optional new. Under 1k words in all
(A §3(iii)). Two items are **fair-play repairs** (corrections, §6).

**The Veil-level question:** *Which way does it lean?* B01 plants a faint bias (the post leans toward the low sun; one
new night datum in Ch 10, with no direction word), B02 already carries the drift (E38, E40, E42), B03 already carries the

points near the camp (M51, RULED).

## 5. The path to about 150k (Q-ES11–13)

The function map (D) finds **about 13.4k words that can go without cutting muscle**:

| Tier | What | Out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pure restatement: recaps, repeated accounting, re-quoted forms (the *None* form is quoted in full four times; Poydras told three times; Carrollton told from two points of view), doubled endings, choreography | ~9.8k |
| 2 | Duplicated scenes or functions (Ch 12's re-walk of Ch 10; Ch 16's Denise scene; Ch 17's second Sal ultimatum; Ch 27's window refusal; Ch 31's triage told again in Ch 32; and others named) | ~2.3k |
| 3 | Folds: **Ch 19 into Ch 18** (same night, same point of view); Ch 43's dawn watch dissolved except the retriever's car | ~1.25k |

That lands near **159k**. The rest needs two things together:

- **Write every addition above as a replacement**, in spans the map names (the retriever's return into Ch 43's dawn
  watch; the pre-event suspense into the slow first fifth of Ch 44; Caldas's moves into the summons paragraphs and Ch 30's
  bus stop; the mystery lines into Ch 12's letter). Net zero, so the book does not regrow to ~165k.
- **Priced cuts of material that works** (Tier 4): low-price items ~2.5k (→ ~156k); with medium-price items ~6k
  (→ ~153k). High-price items (City Park, the waltz, the chair, Jody, the Vein) are not recommended.

**Honest landing: about 153–156k.** A true 150k would need medium-high items (Walt's pump-station tour, Jalen's search
on the lot), each with a named loss. The long chapters (Ch 10, 29, 44 and every chapter over 5k) **pass** the turn
test on average (one turn every 360–580 words); their slack is local and the cuts aim only there. Ch 29 is Act II's
densest chapter and should not be cut by proportion.

the result.
