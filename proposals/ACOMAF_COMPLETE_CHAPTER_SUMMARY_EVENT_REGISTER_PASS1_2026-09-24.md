These are **secondary summaries**, subject to selection and compression. No full novel, word-count locations or independent full-length coding pass is claimed. Descriptions below are short analytical paraphrases, not reproduction of chapter summaries.

**Operational event boundary:** count a change in an actor's practical options, durable relationship/role, institutional position, actionable information, resource/capability, or physical condition. Record an event once at the chapter containing its completed change; preceding setup and following receipts/costs stay attached. For a cross-chapter sequence, anchor to its resolution chapter. One primary tag per row; real events can have multiple kinds. Ongoing trauma, atmospherics, preparation without a changed option, individual blows and repeated indications of the same condition are not automatically rows. A narrative disclosure of an earlier hidden decision is tagged as a reader information reveal, **not** a new chronological event. The source is too condensed to prove that all eligible events are captured.

## Boundary decisions and sensitivity

- AM-01 (Ch. 2) and AM-05 (Ch. 7) are distinct refusals in different contexts; a text-based double-coding pass might merge the pressure trajectory while keeping the separate decisions.
- AM-07 (Ch. 10) groups Ch. 8–10's manifestation evidence into **one** capability inference rather than one event for every visual symptom. Its physical demonstrations require full-text checking.
- AM-13 (Ch. 21) groups the ring retrieval with Feyre's decision to seek combat training. AM-19 (Ch. 30) is the **later actual practice**, not a second discovery of the same ring.
- AM-22 (Ch. 37) treats Book retrieval and rescue by water-wraiths as one mission outcome with a payoff to AM-06; the individual rescue could earn its own event card under finer source evidence.
- AM-26 (Ch. 44; Starfall), AM-28 (Ch. 48; negotiated intimacy), AM-31 (Ch. 53; painting/future) and AM-34 (Ch. 56; friendship rather than fealty) are the four **most boundary-sensitive** relational/agency candidates. Excluding all four yields **41** candidate rows and changes type mix/clusters, but not the result that several middle bands carry distinct noncombat changes.
- AM-37 and AM-38 distinguish the Velaris attack from the end of the specific Attor threat; a tighter scene-level unit might merge them. AM-43 treats the sisters' forced transformations as a **single** mass intervention with two people-specific consequences.
- Chapter 68 discloses the prior High Lady installation and the intact bond. Its **reader reveal** is tracked separately; adding it as a contemporary event would move an earlier occurrence into the finale and create false end clustering. The Chapter 69 undercover return is a new action with a continuing covert role, so it remains a row.
- No candidate automatically maps to Concord Level A/B/C, no relationship tags convert into supernatural effects, and each multi-chapter row remains one source-derived candidate until novel text is checked.

## Interpretation for Concord

The five-window shorthand “ACOMAF is relationship-heavy in the middle” conceals searches, diplomatic refusals, theft, training, resource payoffs and civic danger alongside relationship changes. The signal for B07–B08 is **interleaving and causal carry-forward**: a prior small choice can pay off in a later mission; institutions can close one route while relationships open another; a home location can accumulate changed roles between set pieces. This is a diagnostic analogy, not a suggestion to copy the plot, scene count, romance frequency or ending duration.

This one-book secondary-summary count is **not comparable** to our DCC 1 opening-only pilot or to Rivers' whole-book synopsis. Before plotting peer means or comparing to Concord's 48 B01 episodes and unslotted Loom pool, use equally granular verified sources, a second coding pass, and real word/page positions. Preserve the current B01 no-combat, no premature reveal, and locked order constraints. Do not slot Loom events from this register.
