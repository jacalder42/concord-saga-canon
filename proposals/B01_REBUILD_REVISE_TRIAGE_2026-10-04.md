# B01: rebuild or revise, chapter by chapter — triage proposal

Status: PROPOSAL, 2026-10-04. It applies the approved decision rule (ledger §400, §403) to all 51 B01 pieces. The full
table, rubric and evidence are private, in manuscript `draft-notes/rr-test/TRIAGE.md`. No manuscript text is quoted.

**It does not change** any prose, packet or ruling. **Q-TR1–9 await the author.**

## 1. The rubric, from the test

**Rebuild** wins when about half or more of a chapter is the leads delivering disclosure or method to one another, and
the repaired packet adds a lot. E35's repair added about 36% to the chapter; E46 gained five beats at an unchanged
estimate and carried a scar span.

**Revise** wins where secondary characters with their own errands own the scene and a strong set piece already exists.
E42 has the Dorseys, the supervisor and the wordless chair.

Neither commit counts nor how much draft 3 rewrote decided it; they only break ties. Several of the most-reworked
chapters are among the best staged. Length explains nothing.

**Caveat.** Three chapters is a small test, and the model judges split on two. *Revise the healthy ones* is safe, not
proven better.

## 3. Order of work

1. **Rebuilds, in reading order:** E26 → E35 → E46 → E48. Each is followed by a continuity check against the chapters
   that depend on it.
2. **Revisions, in reading order, per the intervention map.** KEEP chapters get continuity adjustments only.
3. **The book-wide line pass, last** (U5's exemptions apply).
4. **The author's read.**

**Fixes before the test winners enter the draft:**
- E35 B: the redundant levee paragraph; the ferry/table placement that E36 refers back to; a mirrored line the play
  audit pairs with E36.
- E46 B: the counting habit; Inez's glasses against E47's handwriting; one unplanted callback; a wink; a stock phrase;
  two protected lines restored; Seraphine urging people to stay, from version A.
- E42 A: Caro's block name (the continuity rule says she never names the street); a question she couldn't know to ask;
  a small prop slip; the ending line.
