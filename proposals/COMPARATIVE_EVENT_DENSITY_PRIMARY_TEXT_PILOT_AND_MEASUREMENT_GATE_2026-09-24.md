## What “industry standard” can and cannot establish

- Romance Writers of America defines genre romance by a central love story and an emotionally satisfying, optimistic resolution: https://www.rwa.org/proof-of-serious-pursuit . This is relevant only if an individual Concord book is marketed as genre Romance; fantasy with a romance strand need not claim that same per-book promise.
- Reedsy editors' 2026 novel-length guidance gives fantasy/SF about 100k–115k words and romance about 80k–100k, with exceptions: https://reedsy.com/studio/resources/how-many-words-in-a-novel/ . Word length is **not** an event quota, and projected episode length is not manuscript length.
- A serialized-fiction author describes avoiding long runs of sameness in her own release model: https://press.barnesandnoble.com/bnpress-blog/guest-post-sarra-cannon-on-writing-a-serial/ . Her every-episode-twist practice and 16k-word episodes are not normative for Concord's shorter planned episodes.
- A fantasy author explicitly advocates variable pacing and consequence/reflection between activity peaks: https://michael-j-sullivan.com/writing-advice-9-pacing . This is craft counsel, not a distribution percentile.
- Historical-novel corpus research on relational arcs finds genre-distinct timing for alliances, romance, rivalries and authority: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/computational-humanities-research/article/relational-arcs-as-narrative-structure-dynamics-distribution-and-diachronic-change-in-fiction/37947D31A93A7FA276BC3E6BA67C5DFD . Its relationship annotations across 96 novels from 1850–1950 are **not** the Concord causal-event unit and cannot calibrate our numerical count.

**Bottom line:** the checked trade and research sources support reader promises, varying tempo and relational consequence. They do not establish a universal number of A/B/C events per book, episode or 10,000 words.

## Proposed measurement protocol, before any numerical recommendation

1. **Texts / provenance.** Obtain authorized full prose or author-supplied sufficiently detailed scene notes for three unlike comparators (DCC1 external-incident, ACOMAF relational/agency, Rivers investigation/place). Record edition, word count, provenance and coverage. A publisher excerpt may calibrate a rule but cannot join a whole-book denominator. If full licensed text cannot be made available for analysis, stop at qualitative analogies; do not assemble unauthorized full text.
2. **Unit.** One independently carried before → action/discovery → changed state in options, condition, role, relationship, resources, capability, institution or actionable information. Keep secondary tags without double-counting. Mere mentions, blows, atmospherics, repeated symptoms, setup and receipt are not additional rows without a separate later constraint/choice.
3. **Boundary checks.** Double-code at least openings and one quieter middle window independently; reconcile split/merge disputes (e.g., DC-01 and the chapter-1 announcement; DCC ch.4 economics; ACOMAF Starfall, painting and bonds). Retain an alternate low/high *coding sensitivity* register, never present it as a statistical confidence interval on true events.
4. **Position.** Use actual word offsets in the same edition. Report candidates per 10,000 words *only after* complete comparable coding. Also report type mix, clustering by prose decile, longest low-change run in words, causal carry-forward and aftermath lag. Keep reader reward, pressure, set-piece intensity and A/B/C event scope distinct.
5. **Concord comparison.** Only then code B1's 48 narrative episodes from the approved integrated beat bible as **planned turns**, with uncertainty on unscripted prose; compare to source books qualitatively first. Do not equate an episode with an event or count the six supplements as six narrative turns. For Loom, assess each book's independent local changes and carried costs instead of evenly filling bins or spreading B9's endpoint across B7–B8.
