## 1. The verdict

**B01 is two novels sharing 145,000 words.** All three designers reached this independently, in nearly the same words.

**The first novel is a 4.5-star literary novel about a record.** A caseworker left a renewal on her desk for three
weeks. She felt something in the room where a child died, and she will not take either fact off his file. It has a
spine: the blue folder, from Ch 1 to Renée's kitchen in Ch 37. Every panel reader felt it, and four named Ch 37 the best
chapter.

**The second novel is a 3-star speculative investigation that runs beside the first and seldom touches it.** It has no
spine, only a sequence. An event is followed by tending, a curb debrief, a line on Lucien's board and food, about eleven
times. Its failures are structural, not line-level:

1. **There is no antagonist, only a weather system of askers.** About nine figures share one function: someone polite
   arrives, asks for paper, is refused and leaves. Caldas, the face who matters, speaks twice before the climax.
   Seraphine first speaks to any agent at about 66%. The most frightening scene, oxygen offered for her notes, belongs
   to an unnamed woman.
2. **The opposition wants nothing Seraphine is struggling over.** It wants "who had it first", a log and Baz. Her
   struggle is over the Batiste file. The two touch once (Ch 18) and part for twenty chapters (Design B's key
   finding).
3. **The reader holds no genre question to play for.** The rules are good, but no stake is attached to them. The one
   clue with real voltage, the glass notice that came before any glass, is raised twice and never answered.
4. **The emotional summit comes seven chapters early, and the genre word comes last.** Renée's kitchen (Ch 37) sits
   before the lot. The book then climbs a second slope that peaks on *the Veil*, a word no reader was asked to want: no
   panel reader registered the Ch 25 tab plant.
5. **The genre plot pauses rather than resolves.** Seraphine's arc closes, while the opposition neither wins nor loses
   anything.
6. **About 95 named people and a set of orphans.** Names collide (Renée, Renita, R., Ren) and threads are planted and
   dropped.

**What 4.5 takes, in one sentence:** make the second novel serve the first. The opposition should want the record of
Dré's death and the person who felt it first, so that the antagonist's ladder and Seraphine's guilt are the same rope.
Cut to about 115–120k on that spine.

- **Baz:**
  - he arrives at Act II as a POV;
  - he is former Dominion and knows *that*, not *how*;

## 3. Where they diverge (the real decisions)

**D1. Where Renée's kitchen sits.**
- **A and C:** before the lot, as now. The confession is free.
- **B:** the climactic weekend. On Friday Caldas, who has obtained the renewal from the center's own Batiste file, offers
  a deal: her paragraph and log, and his office's Monday letter to the family need not mention the folder. On Sunday
  Seraphine tells Renée everything, the offer included, and offers to withdraw the paragraph. Renée says *"Put it in."*
  The secret can no longer be used.
- **Trade-off:** B fuses the two novels and moves the best chapter to the end. Its risk is that the confession reads as
  forced.

**D2. What the genre plot conclusively answers.**
- **A:** a records request returns the advisory memo. The city was advised to set up a reporting line and **no
  warning**, citing precedent sites including Peterhead. It is filed with the coroner, and Seraphine is suspended.
- **C:** the water in the tins warns a gallery without anyone feeling it first, so Caldas's "first one" is worthless. The
  glass notice is traced lightly to "an advisory body's model".
- **B:** minimal. Caldas's leverage dies with the confession, and the phenomenon stays open.

**D3. Lucien's point of view.**
- **A and B:** kept throughout.
- **C:** his point of view goes dark at Easter, after he reads the tab and receives an order for "anything the witness
  keeps". It returns once, in a new final chapter, where he gives her his own written account.
- **Trade-off:** C adds suspense but costs the back half of a future guide's interior. It needs five POV conversions,
  the most expensive change in any design.

**D4. Seraphine's first meeting with Caldas.**
- **A:** at the funeral, at a distance (about 15%); in person at about 58%.
- **B:** after the Square fall (about 27%). She gives him her name and address by reflex, and that pays at the festival.
- **C:** at her car after Lorraine's (about 41%).

**D5. The frame pieces.**
- **The Interlude:** cut in A and C; kept in B, placed after Ursulines.
- **The Inez supplement:** replaced by the memo in A; cut in C; kept in B, where it pays because Mrs. Batson is written
  back in.
- **A second Transmission:** A only.

**D6. Seraphine's job at the end.**
- **A:** suspended.
- **B and C:** a written warning, removal as Mr. Vidrine's agent, and an honest closure that Denise will read.

**D7. The cream-shoes woman.** Every design folds her into Caldas. All three name the loss: she is the draft's most
original menace, and the book's only female antagonist. One alternative no designer took is worth naming: keep her, and
make her the single face. That would cost Caldas's approved Veil-wide role (B02 E27, B03), so it is high cost.

## 4. The recommended integration

**The spine is B's, with C's want and A's public climax.**

- **Opposition.** Caldas is the one face, from Q-SN2–4.
  - **His want (from C):** *the first one*: the earliest account of the phenomenon and the person who felt it before
    anyone else. On paper that person is Seraphine. Her honest paragraph in Dré's file puts her name first, and her log
    (*what I felt, my words*) is the object he wants.
  - **The irony this gives the book:** her honesty exposes her, and Lucien's *None* hides the only other witness.
- **First meeting (B, Ch 10).** Seraphine meets him after the Square fall and gives him her name and address. That beat
  bookends the festival.
- **Leverage (B):** the renewal, obtained from the center's own file, which the Review has legitimately requested.
- **Climax (A and C):** the festival.
  - The tins warn the gallery without anyone feeling it first, so the want is defeated (C).
  - The gallery refuses him names, and Baz stays (all three).
  - Seraphine leaves her post for Jody and gives up the onset (all three).
- **Renée's kitchen (D1).** Move it to the final weekend, after the Friday offer (B), **subject to a blind test against
  the current Ch 37**. If the test fails, keep it before the lot, with the Friday offer as the trigger for the festival
  instead.
- **The glass notice.** Pay it lightly, traced to "an advisory model" that saw glass coming (C). A's memo is held as an
  option. It is stronger genre, but riskier as a convenience, and its Peterhead precedent touches saga geography.
- **Lucien (D3).** Keep his point of view throughout (A and B). Move his employer admission earlier, to the Friday night.
  - **The closing beat (C's gift, B's staging):** at the foot of her stair he gives her his own written account, *what I
    felt, my words*. It is the inverse of *None*.
  - She corroborates his correction (*"Tell Guidry the witness says so"*).
  - She ends apart from him by choice.
- **The *Veil* word.** At Easter, Seraphine herself sees the folder tab, and Baz takes it back (A). Baz says the word at
  the festival, tied to *people who feel it first*, and human chapters follow it (B). The last words of the book belong
  to Renée, Lucien and Miss Hazel, then the watchers.
- **Frame.**
  - Prologue and Epilogue: kept, each trimmed by about a third.
  - The Transmission: kept.
  - The Interlude: cut.
  - The Inez supplement: kept, shorter, with Mrs. Batson written back in (B).
- **Seraphine's job:** a written warning, removal as Mr. Vidrine's agent, and an honest closure under review (B and C).
  B02 inherits it.
- **Cast:** about 45–55 named people.
  - Merges: Mrs. Whitlow into Miss Hazel, and the cream-shoes woman into Caldas.
  - Renames: Renita and R.; the author picks the new names.
  - Paid in a line each: Keon and Mrs. Batson.
  - Cut: the mistral, Peterhead, Duplessis and the backward-badge youth.
- **Length:** about 116–120k, in three acts of roughly 30k, 54k and 33k.
- **Standing revision rules (no quotas):**
  - an event chapter does not end on a board line if the previous one did;
  - food stays only where it is an act between two people;
  - the board aphorism appears three times in the book;
  - calendar anchors go in first lines through the ten-day window and Act III.

**What it costs.**
- **The cream-shoes woman as her own person.** About 25–30k words of texture go: the goose afternoon, the glass shop,
  Palm Sunday, the Poydras lobby, and many good neighbors.
- **Ambiguity.** The opposition becomes legible, and some of its institutional diffusion is lost.
- **Protected items.** Several protected items are reopened.
- **Prose survival.** On the designers' estimates, about 70–80% of the new book is existing prose. The rest is
  re-staged (the merged Caldas scenes, the moved kitchen) or new (about 4–6k: the Ch 10 meeting, the Friday offer, the
  stair).
- **Downstream:**
  - B02/B03 packets need a note that Seraphine met Caldas in B01 and that the woman is folded into him;
  - the R. rename needs a check against the B02/B03 packets;
  - the existing 10-04→10-12 packet debt is still owed.

## 5. How to test it before any rewrite

1. **Paper test.** Give a two-page synopsis of the integrated outline to two fresh blind readers (the casual and
   urban-fantasy profiles). At the Ch 22 line, ask: who is the opposition, what does he want, and what do you want to
   happen next? **Pass:** both name Caldas and "the first one" or her log, unprompted.
2. **Three sample chapters,** each drafted from a revised packet and compared blind against the current chapters:
   - **Caldas on Mr. Vidrine's step** (the merged face): does he frighten, and does he read as a person?
   - **Renée's kitchen in its new position, with the Friday offer before it:** is Seraphine choosing or being forced? Is
     *"Put it in"* earned?
   - **The festival climax:** did the protagonists act, win and pay?
3. **If the tests pass:** build the integrated outline, then packets (the redraft's only source), then the revision into
   a new folder, with the br draft kept as the checkpoint. Then the standing checks, and the author's read.
