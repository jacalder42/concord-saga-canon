## 2. What the 2026-09-26 answers do to the earlier reconstruction

### They fill the gap mechanism A left open

The 09-19 recovery's leading candidate was **A + B**: authorities contain rather than evacuate,
and retrieval agents obstruct egress. Its own weakness was stated plainly: *"exact delay not
recovered."* It explained why people were **not evacuated**. It did not explain why **so many
people were there**.

**The author's hypothesis answers exactly that.** The post draws people in (link 4); the
perimeter and retrieval slow their exit (links 5–6); the Rupture outruns the model (link 8).
**These are compatible and additive, not competing.** Each supplies a different part of *"it
taking too long for Baz to evacuate the location."* Taken together they satisfy the 09-19
constraint that *"no individual needs to murder him."*

**Compatible is not decided.** Whether mechanisms A and B both happen is still open. The
card shows they fit alongside the ruling; it does not adopt them.

### They redirect the old "Threadnaut relationship" hypothesis

The 09-19 recovery proposed that Tahl's *"publication may force institutions to move sooner,
visibly, or more aggressively"* — MT's effect running **through the institutions**. The author's
stated consequence runs **through the public**: people come. The two can both be true. But the
**ruled** part is the post itself, and the **hedged** part points at the crowd, not at
institutional reaction. The earlier framing is kept as an alternative, not as the lead.

The recovery's best line survives intact and is sharper for it:
**"MT was right about the danger, but being right changed the board."** The coordinates were
accurate, and that accuracy is what drew the crowd.

### They interlock with the epilogue reveal

**This is the card's main structural finding, and it follows from two rulings, not from
invention.** The B03 reconciliation map already stages the epilogue as *"anonymous reporter
processes Warehouse … writes/publishes → reveal: Tahl Morgan."* That map has **one**
publication, **after** the Warehouse.

**Ruling #2 implies an earlier one.** To draw people to the site, the coordinates post must come
**before the Rupture**, in Act III. Ruling #1 makes that post **anonymous** when it appears.
So:

- **Act III:** an anonymous post gives the coordinates. The reader may see it. Nobody on the
  page knows whose it is.
- **Epilogue:** the voice is revealed as Tahl. The reader now knows who posted the
  coordinates that helped fill the site.
- **B4:** Tahl's remorse opens his arc.

**The reveal and the remorse become causally tight rather than adjacent.** That structure is
the author's, arising from his two answers.

**[P] Proposal: the B03 map needs an Act III posting beat**, anonymous, which the epilogue then
attributes. Whether the reader sees the post on the page, only its effect, or learns of it
only in the epilogue is the author's staging choice. Each option changes how the reveal lands.

## 5. What would move the hypotheses to rulings

**Link 4, the crowd:** does the post reach people who can physically come? Who comes? Onlookers,
the curious, people looking for someone, Filament responders? Does the crowd appear on the page
or only in aftermath reports? Does the scene show the post causing the arrival, or does the reader
infer it?

**Link 9, the delay:** what does Baz actually do? Does he try to move the crowd out, and does
someone or something slow him: the perimeter, retrieval agents, the crowd itself? The child's
presence must be explained by the same answer.

**Link 7, the antagonist:** which one. The recovered candidates are **Technarc retrieval (Rook &
Harlow)** and **Dominion's Caldas Ren at the perimeter.** The 09-19 recovery says neither
*"causes the Rupture or directly kills Baz."* An intervention that **delays** fits both the
recollection and that boundary.

**Link 2, a detail the ruling leaves open:** did Tahl know Baz was investigating? The ruling
says the post was of *"the event Baz was investigating"*. That need not mean Tahl knew Baz was
there. **Whether he knew is what his remorse is about**, and it is the author's to decide.
**Answered 2026-09-26: he did not know** ([author answers](../decisions/B03_WAREHOUSE_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md)).

## 6. For the author — answered 2026-09-26

The four questions below were answered the same day. The answers and their scope are in the
[author answers](../decisions/B03_WAREHOUSE_AUTHOR_RULING_2026-09-26.md). In short:

1. **The Act III post:** on the page, visible only through its effect, or learned only in the
   epilogue? → **A visible supplemental text or effect-only; the author is open between those
   two.** The aftermath is visible in the epilogue, hedged: Tahl reads reports of unexpected
   bystanders hampering efforts and a first responder dying (examples, *"or etc."*). Whether
   that responder is Baz, unnamed, or a second death is recorded both ways.
2. **Did Tahl know Baz was there?** → **RULED: no.** He may not learn Baz's name until much
   later (hedged).
3. **Link 7:** Technarc retrieval, Dominion at the perimeter, both, or someone else? →
   **DELEGATED.** Working assumption: Dominion **expected** at the perimeter, Technarc **not
   expected**, and Technarc is the intervening antagonist.
4. **Mechanisms A and B:** keep both alongside the crowd, one, or neither? → **DELEGATED.**
   Working assumption: **both**, alongside the crowd. Mechanism C stays off.

The delegated answers are PROPOSALS the author can override, not rulings. **None blocks the B6
and B8 cards,** which come next.
