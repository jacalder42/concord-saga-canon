### 2.1 The hazard the ruling could not have addressed

Both are **New Orleans** characters. PASS2 §221–224 puts *"visiting Mara Niht"* in the
Velvet Vein web and `Mara/M` in the Filament web — **different webs, one city, and the
Velvet Vein is a public venue a community organizer can walk into.**

**This is not yet a live problem.** All 15 `Mara` mentions in v4.1b are A01; Mara Niht
appears nowhere in B01, and her **first appearance is on the `DO NOT LOCK` list**. So the
collision becomes real at the moment she is first placed, and not before.

**That makes now the cheapest possible moment to settle it** — a naming decision taken
before placement costs nothing; taken after, it edits scenes.

### 2.2 [P] Recommendation — a register rule, not a rename

The recovered material already separates them without any name change:

- **The Filament is `Mara` in neighborhood and civic registers, `M` inside Filament
  channels.** This is the source's own distinction [R], not an invention, and it means her
  two appearances read differently by context rather than by disambiguation.
- **The musician is `Mara Niht` in full on every reader-facing use.** Never bare `Mara`.
- **They do not share a scene** until the author decides otherwise.

**[AUTHOR] to confirm or replace.** The alternatives, for completeness: retire one
reader-facing name (costly — both are ruled protected), or give the Filament a different
given name (cheap today, since her name is *"Mara"* with no surname recovered [R], but it
discards a recovered attribute).

**What this does not touch:** her E08 assignment, her Life Packet, her episode functions,
or any v4.1b beat. A register rule is a prose convention, not a structural change.
