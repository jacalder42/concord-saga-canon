## 3. The rules as recovered

### 3.1 The author's own words

1. **Keep recognisable phrases that are not kitschy:** greetings, goodbyes, affectations, insults, exhalations, rituals; **place names**. (11-11)
2. **The balance is "tricky" and "easy to trip on";** editors and language experts should weigh in. (11-11)
3. **Local, not tourist.** Respect the Cajun/Creole and Castilian/Catalan distinctions and do the due diligence. *"Concord is not Disney World."* (11-10)
4. **No one is a caricature.** (11-15)
5. **Readers should "get" jargon immediately.** (11-30)
6. **Name orthography matters:** the accented Icelandic forms. (11-17)
7. **Names have native and modified forms** worth a system. (12-05)
8. **Italics or dashes** for signal words, and a prose-mechanics style guide before prose. (11-30)

### 3.2 Assistant-proposed, accepted in the chat

The Lexicon Protocol (11-11) is the most complete set:

| Rule | Content |
| --- | --- |
| LR-1 | Keep a word whose meaning is clear from action or reply; no gloss needed |
| LR-2 | First use gets a brief inline gloss; never a footnote |
| LR-3 | At most about 5 per chapter on average, 7 in ritual scenes |
| LR-4 | Choose words whose sound carries the feeling; drop those that stall rhythm |
| LR-5 | Genuine dialect only; no invented blends |
| LR-6 | Ritual lines stay untranslated; their meaning comes through description |
| LR-7 | Jokes and slang are translated, or followed by a reaction that carries the meaning |
| LR-8 | Place and proper names in native spelling (*Reykjavík*) |
| LR-9 | Italics on first use only |
| LR-10 | Point of view sets the translation level: a native speaks freely, an outsider hears a paraphrase |

- **Keep:** greetings and farewells; affectations and exclamations; insults (with a cue); rituals; place names; short honorifics; iconic food.
- **Translate:** slang and jokes; whole sentences and proverbs; anything whose *meaning* carries plot. The headline: *keep foreign words when their sound carries feeling; translate when their meaning carries plot.*
- **Later refinements (CR-Lang, 11-11):** italics once per chapter; at most one untranslated word per scene without a context cue.
- **Consistent across every source:** **no phonetic spelling of accents**. Show cadence, register and idiom instead.
- **Translation bible (11-30):** one word a character keeps, used as a tell, rather than a lexicon.

**Context matters.** The Protocol was written for an earlier world: global "City Nodes" (Istanbul, Cairo, Kyoto, Lagos), invented greetings and resonance slang. Its invented-slang tiers conflict with profile §9 (ordinary words in Veil). Its real-language rules carry over.

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

1. **Gap: no language rule in the stack,** though the author expected a prose-mechanics guide before prose (11-30).
2. **Density figures disagree** (all assistant): about 10% of Seraphine's dialogue (11-10); 3–5 per chapter (LR-3); at most one uncued word per scene (CR-Lang-03). Under C3 rule 4 the later 11-11 figures would govern. The profile's no-quota stance argues for a watch item, not a target.
3. **Italics disagree:** every foreign phrase (11-02); first use only (LR-9); first use per chapter (CR-Lang-02); and the board's "don't flag regional words as alien". The stack has no rule. The redraft italicises its one foreign word and leaves local terms roman. **The author has not ruled on italics for foreign words**; his only words on italics concern signal words.
4. **Glossing:** LR-1 and LR-2 fit together. The later "never via footnote" overrules the 11-02 footnote.
5. **Seraphine's register has two readings** (§4, V1 against V2). Her origin (the author's Option C) governs; the language was never set for it. C3 rule 5 applies: record both and ask.
6. **Lucien:** §12 covers vocabulary, not the German-under-stress tell or his father's French.
7. **Elisabet's spelling:** the author's 11-17 concern and LR-8 point to *Elísabet Arnardóttir*. The repository uses *Elisabet* (registry: *Arnardottir*). The redraft mixes the two forms.
8. **Lost in distillation:** the 12-09 layers and the 12-05 naming system were approved in their chats but never committed. They are recovered approved design, not canon.
9. **Audio:** no source links foreign words to audiobook or ElevenReader delivery, beyond a lexicon pronunciation guide (11-11).
10. **Authenticity:** the author's due-diligence directive (11-10) has no pipeline step. The recovered endearments are unchecked by native speakers.

## 7. Questions for the author

**Q-FL1. Should a compact "Language and register" section join the writer profile (as §12A)?**
*Recommended: yes, about six lines*, from §3.1 and the stable part of §3.2:
- keep greetings, goodbyes, exclamations, insults, exhalations, rituals and place names;
- translate jokes, sentences, and anything whose meaning carries plot;
- meaning comes from context or a reply, never a glossary or footnote;
- no phonetic spelling of accents;
- point of view sets what is understood;
- sparing, and never kitschy or touristic.

**Q-FL2. Density.**
*Recommended:* a **review-side watch item** (`ebci/prose/REVIEW_WATCHLIST.md`) of roughly one such word per scene and a handful per chapter. It is not a drafting quota, in keeping with the profile's no-scheduling stance.

**Q-FL3. Italics for non-English words.**
*Recommended: roman type* for words in a speaker's own language and for local terms; no first-use italics. This departs from LR-9 and CR-Lang-02, which he accepted in 2025, so it needs his ruling. Alternative: italics on first use per book.

**Q-FL4. Seraphine's language.**
*Recommended:* a few words of her family's Louisiana French (forms of address, exclamations), heard more under pressure and carried by cadence, never spelled phonetically. **He decides** which family strand they come from (V1, V2 or both).

**Q-FL5. Per-character tells.**
*Recommended:* carry the §4 tells into identity context as **permissions, not quotas**:
- Lucien: German under stress, French in intimacy;
- Caro: Spanish for affection, frustration and humour;
- Elisabet: Icelandic in panic or tenderness;
- Rex: Singapore rhythm with people he trusts.

Baz's tell is the author's call (French, Arabic, both or neither).

**Q-FL6. The 12-05 naming system** (native forms signal vulnerability; the intimacy names).
*Recommended:* keep it as recovered design. Use a name only when a scene needs it, after a native-speaker check. Its surnames are stale.

**Q-FL7. Elísabet's spelling.**
*Recommended:* native orthography in prose (*Elísabet Arnardóttir*, *Reykjavík*), with the unaccented form kept as a registry alias. This is a naming ruling for him.

**Q-FL8. Authenticity and audio.**
*Recommended:*
- a per-book list of every non-English word, with meaning and pronunciation;
- a native-speaker or sensitivity read of those lines at copyedit.

The same list serves an audiobook or ElevenReader pass.
