whole trilogy, and it includes when a principal character dies.**

## 2. The sharpest evidence: Baz

**Notion, `B3.A3.E13`:** "Baz is crushed but uses his last breath to pull a child free.
Lucien arrives too late." E14: Baz's final words, *"You… don't have to be alone."* The
book's end state records **"Baz: Dead (heroic, foundational)"**, and `01.01` Master
Summary confirms "Baz dies in Book 3."

**The exports, Book 3 epilogue shell `S1.T1.B3.EP.E03`:** "Commitment to Truth — **Baz**,
the Filaments, and Tahl agree to keep recording and reporting."

**Baz is alive at the end of Book 3 in one draft and dead in the other.** That is not a
merge conflict. It is two different stories.

## 3. Other structural divergences

- **Tahl.** Notion: founds MT in `B1.A2.E7`, brushes VT in `B2.A3.E11`, near-slip in
  `B3.A2.E9`. Export: does not appear in Book 1 or Book 2 at all, "enters as

- **Lucien.** Notion: remote analyst → arrives in NOLA in Book 2 → refuses the Dominion
  → broken by Baz's death. Export: a repeating "misalignment #1–#4" mechanic that Notion
  never uses, with no arrival beat.
- **The Veil's failure mode.** Notion escalates Shard → Ghostwave → Fracture → Rupture.
  The export escalates pulse → corridor fragility → drift → southwest → the Slip. The
  export's vocabulary is corridor-and-drift; Notion's is shard-and-rupture.
- **Geography.** The export drives toward **Santa Fe** from Book 2 onward. Notion stays
  in New Orleans and Vienna for the whole trilogy.

## 4. A third layer, and a correction to the ledger

The `Episode expansion process` conversation pasted the **export** bible as its input,
then expanded from it — so the lineage is export → expansion → packets, and Notion sits
outside it.

But the expansion also diverged from what it was given. It planned
**"~22–24 episode titles for Act I"** of Book 1 alone, and its `E16` is
"ACT I CLOSE (PART IV) — Everything Curves Southwest" — a line that in the export bible
belongs to **Book 3's** Act I close.

**This corrects a framing the recovery ledger carries.** "E00–E18" is not Book 1. It is
**Book 1 Act I (E00–E16) plus the first two episodes of Act II**. At 22–24 episodes per
act, Book 1 runs to roughly 70 episodes in the expansion — against Notion's 15 for the
whole book.

So there are three layers, not two: Notion's draft, the exports' reconstructed bible,
and an expansion that follows the second but re-plans as it goes.

## 6. How I read the evidence, without ruling

**For the export line:** it is later, every piece of episode-level work descends from
it, and under the standing precedence rule later recovered Beat Bible material outranks
Notion.

**For Notion:** it is dramatically richer — named scenes, emotional beats, a death with
last words — where the export is a systems progression of overloads and misalignments.
It is labelled Final Beat Bible in each book's own folder. The export is labelled
"Final Canon-**Reconstructed**", which is precisely the provenance the tier system exists
to distrust, and the expansion's own drift from it inside one session shows how loosely
it was held.

A reconstruction that lost Baz's death, Tahl's founding of MT, and an entire romance is
more plausibly a lossy rebuild than a deliberate revision.

**But that is a reading, and the choice is yours.** It decides what the Veil trilogy is.

## 7. The ruling needed

1. **Which draft is canon for the Veil trilogy** — Notion's Final Beat Bibles, the

2. If Notion: does the E00–E16 expansion survive as an expansion of Notion's acts, or is

Until this is settled, migrating any Veil act overlay writes one of two stories into

# The Veil Spine — Rulings and What They Resolve

**Ruled by:** James, 2026-09-19 · **Status:** rulings binding; §4 and §5 open

> Tahl isn't named until Book 3, but MT can be visible before that — possibly B2.
> Caro–Elisabet is important and canon.
> Baz does not work directly with Tahl. Tahl accidentally exposes the incident that Baz
> gets killed at, and his remorse in B4 is his protagonist arc beginning.
> Open on the exact timing of Baz's death; the epilogue sounds like as good a place to
> start as anywhere.

**Neither draft wins.** Each ruling cuts across both, and two of them introduce material
that exists in neither.

---

## 1. What each ruling settles

| Ruling | Notion draft | Export draft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tahl unnamed until B3 | **wrong** — Tahl has named POV episodes in B1 and B2 | **right** — he first appears in `B3.A1` as "proto-investigator" |
| MT visible from ~B2 | **wrong on timing** — MT founded in `B1.A2.E7` | **wrong on timing** — "MT prototype" not until `B3.A2` |
| Caro–Elisabet canon | **right** — a full arc across all three books | **wrong** — neither character appears in its act beats |
| Baz never works directly with Tahl | mostly right — "Baz finds Tahl's work compelling", no collaboration | **wrong** — epilogue shell `EP.E03` has "Baz, the Filaments, and Tahl agree to keep recording and reporting" |
| Tahl's exposure causes Baz's death | absent — Tahl records the Warehouse Incident but does not cause it | absent |
| Tahl's remorse begins his B4 arc | absent — Notion's `B4.A1.E1` opens with Tahl confidently publishing, nicknamed "Threadnaut" | n/a |

**A third source agrees with the ruling on Tahl.** The recovered `B3.A3.E14` packet is
anchored **"FIRST AND ONLY VT BRUSH IN VEIL TRILOGY"**. Notion has two — a brush in
`B2.A3.E11` and a near-slip in `B3.A2.E9`. With Tahl unnamed until Book 3, both of
Notion's early VT events have to go, and the single Book 3 slip stands. The export's act
title for `B3.A3` is literally **"The Slip"**.

---

## 2. The causal chain this creates

Neither draft has this, and it is the most consequential thing in the rulings:

**Tahl's exposure → the incident → Baz's death → Tahl's remorse → his protagonist arc.**

Notion has all the pieces and none of the wiring. In `B3.A2.E9` Tahl "tries to
triangulate the epicenter of the instability"; in `B3.A3.E12` he "records everything,
tears running down his face" while Baz runs into the warehouse. Turning that recording
into *causing* — his triangulation is what draws people to the site — costs one beat and
gives the Veil trilogy its moral engine.

It also fixes Book 4. Notion's `B4.A1.E1` has Tahl publishing a predictive map with no
guilt attached, already a public figure. Under the ruling that episode is where the
remorse lives, and the map is what he does with it.

---

## 3. What this does to Baz's death

Notion places it at `B3.A3.E13` — crushed pulling a child free, Lucien arriving too
late, last words *"You… don't have to be alone."* You are open on timing and suggested
the epilogue.

Moving it there has a specific payoff. Book 3's epilogue is titled **"The First Quiet"**
in Notion — which reads as the silence after a death far better than as a general
aftermath. And the export's epilogue shells run: Tahl after the slip, exhaustion and
clarity · global tension montage · commitment to truth · **"First MT-Style Line — Tahl
drafts the first sentence that resembles his future MT voice."**

If Baz dies in that epilogue and Tahl caused it, then **Tahl's MT voice is born out of
remorse**, in the same four episodes. That is a stronger origin than the one either
draft has, and it uses beats that already exist.

The cost: `EP.E03` as written has Baz alive and collaborating with Tahl, which two
rulings now contradict. That shell needs rewriting whichever way the timing goes.

---

## 4. The MT question this opens

`01.01` Master Summary: "**MT (Missing Thread): Public mortal channel founded by
Tahl.**" If MT is visible from Book 2 and Tahl is not named until Book 3, then either:

- **MT is anonymous in Book 2** and Tahl is revealed as its author in Book 3 — a
  reveal structure, and a good one; or
- **MT exists before Tahl**, and he joins or takes it over — which contradicts "founded
  by Tahl"; or
- **MT in Book 2 is unattributed reportage** that becomes MT proper once Tahl is named.

The first fits everything else you have ruled. But it means the reader meets the
publication before the person, which is a deliberate structural choice worth making
explicitly rather than by default.

---

3. **What survives of Notion's Books 1–2 Tahl episodes** — `B1.A2.E7` "Origin of MT",

4. **Does the Caro–Elisabet arc keep Notion's episode placements** — the Unwanted

5. **The act structure itself is still unresolved.** These rulings settle character and

# The Tahl Reveal — Architecture and Consequences

**Ruled by:** James, 2026-09-19 — the anonymous-then-revealed reading matches intent;
his reveal as MT's author, *and perhaps his name*, may be held until **B4**.

---

## 1. What the reader experiences

| Book | MT | Tahl |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | absent | absent |
| B2 | appears — anonymous posts, unattributed reportage | unseen |
| B3 | established, gaining traction | present but unnamed; he slips into VT; **his exposure causes the incident that kills Baz** |
| B4 | the voice acquires a face | **named, revealed as MT's author, and the remorse arc opens** |

**The reveal does double duty.** By the time the reader learns his name, they have
already mourned Baz — and they learn, in the same book, that the man they are meeting
is the one who caused it. Neither draft had that. It falls straight out of holding the
name to B4.

Notion's `B4.A1.E1` is already the natural landing point. It is titled "The Map No One
Wants", Tahl publishes his resonance-lattice map, Chronicle attacks him, and the
Filaments start calling him "Threadnaut". Adding the reveal and the remorse gives that
episode three jobs it can carry.

---

## 2. A three-stage naming ladder

Notion puts "Threadnaut" in B4. Moving it earlier gives a clean progression:

1. **B2 — the byline.** MT posts appear with no author. The reader knows a voice, not a
   person.
2. **B3 — the nickname.** Filaments call the unknown author *Threadnaut*. He becomes a
   figure without being a man. This is also when he slips into VT and, unknowingly,
   causes Baz's death.
3. **B4 — the name.** *Tahl Morgan*. Byline, nickname and person collapse into one, and
   the guilt arrives with them.

Each stage is cheap to write and each is a reveal.

---

## 3. The metaphysicals know him first

Worth noticing, because it is already canon and it now reads as deliberate.

`08.10 Silence`: "Fascinated by Tahl's disciplined emotional patterns… First brushes
with Tahl disrupt behavioural loops." `08.11 Hope`: "Gains her first flicker of agency
through Tahl… Tahl's warmth becomes a catalyst for change."

**Silence and Hope recognise him while the reader still has only a byline.** Two
constructs built without agency learn a human's emotional signature before humanity
learns his name. That is the prologue's promise paying off — Silence noticing "a
singular cluster of resonance in Louisiana that burns warm" long before anyone is
introduced.

---

## 4. The one mechanical problem: POV for an unnamed character

The ECID has a `POV` field, and the recovered B3 packets fill it with `Tahl`. If he is
unnamed on the page through Book 3, the field is recording something the reader does not
know.

Both obvious fixes are bad. Writing `POV: unknown` loses the canon fact. Writing
`POV: Tahl` implies the reader knows.

**Proposal: keep `POV: Tahl` and add a reader-facing flag.**

```json
"POV": "Tahl",
"pov_named_on_page": false
```

The canon layer stays accurate and queryable — every Tahl POV episode is findable from
B2 onward — while the flag records what the reader actually has. It also makes the
reveal checkable: the flag flips exactly once, at `B4.A1.E1`, and a validator can assert
that it never flips back.

The same flag would serve any later concealed-identity beat without further schema work.

---

## 5. Consequences per book

**B1** — Notion's `A1.E2` "Tahl: The First Pattern" and `A2.E7` "Tahl: Origin of MT"
come out. Book 1 has no MT and no chronicler. Whether their *content* moves to B2 or is
cut is §6.1.

**B2** — MT arrives anonymously. Notion's `A1.E3` "Tahl's Pattern Expands" becomes an
unnamed-author episode. Notion's `A3.E11` "Tahl's First Real Brush with VT" **is cut** —
the recovered packet anchors the one and only Veil brush at `B3.A3.E14`.

**B3** — the unnamed chronicler triangulates, exposes the site, slips into VT, and the
epilogue carries both Baz's death and the first MT-style line. He is *Threadnaut*
throughout.

**B4** — the name lands, the authorship lands, the remorse lands. Notion's existing
Book 4 Tahl material stays; it acquires a cause.

**B5–B6** — unchanged, and now his named lifespan is three books before his death in
B6. Five books of presence, three of them named.

---

## 6. Open

1. **What happens to B1's Tahl content?** "The First Pattern" and "Origin of MT" are two
   good episodes. They can move to B2 as anonymous material, be reassigned to another
   POV, or be cut. Book 1 currently loses two of its fifteen episodes with nothing
   replacing them.
2. **Is "Threadnaut" the B3 nickname, or does it stay a B4 coinage?** The ladder in §2
   needs it early; Notion puts it late.
3. **Does the reader see Tahl's POV at all in B3**, or only MT's output? An unnamed POV
   is workable; an off-page author is a different book. The VT slip in `B3.A3.E14` is
   written as his experience, which implies POV.
4. **Confirm the `pov_named_on_page` flag** (§4), or name a different mechanism — it
   touches the ECID schema and the validator.

# Epilogues as a Structural Unit — Ruling and Consequences

**Ruled by:** James, 2026-09-19 — *"Act 4 is more of a multi-episode epilogue (~3-4)."*
**Status:** ruling recorded; §3 and §4 need follow-up decisions.

---

## 1. Your own prior system already did this

The recovered material carries the answer. `Saga structural archive__part02` holds four
epilogue shells for Book 3, and their SIDs read:

| SID | Title | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `S1.T1.B3.EP.E01` | Tahl After the Slip | Tahl recovers from the VT brush; exhaustion and clarity |
| `S1.T1.B3.EP.E02` | Global Tension Montage | small pulses worldwide show drift has become omnipresent |
| `S1.T1.B3.EP.E03` | Commitment to Truth | Baz, the Filaments and Tahl agree to keep recording |
| `S1.T1.B3.EP.E04` | First MT-Style Line | Tahl drafts the first sentence in his future MT voice |

**`EP` sits in the act slot**, and epilogue episodes number from `E01` within it. Four
episodes — matching your "~3-4" exactly, arrived at independently.

So this is not a new convention to design. It is a recovered one to ratify.

---

## 2. What it settles

**Book 9 has three acts and an epilogue**, not four acts. The Final Beat Bible's
"ACT IV — Afterlight" is the epilogue written as an act, and its six beats compress to
three or four.

**The SID pattern does not need `A4`.** It needs `EP` as an alternative to the act
component:

```
S1.T{1-3}.B{00-09}.(A{1-3}|EP).E{00-99}
```

**The era boundary gets simpler.** Book 9's Act III ends at E15, the Lightfall. The
epilogue is wholly post-Mending. So the split falls between `B09.A3` and `B09.EP` —
a unit boundary, not a mid-act one, and the dual-band problem disappears.

**The pattern is saga-wide, not a Book 9 special case.** Book 3's epilogue is "The First
Quiet", Book 6's is "The Black Parade", Book 9's is "Luminous Thread". At least four
books carry one.

---

## 3. It contradicts a ruling you already made

Decision **D8** reads: *"Epilogues take the next sequential episode number rather than a
reserved marker; they are narrative content and belong in the sequence."*

The recovered form does the opposite — a reserved `EP` marker, with numbering restarting
at `E01`. Both cannot stand.

**I would supersede D8**, for three reasons. The `EP` form already exists in the
author's own material, so adopting it costs nothing and preserves what was written. It
keeps the epilogue out of Act III, which is what makes the era boundary clean. And D8
was ruled to settle where a prologue sits, with the epilogue answered by symmetry rather
than on its own evidence.

**The consequence runs back to the prologue.** If `EP` occupies the act slot, then `PR`
is its symmetric counterpart — `S1.T1.B01.PR.E01` — and **D6's `E00` ruling would be
superseded too**. Without that symmetry, a prologue has to live inside Act I, which
reads oddly for a scene that precedes the act structure.

So this is a three-way choice, and it should be made once:

- **A. `EP` and `PR` both in the act slot.** Symmetric, matches the recovered form for
  epilogues, supersedes D6 and D8. Prologue becomes `B01.PR.E01`, not `B01.A1.E00`.
- **B. `EP` only; prologue stays `E00` inside Act I.** Matches the recovered form for
  epilogues and keeps D6, at the cost of asymmetry.
- **C. Keep D8; the epilogue is the tail of Act III.** Rejects the recovered form,
  reintroduces the mid-act era boundary in Book 9, and makes Act III of every book
  structurally uneven.

I would take **A**. It is the only one where prologue and epilogue are the same kind of
thing, which is what they are.

---

## 4. Book 9's epilogue: six beats into three or four

The Final Beat Bible's Act IV, as written:

| | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| E16 | Seraphine — ascended but grounded; "no longer alone, the world breathes with her" |
| E17 | Lucien — Silence's successor; holds himself gently for the first time |
| E18 | Caro — Hope Reborn; balanced, pearlescent resonance |
| E19 | Elisabet — remains human, becomes the world's emotional anchor |
| E20 | Kade — posts the first LT message; **MT becomes LT** |
| E21 | Tahl's Echo — "Be kind for me", then fades into the Breathable Veil |

E16–E19 are four character codas of one kind. E20 and E21 are distinct events. Two
plausible compressions:

- **Three:** `EP01` the four codas together · `EP02` Kade renames the channel ·
  `EP03` Tahl's farewell
- **Four:** `EP01` the ascended trio · `EP02` Elisabet, the human anchor ·
  `EP03` Kade and LT · `EP04` Tahl's farewell

The four-beat version keeps Elisabet's "remains human" as its own beat against the three
who ascended, which is the contrast the act is built on. Your call.

2. **Three or four episodes for Book 9's epilogue** (§4), and which grouping.
3. Do epilogue episodes restart at `E01` within `EP`, as the recovered form does, or
