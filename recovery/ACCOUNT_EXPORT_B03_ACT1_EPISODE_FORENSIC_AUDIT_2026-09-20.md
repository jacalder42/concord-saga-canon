Scope: Book 3 Act I, E01–E18

Current chronology lock:
**B1–B3: MissingThread → anonymous Threadnaut persona → B3 epilogue reveal = Tahl Morgan.**

Therefore every old named-Tahl beat is decomposed into:
1. FUNCTION
2. IDENTITY EXPOSURE
3. DELIVERY CHANNEL

## Executive finding

Book 3 Act I is where the Dec. 8 expansion's Tahl implementation becomes unusable **as
written**, but the underlying investigative material remains highly recoverable.

The old act promotes Tahl into an openly named, physically present investigator:
- “Tahl enters as proto-investigator”;
- named Tahl POV;
- direct geographic travel toward New Orleans;
- visible contact with the existing cast;
- explicit reports attributed to Tahl.

That directly violates the current reveal architecture.

The correct response is **not deletion**.

Most of the old Tahl material performs one of four valuable functions:
- independent outside-world confirmation;
- public information aggregation;
- investigative pressure on institutions;
- a truth-line parallel to Baz.

Those functions can survive through:
- MissingThread posts;
- anonymous Threadnaut reporting;
- off-page source gathering;
- public datasets;
- Baz discovering/reading the anonymous work;
- institutional reaction to an unidentified reporter.

The key is to keep Tahl's **impact** while withholding Tahl's **identity**.

# I. Act-I macro spine

Recovered durable sequence:

**post-B2 altered baseline → anonymous/public reports detect the same pattern elsewhere →
Baz encounters an outside investigator's work → institutional suppression pressure rises →
MissingThread becomes useful but unverifiable → NOLA evidence and outside evidence
converge → trio tests the external model → anonymous reporter pushes closer to the truth →
institutions begin reacting to the information channel itself → a major event validates
the model → Book 3's truth-line becomes unavoidable.**

### Disposition

**KEEP MACRO / TRANSFORM TAHL DELIVERY.**

The investigative escalation is good.
The named-person implementation is not current.

---

# II. Tahl transform — governing rule

For all E01–E18:

### Allowed pre-epilogue
- MissingThread byline/persona;
- anonymous Threadnaut identity;
- text/audio/data/report fragments that do not expose legal identity;
- Baz corresponding with or reading anonymous work;
- institutional concern about “the reporter/account/source”;
- physical actions that can remain off-page and anonymous;
- reader uncertainty over whether MissingThread is one person, a network, or a movement.

### Not allowed
- “Tahl Morgan” on-page;
- named Tahl POV header;
- unmasked physical introduction;
- ordinary cast calling the reporter Tahl;
- biographical identifiers that solve the reveal;
- direct scenes whose visual description makes later identity reveal trivial;
- narration confirming the anonymous account is a single known individual unless current
  reveal architecture explicitly permits it.

# III. E01–E03 — altered baseline / outside signal

**Disposition: KEEP / TRANSFORM DELIVERY.**

### FUNCTION
Book 3 must immediately show that the B2 “No Reset” state persists and that someone outside
the NOLA trio is independently assembling evidence.

### IDENTITY EXPOSURE
Old source begins attaching the outside signal too directly to Tahl.

### DELIVERY CHANNEL
Use:
- MissingThread post;
- reposted field observation;
- unattributed dataset;
- screenshot/audio fragment;
- anonymous long-form thread.

### Narrative asset
Baz should be interested because the work is **careful and unspectacular**.
That supports the later Kade attraction to MissingThread:
truth delivered without spectacle.

# IV. E04–E06 — Baz discovers the investigator

**Disposition: KEEP FUNCTION / MAJOR TRANSFORM.**

### FUNCTION
Baz recognizes that the anonymous source:
- sees patterns he has missed;
- distinguishes observation from interpretation;
- is willing to publish uncertainty;
- has independent corroboration.

This is the beginning of the **Baz → Tahl → Kade truth lineage**.

### IDENTITY EXPOSURE
Old direct named interaction is superseded.

### DELIVERY CHANNEL
Baz can:
- follow MissingThread;
- exchange encrypted/anonymous messages;
- test one claim against his own records;
- receive a source packet without legal identity;
- argue with the reporter's conclusions through the channel.

### Strong relationship opportunity
Baz can develop **professional respect before personal knowledge**.

That makes the B3 epilogue reveal more meaningful:
the reader and Baz discover there was a person behind a body of work already trusted.

# V. E07–E09 — institutional suppression / information pressure

**Disposition: KEEP / STRONG CURRENT FUNCTION.**

### FUNCTION
Dominion/Technarc begin reacting not only to anomalies but to **who is making patterns
public**.

This is excellent Book 3 escalation.

### Dominion response
- legitimacy;
- classification;
- warnings;
- access restriction;
- concern about destabilizing public confidence.

### Technarc response
- provenance;
- data leakage;
- predictive contamination;
- attempts to identify sources;
- platform/metadata pressure.

### Tahl transform
Institutions should be trying to identify or neutralize **MissingThread / anonymous
Threadnaut**, not openly surveilling “Tahl Morgan.”

This actually improves suspense.

# VI. E10–E12 — convergence of NOLA and anonymous reporting

**Disposition: KEEP / TRANSFORM.**

### FUNCTION
A MissingThread model/report predicts or explains something the trio can independently
test.

### IDENTITY EXPOSURE
No named Tahl in field scene.

### DELIVERY CHANNEL
Best options:
- time-stamped post before the event;
- private anonymous message to Baz;
- source packet mirrored publicly if account disappears;
- report assembled from civilian witnesses.

### Strong payoff
Baz should initially distrust one element, test it, then discover it is right for a reason
he did not expect.

This prevents MissingThread from becoming omniscient.

### Guardrail
Anonymous reporter must be fallible.
Truth without spectacle is not truth without error.

# VII. E13–E15 — reporter gets closer / danger increases

**Disposition: KEEP FUNCTION / REMOVE PHYSICAL REVEAL.**

### FUNCTION
The outside investigator begins approaching institutional or geographic danger.

Old source makes this literal through named Tahl movement/travel.

### Current delivery
We can show danger indirectly:
- posting cadence changes;
- source goes dark;
- a scheduled packet fails;
- a photograph/data point indicates proximity without showing the person;
- institutions issue a warning aimed at the account;
- Baz realizes the reporter is physically closer to the problem than before.

### Important
Do not tell the reader exactly where Tahl lives/travels if that would collapse anonymity.

The anonymous persona can have a **geographic footprint without a disclosed biography**.

# VIII. E16–E18 — Act-I validation / truth-line locks in

**Disposition: KEEP / ACT HINGE.**

### FUNCTION
A significant event validates enough of MissingThread's work that:
- Baz can no longer treat it as peripheral;
- institutions treat the channel as consequential;
- public readers begin following it;
- the trio's investigation expands.

### Tahl transform
The act should end with:
**MissingThread matters.**
Not:
**Tahl joins the team.**

This distinction is essential.

### Best dramatic result
The reader can become attached to the *voice* before knowing the person.

That gives the B3 epilogue reveal emotional and structural payoff rather than functioning
as a trivia answer.

The current reveal architecture benefits if Book 3 Act I supports several plausible reader
interpretations:

- one anonymous journalist;
- a small collective;
- distributed witness network;
- activist data account;
- institutional whistleblower channel.

Do not confirm which is correct too early.

This also aligns with historical Kade material:
Kade later initially misreads MissingThread as movement/network/moral project/resistance
identity.

The ambiguity should therefore be **designed**, not accidental.

# XI. POV rule

Old named Tahl POV is **CONFLICT** pre-epilogue.

Potential replacements:
1. remove POV and deliver artifact;
2. Baz POV reading/reacting;
3. civilian source POV whose testimony reaches MissingThread;
4. institutional POV reacting to anonymous reporting;
5. limited anonymous Threadnaut artifact without embodied identifying narration.

Avoid “anonymous POV” that transparently describes Tahl's body/history and merely omits
the name. That is a cosmetic disguise, not a reveal architecture.

---

# XII. Physical travel rule

Old B3 material has Tahl physically moving toward NOLA.

Current recovery must ask of each travel beat:

**Does the story need the person's body there, or only the information gathered there?**

If only information:
move the function into sources/artifacts/messages.

If physical presence is essential:
keep it off-page or visually/biographically anonymous until reveal architecture permits.

Do not create contrived masks/hoods/voice changers simply to preserve an obsolete scene.

---

# XIII. Institutional arc

Book 3 Act I should evolve the B2 institutional conflict:

B2:
institutions monitor/control anomaly response.

B3:
institutions also monitor/control **narrative and evidence circulation**.

This creates a clean thematic bridge toward later:
- public truth;
- Kade;
- Brightbreak;
- Elias.

The conflict becomes not merely:
“what is happening?”

but:
**“who gets to say what is happening?”**

# XIX. Key editorial conclusion

The old B3 material accidentally spent the Tahl reveal too early.

But it also proves something useful:
Tahl was not added late as a random identity reveal.
The character had substantial investigative function in the old architecture.

The current solution is stronger:
**preserve the investigative gravity while delaying embodiment.**

This allows the reader to know:
- the work;
- the voice;
- the ethics;
- the impact;
before knowing:
- the name;
- the body;
- the biography.

That is a better reveal structure than simply hiding a character who is otherwise already
acting openly beside the cast.
