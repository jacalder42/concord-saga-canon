# 1. Core finding

Caro already has an autonomous Neon engine in Tier-1 canon.

It is not:
- “Seraphine's regulator”;
- “Elisabet's girlfriend”;
- Filament command;
- Resonance expertise;
- abstract humanitarianism.

It is:

**frontline responder → sustained triage → accumulated cost → confrontation with personal limits → sustainable/distributed care.**

Tier-1 establishes:
- Chicago/Pilsen origin;
- municipal/community training;
- EMT/paramedic track;
- frontline emergency medical response;
- triage, rapid assessment, physical intervention and crisis endurance;
- high institutional exposure without institutional power;
- distrust of abstraction/systemic indifference;
- core wound: *if she hesitates, someone will suffer*;
- core belief: *action prevents loss*;
- overextension and delayed rest;
- Neon: sustained triage, endurance costs accumulate;
- Loom: care becomes sustainable through pacing/delegation;
- post-Mending: care remains action-based but shared/communal.

This is sufficient to construct an independent protagonist arc without inventing a new vocation.

# 2. Caro's actual dramatic question

The weak version:
**Can Caro save people?**

She is already competent; repeating this produces competence porn and medic-as-savior flattening.

The stronger Neon question:
**What happens when the number of people who need help exceeds what one competent person can physically carry?**

This directly attacks:
- her wound;
- her belief that action prevents loss;
- her self-neglect;
- her distrust of systems;
- her preference for immediate embodied intervention.

The trilogy-level answer should not be “try harder.”

It should move toward:
**care is still action, but sustainable care requires handoff, trust, coordination, boundaries and systems worth trusting.**

That naturally prepares her endgame guide role without making her metaphysically special during Neon.

# 3. Distinction from the other secondary protagonists

Tools: environmental/geospatial analysis, comparison, interpretation, field research.

## Rex

Tools: engineering, systems analysis, Technarc access/history.

## Lucien
Question: **What does institutional duty mean after the institution loses moral authority?**
Tools: policy/structural analysis, Dominion history, archive/family consequences.

## Caro
Question: **Who is being hurt right now, who is being left out, and how do we keep care functioning when formal systems cannot meet demand?**
Tools: triage, assessment, physical intervention, handoff, improvisation, trust.

This gives Neon four distinct forms of competence rather than four flavors of Resonance investigation.

# 4. Caro's natural world-system lane

Caro belongs where **care capacity becomes finite**.

Useful systems:
- EMS;
- ER/urgent care;
- triage;
- shelters;
- evacuation;
- medication/supply handoff;
- transport access;
- disability/aftermath;
- staffing exhaustion;
- privacy/records;
- unequal access;
- mutual aid interfacing with formal services.

She should see the difference between:
- a phenomenon occurring;
- a person being injured;
- a service becoming overloaded;
- a faction exploiting overload;
- a community building an alternative.

These are not the same event.

# 7. Proposed B04–B06 autonomous arc

## B04 — competence becomes obligation

State:
Caro responds to fracture/aftermath using the thing she knows how to do: move toward need.

Function:
- care systems strained but recognizable;
- she can still make meaningful individual differences;
- grief around Baz and widening crisis makes stopping feel morally unacceptable;
- she increasingly takes extra responsibility because she can.

Critical turn:
**competence recruits more obligation.**

Potential durable state change:
Caro accepts or creates a recurring crisis-care role beyond ordinary shift-level response.

Do not automatically milestone this unless it changes later access/obligation.

## B05 — triage becomes impossible arithmetic

State:
demand exceeds any individual's capacity.

Function:
- simultaneous needs;
- degraded transport/services;
- formal and informal care overlap;
- every choice means leaving someone else waiting;
- Caro's “act first” strength becomes a liability when she cannot personally execute every necessary action.

This is the best book for:
- delegation failure;
- refusal to rest;
- conflict over prioritization;
- learning that handoff is not abandonment.

### Strong candidate book turn
**Caro deliberately hands responsibility to someone else and leaves before the problem is “finished,” because staying would harm the larger response.**

This is small compared with a Colorstorm but enormous for her.

It is a possible **new Caro milestone** if later source/design confirms the state change persists.

## B06 — limits become moral rather than physical

State:
Caro is exhausted enough that continuing personally may make outcomes worse.

Function:
- she must distinguish courage from compulsion;
- accepts that being needed does not mean she must be the person doing every task;
- care becomes networked.

This can intersect Tahl/Santa Fe consequences without requiring Caro at the exact rupture.

End-state:
Caro remains kinetic and action-oriented.
She has **not** learned passivity.
She has learned:
**delegating care is also care.**

That cleanly seeds Loom's sustainable-care architecture.

# 8. Relationship with Elisabet

Caro/Elisabet becomes stronger if their independent arcs differ.

Elisabet can leave to investigate, compare or interpret.
Caro can remain with people who cannot leave.

That creates productive tension:
- evidence vs immediate need;
- mobility vs obligation;
- long-range prevention vs person in front of you;
- each respecting a form of care the other practices differently.

The Neon relationship milestone should therefore **not require permanent co-location**.

A strong version of NEW-RCE1 becomes:

**They deliberately choose the relationship while also choosing not to abandon their separate work.**

That is more distinctive than “fully bonded amid chaos.”

It also establishes a relationship capable of surviving geographic separation.

# 9. Relationship with Seraphine

Caro can ground Seraphine, but Neon should reverse the flow at least sometimes.

Seraphine should see Caro overextending and be unable to accept “Caro is the person who always handles it” as a permanent arrangement.

This keeps Caro from becoming a regulation appliance.

The relationship can evolve from:
**Caro stabilizes Seraphine**
to
**they recognize each other's limits and intervene when the other is disappearing into function.**

No supernatural feedback loop required.
