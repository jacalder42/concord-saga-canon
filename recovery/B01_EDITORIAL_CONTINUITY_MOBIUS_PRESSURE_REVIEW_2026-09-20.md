Status: EDITORIAL REVIEW — NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Review frame

This review adds the recovered **Möbius mechanic** to the previously planned continuity and
pressure/relief audit.

Möbius is not simple repetition.

**Recurring elements return transformed or inverted.**

The audit therefore asks:
- does an early image/action/problem create a later return?
- is the return changed by character choice rather than merely repeated?
- does the local B1 loop support the larger Veil → Neon → Loom loop?
- does Book 1 plant material that Book 9 can answer without making B1 feel like setup?

Recovered load-bearing Seraphine Möbius:
- OPENING: she arrives **too late**; care + urgency + helplessness fractures the Veil.
- MID-SAGA: she risks arriving **too early**; care risks becoming control.
- ENDGAME: Mending requires timing + restraint + shared Intent, not speed/authority/certainty.
- Return: swamp / unfinished care / human choice.

This must remain visible in B1's emotional architecture.

# 2. Editorial verdict on Draft v1

**KEEP THE 36-EPISODE SHAPE. AMEND, DO NOT RE-OUTLINE.**

The draft has a strong causal line and materially improved human texture.

Its main remaining risk is that Act III still produces too many consecutive
**pattern → system failure → aftermath → pattern** beats unless ordinary life and
relationship movement remain active inside the escalation.

Möbius review improves this because several apparent repetitions can become deliberate
returns rather than redundant events.

# 3. Book 1 internal Möbius loops

## M1 — Too Late → Choosing Not to Rush

### Opening
E1–2:
Seraphine arrives too late.
The child dies.
Her wound tells her:
**next time, move faster / carry more / prevent it.**

### Return
E21–22 / Marigny:
the team has a reason to push deeper.
Baz argues for restraint.
Seraphine must tolerate not acting simply because action is available.

### Book-end echo
E36:
prediction succeeds, but observation is more ethical/useful than trying to control the
event.

### Editorial action
Strengthen the Marigny beat so Seraphine—not only Lucien—has something emotionally at
stake in choosing restraint.

This gives B1 a miniature version of the saga-wide:
**too late → temptation to be too early → timing.**

Do not complete the lesson in B1.
She should understand restraint intellectually before she can consistently live it.
