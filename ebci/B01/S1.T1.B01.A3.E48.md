## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Return to the first public place, where people's memory matters; write an honest account; choose the next watch, and call it a bet.
Want:                               To be right on paper, honestly this time: an account under their names, then a post he chooses.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        An honest account, then a post.
  Opposition:                       Baz and Seraphine, over Baz's private column; Seraphine, over what he wrote on the form; the signed page, against what it rested on; Vernice at her stall, who wants to know what they are calling it; a stranger at a table who wants their record.
  Pressed by:                       Guidry, on the phone, closing the file with both statements and the amendment attached; he says honestly that someone upstairs asked for a copy first, and he does not know who. Authority: the coroner; his own part reasonable; the request above him unexplained. Beside the city's complaint notice on the Square's fence, a grey case, zip-tied low, that arrived after. No stated authority; whoever it is wants only the time. Then, for once, someone asks: at a table at the Square's edge, while Baz is away, a person nobody can place sits across from Seraphine, knows that the three keep a record of times, and asks her for it (the page, her log). No name, no institution, no stated agenda; the person lets her understand that the holds on the blocks where her people live could end. It points above, and nobody says who.
  Turn:                             They name it carefully: systemic, which does not mean understood.
  Consequence:                      Lucien reports the door-to-door (nobody who went home had it again; the lot shivered once more). He takes Guidry's call. He finds the case on the fence, and sets his own post away from it. He tells Seraphine and Baz the fact of Guidry's copy, and that the file closed with his letter attached, beside R.'s note from the Institute that the camp report went up in March by another hand, and says nothing more about it. At *my letter* Seraphine nods: she has known since Sunday what it corrected. What passes between them is cool and specific, not a reconciliation. Once, in his own point of view, Lucien names to himself what he feels for her; he does not say it. At the table Seraphine refuses the stranger, plainly, and the refusal has its price: the holds do not end, Mr. Vidrine's tanks stay held, and she knows her refusal is part of why. Sal's job is still stopped, and Denise's write-up is still in Seraphine's file. The honest account records the missed middays and that the bet on its coming faster was wrong. The three choose still surfaces for tomorrow and say it is a bet. Seraphine reads Baz's column: the houses that shook against her own list. She tells him he never put it to her; he answers that he knew what she would say.
  Unresolved:                       Tomorrow's tins; who else is measuring the Square; who asked, and whether they will ask again.
Change:                             They choose a narrow window at the Square and a kind of thing to watch: still water, because glass and ground have each shown a different face and still water is the quietest thing they can watch that nothing has ruled out. Seraphine and Baz: Before, warm, her concealment admitted. After, warm, with a grievance on her side. Seraphine and Lucien: Before, she knows the *None* and has said nothing. After, it is acknowledged between them, coolly and specifically, and not repaired.
Reader experience:                  A place that carries memory because people do; a bet made in the open.
Keep / don't spend:                 People remember, not stone or water. No single site, crowd or institution as the cause; no claim about the world. The bet is said as a bet: nothing they have seen says water must be next, and the failures are findings about these events, not laws. The honest account is one short passage, not a summing-up. Lucien registers the route the file took, never a motive: no column of names, no route drawn on paper, no judgment of the Institute aloud, and nothing sent to it about the cases, the trap or the table. He may think of the Institute as the cover it is; the people behind it stay unnamed. Nobody says who upstairs is, or that the three are watched. The stranger at the table stays unnamed and unplaced: no name, institution, uniform, logo or agenda, no claim to speak for anyone, and Baz never sees them; whether they could have ended the holds is never shown. Lucien's named feeling is his own thought, once, never a declaration; hand on hand stays the ceiling. The nod at *my letter* is knowledge, not forgiveness. No landmark or compass direction for which way he goes. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.
Exit conditions:                    Friday at the Square; posts chosen for tomorrow (Seraphine's, Baz's, one in the middle, and one on Royal to be held by Mrs. Carmouche for Inez); the tins still to fill; the file closed; the case on the fence; the column read; the honest account signed; the stranger refused and gone; the holds still on; Sal's job still stopped; Denise's write-up still in Seraphine's file; the *None* acknowledged between Seraphine and Lucien and not repaired.
Carry from Act II:                  **The verdict owed** (the forecast account, `reports/B01_REDRAFT_ACT_III_RECONCILIATION_AND_PROSE_PACKETS_2026-09-29.md` §2). Until now the page has been scored only in private: Baz's columns on the night of Ursulines, and Lucien's line on the back of the page. This is the first account under all three names, and it stays one short passage. Fixed in it: the two middays missed, and the bet on its coming faster wrong. It may also say, as briefly, that the page was right once on time and place, wrong on what to watch for and on *larger*, and had no line for who got hurt. Baz's column has at least two lines in her favor; Lucien has known of it since the ferry and agreed with Baz not to tell her. Whether she learns that Lucien knew is prose's; if she does, it belongs to the same grievance. The door-to-door was her proposal. The case on the Square fence is not the first: Lucien found one on Dauphine (untold until now), Seraphine one on the Robertson corner, and the two of them watched one taken from Sal's jack post; whether Lucien now tells the others about Dauphine is prose's. Guidry's file closes with Lucien's amendment attached, the letter he mailed on the Friday before Carrollton. Since Sunday Seraphine has known, from the family's copy, that he wrote *None* on the form beside her paragraph and signed it twice; she has not spoken to him about it, and he has given no explanation. R. is his contact at the Institute. On the asking at home, Miss Hazel did not answer her door; if the report touches it, it stays unanswered. Sal's job has been stopped since Lucien's false report; Denise wrote Seraphine up for taking the center's car. The city's holds on deliveries into the glass blocks are still on, Mr. Vidrine's oxygen among them.
