## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Return to the first public place, where people's memory matters; write an honest account; choose the next watch, and call it a bet.
Want:                               To be right on paper, honestly this time: an account under their names, then a post he chooses.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        An honest account, then a post.
  Opposition:                       Baz and Seraphine, over Baz's private column; the signed page, against what it rested on; Vernice at her stall, who wants to know what they are calling it.
  Pressed by:                       Guidry, on the phone, closing the file with both statements and the amendment attached; he says honestly that someone upstairs asked for a copy first, and he does not know who. Authority: the coroner; his own part reasonable; the request above him unexplained. Beside the city's complaint notice on the Square's fence, a grey case, zip-tied low, that arrived after. No stated authority; whoever it is wants only the time. It points above, and nobody says who.
  Turn:                             They name it carefully: systemic, which does not mean understood.
  Consequence:                      Lucien reports the door-to-door (nobody who went home had it again; the lot shivered once more). He takes Guidry's call. He finds the case on the fence, and sets his own post away from it. He tells Seraphine and Baz the fact of Guidry's copy, beside his office colleague's note that the camp report went up in March by another hand, and says nothing more about it. The honest account records the missed middays and that the bet on its coming faster was wrong. The three choose still surfaces for tomorrow and say it is a bet. Seraphine reads Baz's column: the houses that shook against her own list. She tells him he never put it to her; he answers that he knew what she would say.
  Unresolved:                       Tomorrow's tins; who else is measuring the Square.
Change:                             They choose a narrow window at the Square and a kind of thing to watch: still water, because glass and ground have each shown a different face and still water is the quietest thing they can watch that nothing has ruled out. Seraphine and Baz: Before, warm, her concealment admitted. After, warm, with a grievance on her side.
Reader experience:                  A place that carries memory because people do; a bet made in the open.
Keep / don't spend:                 People remember, not stone or water. No single site, crowd or institution as the cause; no claim about the world. The bet is said as a bet: nothing they have seen says water must be next, and the failures are findings about these events, not laws. The honest account is one short passage, not a summing-up. Lucien registers the route the file took, never a motive: no column of names, no route drawn on paper, no judgment of his office aloud, and nothing sent to it about the cases or the trap. Nobody says who upstairs is, or that the three are watched. No landmark or compass direction for which way he goes. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.
Exit conditions:                    Friday at the Square; posts chosen for tomorrow (Seraphine's, Baz's, one in the middle, and one on Royal to be held by Mrs. Carmouche for Mara); the tins still to fill; the file closed; the case on the fence; the column read; the honest account signed.
