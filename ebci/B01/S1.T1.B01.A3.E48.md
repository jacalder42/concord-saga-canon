## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Return to the first public place, where people's memory matters; write an honest account; choose the next watch, and call it a bet; then Baz tells Lucien what he has kept from him.
Want:                               To be right on paper, honestly this time: an account under their names, then a post he chooses; at the end, to know what his own work has been for.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        An honest account, then a post.
  Opposition:                       Baz and Seraphine, over Baz's private column; Seraphine, over what he wrote on the form; the signed page, against what it rested on; Vernice at her stall, who wants to know what they are calling it; a stranger at a table who wants their record; Baz, at the end, who has known more than he said.
  Pressed by:                       Guidry, on the phone, with both statements and the amendment on his file: he tells Lucien he is holding the environmental question open and leaving the manner undetermined, his own call, and that someone upstairs asked for a copy first, and he does not know who. Renée's final certificate does not come. Authority: the coroner; his own part reasonable, his discretion his own; the request above him unexplained (10-03, map §3, Q-SF5, F2). Beside the city's complaint notice on the Square's fence, a grey case, zip-tied low, that arrived after. No stated authority; whoever placed it wants the record of where and when it takes, and the page never says so (10-03, map §0.1). Then, for once, someone asks: at a table at the Square's edge, while Baz is away, a person nobody can place sits across from Seraphine, knows of Lucien's page and Seraphine's log, and asks her for them (10-03, §387). No name, no institution, no stated agenda; the person lets her understand that the holds on the blocks where her people live could end. She wants their record. It points above, and nobody says who. Last, after the posts are chosen, Baz, alone with Lucien (10-03, map §3).
  Turn:                             They name it carefully: systemic, which does not mean understood. Then, alone, Baz tells Lucien.
  Consequence:                      Lucien reports the door-to-door (nobody who went home had it again; the lot shivered once more). He takes Guidry's call. He finds the case on the fence, and sets his own post away from it. He tells Seraphine and Baz the fact of Guidry's copy, and that Guidry is holding the question open with his letter attached, beside R.'s note from the Institute that the camp report went up in March by another hand, and says nothing more about it. At *my letter* Seraphine nods: she has known since Sunday what it corrected. What passes between them is cool and specific, not a reconciliation. Once, in his own point of view, Lucien names to himself what he feels for her; he does not say it. Listening, Lucien realises how the stranger knows of his page and Seraphine's log: they were on the phone taken from him at the jack post; it is his to carry (10-03, §387). At the table Seraphine refuses the stranger, plainly, and the refusal has its price: the holds do not end, Mr. Vidrine's tanks stay held, and she knows her refusal is part of why. Sal's job is still stopped, and Denise's write-up is still in Seraphine's file. The honest account records the missed middays and that the bet on its coming faster was wrong. The three choose still surfaces for tomorrow and say it is a bet. Seraphine reads Baz's column: the houses that shook against her own list. She tells him he never put it to her; he answers that he knew what she would say (kept short). After the posts, Baz and Lucien alone: Baz tells him that the people behind the Institute were already watching this, that it is real and it recurs, that he came to the city partly because of it, and that a man from them has found him, Caldas. He says he tried to tell Lucien on the lot on Wednesday and then chose to wait (10-03, §387). He says nothing of how it works. It lands as betrayal: Baz knew while Lucien was being used. In his own point of view, Lucien's question about his review becomes a belief: it has had him finding these events, for them (10-03, map §3, Q-PI3, Q-PI4, D1, D3). The belief rests only on routes the book has shown: his own letter; the file Guidry's superior copied and sent up; R.'s note that the camp report went up in March by another hand; and the Institute that placed him (10-03, §387).
  Unresolved:                       Tomorrow's tins; who else is measuring the Square; who asked, and whether they will ask again; what Lucien does with what Baz told him, and with Baz; what Guidry decides.
