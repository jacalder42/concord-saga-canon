## Narrative brief
Story job:                          The city's interest acquires a will. Lucien is called to a city meeting about the Square, by a letter that reaches him through the Hollen Institute, his employer; Baz waits in the lobby and watches what is asked for, what is held back, and who does not sign in. Elisabet enters, on her own work. Among the people going up, Baz recognises a man from the work he left, and keeps it to himself (10-03, map §2).
Want:                               To see what the city wants, and who is doing the wanting.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Baz, on the lobby bench while Lucien goes up, wants to see what the city is after.
  Opposition:                       The city's table, which wants the records of where and when, and whatever other records people kept (10-03, §387). Clement, down from his own appointment, refuses to give up the phone that holds his mother's voice. The guard, whose job is the sign-in book. Then Lucien's own caution: he brings three photocopied pages and leaves his notebook at home.
  Pressed by:                       Baz is a visitor without standing in a city building. What presses here is new: a tenth-floor room booked under no name; three people with lanyards turned face in, who do not sign the guard's book; records held for someone referred to only as "upstairs". Two different wants show in it (10-03, map §2): the booked room, the hold upstairs and the question nobody asks Lucien take an interest in what he knows and keeps in his notebook; the three with turned lanyards ask, in Baz's hearing, for what they want: where it happened, when, and what other records anyone kept of it (lists, logs, recordings, Elisabet's pump records among them). What they ask is shown as asked, never summed up as times alone (10-03, §387). One of the people who go up to the booked room is a man Baz knows from the work he left. Baz recognises him from the bench and keeps it to himself; the man does not see him. Authority, stated justification and whose interests it serves: none given. This is where the institutional pressure first has a will behind it. It takes, and it asks the three for nothing. Before Lucien comes down, the guard is told to put everyone waiting on the bench in the sign-in book. Baz will not sign, and waits outside on the plaza, where he sees less; nobody says who asked for the names (10-03, §391, N2).
  Turn:                             The table has the coroner's form with Seraphine's paragraph on it, face up, and nobody asks Lucien whether this has happened to him before.
  Consequence:                      The city holds her paragraph. Lucien chose what the table saw and kept his notebook back. Baz has seen three faces the city did not name, and has recognised a fourth, whom he names to nobody (10-03, map §2). Elisabet has entered, on a visit. Baz's name is not in the guard's book, and he watched the end from the plaza (10-03, §391, N2).
  Unresolved:                       What the city wants, and who booked the room.
Change:                             The institutional pressure stops being procedure and starts to want something specific. Lucien starts keeping things back from an official table, and writes down Baz's question and its answer in his own hand.
Reader experience:                  A civic building that is polite, busy and not quite telling the truth; then an argument between Elisabet and Lucien worth having, in which she flirts with him openly while Baz watches (10-03, map §2).

The man Baz recognises may be named in Baz's head or left unnamed; no institution is given, he does not see Baz, and Baz says nothing of him to Lucien or anyone (10-03, map §2). Elisabet's pump records are on hold; she has met Lucien and gone back to her own work. Clement still has his phone. Baz did not sign the guard's book, and waited out the end on the plaza (10-03, §391, N2).
