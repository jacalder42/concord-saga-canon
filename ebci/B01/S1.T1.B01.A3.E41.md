## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Make the aftermath human before anyone tries another answer, and let Seraphine meet her limit.
Want:                               To be the one who holds it: to get every stranded person home herself.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Get twenty-six people stranded at the center home tonight.
  Opposition:                       Denise, who closes the center on time; the city's held transport; her own body.
  Pressed by:                       The center's closing rule. Authority: Denise, her employer; stated justification: the building, the staff and the center's liability; whose interests: the center's, and in Denise's view Seraphine's. Beside it, the city's hold on transport, whose reason does not reach her. Ordinary friction, with a hold from above behind it.
  Turn:                             After the last drive, someone at a door asks whether this is the place to come, and she does not get up.
  Consequence:                      She takes the center's one car and drives them home in shifts, and tells people she is fine when she is not. Denise writes her up for Mr. Vidrine's tank going out on her signature, on a block taken from her that morning, says the write-up is not her verdict on whether Seraphine was wrong, and still gives her the car; the write-up goes in her file. From the evening she draws her own lesson: the hall where people waited together was steadier, so being looked after must keep it off. She commits to keeping people together. The night on Kerlerec visibly feeds that certainty (no sleep since the car, Lucien's blame from the dawn, the trap she learned of there): she wants one thing she can be sure of, and sets the hall against her own question mark.
  Unresolved:                       Whether she gets up; a theory she now believes and has not tested.
Change:                             She meets the limit of being useful to everyone, and reads it as a lesson about keeping people together.
Reader experience:                  Ordinary care dominating: more of it needed than she has, and some of it left undone.
Keep / don't spend:                 This is her limit met, not mended: nobody looks after her back into shape. No rule about when or how she feels it. The narrator never endorses her theory; it is hers, and so is the link from Kerlerec to it: shown in what she reaches for, never explained. The write-up is Denise's own decision, by the center's rules. At the Vein people pass the rumour's "they" between tables; Trip knows which rumour is going round and never which theory is right. The band's musician is unnamed and is nobody who matters later.
Exit conditions:                    Late that night; the twenty-six are home; the car is back at the center; Denise's write-up for the tank is in Seraphine's file; Seraphine is home; she has told at least one person she is fine.
Carry from Act II:                  Her theory is already hers and already said aloud. Since Renita's block she has said it comes down on people carrying too much, alone, and goes easier on people who are looked after; by her own answer, what would prove her wrong is someone carrying nothing, found just as hard. The Marigny house felt like proof to her. After Ursulines she wrote in her own notebook that Mrs. Arceneaux was not alone, and put a question mark after it. So this evening is not where the theory begins: **the hall is the evidence she chooses to set against her own question mark, and she decides to act on the theory.** That decision is what she argues from at the lot. She has not slept since the night in the car.
