## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Lucien's coping fails in the open while he guards his standing, and he chooses the lie on the record a second time.
Want:                               To keep his standing on Sal's crew, and to keep the coroner's file closed with his statement as it stands.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Hold his place on the job, keep his daily look at the store's gallery post, and get the coroner's confirmation done.
  Opposition:                       Sal, whose rule is plain (come on time or don't come at all) and who has noticed Lucien going grey; the store owner, whose corner is a shop and not a place to stand; Guidry, who needs Lucien's statement confirmed inside his ten days. Under all three, Lucien's own eye, which gives him a wrong measurement.
  Pressed by:                       Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. Sal's contract: his authority is his crew and his client, the gallery has to come off the jacks on schedule, and the interests are his business's. The coroner's procedure: Guidry's authority, statements confirmed within a set period, the record's interests; a reasonable demand. A shop owner's say over her own property.
  Turn:                             Guidry asks him to confirm his statement and mentions that the boy's mother wants the file closed. Lucien chooses the lie again.
  Consequence:                      The false line on a legal record is renewed, now by choice and with the family's wish beside it; its cost is deferred. Earlier, a measurement of his has cost Sal one of a handful of scarce old boards.
  Unresolved:                       The signed confirmation, and what it hides.
Change:                             He grows more exact as his certainty drops; the lie is now something he has done twice.
Reader experience:                  A careful man making himself smaller and more precise, and choosing the same lie again.
Keep / don't spend:                 The lean returns, faint and unnamed, with no landmark and no direction. He tells no one how bad it was and asks no one for help. Sal's worry is Sal's own. Nobody asks Lucien to report or contain anything, and nobody from his office appears. He may be wrong, embarrassed and stubborn on the page.
Exit conditions:                    Some days later. The coroner's form is re-signed and back with Guidry. Lucien is still on Sal's crew, one salvaged board short. He still keeps his daily look at the store's post, on the owner's terms.
