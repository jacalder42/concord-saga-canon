## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Seraphine and Lucien's first real fight, the second rung between them. Fear drives a real disagreement over method, risk and responsibility. Seraphine starts warning her households before anyone is sure, under a name that is not the truth.
Want:                               Seraphine: to be the one who warns them first, needed before the fact. Lucien: a clean sample, and her safe inside his procedure.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Seraphine means to warn her oldest clients who live alone before the next one comes. Lucien means to keep the record clean enough to test.
  Opposition:                       Denise, whose liability rules will not let the center frighten clients with a warning it cannot stand behind. Lucien, who speaks for Baz's need for untold witnesses, and who turns her own written admission from the Square, that she made it worse, against her. Under both, each one's fear for the other.
  Pressed by:                       Seraphine: Denise's liability. Her authority is the employer's; her stated justification is forty-three households and the center's exposure; the interests served are the center's, and Seraphine's as Denise sees them. It is reasonable, and it protects Denise too. Lucien: the Hollen Institute's review wants clean work (authority, his employer on paper; justification, method; interest, the review's report). He knows the Institute is a cover for the people who sent him, and may think about it; they stay unnamed. Beside this ordinary friction, the new will: Lucien brings word that someone at the city's table has her name.
  Turn:                             Seraphine tells him she is not a post for him to check morning and night.
  Consequence:                      The heat checks run under a cover name: a lie to Denise and to forty-three households, and every call is also a warning. The fight costs: she walks out. When she comes back she tells him the folder: the boy's health coverage had lapsed, and the renewal paperwork was sitting on her desk while she worked on it. His procedure is rewritten as seven lines that carry her rule as well as his. One of the seven sets a meeting place: if it comes while they are apart, whoever is at the agreed place holds there until the others come. The morning texts stop for two days, then start again.
  Unresolved:                       Whether the lie holds with Denise. Whether the calls, logged before and after telling, stay clean enough to count.
Change:                             Before: real trust, with care expressed as control on both sides. After: trust that has come through a real disagreement. The repair is changed behaviour, not talk: the working page now carries both methods. What stays changed is the lapse, two days without the morning texts.
Reader experience:                  Two frightened people, each right about something, hurting each other more than they meant to.
Keep / don't spend:                 Fear drives it, not misunderstanding. No declaration; the heat menu is available (touch, proximity, attraction bending a practical choice), as a menu, not a quota; nothing romantic is declared aloud, and no kiss (10-03, map §0.3 G3). The warning names nothing: no days, no hour, no word for what it is. Lucien reports the table as he saw it and says nothing aloud against the Institute. Each half carries only what its point-of-view holder perceives or is told. Seraphine's casework is real work, and the lie is hers to carry. Standing guards: no clock time in narration unless someone does something with it; no named mechanism; the narrator certifies no cause of the phenomenon; the characters may suspect and blame, each other included, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them, but the narrator states no human cause as fact (10-03, map §0.3 G1); no return to the camp; no bearing for Lucien's lean. The calls' log is Seraphine's record of who was told and when it was said; the page does not tally call times (10-03, §391).
Exit conditions:                    Late March, night. Seraphine has the forty-three-name pad and the seven-line page. The calls are under way and logged, telling by telling. Denise believes they are ordinary heat checks. Lucien knows about the folder. No morning text passes between them for the next two days; then the texts resume.
Carry from Act I:                   The Batiste case file is still open at the center. Denise wants it closed before the month-end count the center reports to the state, which is days away; Seraphine promised it for Monday and missed; the closure form will not take a blank date for the last action on the renewal. It stays live by what she does or avoids doing here, never by recollection. Denise has already banned center business in the Square and said she is watching. The blue folder has been on Seraphine's kitchen table since her call with Odile; only Caro and Odile have heard it. Her written admission from the first Square, that she made it worse, is in her own notebook, and Lucien has not seen it: how he comes to have it is on the page. The heat checks are her line of inquiry: she starts them, she logs them, and she reads what they show. If Mr. Fontenot tells her on a call of a minute he lost, it is the one from early March, on his porch, that Inez noted at the time: one episode, which he reports late.
