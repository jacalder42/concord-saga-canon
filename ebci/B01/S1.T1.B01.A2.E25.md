## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Seraphine goes, uninvited, to the family of the boy she could not save, to keep her own account on the record. She learns that a stranger has already been to their door asking for her minute.
Want:                               Her own account on the record: her words kept on the file, in her own name. The motive is mixed and shown in the scene: the truth of what she felt, being believed as the one who was there, and refusing to be corrected.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Keep her paragraph on the coroner's file, against the family's wish to close it.
  Opposition:                       Renée, who wants her son's death closed as an ordinary death. Hers is a mother's authority, and her claim is reasonable: the book's strongest. Lorraine, who wants Seraphine out of her kitchen.
  Pressed by:                       The city is holding the file open with her paragraph in it. And a will has reached the family: since the form went to the city, a polite man wearing a card on a string, turned round, came to the door. He asked Lorraine at what minute the caseworker said she felt it and what she had written, and he wrote down only the time. No authority, justification or interest is given.
  Turn:                             Renée asks what Seraphine did first, since she was there first.
  Consequence:                      Seraphine refuses to take her paragraph off. She does not tell Renée that the boy's health coverage had lapsed while the renewal sat on her desk.
  Unresolved:                       Whether he comes back.
Change:                             Seraphine learns that her words on paper brought a stranger to a grieving family's door, and she keeps them on the paper anyway. Seraphine and Renée: Before, a distance held by grief. After, an open refusal on each side.
Reader experience:                  A kitchen where she isn't wanted, and a question she can't answer honestly.
Keep / don't spend:                 The man at the door is reported, never seen. He is unnamed and polite; he asked about her minute and her words, never about the boy and never about the place, and the narrator never says who sent him. The will takes; it asks the three for nothing. The narrator never blames Renée. The lapsed coverage stays untold. There is no licensing pressure on her. Metairie is not the camp: nobody goes back there, and nothing on the page makes the boy or the place special. The piece is not only its refusal: the kitchen and the family's food stay in view. Standing guards: no clock time in narration unless someone does something with it (the man's interest in her minute is the clock here); no named mechanism; the narrator certifies no cause and no blame; plans stay undated.
Exit conditions:                    Friday afternoon, late March, Metairie. Seraphine's paragraph stays on the file. Renée and Lorraine know she will not take it off; Renée does not know about the lapsed coverage. Seraphine knows, second-hand, about the man at the door. She leaves to drive to the center lot for her six o'clock call to Odile.
