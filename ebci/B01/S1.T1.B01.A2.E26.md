## Narrative brief
Story job:                          The pattern becomes a falsifiable prediction, committed in writing and signed: a window of days, a place, what they will watch for, what counts as a miss, and a choice to prepare people rather than prevent anything. Someone says plainly why knowing where and when would help people. And Baz tells Seraphine and Lucien why he left the work he did before (10-03, map §2).
Want:                               A page that can be wrong. Under it, Baz wants to belong here more than he wants to be right.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Get a page the three can be wrong about, with a clean test inside it.
  Opposition:                       Renita, who would like the stranger to stop sitting on her neighbour's step so the block can get on with its afternoon. Seraphine, who refuses his split-street design, an untold side of the street kept for comparison, because it would leave households like Renita's untold (10-03, map §2).
  Pressed by:                       The block's own rules about who gets told and who sits where. The authority and interests are the neighbours' own, and the justification is their own day. It is reasonable. Nothing from the city is in this piece.
  Turn:                             Baz argues for the untold side in good faith, and at length: without a comparison the page can never show whether telling people helps, and one untold street now could spare many later. Seraphine answers each reason. Only then, and slowly, does he hear what he is proposing: a neighbourhood kept untold so that the observations stay clean, which is what made him leave. He sees it, and it costs him (10-03, §387). He tells Seraphine and Lucien why he left: they kept warnings from a neighbourhood so that what they observed there stayed clean, and people were hurt. He says that much, in his own words, and no more (10-03, map §2).
  Consequence:                      The page is signed; what people are told carries no days and no hour. A bet exists in writing, and its claim is stated once, plainly: inside the window and inside the lines, it comes again, larger than the last (10-03, map §2). Baz signs, with his dissent written on the back. He tells Mr. Benoit himself, his last untold witness, so his clean sample is spent.
  Unresolved:                       Ten days.
Change:                             The pattern becomes a committed, checkable bet with three names under it. Baz chooses belonging over a cleaner test, and records his dissent instead of refusing. Seraphine and Lucien know why he left his old work; neither knows how much he knows about what is happening here (10-03, map §2). The recognition costs him: he has heard himself make the case of the people he left, and he cannot unhear it (10-03, §387).
Reader experience:                  Method with stakes: a page that will change how people behave, and a friend who loses an argument well.
Keep / don't spend:                 The page is a bet, not a finding. Its watch items are the characters' own: Lucien's (the street, the ground, an edge) come from his theory of the ground, and Seraphine's (it coming up slowly) from her ears. The page's one claim is stated once, in terms a reader can hold (the window, the lines, larger than the last), and the calendar bookkeeping around it stays off the page (10-03, map §2). The narrator certifies none of it. Baz's reason for leaving is told once, by him; the people he left are referred to obliquely, in any form, and none is preferred. He does not say how much he knows about the phenomenon, and the page does not narrate it from inside him (10-03, map §2). His rationalisation has real weight before he recognises it: his reasons are argued as good ones, never a straw man, and the recognition is neither quick nor tidy; it costs him visibly and absolves nothing (10-03, §387). Prepare people; never try to stop anything; no exact-second prophecy. The reason to predict is said once, by a person, as their reason: told in time, someone can be sitting down with the stove off when it comes. The narrator does not claim it works, and Baz's dissent stands. Renita keeps her own reasons: she is tired of it, not hostile. The window's dates stay off the page, though its length may be said. Standing guards: no clock time in narration unless someone does something with it (the page is a decision, not a tally); no named mechanism; the narrator certifies no cause of the phenomenon; the characters may suspect and blame, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them, but the narrator states no human cause as fact (10-03, map §0.3 G1); nothing foreshadows how Baz's story ends; that someone may be looking for him can be felt (10-03, map §0.3 G2).
Exit conditions:                    Saturday evening, late March. The page is signed by the three. Lucien keeps it; Baz has a copy with his dissent on the back. Mr. Benoit has been told. The ten-day window has been written, opening the Monday after Easter (10-03, map §2: the bookkeeping cut); and telling has begun inside its lines, each telling recorded. Seraphine and Lucien know why Baz left his old work (10-03, map §2).
