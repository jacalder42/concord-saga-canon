## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Hit Lucien where he most needs control and pay the trust between him and Seraphine; break Seraphine's theory in front of the people she brought; widen the inquiry, not the claim.
Want:                               Lucien: a model of the lot he can hold. Seraphine: the people she brought kept safe, and Lucien kept with her.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Lucien: read the lot. Seraphine: anchor him.
  Opposition:                       The lot itself; Seraphine's own theory; Mrs. Carmouche, who turns round and blames the gathering for bringing it.
  Pressed by:                       An emergency response that lags. Authority: the city's services; stated justification: stretched thin; whose interests: the public's. Ordinary friction.
  Turn:                             Someone on the lot says, flatly, that it came all the same.
  Consequence:                      Lucien holds his plumb line up through the shaking instead of taking cover, and it hangs still while his own view will not resolve; he goes under, and Seraphine anchors him, and he lets her sooner than before. She overreaches after. He calls the roll from Mara's book; Baz walks the streets toward Mandeville after a missing boy, and the boy is found. Seraphine tells Baz and Mara about the camp room the morning after: the pressure there with nobody in it, which she kept back when Baz asked what would prove her wrong. With Caro's printout and the story of the supply boat's crew between them, they agree to widen the inquiry, not the claim, and to ask every name at home tomorrow. Her cost is hers to feel: she told these people it was safer.
  Unresolved:                       Tomorrow, every name; why it came; what they are entitled to say.
Change:                             Shared burden becomes usable: Lucien lets her anchor him sooner than before. Neither apologises, and his blame from the dawn is not withdrawn. Seraphine's theory is broken, by her own admission as much as by the event.
Reader experience:                  The trust built since the night on the corner, working under load, at a price paid by other people.

The camp room is memory only: nobody proposes going back and nobody draws a conclusion about a place from it. The widening stays inside what they have (the city's runs, one far story at second hand), and claims nothing beyond New Orleans. Hand on hand is the ceiling. No landmark or compass direction for which way Lucien goes. Nobody reads minds. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.
Exit conditions:                    Late afternoon on the lot; the boy found; the injured seen to; Mara's book holds the roll; Baz and Mara know about the camp room; the three have agreed to ask every name at home tomorrow.
Required observable:                The ground shakes, and bodies with it, across the lot; loose things jump; the plumb line hangs still while Lucien's view of the place will not resolve, and his notebook keeps both.
Required consequence:               Ordinary injuries (Mr. Lionel's arm, Mrs. LaBranche's knee) and a child separated for a while; Seraphine overextends; her theory breaks in front of the people she told it to.
