## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Lucien buys the time he wants with the truth about himself, draws the window, and hears his lie land on Sal.
Want:                               To be right first: the time of the first call at Carrollton, to set a window.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Get the time of the first call at Carrollton.
  Opposition:                       The young policeman at the sawhorse, who won't give him the complaint line's times, and who reads him a question off an index card someone else wrote. The card's author, whoever that is. Sal. Seraphine, who asks who will be here for the people relying on them if they start looking further.
  Pressed by:                       An index card written above the policeman and taken back when he's done: the police's authority, the log as its reason, his superior's interests. A note from R. at Lucien's office, overnight, on routine business: R. advises him to keep a low profile, and adds unasked that the camp report went up in March, not by R. That is a kindness, and information. His own false report, now acted on by the city. The drift: routes that go above.
  Turn:                             The window is drawn: the next two middays.
  Consequence:                      Refused the line's times, he trades his true answer to the card's question (yes, it has happened to him before) for the time in the police log; his susceptibility is now on someone else's paper. Sal calls, with a cousin's story of a galley crew all putting their forks down at once, and with news: a stop-work on his job, because somebody reported it. Lucien says nothing to him. He writes R. one line back, a question about the route, and doesn't send it.
  Unresolved:                       How far to look, and who stays here for the people here.
Change:                             Lucien is on paper where he can't see who reads it, and his lie has landed on Sal.
Reader experience:                  A bargain at a sawhorse, a friend's bad news he caused, and his silence after.
Keep / don't spend:                 Nobody proposes going back to the camp or talks of it. No tally of intervals and no recital of routes: the window takes two lines, and its time is bought. The forks are hearsay, *similar* at most; no claim beyond the city. Lucien's thoughts register the route, never anyone's motive; he voices no distrust of his office, and the line to R. stays unsent. R. is kind and names no one. The policeman keeps his own part and is nobody's proxy; nobody asks Lucien to report or contain anything. Caro gives one time, as a friend.
Exit conditions:                    The Carrollton neutral ground, the same day. The window is set for the next two middays. Sal's job is stopped by the city; Lucien has not told Sal what he did. The unsent line to R. is in Lucien's notebook. Sal's story of the forks is written down, with question marks.
