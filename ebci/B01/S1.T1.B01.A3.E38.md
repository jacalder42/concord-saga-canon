## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Lucien buys the time he wants with the truth about himself, draws the window, and hears his lie land on Sal.
Want:                               To be right first: the time of the first call at Carrollton, to set a window.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Get the time of the first call at Carrollton.
  Opposition:                       The young policeman at the sawhorse, who won't give him the complaint line's times, and who reads him a question off an index card someone else wrote. The card's author, whoever that is. Sal. Seraphine, who asks who will be here for the people relying on them if they start looking further.
  Pressed by:                       An index card written above the policeman and taken back when he's done: the police's authority, the log as its reason, his superior's interests. A note from R., his contact at the Hollen Institute, overnight, on routine business: R. advises him to keep a low profile, says his name has crossed a desk or two there this spring, and adds unasked that the camp report went up in March, not by R. That is a kindness, and information. Lucien's thoughts go to the names he has given: the pump station's guard wrote down his name and the Institute's in full. The link is visible to the reader, and nothing certifies it. His own false report, now acted on by the city. The drift: routes that go above.
  Turn:                             The window is drawn: the next two middays.
  Consequence:                      Refused the line's times, he trades his true answer to the card's question (yes, it has happened to him before) for the time in the police log; his susceptibility is now on someone else's paper. Sal calls, with a cousin's story of a galley crew all putting their forks down at once, and with news: a stop-work on his job, because somebody reported it. Lucien says nothing to him. He writes R. one line back, a question about the route, and doesn't send it.
  Unresolved:                       How far to look, and who stays here for the people here.
Change:                             Lucien is on paper where he can't see who reads it, and his lie has landed on Sal.
Reader experience:                  A bargain at a sawhorse, a friend's bad news he caused, and his silence after.
Keep / don't spend:                 Nobody proposes going back to the camp or talks of it. No tally of intervals and no recital of routes: the window takes two lines, and its time is bought. The forks are hearsay, *similar* at most; no claim beyond the city. Lucien's thoughts register the route, never anyone's motive. The Institute may be named, and he may think of it as the cover it is; the people behind it are never named or described, he says nothing against it aloud, and the line to R. stays unsent. R. is kind and names no one. The policeman keeps his own part and is nobody's proxy; nobody asks Lucien to report or contain anything. If the policeman asks him to spell a place name, it reads as what it is, a chance to take the answer back, never as a local who cannot spell his own city. Caro gives one time, as a friend.
Exit conditions:                    The Carrollton neutral ground, the same day. The window is set for the next two middays. Sal's job is stopped by the city; Lucien has not told Sal what he did. The unsent line to R. is in Lucien's notebook. Sal's story of the forks is written down, with question marks.
Carry from Act II:                  On Friday night Lucien reported glass disturbance at Sal's Kerlerec house to the city's line, with a day and a time, knowing nothing happened there. Baz refused to be part of it, thinks it wrong, and has promised to tell no one; Seraphine does not know. That Friday morning Lucien had mailed Guidry his amended statement (the true sentence, no reason given), and it reaches Guidry today. If it is on the page here, it is as what follows, never the sending again; his truth reaching the file on the day his lie reaches Sal is left for the reader to see, and nobody points it out. He also sent his routine monthly report to the Institute that Friday, which is the business R.'s note answers. At the pump station, its guard, Curtis, took his name, and Lucien gave the Institute's in full. *Faster* is Baz's bet, from a glazier's order tickets, and Lucien knows it. Lucien found a case beside his own chalk mark on Dauphine the morning after the Marigny ring, and has told no one. He knows about Baz's private column and has agreed with Baz not to tell Seraphine.
