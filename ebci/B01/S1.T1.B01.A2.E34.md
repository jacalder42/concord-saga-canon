## Narrative brief
Story job:                          The block's own rumour takes over from the page, and Seraphine answers it honestly. Care goes on, and helps people without reversing anything.
Want:                               Her own account on the record: to be the person who was there and says so. Here the motive is the truth, and not being made a liar in front of people she serves.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Answer the block's questions straight.
  Opposition:                       Mrs. Arceneaux, who knows Seraphine told Mr. Vidrine and did not tell her, and wants to know why: whether Seraphine was in the dark or chose. Lanette, who wants a comforting lie about the windows: her landlord's men have already pulled two of her old panes, and plywood darkens her front room, so the old-glass idea has cost her (10-03, §391, N10).
  Pressed by:                       The neighbourhood's rumour, turning: *they* are doing it, and there are grey cases on the fences. Its authority is the block's own; its reasons are fear, a real page and real cases; it serves the neighbours' need for someone to hold to account. No will stands behind it, and the narrator neither confirms nor denies it. Two outside wants have reached the block before Seraphine (10-03, §391, N8, N9). Mrs. Arceneaux says a polite woman from the city was on her porch that morning, asking who on the block was told beforehand and who knew; she gave no names, yet. Mr. Vidrine says a young man with his badge turned paid twenty dollars a door for what each household saw and kept (photos, video, a written note), and wrote it down; Lanette took the money; the case went up on the fence after. Neither states an authority or a reason, whose interests they serve is unknown, and neither asks Seraphine for anything.
  Turn:                             Mrs. Arceneaux puts the page and the cases together, and takes up both rumours: that someone is doing it, and that Seraphine brings it.
  Consequence:                      Seraphine tells Mrs. Arceneaux there was a page and her street was in it, and she refuses Lanette's lie. She loses Mrs. Arceneaux, and the block knows. She answers the challenge with the truth of the choice: they could not tell everyone, and chose. She takes its lesson as her own: choosing who counts left someone out. It drives her next choice, toward everyone together and nobody chosen: every household on the block goes on Inez's pad, the untold side first. Visiting Mr. Vidrine, she finds a grey case on the Robertson-corner fence, put there after, carrying the same small asset tag, its code beginning *TD-* (10-03, map §2). Inez comes to the block with her pad, for names for her Friday food boxes. The block's names now matter to three sets of hands: the woman asking who knew, the young man buying what people kept, and Inez's pad, the only one Seraphine chooses to fill (10-03, §391).
  Unresolved:                       The gaps between hits. A neighbour counts two days between them on the block, as her own claim; the earlier one she counts from, by the school on the Wednesday, gets one line of what she saw, as her account, and nobody confirms it. What the woman from the city will do with who knew (10-03, §391, N8).
Change:                             Before: the block trusts her as its caseworker. After: the block knows there was a page, and Mrs. Arceneaux holds a grievance against her. Seraphine has turned from choosing whom to tell toward bringing everyone in.
Reader experience:                  Honesty that costs, among people with reasons of their own.
Keep / don't spend:                 Ordinary ways people steady each other help, and reverse nothing; if anyone says control was never the promise, it is said once, by a person, not as a lesson. No law is claimed, and the block's disagreements stay human, not lore. The case shows no reading, and its tag is noticed, never explained; nobody says whose it is (10-03, map §2), and nobody says the three are watched. Inez asks for names for her own reason; she recruits no one and joins nothing. If the Vein appears, Trip hosts and settles no argument. The instrument is a *case* on the page, so it is never confused with Inez's food boxes. The woman is *from the city* on the page and nothing more; the young man is described, never identified, and nobody connects him or her aloud to the case; neither visit is given an hour beyond *that morning*. The buyer pays for what people saw and kept, not for a time (10-03, §391, the author's caution). Both are reported by neighbours; Seraphine meets neither (10-03, §391, N8, N9).
Exit conditions:                    Saturday. Mrs. Arceneaux's grievance stands. Inez has every household's name on the block, the untold side included, and Seraphine put them there. The Robertson case is still on the fence. Mrs. Arceneaux has given the woman from the city no names, yet. Lanette has the young man's twenty dollars, and plywood in her front room (10-03, §391, N8–N10).
Carry from Act I:                   Inez and Seraphine already know each other: from the phone and the forms, and from the night Seraphine fell asleep at Inez's pantry and left her card for one of Inez's regulars, who has not called (Seraphine does not know her name).
