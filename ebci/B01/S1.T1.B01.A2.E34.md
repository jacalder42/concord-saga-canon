## Narrative brief
Story job:                          The block's own rumour takes over from the page, and Seraphine answers it honestly. Care goes on, and helps people without reversing anything.
Want:                               Her own account on the record: to be the person who was there and says so. Here the motive is the truth, and not being made a liar in front of people she serves.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Answer the block's questions straight.
  Opposition:                       Mrs. Arceneaux, who wants to know whether Seraphine was in the dark or keeping quiet. Lanette, who wants a comforting lie about the windows.
  Pressed by:                       The neighbourhood's rumour, turning: *they* are doing it, and there are grey cases on the fences. Its authority is the block's own; its reasons are fear, a real page and real cases; it serves the neighbours' need for someone to hold to account. No will stands behind it, and the narrator neither confirms nor denies it.
  Turn:                             Mrs. Arceneaux puts the page and the cases together, and takes up both rumours: that someone is doing it, and that Seraphine brings it.
  Consequence:                      Seraphine tells Mrs. Arceneaux there was a page and her street was in it, and she refuses Lanette's lie. She loses Mrs. Arceneaux, and the block knows. Visiting Mr. Vidrine, she finds a grey case on the Robertson-corner fence, put there after. Mara comes to the block with her pad, for names for her Friday food boxes.
  Unresolved:                       The gaps between hits. A neighbour counts two days between them on the block, as her own claim.
Change:                             Before: the block trusts her as its caseworker. After: the block knows there was a page, and Mrs. Arceneaux holds a grievance against her.
Reader experience:                  Honesty that costs, among people with reasons of their own.
Keep / don't spend:                 Ordinary ways people steady each other help, and reverse nothing; if anyone says control was never the promise, it is said once, by a person, not as a lesson. No law is claimed, and the block's disagreements stay human, not lore. The case shows no reading; nobody says whose it is, and nobody says the three are watched. Mara asks for names for her own reason; she recruits no one and joins nothing. If the Vein appears, Trip hosts and settles no argument. The instrument is a *case* on the page, so it is never confused with Mara's food boxes.
Exit conditions:                    Thursday. Mrs. Arceneaux's grievance stands. Mara has the block's names. The Robertson case is still on the fence.
