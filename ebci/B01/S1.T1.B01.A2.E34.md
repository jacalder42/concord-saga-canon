## Narrative brief
Story job:                          The block's own rumour takes over from the page, and Seraphine answers it honestly. Care goes on, and helps people without reversing anything.
Want:                               Her own account on the record: to be the person who was there and says so. Here the motive is the truth, and not being made a liar in front of people she serves.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Answer the block's questions straight.
  Opposition:                       Mrs. Arceneaux, who knows Seraphine told Mr. Vidrine and did not tell her, and wants to know why: whether Seraphine was in the dark or chose. Lanette, who wants a comforting lie about the windows.
  Pressed by:                       The neighbourhood's rumour, turning: *they* are doing it, and there are grey cases on the fences. Its authority is the block's own; its reasons are fear, a real page and real cases; it serves the neighbours' need for someone to hold to account. No will stands behind it, and the narrator neither confirms nor denies it.
  Turn:                             Mrs. Arceneaux puts the page and the cases together, and takes up both rumours: that someone is doing it, and that Seraphine brings it.
  Consequence:                      Seraphine tells Mrs. Arceneaux there was a page and her street was in it, and she refuses Lanette's lie. She loses Mrs. Arceneaux, and the block knows. She answers the challenge with the truth of the choice: they could not tell everyone, and chose. She takes its lesson as her own: choosing who counts left someone out. It drives her next choice, toward everyone together and nobody chosen: every household on the block goes on Inez's pad, the untold side first. Visiting Mr. Vidrine, she finds a grey case on the Robertson-corner fence, put there after, carrying the same small asset tag, its code beginning *TD-* (10-03, map §2). Inez comes to the block with her pad, for names for her Friday food boxes.
  Unresolved:                       The gaps between hits. A neighbour counts two days between them on the block, as her own claim; the earlier one she counts from, by the school on the Wednesday, gets one line of what she saw, as her account, and nobody confirms it.
Change:                             Before: the block trusts her as its caseworker. After: the block knows there was a page, and Mrs. Arceneaux holds a grievance against her. Seraphine has turned from choosing whom to tell toward bringing everyone in.
Reader experience:                  Honesty that costs, among people with reasons of their own.
Keep / don't spend:                 Ordinary ways people steady each other help, and reverse nothing; if anyone says control was never the promise, it is said once, by a person, not as a lesson. No law is claimed, and the block's disagreements stay human, not lore. The case shows no reading, and its tag is noticed, never explained; nobody says whose it is (10-03, map §2), and nobody says the three are watched. Inez asks for names for her own reason; she recruits no one and joins nothing. If the Vein appears, Trip hosts and settles no argument. The instrument is a *case* on the page, so it is never confused with Inez's food boxes.
Exit conditions:                    Saturday. Mrs. Arceneaux's grievance stands. Inez has every household's name on the block, the untold side included, and Seraphine put them there. The Robertson case is still on the fence.
Carry from Act I:                   Inez and Seraphine already know each other: from the phone and the forms, and from the night Seraphine fell asleep at Inez's pantry and left her card for one of Inez's regulars, who has not called (Seraphine does not know her name).
