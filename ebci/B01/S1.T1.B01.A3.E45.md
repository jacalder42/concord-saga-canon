## Narrative brief
Story job:                          Care tried against the phenomenon: it succeeds as care and controls nothing. What comes next follows directly.
Want:                               Inez: everyone on the lot fed and counted by name, in her book.
Conflict:
  Objective:                        Feed everyone, and count them.
  Opposition:                       Seraphine, who argues for the gathering as protection and brings her clients on that promise; Mrs. Carmouche, whose idea the gathering was and who keeps her own notebook; Inez's own question, put to Seraphine, of what the gathering is for.
  Pressed by:                       The sexton's rules for the lot. Authority: the parish; stated justification: the lot and the tables are the church's and the tables go back by Thursday; whose interests: the parish's, and his own trust, kept by being checked. Beside it, the city's advice to stay home (authority: the city; justification: public safety). Ordinary friction, with a public warning.
  Turn:                             It comes while the lot is full.
  Consequence:                      Care succeeds as care: ninety-three people, named and fed, on open concrete. Seraphine told two of her clients, Mr. Lionel and Miss Hazel, that they would be safer among people, and they came. Inez hosted for her own count, not for Seraphine's theory. Lucien turned back a table with a bent brace in front of the sexton. Inez heard Mrs. Whitlow tell Lucien, over coffee while he carried tables, that her coffee went to rings before her table's legs moved.
  Unresolved:                       Whether gathering keeps anything away; what the gathering is for, which Inez asked and Seraphine answered with her theory.
Change:                             The gathering works as a gathering. Whether it keeps anything away is answered only by what comes next.
Reader experience:                  Care that stays care while things are unsteady.
Keep / don't spend:                 The narrator never links the gathering, its practices or the crowd to what comes; characters may believe it and be disagreed with. No grid, ritual or magic; the difference between care and control is shown, not said. Inez hosts for her own reason and does not join the team. Seraphine and Lucien are in it, and it is not their scene: only what Inez sees, hears or is told. Mrs. Whitlow's rings stay her own observation, told over coffee: nobody draws a rule from it, builds a warning on it, or says the rings come first. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.
Exit conditions:                    About 29 April, afternoon; the lot full; ninety-three names in Inez's book; Seraphine's clients there on her word; the first moment of the event, before anyone knows what it is.
Carry from Act II:                  Inez kept from Seraphine how the last crowd on a lot broke up. Lucien is off Sal's crew. Seraphine argues from the theory she recommitted to after the long evening, over her own written doubt. Since Sunday, Seraphine knows that Lucien wrote *None* on the form beside her paragraph; she has not spoken to him about it. Inez knows nothing of it, so the page shows only what Inez can see of the two of them.
