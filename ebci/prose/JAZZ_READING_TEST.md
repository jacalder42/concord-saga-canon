# The jazz reading test

The writer profile's §3A carries the author's Jazz Framework as tendencies for the drafter. This file is the reviewer's
side of it: **a reading test for rhythm and pace**, used at act checkpoints and in revision passes. It came out of the
first jazz-lens read of B01 draft 3 (`reports/B01_DRAFT3_JAZZ_LENS_READ_2026-10-01.md` §8).

**These are questions, not quotas.** No score. **Never recommend music on the page.**

## The test

1. **Returns.** For each recurring element (a place, an object, a phrase, a recital of facts), ask whether it is
   **varied** (something changed: the thing, who meets it, or what it costs) or **repeated**.
   - **A return that re-describes or re-recites counts as a repeat, even when the plot has moved.**
   - Keep the one full telling. Cut the others to what changed.
2. **Rests.** For each quiet, domestic, food, walking or recovery passage, ask: **does it change something** (a
   relationship or a choice), **or only recover?**
   - Rests that change can be slow, wherever they sit.
   - Rests that only recover are short.
   - Shorten a rest before moving it.
3. **Rhythm-section carriage.** For each hook and each payoff, ask **who carries it**: a side character, a joke, a
   candid line, or the protagonist's private restatement.
   - The last reads as exposition. *"Breadcrumb pace should be driven by our rhythm section"* (the author).
   - Count the **kinds** of accent too (humour, sensuality, candour, surprise, a side character's own beat), not only
     their presence. One instrument played everywhere is a drought.
   - Note stretches where hooks are laid with no payoff.
4. **Cadence by position.** Tag every chapter and scene ending by type: a still image, someone not saying, a departure,
   a humour button, a decision, an open line.
   - **Flag runs of five or more of one type in mid-act.**
   - Act endings are judged separately.
5. **Call and response.** Map who answers whom, and from what floor.
   - Protect the pairs that work together, so a cut does not take half of one.
   - Flag solos that go unanswered for longer than a chapter, and acts where the ensemble thins.
6. **For the line pass: no interpretation inside a peak.** In a peak, action, interruption and silence carry the
   rhythm. Explanation and reflection wait until after it, or are cut. **A character's own misreading is not
   interpretation.**

**Elevator stretches.** Also watch for **runs of beats that each land exactly when and as foreshadowed**. That is the
author's *"Without effective syncopation and accent, it's just elevator jazz."* The remedy is cutting or re-ordering
existing beats, not adding new ones.

**Not used:**
- the 2-and-4 pulse, which does not discriminate at chapter level;
- the five-axis checklist.

## When it runs (Q-JZ5)

- At each act checkpoint of B02 drafting, and of later books.
- Once on B02's prose packets after they are derived. That run decides whether the EBCI control layer gains one
  rhythm-section line (who carries this episode's hook or payoff).
- In revision passes, as the source of cuts, compressions and re-assignments.

## Background (Q-JZ6)

**The braid,** from Calder OS 1.8 *Braid & Groove Frameworks* (`recovery/JAZZ_FRAMEWORK_SOURCE_RECOVERY_2026-10-01.md`
§7.2). It is a reader's background, not a rule:
- a **rotating spotlight**: no single thread dominates;
- **tension redistribution**: when one strand tightens, the others compensate;
- **unbraid** when a reveal must land cleanly, a character needs a solo, or the reader needs breath;
- **tighten** as stakes rise and arcs converge.

**Its five-beat scene groove is a scaffold. It stays out of the profile and out of this test.**
