# Book 1, Episode 41 (working label: After the Breaking)

Seraphine's point of view. The center at closing; the drives across the city; the places people sit down, the Velvet Vein among them. The same Wednesday, evening and night. About 3,600 words.

**What this scene is for.** Make the aftermath human before anyone tries another answer, and let Seraphine meet her limit. Ordinary care dominating: more of it needed than she has, and some of it left undone.

**What she wants here.** To be the one who holds it. Tonight, to get the twenty-six people stranded at the center home herself.

**Who pushes back.** Denise, who closes the center on time. The city's held transport. Her own body.

**Pressing on her from outside.** The center's closing rule: Denise's authority as her employer; the building, the staff and the center's liability as the reason; the center's interests, and in Denise's view Seraphine's. Beside it, the city's hold on transport, whose reason does not reach her.

**Already true.**
- Her theory is already hers and already said aloud. Since Renita's block she has said it comes down on people carrying too much, alone, and goes easier on people who are looked after. By her own answer, what would prove her wrong is someone carrying nothing, found just as hard.
- The Marigny house felt like proof to her. After Ursulines she wrote in her own notebook that Mrs. Arceneaux was not alone, and put a question mark after it.
- So tonight is not where the theory begins. The hall is the evidence she chooses to set against her own question mark, and she decides to act on the theory.
- She has not slept since the night in the car on Kerlerec, where she learned of the trap and Lucien blamed her for holding his arm.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- In the hall, before closing: one person among the stranded has an ordinary want she cannot meet, papers or an errand the holds have stopped, and asks her where to take it; she has no answer yet. The person is ready and waiting, not frightened: the hall stays steady.
- Closing time, and the center's one car, which she takes.
- The drives home, in shifts.
- After the last drive, at the Vein, before the door opens: Trip, working her own room, cannot reach everyone either. A hand lifted for her and missed, more than once; a breath at the register. Seraphine sees it, half-rises to say something, and doesn't, because it is not her room and Trip already knows. Trip stays competent and wordless about it; the narrator draws no lesson. It must not soften what follows.
- Then someone at the door asks whether this is the place to come, and she does not get up.

**What it costs or changes.** She drives them home and tells people she is fine when she is not. What it leaves in the body: hands she has to hold with her other hand to write the last trip in the log, a cheek bitten to stay awake at a light, and a signature on the write-up that comes out smaller than her own. Denise writes her up for Mr. Vidrine's tank going out on her signature, says the write-up is not her verdict on whether Seraphine was wrong, and still gives her the car; the write-up goes in her file. From the evening she decides: the hall where people waited together was steadier, so being looked after must keep it off. She commits to keeping people together. The night on Kerlerec visibly feeds that certainty: after it she wants one thing she can be sure of, and sets the hall against her own question mark. She meets the limit of being useful to everyone, and reads it as a lesson about keeping people together.

**Still open.** Whether she gets up. A theory she now means to act on, and has not tested.

**Keep off the page.** Her limit is met, not mended: nobody looks after her back into shape. No rule about when or how she feels it. The narrator never endorses her theory; it is hers, and so is the link from Kerlerec to it, shown in what she reaches for, never explained. The write-up is Denise's own decision, by the center's rules. Nothing is added to her notebook. At the Vein people pass the rumor's *they* between tables; Trip knows which rumor is going around and never which theory is right. Any musician on the Vein's stage stays unnamed and gets no scene of their own. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Late that night. The twenty-six are home; the car is back at the center; Denise's write-up for the tank is in her file; Seraphine is home; she has told at least one person she is fine. A short after at the Vein may run on: the woman from the door goes home on a neighbor's arm, and Seraphine leaves when the chairs go up.

**Rebuild (the section before the sixth trip, if rebuilt).** A rebuild of this one passage, not the chapter.
- *Entrance:* after the fifth trip's lapse at a light; dark has come down; six people are still on the center's steps; she stands under the porch light with the key.
- *Exit:* she has decided that being looked after keeps it off, nearly sure; the question mark in her notebook stays where it is; she goes to get the car.
- *Keep:* the steady hall as the evidence she chooses, set against her own written doubt; her own account of why the Square's crowd does not count against it; the notebook not taken out, and nothing added to it; the night on Kerlerec feeding the certainty, shown only in what she reaches for, with the cat-night memory and the holding on in it, and the grievance left standing; no narrator endorsement.
- *Gain:* one physical anchor in the present (something on the steps, in her hands, or in her body) and one memory, not a stack of them joined one after another. Let the steadiness she later finds in telling Miss Hazel she is sure grow from this same anchor rather than arrive as a separate statement.
- *Guards:* no clock time; no rule about when or how she feels it; the hall stays steady; her theory is hers.
