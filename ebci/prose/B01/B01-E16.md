# Book 1, Episode 16 (working label: Baz Gets to Arrive)

Baz's point of view. The last of his drive in from Houston; the Velvet Vein on a Monday night. Late March, the Monday after the Square, at night. About 2,400 words.

**What this scene is for.** Baz as a person before he is a method: the city's pleasures through a new pair of eyes.

**What he wants here.** To arrive as himself: one of them here, not a method sent for.

**Who pushes back.** Lucien, whose hand keeps going to the notebook because he wants to begin.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Nothing with a will. He is a visitor without standing: nobody here has asked him for anything, and he holds no role in the city.

**Already true.** Baz drove in from Houston, where he was finishing a documentation project for a housing group. He and Lucien once did a tenants' study together in Marseille. Seraphine asked for him to be brought to the Vein on its beans night. He is sleeping on Lucien's floor. Before all of this, Baz and Lucien worked together in Vienna, and Baz left that work; Houston and the Marseille study belong to his life since. Why he left is his own: unspoken here, and never narrated in full from inside him.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Baz arrives, the long way in.
- Lucien and Baz at the Vein, with Seraphine there.
- He puts the case off until the morning: the friend before the case.

**What it costs or changes.** He arrives as a person, and the case waits a night.

**Still open.** The start they agree for the next morning.

**Keep off the page.** Any clue in the meal, and any shadow over him or his plans. He is not Lucien's mentor, professor or subordinate. Trip welcomes him on the Vein's own terms. Seraphine is there, and meets him as a person, not as a case.

**Ends.** Monday night. Baz is staying at Lucien's. They have agreed to start the next morning. Seraphine has met him.
