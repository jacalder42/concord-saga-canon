# Book 1, Episode 14 (working label: A Pulse Over Jackson Square)

Seraphine's and Lucien's points of view, handed over at a break. Jackson Square. About 17 March, midday. About 4,500 words.

**Tempo.** A peak: in the event, shorter sentences and paragraphs, more white space, more action and spoken lines; the quiet before and after may keep the long sentences.

**What this scene is for.** The first undeniable event on a city scale: many people share it at once, and nobody can say what it was. Undeniable in fact, disputed in cause.

**What they want here.** Seraphine: to be the one who reaches the hurt, and to get three hurt strangers seen to. Lucien: to find what failed, with what he has on him.

**Who pushes back.** Clement, the performer, against a man in a Saints jersey who says Clement's amplifier did it. The city's new painted line on the stone, and the order it stands for. Then the thing itself, which nobody controls.

**Pressing on them from outside.** The city's ordinary response: a young policeman taking names and accounts, who writes with more care than the moment seems to need, for a city that will want an official cause. His authority is the police's; his reason, that an incident with injuries needs a record; the interest, the city's. His own part is reasonable.

**Life in the scene.** Clement's music, before it stops, is good, and the people who stop for it are a working crowd with their own afternoons. The reader should want the set to go on.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Before it, at her client's table, asked who he is, Seraphine gives the same answer she gave her landlady the day before, and hears herself give it, a beat late. One internal clause; nothing said aloud; her client unbothered.
- The Square at work. This time Seraphine feels nothing coming.
- A disturbance that some people hear as a low sound. A performer's amp quits, and everyone in the Square goes down where they stand, at once and for seconds, facing every way. Clement's phone, clipped to his rig's stand, keeps recording, and its picture holds steady while the people in it fall.
- Seraphine reaches three hurt strangers. Lucien, down himself, checks the amp on the stone, finds no fault, and reads how the fallen lie.
- Hundreds of people had it at the same moment. The aftermath, disputed; names taken. Lucien, who went down in front of the Square, gives his name, and, asked what he does, the Institute's.
- Afterwards she tells Lucien she will log her own side every time: whether she felt it early, late or not at all, where she was, who was there.

**What it costs or changes.** Minor injuries and a dead rig. Two ordinary recordings exist. Testimony disagrees. The three strangers are seen to. Lucien cannot make what he perceives resolve. Seraphine or Lucien may say, as their own limited finding, that everyone on the steady recording went down at once, as near as one recording can show: a finding about that recording, not a law, and no explanation. Private has become public: what Lucien has hidden since the camp has now happened to the city, and the policeman has his name and the Institute's.

**Still open.** What the city will make of it.

**Keep off the page.** Any mechanism or frequency. The crowd, the performer, or Seraphine and Lucien's closeness as the cause; the page never settles the cause, though people may argue fault, casualty or coincidence, and the amp check proves nothing either way. The Square remembering anything. Light, color or changed geometry. Any link to anything larger or anywhere else. It needs no name: *pulse*, loosely, or nothing. Care reaches individuals; it doesn't calm the Square. The fallen give Lucien no direction, and he draws no rule from them. Whatever he thinks of how the policeman writes stays his impression; nobody says who will read it.

**Ends.** Midday into afternoon, the Square. Clement has his own recording; a boy's phone has another. The amp is silent, and Lucien's check found no fault. The policeman has Lucien's name, the Institute's name and his account. The three strangers Seraphine reached have been seen to. Seraphine has begun a log of her own perception. The piece need not close on a summarizing line: if it ends on her terms, they are said, not set as an epigram.
