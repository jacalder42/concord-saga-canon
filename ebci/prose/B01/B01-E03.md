# Book 1, Episode 3 (working label: The Day Still Happens)

Seraphine's point of view. Errands and ordinary routes in her New Orleans, around Tremé; that night, the Velvet Vein, a working bar in the Marigny. Late February, the same week: the first of two days off. About 3,800 words, of which the Vein is about 1,200 to 1,600.

**What this scene is for.** Grief in ordinary time. She had a life, and a friend, before any of this. And at night, a room that holds her without being asked: a pleasure she chooses.

**What she wants here.** To get through the day in the calm, capable voice she uses on everyone, and to be let off with it. At night, a room that doesn't need her.

**Who pushes back.** Caro, who needs the money from the double shift she is working, wants the real answer instead of the voice, and wants to call out of the shift to stay. At the Vein, Trip's house rules, lightly.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. Caro's shifts: the double is money she needs. Her boss at the center has given her two days, and they end.

**Life in the scene.** Caro has her own day: her station's people, her money, her own errands on the list beside Seraphine's. One small piece of their shared comic history (a running joke of theirs, the kind only two old friends find funny) comes before the confession, so the steady voice has a friendship to betray. It invents no past the cards don't hold; Caro came south from Chicago.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Errands that have to be done, with Caro.
- Caro refuses the voice.
- Seraphine chooses to tell her about the folder: the card's renewal, left unfinished on her desk. It is the first time she has told anyone. When it comes out, Caro, who is never still, goes still, and her stillness is what lets Seraphine keep going. It comes before Caro's disagreement and does not soften it.
- At six, the call to Odile. Seraphine has the sentence ready and doesn't say it.
- That night, the Velvet Vein (below).

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Caro knows now, and goes to her shift. Nothing between them is fixed by her knowing.

**Still open.** The daily evening call to Odile. Monday's beans at the Vein.

**Keep off the page.** Any clue. If Trip places Seraphine in the evening at the Vein, it is as someone Inez's pantry sends people to; Inez is not Seraphine's boss, and nothing suggests she is. Caro needs no introduction and is not on standby; her own day and her own shift are welcome on the page. At the Vein: no talk of the strange, and the passage works without a line of it; Trip explains nothing; the room is nobody's headquarters; the musicians are incidental, and none is set up to matter; Trip's interest in her is regard and house rules, never flirtation; no penalty for her small rule-breaking. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**The Vein, that night.** After the call, home is unbearable, and she goes out rather than sit in it. The Velvet Vein is a real working room hosted by Trip: crowded and warm and busy with its own business, with its own written rules and habits, and nobody in it needs her. Fixed: she stops watching the door; she plays one more song than the house allows, and Trip lets it stand; Trip offers Monday's beans. Its purpose is to be staged, not asserted: **she is looked after without asking.** The regulars have their own business and let her into it on their terms. At least one act of the room's care reaches her, or someone near her, that she did not ask for and does not have to manage: the room, or Trip, handles it, not Seraphine.

**Ends.** That night, after the Vein. Caro knows about the card and has gone to work; nobody else does. Seraphine has kept her call to Odile without telling her. She goes home late; the Vein is a room she means to come back to, and her answer to Caro's midnight check-in surprises Caro. A short after-scene, going home, is allowed.

**Rebuild (the Vein passage).** If the passage is rebuilt rather than revised, it keeps:
- *Entrance:* the same night, straight after the six o'clock call where she didn't say it, and an apartment she can't stay in.
- *Exit:* home late; the Vein a room she means to come back to; Caro's midnight check-in answered with something Caro does not expect of her.
- *Required:* she stops watching the door, and knows it only by the difference; the extra play, and Trip's mark against it, rubbed out; Monday's beans offered, with Trip's dry certainty that she won't come; Trip knows her as someone Inez's pantry sends people to.
- *Guards:* nothing strange; no talk of why; the musicians incidental; Trip's regard, not flirtation; no penalty; nothing makes Inez read as Seraphine's boss; no clock.
- *Gains:* one act of the room's care she did not ask for; the regulars as a community with their own talk that lets her in (an argument with a stranger that she can lose on purpose, say); if grief comes near, strangers' tact, and someone other than Seraphine handling it.

It is regrown inside this chapter. It becomes a chapter of its own again only if the rebuild reads as a patch.
