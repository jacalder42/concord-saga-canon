# Book 1, Episode 20 (working label: Poydras)

Baz's point of view. A city building on Poydras Street: its plaza, security arch and lobby; then the neutral ground outside. Late March, a Thursday, nine days after the Square, morning into lunch. About 3,400 words. The lobby's ordinary traffic and the walk under the oaks keep their room.

**What this scene is for.** The city's interest acquires a will. Lucien is called to a city meeting about the Square, by a letter that reaches him through the Hollen Institute, his employer; Baz waits in the lobby and watches what is asked for, what is held back, and who does not sign in. Elisabet enters, on her own work. Among the people going up, Baz recognizes a man from the work he left, and keeps it to himself. A civic building that is polite, busy and not quite telling the truth; then an argument between Elisabet and Lucien worth having, in which she flirts with him openly while Baz watches.

**What he wants here.** To see what the city wants, and who is doing the wanting.

**Who pushes back.** The city's table, which wants the records of where and when, and whatever other records people kept. Clement, down from his own appointment, who refuses to give up the phone that holds his mother's voice. The guard, whose job is the sign-in book. Then Lucien's own caution: he brings three photocopied pages and leaves his notebook at home.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Baz is a visitor without standing in a city building. What presses here is new: a tenth-floor room booked under no name; three people with lanyards turned face in, who do not sign the guard's book; records held for someone referred to only as "upstairs". Two different wants show in it: the booked room, the hold upstairs and the question nobody asks Lucien are interested in what he knows and keeps in his notebook; the three with turned lanyards ask, where Baz can hear, for what they want: where it happened, when, and what other records anyone kept of it (lists, logs, recordings, Elisabet's pump records among them). Show what they ask as they ask it, never summed up as times alone. One of the people who go up to the booked room is a man Baz knows from the work he left. No authority, reason or interest is given. It takes, and it asks the three friends for nothing.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The lobby: a room booked under no name, three lanyards turned in, and Baz's question to the guard.
- From the bench, Baz recognizes one of the people going up, a man from the work he left. The man does not see him, and Baz keeps it to himself.
- Elisabet's records held for "upstairs", overheard. Clement keeps his phone.
- Before Lucien comes down, the guard is told to put everyone waiting on the bench in the sign-in book. Baz will not sign, and waits outside on the plaza, where he sees less. Nobody says who asked for the names.
- Lucien comes down to him on the plaza. He gave three pages. The coroner's form with Seraphine's paragraph on it lay face up on the table, and nobody asked him whether this had happened to him before.
- Lucien crosses to a stranger's dig: Elisabet, and a first argument. She flirts with him openly, and Baz watches. Her compliment is said once, to his face, with the look; it is not also given in summary.
- Lucien's defense of the city's hold is his own: he has just kept his own records back, and he argues for a body's right to look before it releases with that in him. It is a real position, not one set up to lose.
- The friends come out of the morning hungry. The walk back under the oaks, and the turn toward lunch, belong to them, not to the case.

**What it costs or changes.** The city holds her paragraph. Lucien chose what the table saw and kept his notebook back, and he writes down Baz's question to the guard, and its answer, in his own hand. Baz has seen three faces the city did not name, and has recognized a fourth, whom he names to nobody. Elisabet has entered, on a visit. The pressure stops being procedure and starts to want something specific.

**Still open.** What the city wants, and who booked the room.

**Keep off the page.** The city's people have no employer or organization named and no stated purpose; Baz sees the three faces clearly, and nobody identifies them. The man Baz recognizes may be named in Baz's head or left unnamed; no institution is given, and Baz says nothing of him to Lucien or anyone. Nobody asks Baz or his friends for anything beyond the meeting's questions to Lucien. The unasked question stays unasked. Lucien says nothing against the Institute or the people he works for. Baz knows the meeting only from what Lucien tells him afterward. Elisabet is here on her own research, on a visit: not recruited, not one of the friends, not here for anyone else, no employer named, and she gives no length of stay. When she gives her name formally, it is Elísabet Arnardóttir; day to day she is Elisabet. She flirts because she enjoys being direct, not because anyone is auditioning pairings, and flirting is not her whole self. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it. No named mechanism or system vocabulary. The narrator certifies no cause of the strange events and states no human cause as fact; the characters may suspect and blame, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them. Nothing foreshadows how Baz's story ends; that someone may be looking for him can be felt. Lucien's account of the meeting and Baz's account of the lobby each come once; neither is told back to the other, and no notes are compared twice.

**Ends.** About midday, Poydras. The coroner's form, with Seraphine's paragraph, stays with the city. Lucien has given three photocopied pages and kept his notebook at home, and has written down the guard's answer about the tenth floor. Baz has seen three faces with lanyards turned in, and knows the room was booked under no name. He has recognized one man going up, and has told nobody. Elisabet's pump records are on hold; she has met Lucien and gone back to her own work. Clement still has his phone. Baz did not sign the guard's book, and waited out the end on the plaza. The walk on toward lunch may run on briefly.
