# Book 1, Episode 20 (working label: Poydras)

Baz's point of view. A city building on Poydras Street: its plaza, security arch and lobby; then the neutral ground outside. Late March, a Thursday, nine days after the Square, morning into lunch. About 3,400 words.

**What this scene is for.** The city's interest acquires a will. Lucien is called to a city meeting about the Square; Baz waits in the lobby and watches what is asked for, what is held back, and who does not sign in. Elisabet enters, on her own work. A civic building that is polite, busy and not quite telling the truth; then an argument between Elisabet and Lucien worth having.

**What he wants here.** To see what the city wants, and who is doing the wanting.

**Who pushes back.** The city's table, which wants every record that carries a time. Clement, down from his own appointment, who refuses to give up the phone that holds his mother's voice. The guard, whose job is the sign-in book. Then Lucien's own caution: he brings three photocopied pages and leaves his notebook at home.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Baz is a visitor without standing in a city building. What presses here is new: a tenth-floor room booked under no name; three people with lanyards turned face in, who do not sign the guard's book; records held for someone referred to only as "upstairs". No authority, reason or interest is given. It takes, and it asks the three friends for nothing.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The lobby: a room booked under no name, three lanyards turned in, and Baz's question to the guard.
- Elisabet's records held for "upstairs", overheard. Clement keeps his phone.
- Lucien comes down. He gave three pages. The coroner's form with Seraphine's paragraph on it lay face up on the table, and nobody asked him whether this had happened to him before.
- Lucien crosses to a stranger's dig: Elisabet, and a first argument.

**What it costs or changes.** The city holds her paragraph. Lucien chose what the table saw and kept his notebook back, and he writes down Baz's question to the guard, and its answer, in his own hand. Baz has seen three faces the city did not name. Elisabet has entered, on a visit. The pressure stops being procedure and starts to want something specific.

**Still open.** What the city wants, and who booked the room.

**Keep off the page.** The city's people have no names, no employer and no stated purpose; Baz sees the three faces clearly, and nobody identifies them. Nobody asks Baz or his friends for anything beyond the meeting's questions to Lucien. The unasked question stays unasked. Lucien says nothing against the people he works for. Baz knows the meeting only from what Lucien tells him afterward. Elisabet is here on her own research, on a visit: not recruited, not one of the friends, not here for anyone else, no employer named, and she gives no length of stay. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it. No named mechanism or system vocabulary. The narrator certifies no cause and no blame. Nothing ominous around Baz.

**Ends.** About midday, Poydras. The coroner's form, with Seraphine's paragraph, stays with the city. Lucien has given three photocopied pages and kept his notebook at home, and has written down the guard's answer about the tenth floor. Baz has seen three faces with lanyards turned in, and knows the room was booked under no name. Elisabet's pump records are on hold; she has met Lucien and gone back to her own work. Clement still has his phone.
