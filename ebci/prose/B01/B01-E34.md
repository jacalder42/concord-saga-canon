# Book 1, Episode 34 (working label: The Block Knows)

Seraphine's point of view. The Ursulines block and the Robertson corner, in the Tremé. The next day, a Saturday. About 3,300 words.

**What this scene is for.** The block's own rumor takes over from the page, and Seraphine answers it honestly. Care goes on, and helps people without reversing anything. Honesty that costs, among people with reasons of their own.

**What she wants here.** Her own account on the record: to be the person who was there and says so. Here the motive is the truth, and not being made a liar in front of people she serves. Today: to answer the block's questions straight.

**Who pushes back.** Mrs. Arceneaux, who knows Seraphine told Mr. Vidrine and did not tell her, and wants to know why: whether Seraphine was in the dark or chose. Lanette, who wants a comforting lie about the windows.

**Pressing on her from outside.** The neighborhood's rumor, turning: *they* are doing it, and there are grey cases on the fences. Its authority is the block's own; its reasons are fear, a real page and real cases; it serves the neighbors' need for someone to hold to account. No will stands behind it, and the narrator neither confirms nor denies it.

**Already true.** Inez and Seraphine already know each other: from the phone and the forms, and from the night Seraphine fell asleep at Inez's pantry and left her card for one of Inez's regulars, who has not called.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The block's questions. Mrs. Arceneaux puts the page and the cases together, and takes up both rumors: that someone is doing it, and that Seraphine brings it.
- Seraphine tells Mrs. Arceneaux there was a page and her street was in it, and the truth of the choice: they could not tell everyone, and chose.
- Lanette's lie, refused.
- Visiting Mr. Vidrine, she finds a grey case on the Robertson-corner fence, put there after, carrying a small asset tag whose code begins *TD-*.
- Inez comes to the block with her pad, for names for her Friday food boxes. Seraphine takes the lesson as her own, that choosing who counts left someone out, and it drives her next choice: everyone together, nobody chosen. Every household on the block goes on Inez's pad, the untold side first.

**What it costs or changes.** She loses Mrs. Arceneaux. Before, the block trusted her as its caseworker; after, the block knows there was a page, and Mrs. Arceneaux holds a grievance against her. Seraphine has turned from choosing whom to tell toward bringing everyone in.

**Still open.** The gaps between hits. A neighbor counts two days between them on the block, as her own claim; the earlier one she counts from, by the school on the Wednesday, gets one line of what she saw, as her account, and nobody confirms it.

**Keep off the page.** Ordinary ways people steady each other help, and reverse nothing; if anyone says control was never the promise, it is said once, by a person, not as a lesson. No law is claimed, and the block's disagreements stay human, not lore. The case shows no reading, and its tag is noticed, never explained; nobody says whose it is, and nobody says the three of them are watched. Inez asks for names for her own reason; she recruits no one and joins nothing. If the Vein appears, Trip hosts and settles no argument. The instrument is always a *case*, so it is never confused with Inez's food boxes.

**Ends.** Saturday. Mrs. Arceneaux's grievance stands. Inez has every household's name on the block, the untold side included, and Seraphine put them there. The Robertson case is still on the fence.
