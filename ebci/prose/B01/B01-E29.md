# Book 1, Episode 29 (working label: Marigny Pressure Check)

Seraphine's point of view. The Marigny: an area along Esplanade where it holds, with a grieving house in its middle. The same Saturday, morning to early evening; the window has not opened. About 3,500 words.

**What this scene is for.** Restraint, rewarded. A slow one comes up in the Marigny and holds still, and its middle is a house holding a repast. Seraphine tests the edges and holds back from the house she wants to enter. A choice against instinct, proved sound, and a proof the reader can see is one coincidence.

**What she wants here.** To go into the grieving house and be the one who holds it, and to test what she believes about who it comes to. Today: to reach the house; meanwhile, to walk the edges in and out so Lucien can mark them.

**Who pushes back.** Baz, who argues that nobody on these blocks has been told anything, so going in spends the only clean witnesses they have. A news crew, which climbs the mourners' steps. Her own fear of being too late.

**Pressing on her from outside.** A news crew on a mourner's steps: no authority but the camera's, the story as the reason, the station's interest. Ordinary, and self-serving.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- It comes up and holds still: if anyone calls it a first, it is the first they have mapped that holds still. She walks the edges in and out, and Lucien chalks them. Where the chalked edge crosses from old paving to new without any change, she sees him stop, and say nothing.
- The middle is a house in mourning. Baz argues to stay out, briefly: holding against telling, and nobody restates the page they wrote. Lucien resists, wanting the middle, then comes round, giving way to her look. If it serves, his attraction to Seraphine may bend his acceptance of staying out.
- The crew climbs the steps. Her one move toward the house stops at the road.
- Later that day the house laughs, and the pressure falls. The house feeds them.

**What it costs or changes.** Restraint is rewarded: the edges held all day, and the house got through its day without her. Seraphine takes the fall as proof that people who are cared for are spared it. Her theory feels proved to her. Lucien has seen something at the paving and keeps it to himself.

**Still open.** Whether it counts on the page.

**Keep off the page.** Movement: it holds in one place all day. What she feels stays in the Marigny, and nothing places it anywhere else or links it to anything later. The pressure rises with the crew on the steps and falls with the laughter, in that order, as she perceives it; the narrator never says one caused the other. Lucien's lean on his feet stays his, with no direction, landmark or bearing. Any law stated by the narrator. Any explanation of Lucien's stop at the paving: it is the kind of crossing he once named as something that would prove him wrong, Seraphine does not make that connection, nothing on the page makes it for her, and it says nothing about cause. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it; the chalk marks are Lucien's test, not a tally of times. No named mechanism. The narrator certifies no cause of the strange events and states no human cause as fact; the characters may suspect and blame, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them. Nothing foreshadows how Baz's story ends; that someone may be looking for him can be felt.

**Tempo.** At the peak, shorter sentences and paragraphs, more white space, more action and spoken lines; the long sentences belong to the quiet.

**Ends.** The same Saturday, early evening, the Marigny. Lucien's chalk marks the edge on eleven curbs; the pressure never moved off it, and it has gone. None of the three went into the house. The house has sent them plates. The three are due at the Vein tonight. Lucien has seen the paving crossing and told no one.
