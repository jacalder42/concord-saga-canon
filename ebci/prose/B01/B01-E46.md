# Book 1, Episode 46 (working label: The Rebound)

Lucien's and Seraphine's points of view, alternating, Lucien leading. The church lot and its nearest blocks, within the hour of the gathering; then the evening. The same Wednesday, into the evening. About 5,500 words. At that length, keep two textures the added beats must not displace: her body after the crush, looked after by others, and the lot still working around the table while the three talk.

**Tempo.** In the shaking, short sentences and paragraphs, more white space, more action and spoken lines; the talk afterward may lengthen again.

**What this scene is for.** Hit Lucien where he most needs control, and pay the trust between him and Seraphine. Break Seraphine's theory in front of the people she brought. Widen the inquiry, not the claim. The trust built since the night on the corner, damaged and still working under load, at a price paid by other people.

**What they want here.** Lucien: a model of the lot he can hold; to read it. Seraphine: the people she brought kept safe, and Lucien kept with her; to anchor him.

**Who pushes back.** The lot itself. Seraphine's own theory. Mrs. Carmouche, who turns around and blames the gathering for bringing it.

**Pressing on them from outside.** An emergency response that lags: the city's services, stretched thin, serving the public. Ordinary friction.

**Already true.**
- The camp room is the case Seraphine's own answer on Renita's block named: pressure with nobody in it, nobody carrying anything. Her question mark about Mrs. Arceneaux is the other.
- Lucien's blame from the dawn stands.
- Since Sunday, Seraphine knows that Lucien wrote *None* on the coroner's form, beside her own paragraph, and signed it twice. She has not spoken to him about it.
- Baz has still not told her about his column.
- Lucien has heard Sal's story of the supply boat's crew, and Caro's printout is with Seraphine.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The ground shakes, and bodies with it, across the lot; loose things jump. Lucien holds his plumb line up through the shaking instead of taking cover. It hangs still while his own view of the place will not resolve, and his notebook keeps both.
- The still line is noticed as the clue it is: people went down and the line did not swing. He says what he thinks that means, and that it is only what he thinks.
- Going under, Lucien's need to read the lot does not stop: he keeps trying to take the measure of ordinary things that will not hold still, and tries again. It is shown from inside, at the shaking's tempo, and never explained.
- Lucien going under, and Seraphine: she anchors him, and he lets her sooner than before. She holds his hand knowing what he wrote, and it is not forgiveness. She overreaches after: she goes into the crush at the lot's exit, and Lucien pulls her bodily out of it, as she held his arm at dawn. Nothing structural fails. After the rescue they touch, and the moment is charged, and nothing is declared.
- Someone on the lot says, flatly, that it came all the same.
- The roll: he calls it from Inez's book. It is the lot's own way of counting itself, names said aloud and neighbors answering for each other, and it is Inez's book, not his. Baz walks the streets toward Mandeville after a missing boy, and the boy is found. Back on the lot, the boy has a voice of his own for a moment, a child's practical concern about something that is his. Around them, people argue among themselves about why it came; every cause is in somebody's mouth, and the narrator sides with none of them.
- Her body pays, and others look after it without being asked: by the time she sits down at the table, someone has put something warm around her and someone holds a cup for her because her hands will not. It is exhaustion and shock, nothing she perceives. She lets them, or half lets them.
- When Baz comes back, he starts to tell Lucien, apart from the others, something he has kept from him. Lucien, not yet back from going under and still counting the injured from Inez's book, tells him it will keep. Baz lets it drop.
- The room, admitted: Seraphine tells Baz and Inez about the room where the boy died, as it was the morning after, the pressure there with nobody in it, which she kept back when Baz asked what would prove her wrong.
- Mrs. Carmouche means to report the lot to the city's line. The crew tells her what follows a call; she reports anyway, and this time it is true. Lucien decides to watch the lot at dawn on Friday for whoever brings the case.
- How far to look. With Caro's printout and the story of the supply boat between them, they agree to widen the inquiry, not the claim. **The next move is Seraphine's:** she proposes asking every name at home tomorrow, keeping told and untold apart as her three kinds require, and Baz and Lucien agree. The names are Inez's, and she sets her own terms: streets, not the book, and only the one question. They will meet at midday at the gate with what they have. On the bench, not looking at him costs her, because she knows exactly where he is.
- That evening, in her point of view, the reckoning: it is about accountability. He admits why he signed; his cover limits only who reads his notes. Briefly, he says he has written a correction of his own statement; it excuses nothing, and what it says stays for later. She hears where his answer stops, and says so. She says what the *None* did to her. Her term: he may not submit, paraphrase or speak for her account without her agreement. She leaves dissatisfied, with no touch.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Ordinary injuries (Mr. Lionel's arm, Mrs. LaBranche's knee) and a child separated for a while. Seraphine overextends into the crush and is pulled out, and her theory breaks in front of the people she told it to, by her own admission as much as by the event. What she carries off the lot: ribs that hurt where the crush pressed, knees that will not hold her for a while, hands that remember holding his while she knew what he wrote, and the face of the old woman she promised safety to. Lucien carries concrete ground into his knees and two entries in his notebook he cannot choose between. Shared burden becomes usable: Lucien lets her anchor him sooner than before, and pulls her out when she overreaches. Neither apologizes, his blame from the dawn is not withdrawn, and what she learned on Sunday is not repaired: continued attraction is not repaired trust. The asking at home is the group's next move because of her inquiry.

**Still open.** Tomorrow, every name. Why it came. What they are entitled to say.

**Keep off the page.** The narrator never links the gathering, the practice or the crowd to the event. Nothing learns, answers or responds. No impossible geometry; hanging things hang still; nothing structural fails; no mechanism. The still line is a clue, never an explanation. No claim that it is happening elsewhere. Characters may wonder aloud about a cause and be disagreed with, or left standing. The room where the boy died is memory only: nobody proposes going back, and nobody draws a conclusion about a place from it. The widening stays inside what they have (the city's runs, one far story at second hand), and claims nothing beyond New Orleans. No new notebook entry marks her proposal. The aftermath of the rescue is charged; nothing romantic is said aloud, and the dawn's kiss stays called nothing. Holding his hand, or his holding her, forgives nothing; what he wrote on the form is not raised or settled on the lot. No bearing for which way Lucien goes under. Baz's attempt is seen from Lucien's side only: what Baz meant to say is not said or guessed, it fails for the plain reason on the page, and nothing in it is ominous. Nobody reads minds. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** The evening, after the lot. A short after-scene is allowed where the body needs one: after the crush, after the table, after the reckoning. The boy found; the injured seen to; Inez's book holds the roll; Baz and Inez know about the room; the three have agreed, at Seraphine's proposal, to ask every name at home tomorrow, from streets Inez gives them, and to meet at midday at the gate. Baz began to tell Lucien something and let it drop. Mrs. Carmouche has reported the lot to the city's line; Lucien means to watch the lot at dawn on Friday. The reckoning is done: her term stands, and she has gone home dissatisfied.

**At the table: extracted, not delivered.** This is a disclosure scene with a long list behind it. Let the information be pulled out of people, not set down in order like exhibits.
- **One non-answer.** One question at the table gets a neighboring answer, a deferral or a refusal, and the talk has to go on without it. For example, Baz asks her why she held the room back and she answers with what she did instead; or Inez, asked what she thinks, declines to say. One only.
- **One interruption.** The lot breaks in once, mid-exhibit: the sexton counting, a neighbor arguing cause, the dominoes, the boy. The talk has to find its way back.

**Rebuild: the table** (for a writer rebuilding this passage in place; everything above still holds).
- **Entrance state.** After the ambulance and the boy's return, and after Lucien has told Baz it will keep. The lot has thinned to the people who stayed on purpose. She does not look at Lucien. Baz has cooled: whatever light thing he was about to say, he puts away.
- **Required, in this order where order matters.** Her admission, in her own words: the camp room the morning after first, then Mrs. Arceneaux, and that she kept both back when Baz asked what would prove her wrong. Inez names the room in a few words and turns her book toward the three of them. Baz does not take out his notebook; she reads it as him letting it go, and the narration does not confirm her reading. Caro's printout, and its ninth page. Lucien's two entries, the line and the eye, kept side by side, and his one hypothesis said as only what he thinks. The supply boat's story, at second hand. Widen the inquiry, not the claim. **The next move is Seraphine's.** Inez's terms. Midday at the gate.
- **Exit state.** As in *Ends* for the lot: the three agreed at her proposal, Inez's terms set, the meeting fixed, Baz and Inez knowing about the room.
- **Guards.** Nothing concluded from the camp room; no cause; no clock time; Baz's light remark stays put away; nothing ominous.
- **Gain.** Her exhausted body and others' care for it; the lot still working around the table; the one interruption and the one non-answer above; Inez's own wit. Fewer sentences that sound like testimony.
