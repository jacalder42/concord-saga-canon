# Book 1, Episode 46 (working label: The Rebound)

Lucien's and Seraphine's points of view, alternating, Lucien leading. The church lot and its nearest blocks, within the hour of the gathering. The same Wednesday, into the late afternoon. About 4,100 words.

**What this scene is for.** Hit Lucien where he most needs control, and pay the trust between him and Seraphine. Break Seraphine's theory in front of the people she brought. Widen the inquiry, not the claim. The trust built since the night on the corner, working under load, at a price paid by other people.

**What they want here.** Lucien: a model of the lot he can hold; to read it. Seraphine: the people she brought kept safe, and Lucien kept with her; to anchor him.

**Who pushes back.** The lot itself. Seraphine's own theory. Mrs. Carmouche, who turns around and blames the gathering for bringing it.

**Pressing on them from outside.** An emergency response that lags: the city's services, stretched thin, serving the public. Ordinary friction.

**Already true.**
- The camp room is the case Seraphine's own answer on Renita's block named: pressure with nobody in it, nobody carrying anything. Her question mark about Mrs. Arceneaux is the other.
- Lucien's blame from the dawn stands.
- Baz has still not told her about his column.
- Lucien has heard Sal's story of the supply boat's crew, and Caro's printout is with Seraphine.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The ground shakes, and bodies with it, across the lot; loose things jump. Lucien holds his plumb line up through the shaking instead of taking cover. It hangs still while his own view of the place will not resolve, and his notebook keeps both.
- Lucien going under, and Seraphine: she anchors him, and he lets her sooner than before. She overreaches after.
- Someone on the lot says, flatly, that it came all the same.
- The roll: he calls it from Mara's book. Baz walks the streets toward Mandeville after a missing boy, and the boy is found.
- The room, admitted: Seraphine tells Baz and Mara about the room where the boy died, as it was the morning after, the pressure there with nobody in it, which she kept back when Baz asked what would prove her wrong.
- How far to look. With Caro's printout and the story of the supply boat between them, they agree to widen the inquiry, not the claim. **The next move is Seraphine's:** she proposes asking every name at home tomorrow, keeping told and untold apart as her three kinds require, and Baz and Lucien agree.

**What it costs or changes.** Ordinary injuries (Mr. Lionel's arm, Mrs. LaBranche's knee) and a child separated for a while. Seraphine overextends, and her theory breaks in front of the people she told it to, by her own admission as much as by the event. Her cost is hers to feel: she told these people it was safer. Shared burden becomes usable: Lucien lets her anchor him sooner than before. Neither apologizes, and his blame from the dawn is not withdrawn. The asking at home is the group's next move because of her inquiry.

**Still open.** Tomorrow, every name. Why it came. What they are entitled to say.

**Keep off the page.** The narrator never links the gathering, the practice or the crowd to the event. Nothing learns, answers or responds. No impossible geometry; hanging things hang still; nothing structural fails; no mechanism; no claim that it is happening elsewhere. Characters may wonder aloud about a cause and be disagreed with, or left standing. The room where the boy died is memory only: nobody proposes going back, and nobody draws a conclusion about a place from it. The widening stays inside what they have (the city's runs, one far story at second hand), and claims nothing beyond New Orleans. No new notebook entry marks her proposal. Hand on hand is the ceiling. No bearing for which way Lucien goes under. Nobody reads minds. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Late afternoon on the lot. The boy found; the injured seen to; Mara's book holds the roll; Baz and Mara know about the room; the three have agreed, at Seraphine's proposal, to ask every name at home tomorrow.
