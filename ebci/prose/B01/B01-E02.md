# Book 1, Episode 2 (working label: Too Late)

Seraphine's point of view. The family's camp and the shell road out, at the swamp's edge. Late February, the morning after. About 2,600 words.

**What this scene is for.** To separate grief from something else, and show that the something else persists: care that costs, and a thing that stays when it should not.

**What she wants here.** Her failure on the record. She asks the parish coroner's investigator, Guidry, to write down that she did it wrong.

**Who pushes back.** Guidry, whose procedure records what happened, not blame. Odile, who will not let any of it land on Renée, and who spoke of the card in the night: Seraphine is waiting for her to say it.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. The parish coroner's procedure: the office's authority, an accurate record as its reason, the file's interest. The generator, and a blood result for carbon monoxide that the oxygen given in the ambulance and at the hospital has left equivocal, lead Guidry to ask the parish's contracted firm for a dwelling hazard check: carbon monoxide, ventilation, electrical. The death certificate will issue pending, on the labs.

**Life in the scene.** Someone else's ordinary day goes on near the camp that morning and does not wait for this one: a need of its own, small and loud, that Seraphine notices. It is not a sign of anything. (The outline offered a small child fretting in a car seat; anything that belongs to the people around the camp will do.)

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The aftermath, and her part in it. Guidry's form; her request refused.
- With the boy gone, the pressure is still in the room, with nobody in it carrying anything.
- She tests it herself, walking the road's steps twice: it thins about forty steps out and is gone by about sixty.
- She leaves with a private rule, not yet in words: next time, arrive sooner.

**What it costs or changes.** She doesn't get the record she asked for. What she found on the road, she keeps to herself. Something lingers that doesn't behave like grief, and she won't call it a ghost.

**Still open.** A hazard check will come for the house. The certificate is pending.

**Keep off the page.** The coroner's office, and Guidry's windbreaker, are the parish's; nothing here belongs to a county. The residue stays unnamed: no ghost, no explanation, nothing made of the place. The walk is her own test, and she draws no conclusion from it. Odile's protectiveness is her own, and right. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**Ends.** The morning after. Guidry has said a hazard check will come for the house, and that the certificate will issue pending. Renée's sister has taken Renée to Metairie; Renée gave Seraphine one long look and said nothing. Odile stays. Seraphine leaves the camp alone, with what she found on the road unsaid.
