# Book 1, Episode 49 (working label: Still Water)

Seraphine's point of view. Jackson Square, with separated posts at its quieter edges. The Saturday of the festival weekend, about 2 May. About 5,500 words, with the vignette inside it. The additions fit; keep Miss Tavie's festival (her chair, the spot she used to sit in with her husband, the praline) and the vignette whole.

**What this scene is for.** Pay the model and the book's opening: prediction without explanation or control. They arrive together, and she carries one. One person walks out whole, and nobody can say why. Then she forces the telling of what has been kept from her, and the book ends on one word left in the air, and her first answer to it, done rather than said.

**What she wants here.** To hold her post and watch the still water. Under it, to be the one who holds it, and she chooses how much. At the end, to be told what she has seen and not been told.

**Who pushes back.** The post she promised to keep: tins borrowed from Miss Tavie, filled to the brim and watched. A frightened stranger at the Square's edge. Her own pull to carry more than one. At the end, Lucien and Baz, who have kept it from her.

**Pressing on her from outside.** The gray case is gone from the fence: someone took it away. Whether anyone else measured the window is not known. No stated authority. Yesterday's refusal at the table stands, and the holds are still on. Nobody on the page today asks the three for anything. At the Square's edge a quiet man watches, and Baz goes still at the sight of him; Seraphine sees both. The man comes no closer and asks nothing.

**Already true.**
- Miss Tavie lends the tins. Since St. Roch she asks things of Seraphine as well as giving them.
- Baz keeps the log by the page's rule of three kinds (told before, told after, said after telling), which is Seraphine's.
- Baz's column is still unsettled between him and Seraphine.
- The practice they keep (sit down, the stove off, nobody up a ladder) is a local working rule, nothing more.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The morning, and the posts. Word comes about the oxygen sign-out she put her name to at the depot; whether it has anything to do with yesterday's refusal is honestly unclear.
- At the posts, she sees something changed in Lucien's face since yesterday. She does not know what it is, and she chooses what she shares with him.
- Before anything happens, her one stretch of rest: the carrying is done, the tin is set, nobody needs her yet, and for a while she stops waiting and is only there. It is rest from her job, not foreknowledge and not calm about what is coming, and it is what the event then breaks.
- While the window is open, before anything happens at the Square, a short cut-away to the Velvet Vein before it opens (see the vignette, below).
- Unease rises, and the stranger is caught in it, frightened. She leaves her post before her tin moves, carries that one person, and declines the rest of the Square. She uses the practice on him: he is down and seated, clear of the danger near him, when it peaks, and it visibly changes how it goes for him. When he comes back she learns one true thing about him, a place he is from and one thing he inherited (a tune, or the instrument, from someone in his family), so that the person carried is a person. One fact is not a role. Miss Tavie, who lent the tins and stays by them watching her go, sees the water move and cannot say when. Mrs. Carmouche's post on Royal shivers too; she logs it herself and insists she touched nothing. Baz's post and the middle one stay still.
- What must be seen: still water in tins at two separated posts ripples finely with no wind, inside the window, one logged by Mrs. Carmouche herself and one seen by Miss Tavie with no time to it. People nearby feel a faint unease.
- The cost said aloud. Because she left first, her post records a movement and no onset. Lucien says aloud what her going cost the record, and she accepts it. Once, privately, near his word about cost, one sentence of what she wanted: to be the one who saw it start. She says it to no one, and nothing in it says it worked. The empty place on the fence.
- She forces the telling. From what she has seen herself (Lucien's face changed since yesterday; the quiet man Baz went still at; the file Guidry let go upstairs), she confronts Lucien and Baz together and does not let it go. Lucien's withholding of their name is his own visible choice, with his own reason, not the narration holding something back; and at least one answer comes out of them against resistance. Expect one non-answer (a deferral, or the answer to a neighboring question) and one interruption from the world around them before the telling gets through. They tell her who these people are, without a name for them: Lucien works for them through the Institute, Baz used to, and the quiet man, Caldas, is theirs. They tell her what these people want: the record of where and when it happens, the people who can feel it, and Baz. The people with the cases stay unnamed; Baz may say they are not the same people.
- At the very end, Baz says what he suspects they are seeking: the Veil. It is his suspicion, said once, and nobody answers it. His only ground: before he left Vienna, he once saw the word on something of theirs, and he never learned what it meant. He says so briefly; it explains nothing.
- The after-shock, hers, at once and brief: these people are not counting events, they are looking for something behind them, and her log and Lucien's page are a map toward it, with her in it. She changes how she keeps her log, from now on so that only she can read it: one small, concrete act, not a speech. Nothing says what the Veil is.

**What it costs or changes.** It costs her body, and part of what it leaves belongs to her own act: the hand that held his, or the shoulder he fell against, keeps the shape of it after the general shaking and cold have started. A woman nearby misreads her care in some comic way that certifies nothing about any cause; nobody asks her a question about it. If anyone says what the result means, it is evidence against the chance model they tested, not proof; the model is useful, not authoritative. The quiet things anyone can check (Miss Tavie by the tins, Mrs. Carmouche's own log) may stand as the practice having mattered for Jody; the narration never claims a cause. Arrive together, and carry one: she helps one person, by choice, and not the whole Square. She knows now who these people are and what they want, from Lucien and Baz themselves; nothing between her and Lucien is repaired by it. Her log, kept to be shared until now, becomes something she keeps so that only she can read it.

**Still open.** Who else was measuring. Whether Baz is right, and what the Veil is. What the word meant on the thing Baz saw in Vienna.

**Keep off the page.** They are early because of shared work, not speed. The onset comes off to one side and is never timed to the second. The stranger is Jody, a street musician at the Square's edge: not the Square's earlier performer, not the Vein's musician, not a child, and with no role beyond this scene. The others take the rest of the crowd. No mastery, no claim about the world; the words for it stay scattered. Nobody asks her, or anyone, for the time it started or for their record, and no stranger comes asking. What she sees in Lucien's face is her own evidence: she makes them tell her; she does not guess. The people they work for are never named, and the people with the cases stay unnamed. The telling is who and what they want, never how it works or any explanation. *Veil* is said once in this scene, by Baz, at the very end of the telling, as his suspicion: the narration does not confirm it, nothing says what the Veil is or how it works, and nobody answers it. Keep his Vienna sighting terse. Her after-shock is her own inference, a few short paragraphs, not a lecture; the narration does not repeat the word, and his line stays the blow she is answering. The quiet man is seen, not met. Her one choice stays single, and the tin's timing stays uncertain: nobody draws a rule from it, and nobody says the rings come first. No sound, damage, outage or light; the narration never uses his recovery as proof of a cause; no mechanism; she does not hold the whole Square; no child rescue. Keep the closing exchange short: one interpretation, said once, and one ending, not four. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Saturday at the Square, among people, with the festival still going. The video with Baz; Mrs. Carmouche's own log with her; an empty place on the fence where the case was; Seraphine spent; the three together and going on. She knows who these people are and what they want; Baz's word stands, unanswered. She has begun keeping her log so that only she can read it.

**The vignette, about a page, inside the chapter.** It sits while the window is open, before the onset, as a cut-away: the Velvet Vein before it opens that same day, holding incompatible versions of what has been happening in the city, side by side and no longer argued. Nothing comes after Baz's line and her answer to it; this piece ends there. Someone cares more about an ordinary problem (the door's bell) than about any of them. The regular at its center reads as someone the room knows and the reader can place, not a stranger. The core cast need not appear. No reveal, no cliffhanger, and nobody turns out to be right. The city is changed, and the bar still has to open.
