# Book 1, Episode 49 (working label: Still Water)

Seraphine's point of view, then, at the very end, the two presences from the prologue. Jackson Square, with separated posts at its quieter edges. The Saturday of the festival weekend, about 2 May. About 4,700 words, with the vignette and the reprise after it.

**What this scene is for.** Pay the model and the book's opening: prediction without explanation or control. They arrive together, and she carries one. One person walks out whole, and nobody can say why.

**What she wants here.** To hold her post and watch the still water. Under it, to be the one who holds it, and she chooses how much.

**Who pushes back.** The post she promised to keep: tins borrowed from Miss Tavie, filled to the brim and watched. A frightened stranger at the Square's edge. Her own pull to carry more than one.

**Pressing on her from outside.** The gray case is gone from the fence: someone took it away. Whether anyone else measured the window is not known. No stated authority. Yesterday's refusal at the table stands, and the holds are still on. Nobody on the page today asks the three for anything.

**Already true.**
- Miss Tavie lends the tins. Since St. Roch she asks things of Seraphine as well as giving them.
- Baz keeps the log by the page's rule of three kinds (told before, told after, said after telling), which is Seraphine's.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The morning, and the posts.
- Unease rises, and the stranger is caught in it, frightened. She leaves her post before her tin moves, carries that one person, and declines the rest of the Square. Miss Tavie, who lent the tins and stays by them watching her go, sees the water move and cannot say when. Mrs. Carmouche's post on Royal shivers too; she logs it herself and insists she touched nothing. Baz's post and the middle one stay still.
- What must be seen: still water in tins at two separated posts ripples finely with no wind, inside the window, one logged by Mrs. Carmouche herself and one seen by Miss Tavie with no time to it. People nearby feel a faint unease.
- The cost said aloud. Because she left first, her post records a movement and no onset. Lucien says aloud what her going cost the record, and she accepts it. The empty place on the fence.

**What it costs or changes.** It costs her body. For once, she got there in time. If anyone says what the result means, it is evidence against the chance model they tested, not proof; the model is useful, not authoritative. Arrive together, and carry one: she helps one person, by choice, and not the whole Square.

**Still open.** Who else was measuring.

**Keep off the page.** They are early because of shared work, not speed. The onset comes off to one side and is never timed to the second. The stranger is Jody, a street musician at the Square's edge: not the Square's earlier performer, not the Vein's musician, not a child, and with no role beyond this scene. The others take the rest of the crowd. No mastery, no claim about the world; the words for it stay scattered. Nobody asks her, or anyone, for the time it started or for their record, and no stranger comes asking. Her one choice stays single, and the tin's timing stays uncertain: nobody draws a rule from it, and nobody says the rings come first. No sound, damage, outage or light; his recovery is never used as proof; no mechanism; she does not hold the whole Square; no child rescue. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Saturday at the Square, among people, with the festival still going. The video with Baz; Mrs. Carmouche's own log with her; an empty place on the fence where the case was; Seraphine spent; the three together and going on.

**After the close: a vignette, about a page.** The Velvet Vein before it opens, the next morning, holding incompatible versions of what happened, side by side and no longer argued. Someone cares more about an ordinary problem (the door's bell) than about any of them. The regular at its center reads as someone the room knows and the reader can place, not a stranger. The core cast need not appear. No reveal, no cliffhanger, and nobody turns out to be right. The city is changed, and the bar still has to open.

**Last: the reprise, a few short paragraphs.** The two presences from the prologue return, slightly more engaged than at the start: intrigued by something that happened, not only watching. One still acts toward silence and stillness (he); the other toward hope, reaching and trying (she). They may speak to each other, and may be named if a name arrives naturally. They perceive, and they do not act. No cosmology, no prophecy, no preview of anything later, and no promise that they will reach anything. A thread may appear, as an image only.
