# Book 1, Episode 39 (working label: The Stop-Work and the Stakeout)

Lucien's point of view. Sal's job, the red-tagged gallery on its jacks; a parked car on the street overnight. The Tuesday evening, the night and the Wednesday dawn, about 21–22 April; the window's first midday has passed with nothing. About 3,000 words. The added beats do not displace the long night in the car.

**Tempo.** From the moment the collector appears, short sentences and paragraphs, more white space, more action and spoken lines; the night in the car keeps its long ones. Compress the preparation, the waiting and any explaining afterward; protect the suspense and Lucien's dangerous choice.

**What this scene is for.** The trap proves one thing, access, and costs Lucien his place on Sal's crew, his phone, and something with Seraphine. A long night in a car, one laugh, a kiss that was only cover and went on too long, and a dawn that costs him what he came to keep.

**What he wants here.** To know who reads the city's complaint line. Tonight, to see who comes for the case his false report brought.

**Who pushes back.** Sal, whose job the city stopped on Lucien's lie. The collector, who keeps a schedule, comes for the case, faces Lucien at the post and will not leave without it. Seraphine, whose hand holds his arm as the car goes, against what he wants.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Sal's contract, stopped by a city red tag: the complaint brought the inspector, and the inspector's own finding (a two-story cottage on bottle jacks, its shoring still under way) stopped the job. Sal's interests are his business and his crew. A collector on a schedule, working the dead hour, who comes for the case and takes it: seen and heard, unnamed, naming no employer, asking no one to join anything.

**Already true.**
- Seraphine learns of the false report here, for the first time.
- The Robertson corner case is hers: she found it, wrote it down and texted the others, and it gave Lucien the idea. Lucien's own Dauphine case is still untold.
- Since St. Roch, Miss Tavie asks things of Seraphine as well as giving them.
- Seraphine's work the next morning is the center, where Denise is watching.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- A case on the jack post, under Sal's red tag: it came to the address Lucien gave the line.
- The confession to Sal.
- Miss Tavie sends food with Seraphine, and terms with it: the dish is to come back, and, for the first time in years, she asks when Seraphine will be home. Seraphine hands the food on to him as an instruction. Later in the night she keeps her word and calls Miss Tavie, who is up.
- The night in the car: Lucien tells her the chain, and the telling is summarized; her reaction is the scene. She learns why the case is there, and chooses to stay. One question from her about him, for his own sake (Vienna, say, or who taught him to waltz). The cat; one laugh, at nothing.
- Toward dawn the collector walks toward the case, past their parked car, and the two of them pose as a couple: a cover kiss, which runs a beat too long.
- When it ends they have lost the moment; the collector is already at the post. Lucien gets out. Words at the post; a face, a voice; on the case, a small printed asset tag with a short code beginning *TD-*, like the one Lucien saw on the case at Easter, with a different number. The collector cuts the ties and takes the case. In the struggle Lucien's shoulder meets the jack post of a two-story house on jacks, and his first reflex is the house: his hands go to the post to steady it, off the case. Lucien's phone, with the night's photographs on it, goes with the collector.
- The collector leaves by car. Lucien steps into the road in front of a car already moving, and Seraphine holds his arm. The car goes, its plate behind its lights.
- After: blame; both of them say the kiss was nothing, and that it did not count; a late start.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Lucien confesses to Sal, and is off the job, unforgiven. Sal's loss is concrete and lasting: men of his crew out of work who need the wages, a client who now doubts him, the job stopped for weeks, with his name on the tag. His photographs are gone with his phone, and with them what else he kept on it: a photograph of the signed page, and his messages with Seraphine about her log. He blames her for the arm. She is late for work. What stays in the body: his shoulder from the post; her hand, which she wipes off once she lets go, as if it had done something by mistake. He now holds evidence of access, that whoever places the cases gets the city's complaint information, which he can't use without confessing how he got it, and a face he could know again. Between Seraphine and Lucien, the trust still works, nothing said repairs the blame, and there is a kiss both have called nothing.

**Still open.** Who, and why.

**Keep off the page.** The failure has a practical cause: the kiss that ran long, the struggle at the post, and a car already moving. The collector stays unnamed: no name, no employer named, no plate read, no recruiting; nobody follows the car. Nobody on the page says what the tag belongs to or reads it as anyone's. The case shows no reading and does nothing. The trap shows access only, not who and not why; Lucien may infer more, and suspect, and the page may lean hard, but the narration never states who sent the collector or what causes the strange events. His thoughts register the route, never a stated motive. The narrator takes no side in the blame: Lucien blames Seraphine, and the page neither agrees nor disagrees. Sal's anger is his own. The kiss is cover that two adults both choose; nothing romantic is said aloud, and both call it nothing. Nobody says the three are watched. Baz is not there. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** The Wednesday morning. The case is gone from the jack post, and Lucien's phone with it and the night's photographs; he replaces the phone that day. Lucien is off Sal's job, and Sal knows what he did; Sal's job is stopped, and his crew is without work. Seraphine knows why the case was there, has not slept, and is late for work. Lucien holds what he saw, and the collector's face. The kiss is between them, called nothing by both. Inside the window, nothing has come yet.
