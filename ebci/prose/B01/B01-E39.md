# Book 1, Episode 39 (working label: The Stop-Work and the Stakeout)

Lucien's point of view. Sal's job, the red-tagged gallery on its jacks; a parked car on the street overnight. The Tuesday evening, the night and the Wednesday dawn, about 21–22 April; the window's first midday has passed with nothing. About 2,500 words.

**Tempo.** At dawn, short sentences and paragraphs, more white space, more action and spoken lines; the night in the car keeps its long ones.

**What this scene is for.** The trap proves one thing, access, and costs Lucien his place on Sal's crew and something with Seraphine. A long night in a car, one laugh, and a dawn that comes to nothing he can use.

**What he wants here.** To know who reads the city's complaint line. Tonight, to see who comes for the case his false report brought.

**Who pushes back.** Sal, whose job the city stopped on Lucien's lie. Seraphine, whose hand holds his arm at dawn, against what he wants. The collector, who keeps a schedule.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Sal's contract, stopped by a city red tag issued on the complaint as it was reported. Sal's interests are his business and his crew. A collector on a schedule, faceless, working the dead hour, who takes and asks nothing.

**Already true.**
- Seraphine learns of the false report here, for the first time.
- The Robertson corner case is hers: she found it, wrote it down and texted the others, and it gave Lucien the idea. Lucien's own Dauphine case is still untold.
- Since St. Roch, Miss Tavie asks things of Seraphine as well as giving them.
- Seraphine's work the next morning is the center, where Denise is watching.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- A case on the jack post, under Sal's red tag.
- The confession to Sal.
- The night in the car: Lucien tells her the chain, and the telling is summarized; her reaction is the scene. She learns why the case is there, and chooses to stay.
- Dawn: a small car pulls in facing them with its lights on, so the plate sits behind the glare and the figure is lit from behind. Someone in a rain jacket, hood up, back turned, lanyard turned in, kneels, cuts the ties clean and takes the case. Lucien goes for the car door, stepping into the road in front of a car already moving, and Seraphine holds his arm. The car leaves.
- After: blame, and a late start.

**What it costs or changes.** Lucien confesses to Sal, and is off the job, unforgiven. Sal's loss is concrete and lasting: men of his crew out of work who need the wages, a client who now doubts him, the job stopped for weeks, with his name on the tag. His photograph is glare. He blames her for the arm. She is late for work. He now holds evidence of access, that whoever places the cases gets the city's complaint information, which he can't use without confessing how he got it. Between Seraphine and Lucien, the trust still works, and nothing said repairs the blame.

**Still open.** Who, and why.

**Keep off the page.** The failure has a practical cause: the lights in their faces, the glare in the photograph, and a moving car. The collector has no face, no name, no plate read and no logo; nobody follows the car. The case shows no reading and does nothing. The trap shows access only, not who and not why; Lucien may infer more, and the page certifies nothing; his thoughts register the route, never a motive. The narrator takes no side in the blame: Lucien blames Seraphine, and the page neither agrees nor disagrees. Sal's anger is his own. Nobody says the three are watched, and nobody asks them for anything. Baz is not there. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** The Wednesday morning. The case is gone from the jack post. Lucien is off Sal's job, and Sal knows what he did; Sal's job is stopped, and his crew is without work. Seraphine knows why the case was there, has not slept, and is late for work. Lucien holds what he saw. Inside the window, nothing has come yet.
