# Book 1, Episode 26 (working label: The Page)

Baz's point of view. The block where he has been sitting in the afternoons; Mr. Benoit's, briefly; then wherever the three sit down to write it. The same Saturday, into the evening. About 3,400 words.

**What this scene is for.** The pattern becomes a falsifiable prediction, committed in writing and signed: a window of days, a place, what they will watch for, what counts as a miss, and a choice to prepare people rather than prevent anything. Method with stakes: a page that will change how people behave, and a friend who loses an argument well.

**What he wants here.** A page that can be wrong. Under it, he wants to belong here more than he wants to be right. Today: a page the three of them can be wrong about, with a clean test inside it.

**Who pushes back.** Renita, who would like the stranger to stop sitting on her neighbor's step so the block can get on with its afternoon. Seraphine, who refuses his split-street design because it would leave households like Renita's untold.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The block's own rules about who gets told and who sits where: the neighbors' own authority, their own day as the reason, their own interests. It is reasonable. Nothing from the city is in this piece.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The block sits down on its own, and would like him to sit somewhere else.
- Baz says the split-street design aloud. Seraphine refuses it.
- He tells Mr. Benoit himself, his last untold witness.
- The page is written and signed by the three. What people are told carries no days and no hour. His dissent goes on the back.

**What it costs or changes.** A bet exists in writing, with three names under it. Baz's clean sample is spent: he chose belonging over a cleaner test, and recorded his dissent instead of refusing.

**Still open.** Ten days.

**Keep off the page.** The page is a bet, not a finding. Its watch items are the characters' own: Lucien's (the street, the ground, an edge) come from his theory of the ground, and Seraphine's (it coming up slowly) from her ears. The page expects each event to be bigger than the last. The narrator certifies none of it. Prepare people; never try to stop anything; no exact-second prophecy. Renita keeps her own reasons: she is tired of it, not hostile. The window's dates stay off the page, though its length may be said. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it; the page is a decision, not a tally. No named mechanism. The narrator certifies no cause and no blame. Nothing Baz plans or enjoys is ominous.

**Ends.** Saturday evening, late March. The page is signed by the three. Lucien keeps it; Baz has a copy with his dissent on the back. Mr. Benoit has been told. The ten-day window is written; it opens in nine days, after Easter. Telling has begun inside its lines, and each telling is recorded.
