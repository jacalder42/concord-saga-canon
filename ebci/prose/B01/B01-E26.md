# Book 1, Episode 26 (working label: The Page)

Baz's point of view. The block where he has been sitting in the afternoons; Mr. Benoit's, briefly; then wherever the three sit down to write it. The same Saturday, into the evening. About 3,400 words.

**What this scene is for.** The pattern becomes a falsifiable prediction, committed in writing and signed: a window of days, a place, what they will watch for, what counts as a miss, and a choice to prepare people rather than prevent anything. And Baz tells Seraphine and Lucien why he left the work he did before. Method with stakes: a page that will change how people behave, and a friend who loses an argument well.

**What he wants here.** A page that can be wrong. Under it, he wants to belong here more than he wants to be right. Today: a page the three of them can be wrong about, with a clean test inside it.

**Who pushes back.** Renita, who would like the stranger to stop sitting on her neighbor's step so the block can get on with its afternoon. Seraphine, who refuses his split-street design, an untold side of the street kept for comparison, because it would leave households like Renita's untold.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The block's own rules about who gets told and who sits where: the neighbors' own authority, their own day as the reason, their own interests. It is reasonable. Nothing from the city is in this piece.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Early in the day Isaiah sends Baz, with his mother's typed yes, a clip from his aunt's praline stand on Frenchmen some days before: every hand in the frame stops at once, and nobody there mentions it.
- The block sits down on its own, and would like him to sit somewhere else.
- Baz says the split-street design aloud, once, to Seraphine. Seraphine refuses it. Lucien's wanting it shows in a look.
- Baz argues for the untold side in good faith, and at length: without a comparison the page can never show whether telling people helps, and one untold street now could spare many later. Seraphine answers each reason. Only then, and slowly, does he hear what he is proposing: a neighborhood kept untold so that the observations stay clean, which is what made him leave. He sees it, and it costs him. He tells Seraphine and Lucien why he left: they kept warnings from a neighborhood so that what they observed there stayed clean, and people were hurt. He says that much, in his own words, and no more.
- Someone says plainly, once, why knowing where and when would help people: told in time, a person can be sitting down with the stove off when it comes. It is said as their reason, and Baz's dissent stands.
- He tells Mr. Benoit himself, his last untold witness.
- The page is written and signed by the three. What people are told carries no days and no hour. Its claim is stated once, plainly: inside the window and inside the lines, it comes again, larger than the last. His dissent goes on the back. When the page's lines are drawn, Isaiah's clip falls outside them: Seraphine records it by her rule (each account in its own kind, nobody moved from one to another for convenience), it does not count for the page, and the window does not move.

**What it costs or changes.** A bet exists in writing, with one claim on it and three names under it. Seraphine and Lucien know why Baz left his old work; neither knows how much he knows about what is happening here. The recognition costs Baz: he has heard himself make the case of the people he left, and he cannot unhear it. Baz's clean sample is spent: he chose belonging over a cleaner test, and recorded his dissent instead of refusing.

**Still open.** Ten days.

**Keep off the page.** The page is a bet, not a finding. Its watch items are the characters' own: Lucien's (the street, the ground, an edge) come from his theory of the ground, and Seraphine's (it coming up slowly) from her ears. The page's one claim is stated once, in terms a reader can hold (the window, the lines, larger than the last); the calendar bookkeeping around it stays off the page. The narrator certifies none of it. Baz's reason for leaving is told once, by him; the people he left are referred to obliquely, in any form. He does not say how much he knows about what is happening here, and the page does not narrate it from inside him. His reasons for the untold side are good ones, argued as such, never a straw man; the recognition is neither quick nor tidy, and it absolves nothing. Prepare people; never try to stop anything; no exact-second prophecy. Renita keeps her own reasons: she is tired of it, not hostile. Isaiah's clip is a seen event: nobody times it, compares its timing or fits it to a pattern, and nobody explains it. A second pitch of the design to Lucien. Drafting talk around the page: its writing keeps only its one claim, Seraphine's reason, the three kinds of account, Baz's dissent and his copy. The window's dates stay off the page, though its length may be said. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it; the page is a decision, not a tally. No named mechanism. The narrator certifies no cause of the strange events and states no human cause as fact; the characters may suspect and blame, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them. Nothing foreshadows how Baz's story ends; that someone may be looking for him can be felt.

**Ends.** Saturday evening, late March. The page is signed by the three. Lucien keeps it; Baz has a copy with his dissent on the back. Mr. Benoit has been told. The ten-day window is written, opening the Monday after Easter. Telling has begun inside its lines, and each telling is recorded. Seraphine and Lucien know why Baz left his old work.
