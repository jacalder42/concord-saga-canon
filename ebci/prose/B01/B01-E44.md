# Book 1, Episode 44 (working label: The Second Statement)

Seraphine's point of view. Lorraine's kitchen in Metairie; then her evening call to Odile. The same Sunday, about 26 April, into the evening. About 2,200 words.

**What this scene is for.** Seraphine tells the truth once, to the person with the strongest claim on it, and puts her own account on the record whole; then she finds what Lucien put on the same record, beside her words. A truth told that buys nothing back, and a truth found that nobody told her.

**What she wants here.** Her own account on the record, in her own name, including what she does not claim; to be believed as the one who was there. Its motive is mixed: the truth, and a refusal to be corrected off the file. Today, to tell Renée, once, that Dré's health coverage had lapsed while its renewal sat on Seraphine's own desk, and to keep her words on the file.

**Who pushes back.** Renée, whose claim is the strongest in the book: it is her son, and she wants the death closed as an ordinary one; she asks what the lapse had to do with it. Lorraine, her sister, who wants Seraphine out of her kitchen. Odile, who has told her she need not keep calling.

**Pressing on her from outside.** The coroner's file is about to close: the coroner's authority; both statements and the record as the reason; the record's interests. Under it, the benefits system whose renewal fell through a crack (a notice sent to an old address), and her own caseload, where the paperwork waited: ordinary friction, and her own share in it. Behind it, the man who came to the family's door after the form went to the city: a will that reached them through her words, unnamed, and nobody knows whether he will come back.

**Already true.**
- Seraphine went to Lorraine's kitchen uninvited once before, after Renée had asked Guidry to close the file and been told the second statement had to be reviewed. Renée asked her the question she had offered to answer, and Seraphine kept her paragraph on the file.
- Renée has said men came to her sister's door asking what minute.
- Seraphine has told Lucien about the lapse, as well as Caro and Odile.
- She has known for weeks that Lucien's *low blood pressure* was a lie, but not what he put on the form. When Guidry gave her a copy of her statement, he said hers she could have and the engineer's was the family's.
- The engineer's form, as Lucien signed it: its employer line names the Hollen Institute; its observation line asks for conditions observed affecting the health or safety of occupants, including the inspector. Her own paragraph is on it, in her words.
- Lucien has since sent Guidry an amended statement. Seraphine does not know it exists, and the family's copy is older than it.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The kitchen, uninvited a second time.
- The lapse, told, and the second statement read to Renée: what she saw, and what she does not claim. Renée tells her to put the lapse in the record, under what she has written.
- Then the family's copy of the engineer's statement, which Renée holds. Seraphine sees it: on the observation line, beside her own paragraph, Lucien wrote *None*, and signed it twice. How it reaches her eyes is the prose's.
- The evening call to Odile.

**What it costs or changes.** She will not take her words off the file. The lapse goes into the statement. There is no forgiveness. Before, Renée unforgiving and the lapse untold; after, Renée unforgiving, the lapse told, and Seraphine's account on the record with the thing against her in it. The *None* reaches her with no explanation, and it changes how she judges Lucien and what she does with him in the days that follow. Before, trust between them that has come through a fight, with his blame from the dawn standing; after, she knows he denied on the record what happened to him in the room she stood in, beside her own words, and nothing is said or repaired. On the call, Odile shows she knows Seraphine told Renée.

**Still open.** The family asks whether the man will come back, and nobody can say. The file will close as it stands. What she will do with what she now knows about Lucien.

**Keep off the page.** The narrator never says whether the lapse made any difference to the boy's death, or whether anyone could have done the renewal sooner; characters may ask it and blame. No forgiveness and no reconciliation; Renée's refusal to forgive stands. The discovery is hers alone: Lucien is not there and gives no explanation, nobody explains why he wrote it, and no call or talk with him repairs it in this chapter. This is the family's house: nobody goes back to where the boy died, or talks of going. The man at the door is only reported, never seen or named; he asked about her minute and her words, never the boy or the place. Nobody asks Seraphine for names or a list. Nothing makes the boy special. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Sunday evening. The second statement, with the lapse in it, is with Guidry. Seraphine is back in the city in time for Odile's call. Renée is unforgiving. The file is due to close as it stands. Seraphine knows Lucien wrote *None* and signed it twice, and has not spoken to him about it.
