# Book 1, Episode 24 (working label: The Pump Station)

Lucien's point of view. A city drainage pump station on the Mid-City or canal side, at night. The same Friday night, after the river. About 3,000 words.

**What this scene is for.** Lucien acts on his wrong theory. He and Elisabet go into a city drainage pump station at night and photograph a week of the held paper charts, and the supervisor catches them and takes his name. A night raid on paper: the thrill of taking, the cold of being caught, and a man too sure to listen.

**What he wants here.** To be right before the fact: the charts that will prove the ground. Tonight: the city's held pump charts, himself.

**Who pushes back.** The shift supervisor, whose rule is that nobody is inside at night without leave: his station, his rule. Behind him, the hold on the records. Beside Lucien, Elisabet's caution about what charts can show, which he does not hear; and her flirting, which runs under the raid and which he is too sure of himself to listen to either.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The drainage board's procedure: the board's authority; the reason, that nobody unauthorized is inside at night; the public's infrastructure as the interest. It is reasonable, and Lucien breaks it. Trespass is the law's. The hold on the records has no stated authority, reason or interest. The supervisor answers to his own rules, not to the hold.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The way in, on Elisabet's access letter.
- A week of charts, photographed.
- The supervisor finds them, and takes Lucien's name and his employer's, which Lucien gives in full, as his papers have it: the Hollen Institute for Civic Continuity. Walt, the night operator who let them in on Elisabet's letter, is still there, and has one more line before they leave.
- Elisabet tells him the pumps run most of the day in a wet spring. He doesn't hear it.

**What it costs or changes.** Lucien has photographs of a week of charts and believes they confirm the ground. The supervisor has now seen the access letter that got them in, so Elisabet's access is exposed. Lucien has broken a reasonable rule for his theory, on his own account, and his name is on someone's record. He does not tell the Institute about the night.

**Still open.** The supervisor has his name.

**Keep off the page.** The supervisor keeps his own motive, his station and his rule. He is nobody's proxy, and nobody says where the name goes next. The charts show pumps running and prove nothing about the Square; the page never lets them. Lucien gives the Institute's name because it is what his papers say; he tells it nothing, and what he thinks of what it covers stays in his head, its senders unnamed. What the night costs Walt stays open. Elisabet goes in for her own work and her own want, by her own choice; her flirting is hers, for the pleasure of being direct. Not downtown, and not the Warehouse District. The piece is not only its penalty: the station's ordinary night life stays in view. A second explanation of how the chart recorders work: Walt gives it a line at most. A count through the frames as they photograph: the taking is brief, the act and its risk. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it; the charts are the thing taken, and the page does not read times off them. No named mechanism. The narrator certifies no cause of the strange events and states no human cause as fact; the characters may suspect and blame, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them. No bearing for Lucien's lean.

**Ends.** The same night, after they leave. Lucien and Elisabet each hold the photographs. The supervisor has Lucien's name and the Institute's, and has seen Elisabet's access letter. The Institute has not been told. Walt let them in.
