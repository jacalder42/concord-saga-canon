# Book 1, Episode 24 (working label: The Pump Station)

Lucien's point of view. A city drainage pump station on the Mid-City or canal side, at night. The same Friday night, after the river. About 3,000 words.

**What this scene is for.** Lucien acts on his wrong theory. He and Elisabet go into a city drainage pump station at night and photograph a week of the held paper charts, and the supervisor catches them and takes his name. A night raid on paper: the thrill of taking, the cold of being caught, and a man too sure to listen.

**What he wants here.** To be right before the fact: the charts that will prove the ground. Tonight: the city's held pump charts, himself.

**Who pushes back.** The shift supervisor, whose rule is that nobody is inside at night without leave: his station, his rule. Behind him, the hold on the records. Beside Lucien, Elisabet's caution about what charts can show, which he does not hear.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The drainage board's procedure: the board's authority; the reason, that nobody unauthorized is inside at night; the public's infrastructure as the interest. It is reasonable, and Lucien breaks it. Trespass is the law's. The hold on the records has no stated authority, reason or interest. The supervisor answers to his own rules, not to the hold.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The way in, on Elisabet's access letter.
- A week of charts, photographed.
- The supervisor finds them, and takes Lucien's name and the name of his office.
- Elisabet tells him the pumps run most of the day in a wet spring. He doesn't hear it.

**What it costs or changes.** Lucien has photographs of a week of charts and believes they confirm the ground. The supervisor has now seen the access letter that got them in, so Elisabet's access is exposed. Lucien has broken a reasonable rule for his theory, on his own account, and his name is on someone's record. He does not tell his office about the night.

**Still open.** The supervisor has his name.

**Keep off the page.** The supervisor keeps his own motive, his station and his rule. He is nobody's proxy, and nobody says where the name goes next. The charts show pumps running and prove nothing about the Square; the page never lets them. Lucien gives his office's name because it is true; he voices no distrust of it and tells it nothing. Elisabet goes in for her own work and her own want, by her own choice. Not downtown, and not the Warehouse District. The piece is not only its penalty: the station's ordinary night life stays in view. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it; the charts' times are the thing taken. No named mechanism. The narrator certifies no cause and no blame. No bearing for Lucien's lean.

**Ends.** The same night, after they leave. Lucien and Elisabet each hold the photographs. The supervisor has Lucien's name and his office's, and has seen Elisabet's access letter. Lucien's office has not been told.
