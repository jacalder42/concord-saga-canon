# Book 1, Episode 37 (working label: The Long Hum)

Seraphine's point of view. The care center; clients' homes; the Elmwood oxygen depot. A Monday, about 20 April. About 2,500 words.

**What this scene is for.** Open the last act: the city's pressure reaches Seraphine's work through her boss's own decision, and she works around it with the system's own procedure. A changed city lived inside one working day: holds, routes, closures, and a woman who knows the procedures better than the people enforcing them.

**What she wants here.** To be the one who holds it: to keep Mr. Vidrine on oxygen through a city on hold. Today, to get him a tank while deliveries are held.

**Who pushes back.** Denise, who after a call from the city takes the Ursulines block off Seraphine's caseload: her own decision, to protect the center and, as Denise sees it, Seraphine. The depot's hold on tanks. Mr. Duplessis, another client, who shrugs that he can go without for a week.

**Pressing on her from outside.** A call from the city reaches her caseload through Denise, who decides for herself. Denise's authority is the care center's; her stated reason is clearance and liability; she serves the center's interests, and Seraphine's as she sees them. The city's hold on oxygen deliveries is stated as safety; whose interests it serves is not clear. Nobody says who called.

**Already true.**
- Mr. Vidrine lives on Ursulines, so the block taken off her caseload includes him.
- Seraphine told Mrs. Arceneaux about the page, and the block has turned on her: some say she is always there first.
- With Denise, her calls are covered as heat checks, which Denise agreed to in exchange for the Batiste file being closed that day. Seraphine left the form unsaved and did not go back to tell her. Denise said she was watching.
- Inez has added the Ursulines households to her Friday boxes.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The caseload call, and Denise's decision.
- A day already adjusted for: holds, routes, Mr. Duplessis.
- The depot: a held tank, and the clerk's own procedure.
- Carrollton, by a call from Caro.

**What it costs or changes.** At the depot she talks a held tank out of the clerk using the depot's own procedure, on an account she is no longer cleared for. Mr. Vidrine has air, and her name is on the sign-out. Before, Denise protects her and is already watching her. After, the block is off her caseload, Denise's trust is lower, and Seraphine has acted outside her clearance, on paper.

**Still open.** Carrollton.

**Keep off the page.** The call reaches Denise; the decision is Denise's own, and nobody on the page is anyone's proxy or says who is upstairs. Seraphine never argues against the system aloud; she uses its procedure. If people speak of a hum, it stays ambiguous (the pipes, their nerves, or pressure): no center, no source, no explanation. The depot is only a depot, and nothing is attributed to it. No new recurring face from the city's services. Her day does not open on paperwork. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** About 20 April. The tank is delivered to Mr. Vidrine. The Ursulines block is off her caseload. Her name is on the depot's sign-out. Caro has told her about Carrollton.
