# Book 1, Episode 23 (working label: It Is Beautiful for a Minute)

Lucien's point of view. The river, below the levee walk behind the Cathedral. The same Friday evening. About 1,800 words. Keep the easy talk on the steps before the note; the additions are small and must not turn it into setup.

**What this scene is for.** Wonder without punishment, shared by the three of them.

**What he wants here.** To take the reading: something harmless and strange, measured while it lasts.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Before anything strange, an ordinary evening on the steps. Baz's care is food bought and handed round, the last of it to Lucien. Lucien's attention catches on Seraphine, on something ordinary like her bare feet on the concrete, and he puts his eyes on the river instead. It comes before the note and is never tied to it.
- Something harmless and strange, heard together.
- Music reaches them, a trumpet practicing, and the three find themselves rooting for the player. Lucien stops counting. Afterward the phone has kept the reading anyway; he saves it, because he wants it, not because he must, and tells nobody. Someone admits it is beautiful before anyone reduces it to evidence, most likely Lucien.

**What it costs or changes.** Nothing is measured into evidence; the evening is kept. The saved reading is a private keepsake, kept by choice.

**Still open.** Nothing carried forward but the evening, and a reading he has not mentioned.

**Keep off the page.** Harm. Any new rule, any mechanism, and any danger to justify it. What they perceive is only as objective as three people agree it is. Nothing is written down and nobody says a time; the saved reading stays his, and untold. No color as a detector and no impossible geometry. No bearing for Lucien's lean. Nothing foreshadows how Baz's story ends; that someone may be looking for him can be felt.

**Ends.** The same evening, at the river. The three were there; nothing was written down. The reading is saved on Lucien's phone, and he has told no one.
