# Book 1, Episode 23 (working label: It Is Beautiful for a Minute)

Lucien's point of view. The river, below the levee walk behind the Cathedral. The same Friday evening. About 1,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** Wonder without punishment, shared by the three of them.

**What he wants here.** To take the reading: something harmless and strange, measured while it lasts.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Something harmless and strange, heard together.
- Music reaches them, a trumpet practicing, and he lets the count go: the reading is his to keep, and he chooses not to. Someone admits it is beautiful before anyone reduces it to evidence, most likely Lucien.

**What it costs or changes.** Nothing is measured into evidence; the evening is kept.

**Still open.** Nothing carried forward but the evening.

**Keep off the page.** Harm. Any new rule, any mechanism, and any danger to justify it. What they perceive is only as objective as three people agree it is. Nothing is written down and no time is taken. No color as a detector and no impossible geometry. No bearing for Lucien's lean. Nothing ominous around Baz.

**Ends.** The same evening, at the river. The three were there; nothing was recorded.
