# Book 1, Episode 22 (working label: Working)

Lucien's point of view. On the move: the Square, then the Esplanade neutral ground at one of Elisabet's well lids. Late March, Friday, the next day. About 2,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** Scattered incidents become a working pattern the narrator never certifies, and Lucien commits to a cause of his own: the ground. He trades with Elisabet for the pump records instead of asking his office. Discovery, briefly a pleasure; then the itch of wanting to be right.

**What he wants here.** A model that says it first: to be right before the fact, on paper. Today: to turn the two recordings, the times and the places into a working pattern that dares a prediction, and to get the pump times behind it.

**Who pushes back.** Elisabet, whose objection is that his pattern may only record where he and Seraphine happened to be standing. Behind her, the hold on the pump records she was promised.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The records are held for "upstairs", with no authority, reason or interest given: the will from Poydras, now standing between him and the data. And Elisabet's own permit, which is hers to risk.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The pieces come together: recordings, times, places.
- Lucien floats the ground to Elisabet: the pumps pull the water out, and the city settles. She attacks where they were standing.
- He writes her objection under his own line, credited to her, and does not cross his out. She agrees to a trade for the held charts.

**What it costs or changes.** He is committed to the ground, a hypothesis he holds as his. He has a pact with Elisabet for the charts, made outside his office. He has gone from a pattern to a cause of his own, and from his office's channels to a private trade.

**Still open.** The charts.

**Keep off the page.** The narrator certifies no pattern, no edge and no cause. The two recordings were lined up already; the page does not go through them again frame by frame. Elisabet contests: she is not persuaded, not recruited, and has her own work and her own want. Seraphine's sense of it coming is her observation about herself, never evidence. The pump station on Elisabet's trace is on the Mid-City or canal side, never downtown. Lucien says nothing against his office; he simply doesn't ask it. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it. No named mechanism. No bearing for Lucien's lean. Nothing ominous around Baz.

**Ends.** Friday, late March, at Elisabet's well lid on Esplanade. Lucien has a written working pattern with Elisabet's objection under it, in her name. He holds his theory of the ground. He and Elisabet have agreed to get the pump charts together. His office has not been asked. Baz has his copies of the two recordings; the originals stay with their owners.
