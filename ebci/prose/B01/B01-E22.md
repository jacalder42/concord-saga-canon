# Book 1, Episode 22 (working label: Working)

Lucien's point of view. On the move: the Square, then the Esplanade neutral ground at one of Elisabet's well lids, where Seraphine comes with lunch. Late March, Friday, the next day. About 2,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** Scattered incidents become a working pattern the narrator never certifies, and Lucien commits to a cause of his own: the ground. He trades with Elisabet for the pump records instead of asking his office. Seraphine is there, bringing lunch and her own accounts of where it came and who was near, and Elisabet flirts with both of them. Discovery, briefly a pleasure; then the itch of wanting to be right.

**What he wants here.** A model that says it first: to be right before the fact, on paper. Today: to turn the two recordings, the places and what people felt into a working pattern that dares a prediction, and to get the pump records behind it.

**Who pushes back.** Elisabet, whose objection is that his pattern may only record where he and Seraphine happened to be standing. Behind her, the hold on the pump records she was promised. And Elisabet's flirting, open and both ways: with Lucien in front of Seraphine, and with Seraphine in front of Lucien.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The records are held for "upstairs", with no authority, reason or interest given: the will from Poydras, now standing between him and the data. And Elisabet's own permit, which is hers to risk.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The Square walked again, in a paragraph at most. What it yields: the three sites in a row on his page, with Seraphine's accounts beside them in the margin, in another pencil.
- The pieces come together: recordings, places, her accounts in the margin.
- Lucien floats the ground to Elisabet: the pumps pull the water out, and the city settles. She attacks where they were standing.
- Seraphine arrives with lunch, and the margin comes into it. Elisabet flirts with Lucien in front of her, and with her in front of Lucien.
- He writes her objection under his own line, credited to her, and does not cross his out. She agrees to a trade for the held charts.

**What it costs or changes.** He is committed to the ground, a hypothesis he holds as his. He has a pact with Elisabet for the charts, made outside his office. He has gone from a pattern to a cause of his own, and from his office's channels to a private trade.

**Still open.** The charts.

**Keep off the page.** The narrator certifies no pattern, no edge and no cause. The two recordings were lined up already; the page does not go through them again frame by frame. Elisabet contests: she is not persuaded, not recruited, and has her own work and her own want. She flirts because she enjoys being direct, not because she is auditioning pairings, and flirting is not her whole self; nothing romantic is declared. Seraphine's sense of it coming is her observation about herself, never evidence. The pump station on Elisabet's trace is on the Mid-City or canal side, never downtown. Lucien says nothing against his office; he simply doesn't ask it. How a chart recorder works is explained once, here, by Elisabet. No tally of times: the pattern is places and people's accounts. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it. No named mechanism. No bearing for Lucien's lean. Nothing foreshadows how Baz's story ends; that someone may be looking for him can be felt.

**Ends.** Friday, late March, at Elisabet's well lid on Esplanade. Lucien has a written working pattern with Elisabet's objection under it, in her name. He holds his theory of the ground. He and Elisabet have agreed to get the pump charts together. His office has not been asked. Seraphine came with lunch and has seen Elisabet flirt with them both. Baz has his copies of the two recordings; the originals stay with their owners.
