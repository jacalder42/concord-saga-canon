# Book 1, Episode 35 (working label: Baz and Lucien, Off the Clock)

Baz's point of view. Across the river: the ferry, Algiers, and back. The Sunday. About 2,900 words. The additions fit inside it; keep the crawfish table and the talk about a flat floor as play, and keep the crossing about the two of them, not about travel.

**What this scene is for.** The friendship apart from the case, and one thing Baz keeps from Seraphine. Friendship, on a day off.

**What he wants here.** To belong here: a day off with his friend, in a city he is choosing.

**Who pushes back.** Lucien, who asks whether he has told her.

**Pressing on him from outside.** On the step before they go, Mr. Ledet tells Baz that a man asked after the car with the Texas plate, saying he was from the rental company; Mr. Ledet told him he knew no Frenchman. Nothing more is given, and nobody says who he was.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Mr. Ledet's report, on the step.
- A day off, across the river. Its pleasures are particular: Baz's is showing someone how a place eats; Lucien's, once he stops resisting, is being shown.
- On the way out, Lucien does not explain the crossing. Something about how the boat goes across the current is plainly the kind of thing he would explain, and he chooses not to; Baz notices, and finds that he wanted the explanation. On the way back Lucien gives it in a sentence, with no numbers, and asks whether Baz wanted it. It is play, not a joke he has built, and it costs neither of them anything.
- Baz chooses to stay in New Orleans anyway, with plans that carry no dates. If there is room: a sentence or two of why choosing a city matters to a man who has always arrived in places after something came down there, undated and with nothing an attentive reader could take for an omen; a stranger's local tradition for some later season, with no date put on it; and some visible sign that Lucien is off duty, the kind someone would tease him about.
- Lucien asks whether he has told her. Baz says no, and tells Lucien about his column, briefly: the moved sheet, Lucien's question, and the silence they then share. What breaks Baz open is his own method turned on him: Lucien leaves the kind of room Baz leaves for witnesses, and Baz recognizes it, and talks into it. This happens inside the existing exchange, not as a second scene. Baz's misreading of Lucien's last look stays a misreading.

**What it costs or changes.** The concealment is now held by the two of them. Baz goes home with a pact he did not plan on, and a friend who now keeps slightly ahead of him on the walk. Before, old friends working a case; after, friends with a life apart from it, and a thing they are both keeping from her. Baz keeps something from Lucien too, and the page does not narrate it.

**Still open.** Their plans, undated.

**Keep off the page.** The case stays off the day, except the column and Mr. Ledet's report. Baz restating the column's terms. Baz never sees the man who asked, and nobody names who sent him. Nothing foreshadows how Baz's story ends, and that someone may be looking for him can be felt; his plans have no dates and take nobody out of the city. Lucien may say he is from Vienna; there is no Vienna scene.

**Ends.** Sunday evening, back across the river. Baz is staying, knowing someone has asked after his car. Lucien knows about the column; Seraphine does not.
