# Book 1, Episode 25 (working label: Metairie)

Seraphine's point of view. Lorraine's kitchen in Metairie, where Renée is staying with her sister. Late March, a Saturday afternoon. About 2,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** Seraphine goes, uninvited, to the family of the boy she could not save, to keep her own account on the record, and learns that a stranger has already been to their door asking for her minute. A kitchen where she isn't wanted, and a question she can't answer honestly.

**What she wants here.** Her own account on the record: her words kept on the file, in her own name. The motive is mixed, and shown in the scene: the truth of what she felt, being believed as the one who was there, and refusing to be corrected. Today: to keep her paragraph on the coroner's file, against the family's wish to close it.

**Who pushes back.** Renée, who wants her son's death closed as an ordinary death. Hers is a mother's authority, and her claim is reasonable. Lorraine, who wants Seraphine out of her kitchen. Renée has a lever: while her son's death certificate stays pending, the insurance and benefits wait, and Seraphine's paragraph is part of what keeps the question open. When she says there is no certificate, she means no final one: a pending certificate was issued after the death, and the final one is what the family lacks. She may ask Seraphine to amend it, or go to the coroner herself.

**Pressing on her from outside.** The city is holding the file open with her paragraph in it. And a will has reached the family: since the form went to the city, a polite man wearing a badge on a lanyard, turned round, came to the door. He asked Lorraine at what minute the caseworker said she felt it and what she had written, and he wrote down the minute. What he wants can be inferred: the record of her minute, and what she wrote of it. He gave no authority, reason or interest; Lorraine saw that his badge was turned.

**Already true.** On the phone after the funeral, Seraphine told Odile that Renée can ask her anything at all. Renée has not called. Renée asked Odile what Seraphine told the inspector, and knows from Guidry that there are two statements on the file. Seraphine has a copy of her own statement from Guidry; the engineer's was refused to her as the family's. So going uninvited is her decision after an offer nobody took up, and Renée's question is the *anything* she offered to answer.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Lorraine's kitchen, uninvited.
- The house goes on with its own life around her: food cooking, funeral food from neighbors, a child at the table listening while pretending not to, and somebody who feeds her whether or not she is wanted there.
- Lorraine, angry, tells her about the man at the door.
- Renée asks what Seraphine did first, since she was there first. Seraphine does not tell her that the boy's health coverage had lapsed while the renewal sat on her desk.
- The refusal: the paragraph stays.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Seraphine learns that her words on paper brought a stranger to a grieving family's door, and she keeps them on the paper anyway. She leaves with the plate they gave her uneaten on the table and the door shut behind her. Between her and Renée: before, a distance held by grief; after, an open refusal on each side.

**Still open.** Whether he comes back. Whether Renée goes to the coroner.

**Keep off the page.** The man at the door is reported, never seen. He is unnamed and polite; he asked about her minute and her words, never about the boy or the place, and nobody says who sent him. Nothing about him is a clue beyond the turned badge. He takes, and asks Seraphine and her friends for nothing. The narrator never blames Renée. The lapsed coverage stays untold. In this kitchen *the card* means Dré's coverage card; the stranger wore a badge. No pressure on Seraphine's license. Nobody goes back to where the boy died, and nothing on the page makes the boy or the place special. The piece is not only its refusal: the kitchen and the family's food stay in view. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it; the man's interest in her minute is the clock here. No named mechanism. The narrator certifies no cause of the strange events and states no human cause as fact; the characters may suspect and blame, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them. Plans stay undated.

**Ends.** Saturday afternoon, late March, Metairie. Seraphine's paragraph stays on the file. Renée and Lorraine know she will not take it off; Renée does not know about the lapsed coverage. Renée has said what the pending certificate holds up for her family. Seraphine knows, second-hand, about the man at the door. She leaves in time for her evening call to Odile. A short after-scene in her car before she drives off is allowed.
