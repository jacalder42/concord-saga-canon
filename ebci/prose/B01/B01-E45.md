# Book 1, Episode 45 (working label: Inez's Gathering)

Inez's point of view. A church lot, open concrete, with canopies and borrowed tables. A Wednesday, about 29 April. About 3,200 words.

**What this scene is for.** Care tried against what is happening: it succeeds as care and controls nothing. What comes next follows directly. Care that stays care while things are unsteady.

**What she wants here.** Everyone on the lot fed and counted by name, in her book.

**Who pushes back.** Seraphine, who argues for the gathering as protection and brings her clients on that promise. Mrs. Carmouche, whose idea the gathering was, and who keeps her own notebook. Inez's own question to Seraphine: what is the gathering for?

**Pressing on her from outside.** The sexton's rules for the lot: the parish's authority; the lot and the tables are the church's and go back by Thursday; the parish's interests, and his own trust, kept by being checked. Beside it, the city's advice to stay home, for public safety.

**Already true.**
- Inez kept from Seraphine how the last crowd on a lot broke up.
- Lucien is off Sal's crew.
- Seraphine argues from the theory she recommitted to after the long evening, over her own written doubt.
- Since Sunday, Seraphine knows that Lucien wrote *None* on the coroner's form, beside her own paragraph; she has not spoken to him about it. Inez knows nothing of it; she sees only what the two of them do.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The lot set up, under the sexton's rules.
- Seraphine's argument, and Inez's own count. Seraphine has told two of her clients, Mr. Lionel and Miss Hazel, that they would be safer among people, and they came. Lucien turns back a table with a bent brace in front of the sexton. Inez overhears Mrs. Whitlow tell Lucien, over coffee while he carries tables, that her coffee went to rings before her table's legs moved.
- The lot full, and it comes.

**What it costs or changes.** Care succeeds as care: ninety-three people, named and fed, on open concrete. Inez hosted for her own count, not for Seraphine's theory. Whether the gathering keeps anything away is answered only by what comes next.

**Still open.** Whether gathering keeps anything away. What the gathering is for, which Inez asked and Seraphine answered with her theory.

**Keep off the page.** The narrator never links the gathering, its practices or the crowd to what comes; characters may believe it and be disagreed with. No grid, ritual or magic; the difference between care and control is shown, not said. Mrs. Whitlow's rings stay her own observation, told over coffee: nobody draws a rule from it, builds a warning on it, or says the rings come first. Inez hosts for her own reason and does not join the friends. Seraphine and Lucien are here, and it is not their scene: only what Inez sees, hears or is told, and nothing explains what is between them. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** About 29 April, afternoon. The lot full; ninety-three names in Inez's book; Seraphine's clients there on her word; the first moment of the event, before anyone knows what it is.
