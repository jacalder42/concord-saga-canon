# Book 1, Episode 11 (working label: The Things He Still Does)

Lucien's point of view. A laundromat near his lodging. Mid-March, a Saturday. About 2,200 words.

**What this scene is for.** The ordinary man under Lucien's control: off duty, laughed at and laughing. A rest, straight after his worst choice; it may end on a small choice of his own, if that serves it.

**What he wants here.** Laundry day done by his own rules: wash, dry and fold his week the way he always has.

**Who pushes back.** Nia, a girl at the next table, who helps and watches in equal measure. His own rules.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Laundry day, by his rules.
- A small domestic failure, embarrassing and nothing to do with anything else. A button put right.
- He laughs, and gives the ruined sweater away.

**What it costs or changes.** Nothing that matters. One of his rules fails, and he enjoys it.

**Still open.** Nothing pressing.

**Keep off the page.** Anything strange, any case, any clue: this is his life, not his work. The lean. Nia belongs to this scene only, and nothing sets her up.

**Ends.** The same day. Lucien leaves the laundromat with his washing done and without the sweater, and goes to eat.
