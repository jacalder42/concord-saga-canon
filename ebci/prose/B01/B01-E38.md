# Book 1, Episode 38 (working label: The Card and the Trade)

Lucien's point of view. The Carrollton neutral ground, at a police sawhorse; a grill nearby. The same Monday, about 20 April, the day of the Carrollton report. About 3,500 words. The neutral ground as a place people live for an afternoon keeps its room.

**What this scene is for.** Lucien buys the time he wants with the truth about himself, draws the window, and hears his lie land on Sal. A bargain at a sawhorse, a friend's bad news he caused, and his silence after.

**What he wants here.** To be right first. Today, the time of the first call at Carrollton, to set a window.

**Who pushes back.** The young policeman at the sawhorse, who won't give him the complaint line's times, and who reads him a question off an index card someone else wrote. Sal. Seraphine, who asks who will be there for the people relying on them if they start looking further.

**Pressing on him from outside.** An index card written above the policeman and taken back when he's done: the police's authority, the log as its reason, his superior's interests. A note from R., his contact at the Hollen Institute, overnight, on routine business: R. advises him to keep a low profile, says his name has crossed a desk or two there this spring, and adds, unasked, that the report on the camp went up in March, and not by R. It is a kindness, and information. With R.'s note, Lucien lets himself ask what his review is for. His own false report, now acted on by the city.

**Already true.**
- On Friday night Lucien reported glass disturbance at Sal's Kerlerec house to the city's line, with a day and a time, knowing nothing happened there. Baz refused to be part of it, thinks it wrong, and has promised to tell no one. Seraphine does not know.
- That Friday morning Lucien mailed Guidry his amended statement: the true sentence, no reason given. It reaches Guidry today. If it is on the page here, it is as what follows, never the sending again.
- He also sent his routine monthly report to the Institute that Friday. R.'s note answers that routine business; Lucien asked R. nothing.
- At the pump station, its guard, Curtis, took his name, and Lucien gave the Institute's in full.
- *Faster* is Baz's bet, from a glazier's order tickets, and Lucien knows it.
- Lucien found a case beside his own chalk mark on Dauphine the morning after the Marigny ring, and has told no one.
- He knows about Baz's private column, and has agreed with Baz not to tell Seraphine.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The sawhorse: the line's times refused, the card, the trade.
- The window drawn: the next two middays. Lucien says it aloud, near the line at Carrollton.
- Sal: the forks, and the stop-work. Lucien's silence.
- Freddie's smoker feeds the whole grass, on the house; nobody organized it. At the grill, he sees Seraphine has carried the second plate since the smoker, saving him for last: her care goes round the whole grass first. Someone waiting nearby may take the two of them for a pair; neither of them says anything about it.
- Guidry's voicemail, after the sawhorse: the letter is attached to the file; Lucien is to get himself looked at and say so; Guidry has called the firm, and Lucien is off its site visits for that office until they talk.
- R.'s note, and a line written back and kept. His thoughts go to the names he has given, the pump station's among them.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Refused the line's times, he trades his true answer to the card's question (yes, it has happened to him before) for the time in the police log. His susceptibility is now on someone else's paper, in another man's notebook, buttoned into a pocket that goes back up at end of shift. Sal calls or comes by with a cousin's story of a galley crew all putting their forks down at once, and with news: a stop-work on his job. Somebody reported it; the city's inspector came, found a two-story cottage on bottle jacks with its shoring still under way, and red-tagged it. Lucien says nothing to him; Sal's kindness on the phone is worse to hear than anger would be. He writes R. one line back, a question about the route, and doesn't send it. Lucien is on paper where he can't see who reads it, and his lie has landed on Sal. He has begun to ask himself what his review is for, and has not answered it.

**Still open.** How far to look, and who stays here for the people here.

**Keep off the page.** Nobody proposes going back to where the boy died, or talks of it. No tally of intervals and no recital of routes: the window takes two lines, and its time is bought. The forks are hearsay, *similar* at most; no claim beyond the city. Lucien's thoughts register the route, never anyone's motive. The Institute may be named, and he may think of it as the cover it is; the people behind it are never named or described, he says nothing against it aloud, and the line to R. stays unsent. His question about his own review stays a question, never a conclusion. The card from above and R.'s note are enough: don't keep returning to the people who sent him. R. is kind and names no one. The link between the pump station and R.'s note is the reader's to see; nothing confirms it. The policeman keeps his own part and is nobody's proxy; nobody asks Lucien to report or contain anything. If the policeman asks him to spell a place name, it reads as a chance to take the answer back, never as a local who cannot spell his own city. Caro gives one time, as a friend, and it is hers to give; nothing entrusted to her leaves. His truth reaching the file on the day his lie reaches Sal is for the reader to see; nobody points it out. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** The Carrollton neutral ground, the same day. The window is set for the next two middays. Sal's job is stopped by the city; Lucien has not told Sal what he did. The unsent line to R. is in Lucien's notebook. Sal's story of the forks is written down, with question marks. Lucien has begun to ask what his review is for. Guidry has his letter, and Lucien is off the firm's site visits for that office until he calls Guidry. The grass empties around him, and he may stay on as it does.
