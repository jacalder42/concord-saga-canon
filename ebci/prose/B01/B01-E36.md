# Book 1, Episode 36 (working label: The City Reacts)

Baz's point of view. Mr. Lacoste's glass shop, and the blocks his order tickets name. About 17 April. About 3,600 words.

**What this scene is for.** Close Act II on a city adapting and on Baz's bet that it is coming faster, while Lucien, over Baz's refusal, sets a trap on the city's complaint line. A morning of work Baz is good at, and an act closing on a city, and a friend, each doing something he wouldn't.

**What he wants here.** To copy the glazier's spike: every order for broken glass in the city, with its date and time. Underneath, to belong here by being good at a morning's work.

**Who pushes back.** Mr. Lacoste, whose tickets stay on his spike: his shop, his records, his business. Then Lucien, who wants Baz in on a false report to the city's line.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The city's complaint notice asks residents for the date and time of what they saw, and tape is going up on windows citywide: the city's authority, public safety as the reason, interests of its own. A man from the city has already been through Mr. Lacoste's tickets and photographed them, taking only the times; his card hung on a string, turned so nobody in the shop could read it. Something wants only the time.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- A trade opens the spike: Baz works the counter for the morning, sells glass well, and copies the tickets.
- He is in the shop when a new sheet cracks while the old glass in the crate makes no sound, and the old-glass idea dies for him.
- The tickets show glass going on blocks nobody reported, closer and closer together. He takes them to people nobody warned, asks whether everyone stopped at once, and finds hits nobody reported. His conclusion, held as a bet: it is coming faster.
- The trap, offered and declined without a fight. Lucien, who has just written Guidry a correction letter, the true sentence his form left out, and sent the Institute his ordinary report, files a false report to the line anyway, naming Sal's job, where he knows nothing happened.

**What it costs or changes.** Before, two friends who agree on method; after, two friends who disagree about a lie, and stay friends. Baz holds a bet about the pace.

**Still open.** Whether a case will come to Sal's job.

**Keep off the page.** The crack is seen, not explained; nobody certifies a cause, and the tape stays up in public. No claim beyond the city, and the city's words for it stay scattered. The man with the card is only reported; nobody says whose he is. What Lucien does away from Baz reaches Baz only as Lucien tells him. Lucien's report to the Institute is his ordinary weekly one and stays off the page: he asks it nothing and voices no distrust, and Baz knows of it only as Lucien mentions it. Baz never sees a case or its collector. His *faster* is a bet, not a finding. Nothing ominous in Baz's pleasures.

**Ends.** About 17 April. Baz holds copies of the tickets. Guidry has Lucien's amended statement. The false report is filed, naming Sal's job. Lucien's ordinary report to the Institute is sent. Baz and Lucien disagree, and are still friends.
