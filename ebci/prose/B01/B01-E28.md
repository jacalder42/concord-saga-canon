# Book 1, Episode 28 (working label: A Place to Stop)

Caro's point of view. A road crash before dawn; then outside her station, on a neutral ground where Elisabet is working at a well lid. Early April, a Saturday before dawn, at the end of a night shift; the window still ahead. About 2,900 words.

**What this scene is for.** A public impression becomes private safety. The city's hold acts on Elisabet, and she leaves. Caro competent first, then unexpectedly safe; a stranger who leaves just as she has been met.

**What she wants here.** To be asked nothing after a shift: to finish it and be asked for nothing more.

**Who pushes back.** First a crash and a trapped young man. Then Elisabet, who asks her to step back off the ground over the well. Then the city, which has revoked Elisabet's access letter.

**Pressing on her from outside.** On Caro, only her shift, which is ordinary. In front of her, the city revokes Elisabet's access letter after the pump-station night: the city's authority; the infrastructure access rules, which Elisabet broke, as the reason; the interests of the city's infrastructure, and of whoever is holding the records. The hold acts. Nobody on the page says who decided it.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- A crash before dawn. Caro holds a trapped man's head still until he is out.
- Elisabet at a lid across from the station. Caro, who after a shift takes instructions from no one, steps back because Elisabet asks.
- The revocation, and Elisabet's season here is over. It came as two notices the same morning, her access letter for the stations and, separately, her permit for the wells, each with a name typed under an empty signature space. In passing she mentions an unsigned email offering to buy her raw well records, which she declined. Before she goes, she sends Lucien her well readings with the pumps taken out, unasked, in Caro's sight, with a parting shot to him that is also a flirt; Caro watches it with amusement and interest. The readings show nothing at the time of the Square. Then she leaves for her other wells. *October*, the one date she gave on television, is in Caro's mind as she goes, the only time anyone has for her; Elisabet promises nothing about coming back.

**What it costs or changes.** Before, Caro's impression of Elisabet's sharp public self, from the television; after, recognition, and instinctive safety: Caro is seen without being managed, and is interested in how Elisabet flirts. A place to stop. Elisabet is gone.

**Still open.** The night out.

**Keep off the page.** A romance episode, a biography, a mechanics lesson, and anything magical that calms anyone. Elisabet is here for her own reasons and leaves on her own terms, and her feelings about the revocation are hers. The revocation reaches the page as Elisabet has it: nobody is a proxy, and nobody says who decided it. The readings are Elisabet's, and the page claims no more for them than she does. The piece is not only its loss: the ordinary dawn, the patient and the station stay in view. Caro hands off none of her own unfinished work. Caro's amusement and interest stay small: no jealousy and no declaration. The page carries only what Caro sees or is told. Who sent the email, and who signed nothing, stays unknown and unnamed; the offer is for her records as she kept them, with no stress on the times in them, and she mentions it rather than dwelling on it. How long Caro holds his head is felt, never counted. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it. No named mechanism. The narrator certifies no cause of the strange events and states no human cause as fact; the characters may suspect and blame, and the page may imply heavily, including about the unnamed people pressing on them.

**Ends.** Dawn, a Saturday in early April. Elisabet has gone to her other wells, and her access letter here is revoked. Lucien has her well readings with the pumps taken out. Caro and Elisabet have exchanged names. Elisabet has declined an unsigned offer for her well records. *October* is the only date anyone has for her. Caro is going off shift, and tonight's plan stands.
