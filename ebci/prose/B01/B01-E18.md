# Book 1, Episode 18 (working label: The Neighborhood Spike)

Baz's point of view. A residential block: Renita's porch and kitchen, and the corner where the utility's barrels stand. Late March, the Wednesday, his third day. About 3,500 words.

**What this scene is for.** Baz sees one himself, as the control on everything he has been told, and asks each of his friends what would prove them wrong. Three people reading the same minutes three ways, and the reader knowing one thing Baz doesn't.

**What he wants here.** To see it with his own eyes, and to be one of them here. Today: to witness one himself, and set his friends' readings against what he saw.

**Who pushes back.** Renita, who did not ask for a stranger in her kitchen. His friends' readings, which pull apart: Lucien reads the ground, Seraphine reads the people.

**Pressing on him from outside.** A block's suspicion of strangers, earned: the street doesn't know him, and its interests are its own. Ordinary.

**Already true.** Seraphine keeps a log of her own perception: whether she feels it early, late or not at all, where, and who was there. She shows it to no one. Baz may see her write in it; he never reads it.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The block, and a stranger among neighbors.
- Along one block it comes up slowly and holds: everyone in reach stops what their hands are doing at once and starts again at once, a stove is left burning, and afterward people don't talk about it. At the corner it is there on one side of the barrels and not the other.
- Seraphine turns Renita's fire off without being asked. Lucien, down at the corner, works his way back on his knees until it thins.
- Baz asks each of them what would prove them wrong. Seraphine states her theory: it finds people weighed down past what they can hold, and spares those who are looked after. She names what would prove her wrong, someone having it with nothing weighing on them, and does not say whether she has seen that.
- Baz has seen it.

**What it costs or changes.** Baz is a witness now, with his own sighting and three readings of it. Seraphine's theory, and what would prove it wrong, are on the record. The three work as three for the first time.

**Still open.** Which of them is right.

**Keep off the page.** A mechanism. Feeling, grief or care as the cause. The narrator confirming Seraphine's theory or Lucien's edge: what Lucien finds at the corner is what he saw, not a rule. Baz perceives only what he sees and is told; he writes down her answer, never that she is holding anything back. No formal name for it. No lore and no council of specialists: what the block knows is useful and is not the answer. Any link to anywhere else. Ordinary consequences only. Nobody asks Baz for anything.

**Ends.** Late afternoon, the same day. The three are together at the corner by the barrels. Renita's fire is off, and her pot is scorched. Baz's notebook holds his own sighting, the block's accounts and each friend's answer to his question.
