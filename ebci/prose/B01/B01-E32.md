# Book 1, Episode 32 (working label: The Pulse Strikes)

Baz's point of view. Ursulines, in the Tremé: one residential block of older houses, inside the area the page predicted. About 10 April, a Friday, the window's fifth day. About 4,000 words.

**What this scene is for.** The prediction partly succeeds and control fails: right about when and where, wrong about everything else. The model worked, and then the humbling.

**What he wants here.** A page that can be wrong, scored honestly: the hit and the miss on record, in his own hand. Today: to record what happens against what the page said, keeping what he sees apart from what it means.

**Who pushes back.** Mrs. Arceneaux, who runs her house, her platter and her afternoon her own way, and whose side of the street was not told: it was not on Seraphine's list, so the three's choice at Easter left it out. The 911 dispatcher, whose line lags. Then the event, which comes in a form nobody wrote down.

**Pressing on him from outside.** A city's systems under strain. The 911 line lags: the dispatcher's procedure, for public safety, in the public's interest, and reasonable. The city has begun taping its windows. Ordinary friction; no will shows in it here.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Positions taken inside the window; an ordinary afternoon on the block. The days of waiting before it stay a sentence of summary, not a scene. Mrs. Arceneaux's Friday plates are her standing on the block: her trade, her line, her rules, and half the street coming through her gate.
- Windows shudder indoors, across a few older houses on one block, for seconds, while the street stays still. Of two identical houses side by side, one's glass shakes and the next one's doesn't. It comes sharp: no low sound and no slow build comes first. Phones and recorders keep working, and what they catch is degraded. The 911 line lags. Baz's way with people is to leave room after what he says; on a line that lags, the skill fails him once, and he has to find another way to talk. The dispatcher's moment of human laughter stays, and Mrs. Arceneaux hears it.
- Fear, falls and small injuries on the block. Mr. Vidrine, who had been told, sat down. Mrs. Arceneaux, who had not, fell from her stool. Seraphine goes house to house, person after person, past what she can carry.
- Afterward the block argues about what it felt, in its own competing words, and at least one resident revises her own memory aloud as the others talk. Every cause offered stays in someone's mouth, uncorrected; nobody's wrong word is fixed by the page.
- Lucien says, in a line, what his own body did while it happened, though nothing was underfoot. Baz hears only what he says, and it does not soften that Lucien stayed on his feet.
- Lucien watched the pavement for the ground and got nothing. Baz sees him lay his level on two identical sills, and Lucien writes down that it was not the ground.
- Baz's log: what the page got right and what it got wrong. He starts a private column testing the block's rumor that she brings it, against her list of households, and tells no one.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** The page was right on time and place and wrong on the rest. Its expectation that each event would be larger fails, and it had nowhere to record who got hurt. Lucien's theory of the ground is dead. The untold side paid for the three's choice. Seraphine is spent. Baz holds a column Seraphine doesn't know about.

**Still open.** What kind of thing this is.

**Keep off the page.** A mechanism or a stated cause. A lamp failing, a citywide wave, light, color or impossible geometry. A formal name for it. Breath, grounding or care stopping it. Seraphine trying to help "the city": she only overreaches, person after person, and never makes an attempt on the event. Any rule said as law, such as that glass carries it or that it keeps to one material. The page's advance commitment stands as written. The untold side was left out by a limit on reach, not by method: the cost is theirs, Baz knows it, and nobody calls it a control. If the city's taping comes up, at most one clause connects it to the glass orders the city has been seeing; nothing makes it foreknowledge. No explanation, and no claim beyond this block. What Lucien perceives stays his until he tells it; Baz sees only what Lucien does. Every cause offered (the glass, the ground, the amp, her) stays in someone's mouth. The column is Baz's private test, not a verdict on her. A long scoring: it is short and stays on the block (the hit and the miss, the told and the untold with its caveat, that the page had no line for the hurt, the column begun), with no recap of the page's terms. If Lucien says the ground was finished for him before today, at the Marigny crossing, he says it in a line, his own way, and Baz hears only what he says. No clock time in the narration unless someone uses it, as the log and the lagging line do; the page is scored on where and what, not by a count of minutes.

**Tempo.** In the event, shorter sentences and paragraphs, more white space, more action and spoken lines; the long sentences belong to the quiet.

**Ends.** Later the same day. The block is rattled, with small injuries. Seraphine is spent. Lucien has laid his level on two identical sills and written his result down. Baz holds his log (the commitment, the hit, the miss) and the column, untold. Sitting on the curb together toward evening may run on as a short after-scene.
