# Book 1, Episode 15 (working label: The Unsent Letter, and the Call)

Lucien's point of view. The Square returning to itself; the streets of Seraphine's walk; Lucien's room and his map; wherever he makes the call. Mid- to late March, the days after the Square, ending on a Sunday. About 4,200 words.

**What this scene is for.** The close of Act I: it was not the Square alone, and Lucien chooses a person over the proper channel. A friendship with history; help coming, and the problem larger than the people asking.

**What he wants here.** To report it properly, inside his mandate, and be right on paper: to put what he has seen through the Institute's proper channel, as the posting requires.

**Who pushes back.** His own proper letter, against the people who had it and were never asked. The Institute's channel, which would answer with instruments.

**Pressing on him from outside.** The Institute's reporting line, for its Regional Review: his employer's authority, on paper; the reason, that observations outside the review belong on the record; the Institute's interest. Ordinary and reasonable, with no will behind it.

**Life in the scene.** The people who had it elsewhere had it in their own rooms (a kitchen, a waiting room, a clinic lobby) and keep their own reasons for not saying so. The Square's regulars take their days back, unevenly, and leave one patch of it alone.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The Square returns to itself, imperfectly. People who had it elsewhere in the city, and were never asked.
- He walks Seraphine's route step for step, and his map won't close. Once on the walk his attention slips from the count to her, and the procedure covers it: a count of his own goes wrong, and he corrects it in his own way and writes no cause for it. It is no echo of any count she lost; no touch; her own notebook stays hers.
- The letter, drafted and not sent.
- He admits to Seraphine that it is beyond him. He calls Baz instead of writing to the Institute, and starts saying *we*. Baz agrees to come.
- On that call, before he rings Baz, she claims her log as hers alone. He means his agreement as a release; she hears it otherwise, and the call ends short.

**What it costs or changes.** He has gone from the proper channel to a person for the first time, and he did it as friendship, not distrust.

**Still open.** Baz is coming.

**Keep off the page.** Any distrust of the Institute, stated or implied. He may know what the Institute is a cover for, and think it; the people behind it stay unnamed, and that knowledge is not his reason here. His reason for not sending the letter is method: instruments in the Square would find nothing out of order and miss what happened to people, and only asking can reach that. He doesn't delete the letter. Baz is a former colleague from Vienna who left the people behind the Institute; Lucien knows Baz left, not why. He was never a subordinate, and has a life of his own since. The call is still friendship; nothing ominous about it. On Seraphine's route, no direction and no landmark. Every comparison stays imperfect, and nothing is claimed beyond the city.

**Ends.** Sunday. The letter to the Institute, recommending that an institution put instruments in the Square, is drafted and unsent in Lucien's folder. Baz has agreed to come and is due the next day. Seraphine knows he is coming. She keeps her log as hers. Her reply about Baz is short and easy again, and names Monday at the Vein.
