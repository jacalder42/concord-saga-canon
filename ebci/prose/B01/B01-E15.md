# Book 1, Episode 15 (working label: The Unsent Letter, and the Call)

Lucien's point of view. The Square returning to itself; the streets of Seraphine's walk; Lucien's room and his map; wherever he makes the call. Mid- to late March, the days after the Square, ending on a Sunday. About 4,200 words.

**What this scene is for.** The close of Act I: it was not the Square alone, and Lucien chooses a person over the proper channel. A friendship with history; help coming, and the problem larger than the people asking.

**What he wants here.** To report it properly, inside his mandate, and be right on paper: to put what he has seen through his office's proper channel, as the posting requires.

**Who pushes back.** His own proper letter, against the people who had it and were never asked. The office's channel, which would answer with instruments.

**Pressing on him from outside.** His office's reporting line: his employer's authority; the reason, that observations outside the study belong on the record; the office's interest. Ordinary and reasonable, with no will behind it.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The Square returns to itself, imperfectly. People who had it elsewhere in the city, and were never asked.
- He walks Seraphine's route step for step, and his map won't close.
- The letter, drafted and not sent.
- He admits to Seraphine that it is beyond him. He calls Baz instead of the office, and starts saying *we*. Baz agrees to come.

**What it costs or changes.** He has gone from the proper channel to a person for the first time, and he did it as friendship, not distrust.

**Still open.** Baz is coming.

**Keep off the page.** Any distrust of his office, stated or implied. His reason for not sending the letter is method: instruments in the Square would find nothing out of order and miss what happened to people, and only asking can reach that. He doesn't delete the letter. Baz is a former or adjacent colleague, never a subordinate, with a life of his own; nothing ominous about the call. On Seraphine's route, no direction and no landmark. Every comparison stays imperfect, and nothing is claimed beyond the city.

**Ends.** Sunday. The letter, recommending that an institution put instruments in the Square, is drafted and unsent in Lucien's folder. Baz has agreed to come and is due the next day. Seraphine knows he is coming.
