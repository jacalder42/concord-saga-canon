# Book 1, Episode 33 (working label: The Pulse Strikes)

Baz's point of view. One residential block of older houses in the Tremé, New Orleans. An evening in late March.

**What this scene is for.** Their prediction comes true in time and place, and is wrong about everything else.

**Going in.** Days ago Baz wrote down, in advance, a window, this place, what they expected (something like the Square: a low thrum in the open air) and what would count as a miss. Tonight he, Seraphine and Lucien are in position. They chose to be ready for people, not to try to stop anything. An ordinary evening goes on around them.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Inside the window, windows shudder across a few buildings on the block, for seconds. It happens indoors, in people's homes. No thrum comes.
- Phones and recorders keep working, but what they catch is degraded. Calls lag.
- People are frightened; some fall; there are small injuries.
- Seraphine goes from person to person to person, more than she can carry, and it costs her.
- Lucien tries his usual way of reading the street and gets nowhere.
- Baz writes down what the prediction got right and what it got wrong.

**How Baz sees.** Keep what he observes separate from what anyone thinks it means. He records the contradiction; he doesn't explain it.

**Keep off the page.** Any cause or mechanism. Anything spreading across the city. Light, color or impossible shapes. A name for it. Anyone's breathing or grounding stopping it. Seraphine trying to help the whole city rather than the people in front of her.

**Ends.** A block rattled, Seraphine spent, and Baz's log holding the hit and the miss.
