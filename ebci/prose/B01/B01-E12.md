# Book 1, Episode 12 (working label: The Dual Collapse)

Seraphine's point of view, then Lucien's, handed over at a break. A street in the city at night, near the corner store; then wherever they end up sitting. Mid-March, night. About 4,000 words.

**What this scene is for.** The first real step between Seraphine and Lucien: two people seen clearly by each other.

**What they want here.** Each wants to hide a failure and keep what is happening to them out of the other's sight. Neither can hold together alone.

**Who pushes back.** Their own fear.

**Life in the scene.** Each gives the other something concrete, in their own way: hers is something to hold or drink, offered at arm's length; his is a count. Neither is a cure.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Seraphine, failing to carry what she perceives. Her walk: a long stretch of blocks with no edge she can find.
- Lucien, failing to control what he sees. His fall.
- Each finds the other had it at the same minute.
- She learns that his excuse in the room, low blood pressure, was a lie. She does not learn what he wrote on the form.

**What it costs or changes.** Real trust. Before, they were uncertain counterparts, each failing alone. After, each has seen the other's distress before their own, has grounded the other with something concrete, and has let the other's way of knowing count. They exchange numbers. What the night leaves in her body: legs that gave out under her, knees that knelt on concrete, the cold of a stranger's step. It is still with her on the walk home.

**Still open.** Their agreement to tell each other the time if it comes again.

**Keep off the page.** What Lucien wrote on the form; she has her own statement from the coroner's front desk, and Guidry's rule stands: hers she can have, his belongs to the family's file. A county: caseload numbers go to the state. Neither fixes the other. Their meeting causes, calms or triggers nothing in the world. Her walk is her experience and his fall is his; nobody else on the street is affected, and nothing public happens; others may be about (a late walker, a lit window, a porch) and may see two people on a step, and that is all they see. No touch between them: the space on the step stays. No clue.

**Ends.** Night. Each has the other's number. They have agreed to tell each other the time if it happens again. Neither has said what it means. A short after-scene is allowed: the walk to her door, and her body after.
