# Book 1, Episode 6 (working label: The Waiting Room)

Seraphine's point of view. A crowded public benefits office off Tulane Avenue, on a weekday morning. Early March, her third day back at work. About 2,900 words.

**What this scene is for.** The strange moves from private perception into public behavior. Competence under pressure, in a room with its own life.

**What she wants here.** To get Keon, eighteen, her client, through the office with the card he needs to start a job on Monday.

**Who pushes back.** A man on a warning at his own job, who can't be late again, pushing at the clerk. The guard by the door, deciding who in the room is the problem, with his eyes on Keon.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. The office's discipline: the guard's authority is the office's, his reason is order in a public room, and the interests are the office's and his own long tenure. The man's employer, whose warning is on him. Her boss's doubt about whether she is ready to be back.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The office at work: Keon, the man on a warning, the guard.
- Everyone in reach stops what their hands are doing at the same moment.
- Keon moves on his own, from his grandmother's training, to pick up an old man's scattered papers under the guard's eye. Seraphine fails to stop him.
- Everyone starts again at the same moment. Afterwards almost nobody mentions it. Her doubt comes after, and her fear of what the guard saw. She writes down that everyone in the room went together.

**What it costs or changes.** The moment passes with nobody hurt. Keon's move risked his appointment and her own standing on her third day back; it held, and Keon keeps his place for now. A room of strangers did something together, and now behaves as if it hadn't.

**Still open.** Whether it happens again.

**Keep off the page.** A stated cause, a law stated by the narrator, anyone's thoughts read, any link to the camp. Everyone has an ordinary reason to be there, and nobody is a puppet; the guard and the man on a warning keep their own motives. She calms no one by presence, only by what she does and says. Keon's move is his own, not hers to arrange; she reaches for him too late, and does not decide for him. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**Ends.** The same morning. Keon has his receipt and keeps his start date; the card follows by post. Seraphine has a line in her notebook about everyone going together, and goes back to the center.
