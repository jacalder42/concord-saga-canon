# Book 1, Episode 43 (working label: Something Strange That Doesn't Hurt)

Baz's point of view. City Park. A Sunday afternoon, about 26 April. About 2,600 words.

**What this scene is for.** Wonder that belongs to one person, and Baz as a person, apart from his method. A rest in the run toward the end, whose recovery looks like his own, not like anyone else's. Then the people he left find him, in an ordinary place, and he decides he must tell Lucien.

**What he wants here.** To keep something that is his: a Sunday afternoon of his own. Then, to keep Lucien out of what has followed him.

**Who pushes back.** His own method, which wants everything written down. Then Caldas Ren, who finds him on a path in the park.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Caldas: he knows Baz by name and gives his own; whether Baz has met him before is the prose's. Unhurried, on a Sunday, in the ordinary park. He claims no authority and names no organization; he says only that people would like a conversation. He knows where Baz sleeps, and lets him understand that it touches Lucien. A quiet threat: no violence, no raised voice.

**Already true.**
- Baz has told Lucien he is staying in the city and will find a room of his own. For now he is still on Lucien's floor.
- He refused to be part of the false report, told Lucien it was wrong, and promised to tell no one, so he knows it named Sal's job.
- His private column has two lines in Seraphine's favor, and he has not told her about it.
- Home, for the wind, is Marseille and his father's port.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- An ordinary Sunday in the park. Keep the approach short: the way to the park, not the park. On the way, one glimpse of the Sunday city going on outside any plot is allowed if it does not slow him down.
- The park is a scene, not a report. Staged moment by moment, with his pleasure in it while it happens: a small comic contest with a creature over food that a child finds funnier than anything; a stranger short a player who puts him in a game he does not know; his bad play, one save made with the wrong part of him, the rules learned by breaking them; afterward he writes the rules down for fun, and a stranger asks what he is writing. He is absorbed into somebody else's Sunday. This is where Caldas's later remark about the game is earned.
- At rest, his method keeps running anyway: he catches himself reading a stranger the way he reads a witness, notices that nobody sent him to, and lets it go. It is the run-up to the capped pen.
- He learns, concretely, what the stop-work costs Sal: the men off work, the client's doubt, the weeks. How he learns is the prose's.
- First, whole and on its own, a dry wind comes through the park that feels like the wind at home. It reaches his body before he names it (skin, lips, eyes, the breath he holds to make it last), and it brings back one more layer of home than the first thought does. Not wanting to find out what it was is new to him. He does not write it down: the capped pen, the deleted line.
- Then Caldas, on the path.

**What it costs or changes.** His first withholding from his own method: one thing stays out of the notebook, by his choice, and the clean line under the day's last entry stays clean. Knowing what it cost Sal, he chooses not to raise it with Lucien. Then Caldas's visit turns the afternoon, and what it leaves is cold in his own body that is nothing like the wind: keeping what he knows from Lucien no longer keeps Lucien safe, and Baz decides he must tell him. The afternoon ends on that decision.

**Still open.** Sal stays unsaid between Baz and Lucien. What Baz will tell Lucien, and when. Whether Caldas comes back.

**Keep off the page.** Nothing ominous in his pleasures or plans: the park is pleasure while it happens, never a last good day, and nothing frames the afternoon as farewell. The wind is no mechanism, no danger and no report: not shared as evidence, not logged, and whether it is anything at all stays unknown; the visible world moves with it like weather, and a stranger may take it for weather. The text he sends Lucien instead of the truth is an evasion, and stays one. Caldas is an ordinary man in an ordinary place: no violence, no organization named, nothing explained about what he wants beyond the conversation; the threat is in what he knows and whom it touches. The feeling of being looked for may be felt; the menace stays in the present, in being found, and points at nothing later. Any plan stays undated. Baz has never seen a case or the collector, and Caldas carries nothing to do with them. What Baz has kept from Lucien is not said here.

**Ends.** Sunday afternoon in the park. The notebook without the wind in it. Baz knows what the stop-work costs Sal's crew. Nothing said to Lucien about Sal. Caldas has found him and gone. Baz has decided to tell Lucien, and has not yet. A short after may run on: Baz standing on the path once Caldas has gone, until somebody at a grill looks over.
