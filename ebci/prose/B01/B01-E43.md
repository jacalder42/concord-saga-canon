# Book 1, Episode 43 (working label: Something Strange That Doesn't Hurt)

Baz's point of view. City Park. A Sunday afternoon, about 26 April. About 1,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** Wonder that belongs to one person, and Baz as a person, apart from his method.

**What he wants here.** To keep something that is his: a Sunday afternoon of his own.

**Who pushes back.** Nobody. Only his own method, which wants everything written down.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Nothing.

**Already true.**
- Baz has told Lucien he is staying in the city and will find a room of his own. For now he is still on Lucien's floor.
- He refused to be part of the false report, told Lucien it was wrong, and promised to tell no one, so he knows it named Sal's job. How he knows the job was stopped is the prose's; he may have been told.
- His private column has two lines in Seraphine's favor, and he has not told her about it.
- Home, for the wind, is Marseille and his father's port.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- An ordinary Sunday in the park.
- A dry wind comes through the park that feels like the wind at home, and he does not write it down.

**What it costs or changes.** His first withholding from his own method: one thing stays out of the notebook, by his choice. He knows Sal's job was stopped, and does not raise it with Lucien.

**Still open.** Sal stays unsaid between Baz and Lucien.

**Keep off the page.** No mechanism, no danger, no report. The wind is not shared as evidence and not logged, and whether it is anything at all stays unknown. Nothing ominous in his pleasures or plans; any plan stays undated. He has never seen a case or the collector.

**Ends.** Sunday afternoon in the park. The notebook without the wind in it. Nothing said to Lucien about Sal.
