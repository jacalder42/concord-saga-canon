# Book 1, Episode 40 (working label: Civic Fractures)

Mara's point of view. Her food run: her hall and phone, then a lot where her line forms. A Wednesday, about 22 April. About 3,300 words.

**What this scene is for.** Coordination failure, not spectacle: the city's seams show through practical work.

**What she wants here.** Every person on her list fed and accounted for by name, in her own book, and the list kept whole. Today, to keep her six food boxes going on a day the delivery does not come.

**Who pushes back.** The people in her line, pulling it toward opposite word about when to be indoors: an older woman in a church hat, a mother with a toddler, and those who side with each. The rumor's *they*, which the line treats as somebody with a plan. Then fear and conflicting information.

**Pressing on her from outside.** The city's hold stops her truck: the city's authority, a safety call its stated reason, the city's interests as far as she can tell. Around her line, the rumor's *they* is spoken of as an organized someone. Nobody names who, and neither does the page.

**Already true.**
- After Ursulines, Mara added that block's households to her Friday boxes, with counts Seraphine gave her. Mrs. Arceneaux is on the list.
- Mara and Seraphine know each other through the phone, the forms and the pantry night.
- Today is the second of two middays the three friends had marked. Mara does not know that, and the page does not tell her.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The truck held, and the boxes rationed.
- The line on the lot, and the rumor's hour.
- The line breaks over the conflicting word, and nobody makes room for anybody. Mara decides alone.

**What it costs or changes.** On paper, she strikes Mrs. Batson off the list (a son is coming home to her), and she keeps what happened on the lot from Seraphine. Cohesion breaks under fear, conflicting information and contagion. Mara's book stays whole because she chose who came off it.

**Still open.** The hour the rumor named passes with nothing. Seraphine does not know about the lot.

**Keep off the page.** Not mind control, and nothing strange: people do all of this themselves, and nothing strange happens today. Officials and offices stay unnamed; the *they* is the line's word, never the narrator's, and nobody says who is behind anything. No meeting scene carries it. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** The same Wednesday. The hour people feared has passed with nothing; the line has broken up; Mara holds her book, with Mrs. Batson struck; Seraphine has not been told about the lot.
