# Book 1, Episode 42 (working label: Nobody Is Graceful When Exhausted)

Caro's point of view. A client family's newly rented house, being painted by friends. A Saturday, about 25 April. About 3,900 words. Keep the back-steps meal and the one physical mishap as rest; the additions must not crowd them out.

**What this scene is for.** Exhaustion, with its irritation, absurdity and affection; Caro's own will on the page. Friends at their worst, and fond anyway.

**What she wants here.** A day that is hers: one basic task done, and her own record kept straight. Today, to get Seraphine down and resting.

**Who pushes back.** Seraphine, who will not come down off the chair. Lucien, who gets there first and holds the chair instead. The family's eldest girl, whose invitation brought Seraphine here. Her own supervisor.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Her supervisor has a memo: the run logs now go up on paper at the end of every shift, he signs for them, and nobody prints a run for anyone else without his signature. Her ambulance service's authority; public-health aggregation as the reason; whose interests, unclear to her and to him. Nobody says who reads them.

**Already true.**
- At the diner Caro refused Seraphine the dispatch times, and neither gave ground. The printout is Caro reversing her own refusal on her own terms, not a concession won from her.
- Caro is the one friend Seraphine has told about the folder.
- Seraphine kept the caseload cut (the Ursulines block) from Caro; Caro heard of it from someone else.
- After her dawn with Elisabet, Caro looked her up and put the phone away unread.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The logs, printed anyway. She strips them to what cannot find a person, and knows that does not settle her own objection: she refused exactly this at the diner, and prints it anyway, for her own reason, and owns it.
- The painting, and the chair. The painting is everyone's ordinary want for a day: how each person paints shows who they are, and it can go wrong once, comically, for Caro.
- Once, Caro tests what she has seen between Seraphine and Lucien with a tease she could deny, off something the eldest girl says about the chair. Seraphine does not take it. The refusal pushes Caro toward the sink. It goes no further than what Caro already knows, and it is the only such beat: Caro does not also read Seraphine's body for signs.
- The anger, and staying: Seraphine says she is fine, and Caro answers that she knows, and lets it stand. Caro has known her for years and can hear which of Seraphine's voices that word comes in; that is why she does not argue. The friendship has a history here, not only a present quarrel.
- Before the day ends, the anger gets its answer: Seraphine says why she kept the news back, and Caro says what it cost her to hear it elsewhere.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Caro prints the run logs and gives them to Seraphine without being asked. She texts Lucien behind Seraphine's back. She is angry about the caseload cut, and paints anyway. She goes home with paint on her hands, knees sore from the floor, a shift in the morning, and the memory of covering for her friend in front of a stranger. The friendship holds, with Caro's own will visible: she gave, unasked, what upstairs was also taking. The anger ends answered, not reconciled.

**Still open.** The walls still want a second coat. Her employer does not know about the printout.

**Keep off the page.** Ms. Dorsey's resentment of the help is hers, said her own way, not the retort other people in the book use. Nothing supernatural; no jokes at the expense of anyone's suffering. The printout shows falls and lift-assists at the times of the events they know, in parts of the city the three had no word from, and nothing beyond New Orleans. It is a gift of paper, not Caro handing her own work to someone else. The caseload anger is answered in words, never by the painting or an embrace. Nobody says who upstairs is. Between Seraphine and Lucien, nothing romantic is said aloud; Caro's tease stays deniable, goes no further than what she already knows, and adds no new grievance about him. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** The end of the painting day. The logs are with Seraphine; Caro's anger has been spoken and answered; the room is unfinished; Caro is due back on shift; her employer does not know.
