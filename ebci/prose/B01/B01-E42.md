# Book 1, Episode 42 (working label: Nobody Is Graceful When Exhausted)

Caro's point of view. A client family's newly rented house, being painted by friends. A Saturday, about 25 April. About 3,200 words.

**What this scene is for.** Exhaustion, with its irritation, absurdity and affection; Caro's own will on the page. Friends at their worst, and fond anyway.

**What she wants here.** A day that is hers: one basic task done, and her own record kept straight. Today, to get Seraphine down and resting.

**Who pushes back.** Seraphine, who will not come down off the chair. Lucien, who gets there first and holds the chair instead. The family's eldest girl, whose invitation brought Seraphine here. Her own supervisor.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Her supervisor has told her the run logs are now sent up the line, and that only their times are wanted there: her ambulance service's authority; public-health aggregation as the reason; whose interests, unclear to her. Nobody says who.

**Already true.**
- At the diner Caro refused Seraphine the dispatch times, and neither gave ground. The printout is Caro reversing her own refusal on her own terms, not a concession won from her.
- Caro is the one friend Seraphine has told about the folder.
- After her dawn with Elisabet, Caro looked her up and put the phone away unread.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The logs, printed anyway.
- The painting, and the chair.
- The anger, and staying: Seraphine says she is fine, and Caro answers that she knows, and lets it stand.

**What it costs or changes.** Caro prints the run logs and gives them to Seraphine without being asked. She texts Lucien behind Seraphine's back. She is angry about the caseload cut, which she heard of from someone else, and paints anyway. The friendship holds, with Caro's own will visible: she gave, unasked, what upstairs was also taking.

**Still open.** The walls still want a second coat. Her employer does not know about the printout.

**Keep off the page.** Nothing supernatural; no jokes at the expense of anyone's suffering. The printout shows falls and lift-assists at the times of the events they know, in parts of the city the three had no word from, and nothing beyond New Orleans. It is a gift of paper, not Caro handing her own work to someone else. The caseload anger is not settled by the painting. Nobody says who upstairs is. Hand on hand is the ceiling for Seraphine and Lucien; his help touches the chair, not her. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** The end of the painting day. The logs are with Seraphine; the room is unfinished; Caro is due back on shift; her employer does not know.
