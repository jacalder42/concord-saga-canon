# Book 1, Episode 4 (working label: The Inspector's Line)

Lucien's point of view. His working days in the city: his rented room, Sal's restoration job on Kerlerec, a corner store near it. Then the family's camp at the swamp's edge, for a site visit, and the city again. Late February, from the days after the death to the Saturday of the visit. About 3,600 words.

**What this scene is for.** Lucien, introduced by how he sees: New Orleans through a precise, private man. Then his own perception turns unreliable, and he puts a lie on a legal record. Two ways of knowing meet badly.

**What he wants here.** His standing: a clean, bounded job, and a box on the form for what he is told. At the site visit for the coroner's file, he tries to fit Seraphine's account into the form's boxes and keep his own finding clean.

**Who pushes back.** Seraphine, who insists her account goes on the form in her own words.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. His posting: a legitimate, bounded professional brief, with a report owed. The coroner's form and its fixed lines: the office's authority, an accurate record as its reason, the record's interest. Sal's schedule: a contractor's authority over his own job and crew, in his own interest.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- His working days. Once, a corner post reads out of true, toward the low sun. He checks it, and it is plumb.
- The site visit. Her account doesn't fit his categories; he writes it in as she says it.
- In the room where the boy died, while he is taking her account, something takes him bodily and he goes down. He tells her less than happened.
- He writes that he found nothing on the inspector's line.

**What it costs or changes.** Both are on the form now: her paragraph in her own words, and his *None*, a false statement on a legal record. Before, they were strangers. Now she is his witness, and her words sit beside his lie, which she doesn't know about.

**Still open.** The lie on the form.

**Keep off the page.** Seraphine is a witness and a person, not a mystic or an expert. Nothing objective bends: every check he makes finds nothing wrong. The post's lean is faint and unnamed; *toward the low sun* is the only bearing he gets, and he doesn't map, name or interpret it. Who sent him, and under what cover: no institution, sponsor, family history or superior. He may be from Vienna; no scene there. The camp is a house where a boy died, and nobody infers anything about the place. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**Ends.** After the Saturday visit. The form, with her paragraph and his *None*, is with Guidry. Lucien is back in the city and has told no one how bad it was.
