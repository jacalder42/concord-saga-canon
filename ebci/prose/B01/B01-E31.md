# Book 1, Episode 31 (working label: Easter, and a Case on the Chalk)

Lucien's point of view. The Marigny, along the chalked curbs off Esplanade; Mr. Ledet's house; that evening, wherever the three meet. Easter Sunday, early April; the window opens the next day. About 3,000 words.

**What this scene is for.** Someone else has been at Lucien's evidence, and he chooses the Easter table over waiting for them. That evening the three face a limit: they cannot warn every household in the window's blocks, so they choose whom to tell. A small, cold find on a holiday morning, a warm, ordinary table, then an evening's hard arithmetic about who gets told.

**What he wants here.** To be right first, on paper: to re-walk the edges he chalked on Saturday before the window, and be ready.

**Who pushes back.** Seraphine, who tells him to let it be. Trip, who refuses him the Vein's front window as a watch post: it is her room and her customers' window, and she is right to keep it. Mr. Ledet, who is expecting him for Easter. Then his own want to catch whoever it was. In the evening, the window's blocks themselves: more doors than three people can reach before it opens.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Someone else's will, inside his evidence: a grey case, unmarked but for a small printed asset tag whose code begins *TD-* and a number, zip-tied low on one of his chalk marks, with a cable running into the drain. It arrived after the event there, from nobody he knows. It states no authority and no reason, and whose interests it serves is unknown. It takes, and asks nothing.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The waltz is still with him: the night before has changed how he is with her, and their teasing goes on.
- A message from R., his contact at the Hollen Institute: the March report is overdue, and is there anything of note in the city? Lucien drafts a reply, deletes it, and sends nothing.
- The chalk re-walked, in a few lines; Trip's refusal of the window, a bargain, given whole. A grey case on his Dauphine mark, with its small asset tag.
- He wants to wait for its owner, and doesn't. He leaves it untouched and goes to Mr. Ledet's.
- Mr. Ledet's Easter.
- That evening: they cannot tell everyone in the window's blocks before it opens, so they choose whom to tell, Seraphine's households. It is triage, a choice about who counts, and all three know someone on those blocks is left out.

**What it costs or changes.** Without saying it, he sees that someone else has put out instruments of their own, the approach he once considered recommending. That is recognition, not evidence that anyone read his letter. In his own head, an ugly want: that the next one comes as the page says, even if it lands on someone, so the page will be right. To want it, he sets aside what he saw at the Marigny crossing and Elisabet's flat readings, and tells no one. His evidence is no longer only his: someone else measures where it has been, and arrives after. The three have chosen who counts.

**Still open.** The case stays where it is. The households on the window's blocks who have not been told.

**Keep off the page.** The case shows no reading and does nothing; its only mark is the asset tag, and nobody explains it or reads it as anyone's. Nobody says whose it is, and nobody says the three of them are watched. Lucien voices no distrust of the Institute aloud and tells it nothing; he may think about what it covers, its senders unnamed; he registers the case as a route, never as anyone's motive. The waltz's aftermath may carry teasing and touch. Anything romantic said aloud, and any kiss. Touch, nearness and attraction bending a practical choice are available, never required. If the Marigny's score comes up, one clause says why it is neither hit nor miss: it came before the window opened, so the page's terms could not count it. The choice of whom to tell is a limit on reach, never a test: nobody is left untold to keep a sample clean, and Lucien's ugly want stays in his own head. No setting of watches or counting of fences in place of acting. The Vein does not become a headquarters. R.'s message names no one beyond R. and asks for nothing specific; Lucien's unsent draft stays unsent, and he says nothing against the Institute.

**Ends.** Easter Sunday evening. The case is untouched on the chalk mark, and Lucien has seen its tag. Lucien has been to Mr. Ledet's. The three have chosen to tell Seraphine's households in the window's blocks, and know the rest go untold. The window opens tomorrow, with the page's one claim waiting on it. R.'s message is unanswered.
