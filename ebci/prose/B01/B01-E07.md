# Book 1, Episode 7 (working label: Instability in the Square)

Seraphine's point of view. Jackson Square. Early March, the Saturday after the benefits office; Lent. About 3,000 words.

**What this scene is for.** The strange repeats in a known public place and becomes a pattern. The unease of a repeat, and a helper who has overreached.

**What she wants here.** To be there first. On a Saturday visit to a client who works the Square, she feels it coming up and tries to be where it will matter before anyone else moves.

**Who pushes back.** The street preacher, whose corner of the Square is his and who doesn't want her in it. A groomsman in a wedding party crossing the Square.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. The Square's ordinary policing: public order in a shared place, for the people who use it and for the city's interest in a quiet Square.

**Life in the scene.** Her client at the Square has her own day, her own trade and her own terms, and her own loss when the table goes over. The Square is a working place first: a band at its corner, painters on the fence, a wedding in Lent.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The Square at work, and a wedding in Lent.
- A pressure rises slowly among the people there. She feels it coming and goes to the preacher before anyone moves.
- The wedding party goes brittle. The preacher tells the crowd she knew it was on its way before anyone did. Then it lets go, all at once.
- She writes down that she made it worse.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Her early move, made in public, made the moment worse, and she records that it did. It happened again, under different circumstances, and this time she handled it less cleanly. What it leaves: a palm skinned on the flagstones, her client's cards soaking and a page unsigned, and the preacher's claim about her said aloud in public.

**Still open.** It has happened twice.

**Keep off the page.** A stated cause; calming by presence; anyone's thoughts read; a law stated by the narrator. The preacher's accusation is his, and the page neither confirms nor refutes it. Her wish to be first shows in what she does and is never labeled. The mounted officer's reading is an ordinary one. The repeat matters more than spectacle. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**Ends.** Days after the benefits office. Seraphine leaves the Square with a scraped palm and her notebook entry. A mounted officer has taken it for a wedding scuffle. The preacher's claim has been said aloud, in public. She has thought of the engineer and has no way to him but Guidry's paperwork; first, she means to ask someone who hears things, Monday evening. A short after-scene in the car, with her hand and the notebook, is allowed.

## S02 — a public record, after the Square

**What it is for.** A public record that describes what happened without understanding it. The reader's response should be *was that similar?*, never *this is happening everywhere*.

**What it holds.** Witness accounts of the Square that disagree. The city's procedural answer: a line to be painted there. And one small, unrelated item from far away: a fish auction somewhere distant, at which something may or may not have been similar.

**Keep off the page.** Anything but ordinary public language. Any confirmation that it is widespread, any network or pattern stated, any coordination between places. No organization, group or antagonist named; the city speaks only as the city. No explanation of how anything works. Nothing about the camp. The far item stays small and ordinary, and is left alone.

**Scale.** A short piece, about a page (about 600 words). Its form is open: a news brief, a civic notice, or similar.
