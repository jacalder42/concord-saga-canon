# Book 1, Episode 48 (working label: The Square Remembers)

Lucien's point of view. Jackson Square. A Friday, about 1 May, the start of a festival weekend. About 3,500 words.

**What this scene is for.** Return to the first public place, where people's memory matters; write an honest account; choose the next watch, and call it a bet. A place that carries memory because people do; a bet made in the open.

**What he wants here.** To be right on paper, honestly this time: an account under their names, then a post he chooses.

**Who pushes back.** Baz and Seraphine, over Baz's private column. The signed page, against what it rested on. Vernice at her stall, who wants to know what they are calling it.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Guidry, on the phone, closing the file with both statements and Lucien's amendment attached. He says honestly that someone upstairs asked for a copy first, and he does not know who: the coroner's authority, his own part reasonable, the request above him unexplained. Beside the city's complaint notice on the Square's fence, a gray case, zip-tied low, that arrived after. No stated authority; whoever it is wants only the time. Nobody says who.

**Already true.**
- Until now the page has been scored only in private: Baz's columns on the night of Ursulines, and Lucien's line on the back of the page. This is the first account under all three names.
- It stays one short passage. Fixed in it: the two middays missed, and the bet on its coming faster wrong. It may also say, as briefly, that the page was right once on time and place, wrong on what to watch for and on *larger*, and had no line for who got hurt.
- Baz's column has at least two lines in her favor. Lucien has known of it since the ferry, and agreed with Baz not to tell her. Whether she learns that Lucien knew is the prose's; if she does, it belongs to the same grievance.
- The asking at home was her proposal.
- The case on the Square's fence is not the first: Lucien found one on Dauphine (still untold), Seraphine one on the Robertson corner, and the two of them watched one taken from Sal's jack post. Whether Lucien now tells the others about Dauphine is the prose's.
- The amendment on Guidry's file is the letter Lucien mailed on the Friday before Carrollton.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The Square's life, and the asking at home reported: nobody who went home had it again; the lot shivered once more.
- Guidry's call, and the case on the fence. Lucien sets his own post away from it.
- He tells Seraphine and Baz the fact of Guidry's copy, beside R.'s note that the report on the camp went up in March by another hand, and says nothing more about it.
- The honest account, carefully. They name it systemic, which does not mean understood.
- The column: Seraphine reads Baz's column, the houses that shook against her own list. She tells him he never put it to her; he answers that he knew what she would say.
- The bet and the posts: still surfaces for tomorrow, said aloud as a bet.

**What it costs or changes.** They choose a narrow window at the Square and a kind of thing to watch: still water, because glass and ground have each shown a different face, and still water is the quietest thing they can watch that nothing has ruled out. Seraphine and Baz: before, warm, her own concealment admitted; after, warm, with a grievance on her side.

**Still open.** Tomorrow's tins. Who else is measuring the Square.

**Keep off the page.** People remember, not stone or water. No single site, crowd or institution as the cause; no claim about the world. The bet is said as a bet: nothing they have seen says water must be next, and the failures are findings about these events, not laws. The honest account is one short passage, not a summing-up. Lucien registers the route the file took, never a motive: no column of names, no route drawn on paper, no judgment of his office aloud, and nothing sent to it about the cases or the trap. Nobody says who upstairs is, or that the three are watched. No bearing for which way he goes. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Friday at the Square. Posts chosen for tomorrow: Seraphine's, Baz's, one in the middle, and one on Royal, held by Mrs. Carmouche for Mara. The tins still to fill; the file closed; the case on the fence; the column read; the honest account signed.
