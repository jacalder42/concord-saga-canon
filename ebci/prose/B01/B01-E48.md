# Book 1, Episode 48 (working label: The Square Remembers)

Lucien's point of view. Jackson Square, and a table at its edge. A Friday, about 1 May, the start of a festival weekend. About 3,500 words.

**What this scene is for.** Return to the first public place, where people's memory matters; write an honest account; choose the next watch, and call it a bet. A place that carries memory because people do; a bet made in the open.

**What he wants here.** To be right on paper, honestly this time: an account under their names, then a post he chooses.

**Who pushes back.** Baz and Seraphine, over Baz's private column. Seraphine, over what he wrote on the form. The signed page, against what it rested on. Vernice at her stall, who wants to know what they are calling it. A stranger at a table who wants their record.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Guidry, on the phone, closing the file with both statements and Lucien's amendment attached. He says honestly that someone upstairs asked for a copy first, and he does not know who: the coroner's authority, his own part reasonable, the request above him unexplained. Beside the city's complaint notice on the Square's fence, a gray case, zip-tied low, that arrived after. No stated authority; whoever it is wants only the time. Then, for once, someone asks: at a table at the Square's edge, while Baz is away, a person nobody can place sits across from Seraphine, knows that the three keep a record of times, and asks her for it (the page, her log). No name, no institution, no stated agenda; the person lets her understand that the holds on the blocks where her people live could end. Nobody says who.

**Already true.**
- Until now the page has been scored only in private: Baz's columns on the night of Ursulines, and Lucien's line on the back of the page. This is the first account under all three names.
- It stays one short passage. Fixed in it: the two middays missed, and the bet on its coming faster wrong. It may also say, as briefly, that the page was right once on time and place, wrong on what to watch for and on *larger*, and had no line for who got hurt.
- Baz's column has at least two lines in her favor. Lucien has known of it since the ferry, and agreed with Baz not to tell her. Whether she learns that Lucien knew is the prose's; if she does, it belongs to the same grievance.
- The asking at home was her proposal. Miss Hazel did not answer her door; if the report touches it, it stays unanswered.
- The case on the Square's fence is not the first: Lucien found one on Dauphine (still untold), Seraphine one on the Robertson corner, and the two of them watched one taken from Sal's jack post. Whether Lucien now tells the others about Dauphine is the prose's.
- The amendment on Guidry's file is the letter Lucien mailed on the Friday before Carrollton.
- Since Sunday, Seraphine has known, from the family's copy, that he wrote *None* on the form beside her paragraph and signed it twice. She has not spoken to him about it, and he has given no explanation.
- R. is his contact at the Hollen Institute.
- Sal's job has been stopped since Lucien's false report. Denise wrote Seraphine up for taking the center's car. The city's holds on deliveries into the glass blocks are still on, Mr. Vidrine's oxygen among them.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The Square's life, and the asking at home reported: nobody who went home had it again; the lot shivered once more.
- Guidry's call, and the case on the fence. Lucien sets his own post away from it.
- He tells Seraphine and Baz the fact of Guidry's copy, and that the file closed with his letter attached, beside R.'s note that the report on the camp went up in March by another hand, and says nothing more about it. At *my letter* Seraphine nods: she has known since Sunday what it corrected. What passes between them is cool and specific, not a reconciliation.
- The table: the stranger asks, and Seraphine refuses, plainly. The refusal has its price: the holds do not end, Mr. Vidrine's tanks stay held, and she knows her refusal is part of why.
- The honest account, carefully. They name it systemic, which does not mean understood.
- The column: Seraphine reads Baz's column, the houses that shook against her own list. She tells him he never put it to her; he answers that he knew what she would say.
- The bet and the posts: still surfaces for tomorrow, said aloud as a bet.
- Once, in his own point of view, Lucien names to himself what he feels for her. He does not say it.

**What it costs or changes.** They choose a narrow window at the Square and a kind of thing to watch: still water, because glass and ground have each shown a different face, and still water is the quietest thing they can watch that nothing has ruled out. Seraphine and Baz: before, warm, her own concealment admitted; after, warm, with a grievance on her side. Seraphine and Lucien: before, she knows and has said nothing; after, it is acknowledged between them, coolly and specifically, and not repaired. Sal's job is still stopped, and Denise's write-up is still in Seraphine's file.

**Still open.** Tomorrow's tins. Who else is measuring the Square. Who asked, and whether they will ask again.

**Keep off the page.** People remember, not stone or water. No single site, crowd or institution as the cause; no claim about the world. The bet is said as a bet: nothing they have seen says water must be next, and the failures are findings about these events, not laws. The honest account is one short passage, not a summing-up. Lucien registers the route the file took, never a motive: no column of names, no route drawn on paper, no judgment of the Institute aloud, and nothing sent to it about the cases, the trap or the table. He may think of the Institute as the cover it is; the people behind it stay unnamed. Nobody says who upstairs is, or that the three are watched. The stranger stays unnamed and unplaced: no name, institution, uniform, logo or agenda, no claim to speak for anyone, and Baz never sees them; whether they could have ended the holds is never shown. Lucien's named feeling is his own thought, once, never a declaration; hand on hand stays the ceiling. The nod is knowledge, not forgiveness. No bearing for which way he goes. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Friday at the Square. Posts chosen for tomorrow: Seraphine's, Baz's, one in the middle, and one on Royal, held by Mrs. Carmouche for Inez. The tins still to fill; the file closed; the case on the fence; the column read; the honest account signed. The stranger refused and gone; the holds still on; Sal's job still stopped; Denise's write-up still in Seraphine's file; what Lucien wrote on the form acknowledged between them, and not repaired.
