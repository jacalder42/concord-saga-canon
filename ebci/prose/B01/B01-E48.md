# Book 1, Episode 48 (working label: The Square Remembers)

Lucien's point of view. Jackson Square, and a table at its edge. A Friday, about 1 May, the start of a festival weekend. About 4,200 words.

**What this scene is for.** Return to the first public place, where people's memory matters; write an honest account; choose the next watch, and call it a bet. Then Baz tells Lucien what he has kept from him. A place that carries memory because people do; a bet made in the open; and a friend's confession that makes Lucien's spring into something else.

**What he wants here.** To be right on paper, honestly this time: an account under their names, then a post he chooses. At the end, to know what his own work has been for.

**Who pushes back.** Baz and Seraphine, over Baz's private column. Seraphine, over what he wrote on the form. The signed page, against what it rested on. Vernice at her stall, who wants to know what they are calling it. A stranger at a table who wants their record. Baz, at the end, who has known more than he said.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Guidry, on the phone, with both statements and Lucien's letter on his file: he is holding the question of the house open and leaving the manner undetermined, his own call; and someone upstairs asked for a copy first, and he does not know who. The family's final certificate does not come. Beside the city's complaint notice on the Square's fence, a gray case, zip-tied low, that arrived after. No stated authority. Then, for once, someone asks: at a table at the Square's edge, while Baz is away, a woman nobody can place sits across from Seraphine, knows that the three keep a record of times, and asks her for it (the page, her log), and keeps asking for the times. No name, no institution, no stated agenda; she lets Seraphine understand that the holds on the blocks where her people live could end. Nobody says who.

**Already true.**
- Until now the page has been scored only in private: Baz's columns on the night of Ursulines, and Lucien's line on the back of the page. This is the first account under all three names.
- It stays one short passage. Fixed in it: the two middays missed, and the bet on its coming faster wrong. It may also say, as briefly, that the page was right once on time and place, wrong on what to watch for and on *larger*, and had no line for who got hurt; and what held (sitting down, the stove off, nobody up a ladder).
- Baz's column has at least two lines in her favor. Lucien has known of it since the ferry, and agreed with Baz not to tell her. Whether she learns that Lucien knew is the prose's; if she does, it belongs to the same grievance.
- The asking at home was her proposal. Miss Hazel did not answer her door; if the report touches it, it stays unanswered.
- The case on the Square's fence is not the first: Lucien found one on Dauphine (still untold), Seraphine one on the Robertson corner, and the two of them watched one taken from Sal's jack post. Whether Lucien now tells the others about Dauphine is the prose's.
- Lucien watched the church lot at dawn this morning for whoever would bring a case after Mrs. Carmouche's report. Nothing came; the case was already on the Square's fence, where they had chosen to keep their watch.
- On Thursday afternoon Lucien went to the city's inspector and withdrew his false complaint on the record, under his own name, putting his name and the Institute's on one more form against R.'s advice. It answered the question Sal's client had about Sal's license, so the client stays; the red tag stays until reinspection, and the lost weeks stay lost.
- The letter on Guidry's file is the one Lucien mailed on the Friday before Carrollton. Seraphine knows he wrote a correction; what it says, and what came of it, are news to her today.
- Since Sunday, Seraphine has known, from the family's copy, that he wrote *None* on the form beside her paragraph and signed it twice. On Wednesday evening they had it out: she set her term (he does not speak for her account without her agreement), and she left dissatisfied.
- R. is his contact at the Hollen Institute. Since Carrollton, Lucien has been asking himself what his review is for.
- Since Sunday, a man has found Baz in City Park and let him understand it touches Lucien. Lucien does not know this.
- Sal's job has been stopped since Lucien's false report. Denise wrote Seraphine up for taking the center's car. The city's holds on deliveries into the glass blocks are still on, Mr. Vidrine's oxygen among them.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The Square's life, and the asking at home reported: nobody who went home had it again; the lot shivered once more. The dawn watch and the withdrawal are reported, not replayed.
- Guidry's call, and the case on the fence. Lucien sets his own post away from it.
- He tells Seraphine and Baz the fact of Guidry's copy, and that Guidry is holding the question open with his letter on the file, beside R.'s note that the report on the camp went up in March by another hand, and says nothing more about it. At *my letter* Seraphine nods: she has known since Sunday what it corrected. What passes between them is cool and specific, not a reconciliation. Her part of the honest account is in her own hand.
- The table: the woman asks, and Seraphine refuses, plainly. It is a contest, and the woman leaves without the record; nothing suggests the three now control every copy. The refusal has its price: the holds do not end, Mr. Vidrine's tanks stay held, and she knows her refusal is part of why.
- The honest account, carefully. They name it systemic, which does not mean understood.
- The column, briefly: Seraphine reads Baz's column, the houses that shook against her own list. She tells him he never put it to her; he answers that he knew what she would say. It stays unsettled.
- The bet and the posts: still surfaces for tomorrow, said aloud as a bet.
- Once, in his own point of view, Lucien names to himself what he feels for her. He does not say it: to say it, he would first have to tell her who reads his notes.
- After the posts, Baz and Lucien alone. Baz tells him that the people behind the Institute were already watching this, that it is real and it happens again, that he came to the city partly because of it, and that a man from them, Caldas, has found him. He says nothing of how it works. It lands as betrayal: Baz knew while Lucien was being used. In his own point of view, Lucien's question about his review becomes a belief: it has had him finding these events, for them.

**What it costs or changes.** They choose a narrow window at the Square and a kind of thing to watch: still water, because glass and ground have each shown a different face, and still water is the quietest thing they can watch that nothing has ruled out. Seraphine and Baz: before, warm, her own concealment admitted; after, warm, with a grievance on her side. Seraphine and Lucien: before, she knows and has said nothing to the letter; after, it is acknowledged between them, coolly and specifically, and not repaired. Lucien and Baz: before, friends, with Baz keeping what he knows; after, told, betrayed, and not repaired. Sal's job is still stopped, and Denise's write-up is still in Seraphine's file.

**Still open.** Tomorrow's tins. Who else is measuring the Square. Who asked, and whether they will ask again. What Lucien does with what Baz told him, and with Baz. What Guidry decides.

**Keep off the page.** People remember, not stone or water. No single site, crowd or institution as the cause; no claim about the world. The bet is said as a bet: nothing they have seen says water must be next, and the failures are findings about these events, not laws. The honest account is one short passage, not a summing-up. One closing, not two. Lucien registers the route the file took, never a motive: no column of names, no route drawn on paper, no judgment of the Institute aloud except to Baz, alone, and nothing sent to it about the cases, the trap or the table. He may think of the Institute as the cover it is; the people behind it stay unnamed, spoken of only obliquely. Baz says *that* they watch this and why he came; never how it works, what it is, or any explanation. The narration does not say Baz was wrong to keep it. Nobody says who upstairs is, or that the three are watched. The woman at the table stays unnamed and unplaced: no name, institution, uniform, logo or agenda, no claim to speak for anyone, nothing on her that the cases carry, and Baz never sees her; whether she could have ended the holds is never shown. Lucien's named feeling is his own thought, once, never a declaration; nothing romantic is said aloud, and the dawn's kiss stays called nothing. The nod is knowledge, not forgiveness. Seraphine does not hear what Baz tells Lucien. No bearing for which way he goes. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Friday at the Square, then Baz and Lucien alone. Posts chosen for tomorrow: Seraphine's, Baz's, one in the middle, and one on Royal, held by Mrs. Carmouche for Inez. The tins still to fill; the certificate still pending, Guidry holding the question open; the case on the fence; the column read; the honest account signed. The woman refused and gone; the holds still on; Sal's job still stopped; Denise's write-up still in Seraphine's file; what Lucien wrote on the form acknowledged between them, and not repaired. Baz has told Lucien, alone, and Seraphine does not know it. Lucien believes his review has had him finding these events for the people behind the Institute.
