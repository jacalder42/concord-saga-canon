# Book 1, Episode 17 (working label: Originals, by Consent)

Baz's point of view. The streets, on foot; Isaiah's family's home; Jackson Square. Late March, the Tuesday, the day after he arrives. About 2,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** Baz's method begins: he gets the Square's two recordings as originals, from the people who made them, with their consent. A stranger earning a family's yes by patience, and the first thing in the case that two machines agree on.

**What he wants here.** To belong here, beginning with being trusted in someone's kitchen. Today: the two recordings of the Square, from their makers, by consent.

**Who pushes back.** Isaiah's mother, who says no. Lucien, who would take the copy already going around, or leave it to the proper channel, where the police or the city could ask for the files and the files would then be theirs.

**Pressing on him from outside.** Others with more standing want the same evidence. The police could ask for it, a reasonable claim that Lucien voices, and the city has asked Clement for the phone itself, beside the video he already sent: ordinary evidence practice, the original device and its own record of when. Their authority; their reason, evidence of a public incident; their interests, not the families'. Ordinary.

**Already true.** Isaiah is the teenage boy in a school uniform who was in the Square on a school day and filmed it on his own phone, from lower down than Clement's. Clement told the officer the video is his and he would send it himself. Clement's amp is dead; he plays on a borrowed one.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Isaiah's family: the mother's no, and Baz stays, unhurried and funny, until she changes her mind.
- Clement, who talks to nobody before he has sung. Baz waits for that too.
- The two recordings, lined up by the amp's pop: both carry a low sound before the amp quits.

**What it costs or changes.** Baz holds copies by consent, and the order on the two recordings is there for anyone to hear. Nobody certifies what it means. His method is on the page: testimony, memory and consent, beside Lucien's evidence and not instead of it.

**Still open.** What the low sound is, and whose explanation of the amp holds.

**Keep off the page.** Baz never pressures the mother or goes round her. The originals stay with their owners. Fault, casualty and coincidence stay in people's mouths; the page settles nothing. Isaiah is an ordinary boy with a phone, and nothing makes him special. Lucien's evidence is joined, not replaced.

**Ends.** Tuesday. Baz holds copies of both recordings, given by their makers; the originals stay with them. Lucien has heard the two lined up.
