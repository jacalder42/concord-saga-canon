# Book 1, Episode 8 (working label: Closing Time)

Inez's point of view. Her neighborhood food pantry, in the Knights' hall, at closing. Early March, a rainy weekday evening after the Square. About 2,500 words.

**What this scene is for.** Inez's entrance makes her role clear: she runs this pantry and keeps her own book, and she is not Seraphine's employer. Seraphine isn't the only one noticing, and Inez keeps her own record. Care that predates the crisis, kept by someone with her own reasons.

**What she wants here.** To close on time and keep her system whole: a list to finish after a day of ordinary tasks, and her own notebook to keep.

**Who pushes back.** Seraphine, arriving soaked and exhausted just as Inez closes.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. Closing time and the hall's rules: its hours, set by the building's use, serving its owners and Inez's own evening.

**Life in the scene.** Inez's care is feeding people before she talks to them; she does it without being asked and without watching them eat. Before Seraphine comes, the page sees that care done once for someone else in the day's last minutes: practical, unasked, with her own dry humor in it. It must not echo either of the strange lines in her notebook.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- Inez's ordinary day, and her notebook.
- Seraphine arrives at closing, and Inez helps first. Somewhere in it the scraped hand is tended: Inez cleans it, Seraphine flinches, and Inez gives her plain orders about it.
- Inez's notebook holds two earlier strange lines, one a neighbor's account of a fight at the laundromat.
- Seraphine asks her outright whether anyone else she knows has had it happen to them. Inez tells her plainly that she has one account, and it's a neighbor's, not hers to give.
- Seraphine accepts the refusal and leaves a card for the neighbor, through Inez, with her number, asking nothing. Inez keeps it in her notebook and promises nothing.
- Seraphine falls asleep at the table.

**What it costs or changes.** Inez feeds her and lets her sleep. Seraphine doesn't get the account. Before, Seraphine was someone Inez saw at work; now Inez has looked after her unasked, and holds something Seraphine asked for and didn't get.

**Still open.** The neighbor's account, which Inez wouldn't give, and the card left for her.

**Keep off the page.** The pantry is ordinary: not a front, no recruiting, no organization. Inez's refusal is spoken, and it's about someone else's story, not a secret of her own. The page doesn't need to argue it. Her notebook records what people told her and draws no conclusion; Mr. Fontenot is not in it. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**Ends.** Night, the pantry. Seraphine is asleep at the table, fed, under a blanket Inez found her, and Inez keeps the room around her: the hall's meeting goes to its other end. The notebook, with its earlier lines and Seraphine's card, is with Inez. No waking scene.
