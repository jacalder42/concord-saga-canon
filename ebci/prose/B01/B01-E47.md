# Book 1, Episode 47 (working label: The Wide Quiet)

Seraphine's point of view (it may read as ensemble). The lot, at its gate; the city as far as she can hear it. A Thursday, about 30 April, the day after the lot. About 3,200 words.

**Tempo.** In the quiet, short sentences and short paragraphs, white space, few asides. Nothing that happens is cut; only the sentences get shorter.

**What this scene is for.** The negative-space climax. Silence, then sound returning.

**What she wants here.** To go and find where it ends: to reach the edge.

**Who pushes back.** Her own urge, against the line of her procedure that tells her to hold her place. An old man holding a boy on a porch nearby.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Nothing.

**Already true.**
- The procedure is the seven lines the two of them wrote together after the fight, in Lucien's hand; the sixth, about staying at the meeting place, is the one she said before he could write it. She keeps the page folded in the back of her log.
- The three agreed yesterday, on the lot, to meet at its gate at midday with whatever they had.
- Today is the asking at home, which she proposed. Miss Hazel, who went home from the lot with someone else after telling Seraphine it came all the same, is on her sheet.
- Since Sunday she knows what Lucien wrote on the form. He is still the one she cannot reach.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- The morning's doors: ordinary people's ordinary answers, written down without her deciding whether she believes them. Lucien texts to ask what of hers he may give the city's line, which has asked him for an account of the lot; she gives him only that she was there. Miss Hazel's door does not open.
- At the gate, early. Across the street an old man on his porch watches a small boy play without seeming to watch.
- The quiet. The old man takes the boy onto his lap and holds him there, where they were sitting; he explains nothing, takes him nowhere, and looks across at her. His care asks nothing of her.
- The sound comes back.
- Lucien calls once it has, and she answers without letting go of the gate.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** She stays at the gate. The procedure they wrote, in the line she said herself, holds her. The city goes quiet, and the absence frightens: she lets herself know she is afraid, and afterward one hand stays on the warm gate while the other answers the phone.

**Still open.** Lucien, whom she cannot reach while it lasts.

**Keep off the page.** Not compressed in what happens, not comic, not fully explained; no exposition filling the silence. No rule about when or how she feels it. Nothing is measured. A call she places that gives her nothing ends unexplained: nothing says the quiet cut it, that the phone failed, or that it reached him. Miss Hazel's door stays closed to her on the asking at home: noticed, and not reopened. If the quiet brings back any memory, it stays a memory. No clock time in the narration unless someone does something with it.

**Ends.** Sound is back. Seraphine is at the lot's gate, where she stayed. A short after may run on: the porch across the street settling, a greeting between them, and Lucien's call answered at the gate.
