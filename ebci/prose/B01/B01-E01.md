# Book 1, Episode 1 (working label: The Sick Child)

Seraphine's point of view. A family's camp at the swamp's edge, outside New Orleans. Late February, at night. About 2,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** A human cost before anything is explained: grief in a real place, among people who live there.

**What she wants here.** To keep Dré alive until the ambulance comes. He is seven, a child she knows through her work. His grandmother, Odile, called her, and she drove out off-shift.

**Who pushes back.** Odile, who will not let her bring up the lapsed card tonight. Then the road: the ambulance needs half an hour to cover it.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. The parish road and dispatch: the emergency system's own authority and protocol, serving the public, and unable to make the distance shorter. The card: Dré's coverage lapsed in January because the notice went to an old address; the renewal sits unfinished in Seraphine's blue folder, and the unfinished part was hers. Her employer's rules about acting outside her hours.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- She drives out and reaches the family: Odile, and Renée, Dré's mother.
- She works on the boy herself, on the dispatcher's count. A pressure in her ears fills the room evenly and comes from no one in it.
- She starts to own the card, and Odile stops her.
- The ambulance comes too late. Dré dies. She stays.

**What it costs or changes.** She chose to run the resuscitation herself, and she chose to leave her part in the card unsaid when Odile stopped her. It stays unsaid, at a risk to her professional standing and to Renée's trust.

**Still open.** A pressure in that room that doesn't behave like grief. The card, unsaid.

**Keep off the page.** Whatever she perceives stays felt: unconfirmed, unnamed, unexplained, never read as anyone's thoughts. The swamp is where people live; the camp is a family's home, and nobody calls it special. Dré is an ordinary boy with a name; nothing of him lingers or reaches out. Grief and feeling cause nothing. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**Ends.** Night, at the camp. Seraphine is still with Renée and Odile. The blue folder, unfinished, is in her car.
