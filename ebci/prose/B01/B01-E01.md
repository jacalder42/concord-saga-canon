# Book 1, Episode 1 (working label: The Sick Child)

Seraphine's point of view. A family's camp at the swamp's edge, outside New Orleans. Late February, at night. About 2,800 words.

**What this scene is for.** A human cost before anything is explained: grief in a real place, among people who live there.

**What she wants here.** To keep Dré alive until the ambulance comes. He is seven, a child she knows through her work. His grandmother, Odile, called her, and she drove out off-shift.

**Who pushes back.** The road: the ambulance needs half an hour to cover it. Then Renée's grief, which no procedure reaches.

**Pressing on her from outside.** Ordinary friction, with no will behind it. The parish road and dispatch: the emergency system's own authority and protocol, serving the public, and unable to make the distance shorter. The card: Dré's coverage lapsed in January because the notice went to an old address; the renewal sits unfinished in Seraphine's blue folder, and the unfinished part was hers. In the emergency she has forgotten it. Her employer's rules about acting outside her hours: her workplace, the Beaulieu Center for Family Services, is named once here, in full, and is *the center* after that.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- She drives out and reaches the family: Odile, and Renée, Dré's mother.
- She works on the boy herself, on the dispatcher's count. A pressure in her ears fills the room evenly and comes from no one in it. Odile says something about the card, and it reminds Seraphine of the renewal she had forgotten.
- The ambulance comes too late. The crew takes Dré, on oxygen, to the hospital, and Renée rides with him. Dré dies.
- Renée's grief is specific and believable: failed comprehension, a repeated practical act, being unable to leave him, anger aimed somewhere unexpected. She does not simply leave the room.
- Afterwards, in the quiet, Seraphine stays with Odile, and keeps the renewal to herself.

**What it costs or changes.** The child dies, and Seraphine stays with Odile. She chose to run the resuscitation herself. Reminded of the renewal, she says nothing of it that night. It stays unsaid, at a risk to her professional standing and to Renée's trust.

**Still open.** A pressure in that room that doesn't behave like grief. The renewal, unsaid.

**Keep off the page.** Odile's word that the card is why nobody called sooner is her account of the family's decision, not the page's explanation of Dré's death. Odile knows the coverage lapsed; she does not know the renewal sat on Seraphine's desk, and Seraphine does not tell her tonight. Odile may keep her dryness, and her hand on Seraphine's knee. Whatever Seraphine perceives stays felt: unconfirmed, unnamed, unexplained, never read as anyone's thoughts. The swamp is where people live; the camp is a family's home, and nobody calls it special. Dré is an ordinary boy with a name; nothing of him lingers or reaches out. Grief and feeling cause nothing. No clock time in the narration unless someone acts on it.

**Ends.** Night, at the camp. Renée has gone with Dré to the hospital. Seraphine is still with Odile, and has told no one about the renewal. The blue folder, unfinished, is in her car. A generator runs down the bank from the house, as it has all evening.
