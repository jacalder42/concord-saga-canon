# Book 1, Episode 19 (working label: Three Smart People Having Fun)

Seraphine's point of view. Her car, across the city; then a shop. The same Wednesday, into the evening, after the block. About 2,000 words. The curb where she has to pull over stays the one set piece; the counter and the long way home keep their room.

**What this scene is for.** Competence as pleasure, before the investigation becomes a burden: three smart people enjoying each other.

**What she wants here.** A stretch of time that doesn't need her, and to win: the argument about how the city's street names are said.

**Who pushes back.** Lucien's rule that the names are said as if read in English, against her view that a name is said the way the city says it.

**What happens. Only this is fixed:**
- A small question, and the three of them at it. She corrects Baz first, and enjoys it.
- The names are heard. The city's way of saying its own street names is the thing the three play with, and the reader has to hear it: where a name's pronunciation is the joke, its spoken form may be spelled on the page, once, as the thing they are playing with. This is the names, not anyone's accent.
- Lucien's rule wins once before it loses: on one name his reasoning actually lands, and she enjoys watching his mind work enough to slow the car for it. A derivation, not a quip; one beat, not another round.
- They arrive at the point that the names can only be learned by hearing them and saying them back.
- Whatever is shortened, these stay: her catching his private smile in the mirror, and holding it; the long way home; the names said under his breath in the back seat, with the window down; and one more name called after him at the gate, to keep him there a second longer.

*Everything else is yours, including what people say to each other, how often, and in what order.*

**What it costs or changes.** Something small gets solved, for the fun of it. She is at ease with them for the first time since the boy died.

**Still open.** Nothing pressing. The teasing between her and Lucien, which does not stop here.

**Keep off the page.** Any clue or case. Anything romantic said aloud between her and Lucien, and any kiss: the teasing stays light.

**Ends.** Evening. The three together, lighter than they were.
