# The table read: a method for conflict and disclosure scenes

Status: PRODUCTION METHOD, PILOT, 2026-10-06 (`decisions/TABLE_READ_PILOT_AND_ACTION_OVER_STATEMENT_AUTHOR_INSTRUCTION_2026-10-06.md`).
- **Not in the drafting stack:** neither the profile nor the packets cite it.
- **Not canon.**
- **It changes no rule.**

**Why.** When one writer knows everything and voices everyone, characters leak knowledge, answer too neatly, sound
alike, and state their feelings. A table read gives each character only what that person has.

## 1. Roles

| Role | Holds | Never holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Director** | The book up to the scene, the packet, canon. Writes the scene card and the actor cards | — |
| **Actor** (one per character) | The scene card and their own private card | Other actors' cards, the outcome the scene must reach, facts the character does not know at that moment |
| **Writer** | The transcript, the scene card, the outcome card, the profile, and the chapter around the scene | — |
| **Checker** | The writer's scene and the book | — |

## 2. The cards

**Scene card** (all actors): where and when; the light, the weather and the noise; what is physically there; who is
present and what each can see of the others.

**Actor card** (one actor only):
- who they are, from their card, in a few lines;
- **what they know right now, and only that**;
- what they want from this conversation;
- what they will not say, and why;
- what they are afraid of;
- where they stand with each other person (the relationship rung);
- the voice line and dialogue-move line from the voice sheets, and their physical habits;
- anything they have already decided to do or say tonight, if the scene requires it, written as an intention, not
  a line.

**Outcome card** (writer only): what must be true after the scene, as states; what the page must not confirm; the
guards.

## 3. Playing

- Actors take turns. Each turn is one of:
  - an action;
  - a line;
  - an action and a line;
  - silence (*[nothing]*).
- They may interrupt, refuse, mishear, change the subject, or leave.
- **They show feeling through action and choice, not by naming it** (profile §5, *Action carries what speech would
  announce*).
- No narration and no inner monologue: only what a camera and a microphone would catch, plus one bracketed line of
  private intention per turn for the writer.
- **A turn limit** (about 16–24 turns) with a stop condition.
- The director may inject one event: a knock, a dog, a phone.

## 4. Writing

The writer renders the transcript in the POV character's prose under the profile:
- **Cut hard.** Expect to keep 40–60% of the lines.
- Keep the subtext, and the replies that answer the situation rather than the words.
- Other people's inner states appear only through what the POV character perceives.
- The outcome card must be true by the end. Where the transcript misses it, adjust minimally and flag it.

## 5. Use

Use it for **conflict and disclosure scenes**. Scenes that already work, and quiet scenes, do not need it.

**Pilot result (2026-10-06, `reports/TABLE_READ_PILOT_RESULT_2026-10-06.md`).** Full play beat the draft in the
interrogation scene and lost the other two. The losses came from the format: a gesture every turn, and lean,
telegraphic speech. Full play is therefore for **pressure scenes**. Everything else uses §6.

**Amendments from the pilot (2026-10-06):**
- In full play, **an action is optional**: a turn may be a line alone, and most should be.
- The writer keeps a gesture only when it carries something the line does not.

## 6. The screenplay model: performance against a draft

*Added 2026-10-06 by author instruction (`decisions/B01_SCREENPLAY_MODEL_PERFORMANCE_REVISION_AUTHOR_INSTRUCTION_2026-10-06.md`).*
In the author's words: *"The writer has generated the script, the actors interpret the lines with their own voice and
mannerisms, and the director holds it all together."*

| Role | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Writer** | Drafts the chapter under the profile and packet. The draft is the script: it fixes what happens, what each line does, and what each person knows |
| **Actor** (one per character, or one company actor for minor parts) | Reads the script and their own card. Re-performs **only their character's lines and small physical business**: how this person would say it, cut it, interrupt, deflect, joke, or leave it unsaid. Proposes; does not edit |
| **Director** | Merges the performances into the chapter. Holds the scene's outcome, the guards, continuity and length. Caps gestures. Rewrites narration that states a feeling the scene already shows |

**Actor rules:**
- Change *how*, never *what*. The line's job in the scene, and every fact it carries, stays. Add no knowledge the
  character lacks.
- A line that already sounds like the person stays. There is no quota.
- Prefer cutting to adding. A stated feeling may become an action or a silence.
- Do not add a gesture to every line. A small new moment (one sentence) is allowed rarely, flagged.
- No tics or catchphrases. No phonetic dialect. The guards bind.

**Director rules:**
- Accept, adapt or decline each proposal.
- Where two actors' proposals collide, play the scene, not the line.
- Keep the chapter within about ±5% of its length.
- Log what was taken and declined.
