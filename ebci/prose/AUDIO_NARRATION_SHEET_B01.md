# Audio narration sheet: B01, merged draft

**What it is for.** The author's instruction: the casting note goes *"in repo not in prose"*. Its first use is the
author's own listen in ElevenReader (AI text-to-speech): a list to check the engine against, so he can spot
mispronunciations quickly. Its second use, later, is as a brief for a human narrator.

**What it does not change:** no card, rule, ruling, packet, grid, registry row, writer-profile line or manuscript text.
It decides no pronunciation. Where no source states one, the reading below is a proposal marked **author to confirm**.
It holds no manuscript prose. It names single words and names only, and it describes scenes by function.

**Sources:** the merged B01 text (`concord-saga-manuscript/B01/merged-2026-10-05/`, beta-panel pass; every entry was
grep-checked against it); the private language ledger (`draft-notes/post-merge-review/b01-language-ledger.csv`);
`canon/characters/`; `canon/cast_registry.csv` (B12–B21); `decisions/B01_LIVED_IN_LOUISIANA_LANGUAGE_AUTHOR_ANSWERS_2026-10-05.md`;
`proposals/WRITER_PROFILE_12B_LOUISIANA_SPEECH_SIDE_BY_SIDE_2026-10-05.md` (a proposal, not applied);
`proposals/DIALOGUE_VOICE_SHEETS_2026-10-05.md` (review-side); writer profile §12A; the B01 packets' POV lines.

**Chapter keys** are episode numbers. In the reading copy, E01–E04 are Chapters 1–4 and **E06–E49 are Chapter (E − 1)**,
because E05 was folded into E03. S02 is the Supplement between Chapters 6 and 7. I01 is the Interlude after Chapter 35.
E00 is the Prologue and E50 the Epilogue.

**Notation.** Stress is in CAPITALS. **Local** = the New Orleans or Louisiana pronunciation; **naive** = what an outsider
or a TTS engine is likely to say. **[F]** = stated by a source; **[R]** = this sheet's recommendation;
**[ATC]** = author to confirm.

### 1.7 TTS traps

- **St. = Saint** in every street, church and society name above. Engines often read *St.* as *street*. The one
  exception is a written note in which *St* stands for street (E42: *Annette St*).
- **SS office** (E06): Social Security, in Seraphine's notes. A TTS reads it *ess-ess*. That is acceptable, because it
  is what the note says, but a human narrator may prefer *Social Security* [ATC].
- **wound** occurs eleven times, always the past tense of *wind* (WOWND: a cord, wires, tape). Never the noun.
  Engines often say WOOND. E02, E04, E07, E11, E17, E20, E23, E24.
- **record** (noun RECK-ord, verb ruh-KORD). It is a key word in this book. Check E15–E36 in particular.
- **live**, adjective (LYVE): a call still live (E09), live power (E24), a live oak (E19). Verb elsewhere.
- **bass** = BASE, the instrument (E30, E32). **bow**: one a ribbon (BOH, E21), one from the neck (BOW, E30).
  **lead** = LEED (E30). **tear** = TAIR (E15). **present**, adjective, throughout.
- **read**, past tense (RED), 188 times. Engines usually manage it; listen at the start of sentences.
- **con** (E43), **Mais** (E01, E33), **cher** (E01, E34): the engine will read them as English.
- **Numbers and tags:** *911* is nine-one-one (E01, E37, E46); *311* is three-one-one (E27); ticket numbers *B-38,
  B-44, B-45, B-52* (E06); tags *TD-4462*, *4417* (E31, E39): read as digits; *91 5/8* (E10) is ninety-one and
  five-eighths; *23:51* (E12) is twenty-three fifty-one [ATC]; *30.00*, *12.00* (E03) are dollars.
- **Abbreviations in notes:** *Mr. V.*, *Mr. L.*, *Mrs. LaB.*, *S.* (initials, read as letters); *Sun.*, *Wed. a.m.*,
  *approx* (read as full words [R]); *Ms.* MIZ; *Mrs.* MISSiz.
- **Signs in capitals** (S02 and elsewhere): read them as words, not letter by letter.
- **Italics are invisible to TTS.** Texts, notes, remembered speech and the presences' speech are italic, with no
  quotation marks. ElevenReader will read them in the narration voice. See §3.

### 1.8 E19, the street-name game

E19 is a scene about pronunciation: Lucien tries the street names as written, Seraphine and locals give the city's
way, and he keeps a list. The page spells **Bur-gun-dy**, **Ka-lie-oh-pee**, **Mel-po-meen** and **Chop-uh-too-lus**
out phonetically, once each, by design (profile §12A, Q-CA4). Lucien also says **Chartres** in perfect French and
**Tchoupitoulas** letter by letter. **A narrator must perform the wrong versions as wrong** and the right ones as local.
This is the chapter most likely to fail in TTS: listen to all of it.

## 3. Performance notes

- **Prologue (E00), Interlude (I01) and Epilogue (E50).** Two presences high above the weather. The page does not name
  them, so the narration must not either: no names in any credits or chapter announcements, no character voices, and no
  gender or age assigned. Their speech is in italics, with no quotation marks or tags. [R] Read them close to the
  narration, quieter and slower, with longer pauses between their short lines, and leave a clear space before the next
  speaker. Their two closing words in E00 and E50 echo each other: give them the same reading both times. The Interlude
  has no speech; read it as the prologue's narration. E50 says *the Veil* once: give it no special weight.
- **POV in the headings** (author, 2026-10-05). Chapter headings name the POV: *Chapter 1 · Seraphine*. Multi-POV
  chapters name both in on-page order: E12 and E14 Seraphine then Lucien; E21 Seraphine, Lucien, Seraphine; E46
  alternating, Lucien first. *Prologue*, *Supplement*, *Interlude* and *Epilogue* carry no name: the presences are never
  named. POV changes only at section breaks. One flag: E49 has a short cutaway at the Velvet Vein with no protagonist
  present, under a *Seraphine* heading. In the listening EPUB the chapter number and the name are separate headings.
- **Section breaks** are a centred `---` (286 in the book). [R] Use a two- to three-second pause and no sound. Engines
  may read the line as nothing, or skip the pause. Check the first few.
- **The Supplement (S02)** is a newspaper page in three parts: a local news item, a city notice to users of Jackson
  Square, and a column of other items (Scotland, Loyola). [R] Use a slightly brisker news-reading voice. Read the
  bold headlines as headlines, with a beat after each. The capitalised standing heads are words, not letters. Quoted
  sources (a vendor, a visitor, the card reader) are reported speech: no character voices, though the card reader is
  Vernice to anyone listening closely. **Spoken frame** [R, ATC]: a human narrator may introduce it as *From the
  newspaper*. ElevenReader will read the heading *Supplement*.
- **Dialogue cut-offs.** About 91 lines end in an em dash, an interruption. [R] Cut the word off sharply and leave no
  trailing breath. Engines often read the dash as a pause and finish the word, or drop it.

## 4. Casting note

**The author's choice.** The options below are this sheet's analysis, not a recommendation adopted anywhere.

**A. One narrator with real Louisiana speech** (ideally a New Orleans or Acadiana native, able to do downtown, Yat and
Cajun colour without caricature).
- *For:* the place is the book's grounding, and most of the cast is local. E19 depends on a narrator who *knows* the
  right pronunciations. One voice suits a single-voice third-person narrator (§1), and it is cheaper and simpler.
- *Against:* Lucien, Baz and Elisabet then depend on that narrator's European accents, which are harder to do well
  than Louisiana is to fake badly. Lucien's chapters are about a quarter of the book.

**B. A duet**: a Louisiana narrator for Seraphine's, Inez's and Caro's chapters, and a second narrator, with a light
European accent, for Lucien's and Baz's chapters (or Lucien's only).
- *For:* POV changes become audible without labels. Lucien's outsider ear for the city (E19 above all) is heard from
  the outside. It matches his and Seraphine's two ways of hearing the same place.
- *Against:* E12, E14, E21 and E46 switch POV mid-chapter, so the narrators must hand off at a section break.
  Dialogue must match across narrators: each must perform the other lead's voice and the shared locals consistently.
  Baz's chapters pull a third accent into the second narrator's range. The presences (E00, I01, E50) need a decision:
  one narrator, or both. It costs more, and the second narrator must not *other* the city.
- **Caro's chapters** (E28, E42) would sit with the first narrator in either option. Her Chicago voice needs a
  narrator who can drop the Louisiana colour.

**C. For the ElevenReader listen now:** a single neutral American voice will mispronounce most of §1.3. [R] Use this
sheet as a checklist rather than expecting the app to fix it. Whether the app accepts a custom pronunciation list is
not checked here. ElevenLabs' production tools have offered pronunciation dictionaries; a human-narrator brief would
carry §1 regardless.
